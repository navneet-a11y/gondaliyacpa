## The angle — three subjects no other page in this category touches

The four pages published in this category (607–610) are about working, owning a business, or
holding American status. A snowbird does none of those things. They are a Canadian resident who
spends part of the year in the United States and very often owns property there, and that
produces three exposures that appear nowhere else on the site:

1. **Presence, not work.** The **substantial presence test** can make a long-staying visitor a US
   tax resident on days alone. The instrument that addresses it is the **closer connection
   exception**, claimed on **Form 8840**. Row 610 counts days *worked*; this page counts days
   *present*, and the relevant form is different.
2. **US real property.** A Florida or Arizona home brings **FIRPTA** withholding on sale, a
   possible **Form 1040-NR** rental filing, and a genuinely counter-intuitive Canadian point:
   **personal-use property is excluded** from specified foreign property, so the vacation home
   many people assume is a Form T1135 item usually is not — until it earns rent.
3. **US estate tax exposure on US-situs assets.** A non-resident who dies owning American real
   property or certain American securities can be within the US estate tax regime, with relief
   under **treaty Article XXIX B**. Nothing else on this site mentions US estate tax at all, and
   it is the single most under-advised exposure this group carries.

Deliberately **not** this page: employment income and Article XV (row 610), citizenship-based
taxation (608), permanent-resident status and expatriation (609), corporate structuring (607).
