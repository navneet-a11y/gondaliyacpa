## The angle — the business that owns no inventory

Every other e-commerce page on the site assumes the seller owns stock. Apparel Brands (49479),
Direct-to-Consumer Brands (53436), Wholesale E-Commerce Sellers (53789) and Electronics Resellers
(49609) are all inventory businesses. Print-on-demand is the opposite and that inverts the file:

1. **No inventory, so no inventory accounting.** Nothing to value under ITA subsection 10(1), no
   year-end count, no shrinkage. What replaces it is a cost of sales that is purely a supplier
   invoice per order, which is simpler to record and much easier to get wrong on timing.
2. **The supplier is usually foreign and charges no GST/HST.** That makes the self-assessment rules
   for imported taxable supplies live, and it means there is far less input tax credit than an
   owner expects, which is the single most common cash surprise on these files.
3. **The platform may be the one collecting tax, not you.** Canada's distribution platform operator
   rules can put the GST/HST collection obligation on the marketplace. Whether they apply changes
   what the business must register for and remit.
4. **The asset is the artwork.** There is no warehouse; the value is intellectual property, which
   raises the question of what is deductible now and what is a capital expenditure.

Deliberately **not** this page: inventory and landed cost (49479, 53436, 53789), secondhand goods
(49609), course and digital download products (53437).

| 2 | **No inventory is held**, so there is nothing to value under **ITA subsection 10(1)**. Cost of sales is the per-order supplier charge, and it is matched to the order it belongs to rather than to the month the card was charged. |
| 3 | **GST/HST registration** is required once worldwide taxable supplies exceed **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration can also be voluntary below that, which is often worth it when input tax credits exist. |
| 4 | **Place of supply rules** set the rate by the customer's province, so one Ontario business can be charging several different rates in the same week. Ontario is **13%**. |
| 5 | **Exports are generally zero-rated** under **Schedule VI Part V of the Excise Tax Act**, so sales shipped to customers outside Canada generally carry no GST/HST while still counting as taxable supplies for registration. |
| 6 | **Foreign suppliers usually charge no GST/HST.** Where a service or intangible is acquired from a non-resident for use in Canada, the **imported taxable supply** self-assessment rules in the Excise Tax Act can require the recipient to account for the tax. |
| 7 | **Distribution platform operator rules** can make the marketplace responsible for collecting and remitting GST/HST on sales made through it. Whether they apply to a given platform and sale is checked rather than assumed. |
