## The angle — the adviser whose own classification is the thing they advise on

Nothing on the site covers health and safety consulting. The nearest neighbours are Engineering
Consultants (53519), Environmental Consultants (53518), Corporate Trainers (53955), Grant Writing
Consultants (54044) and Staffing Agencies (49811), and none of them touches the machinery specific
to this practice. The site also already carries a **WSIB Reporting & Compliance Services** page
(28197) that no consultant page currently links to, and which belongs here more than anywhere.

Four subjects are specific to this consultant and appear on no page on the site:

1. **The personal services business risk, pointed back at the adviser.** This is the single most
   expensive thing on the file and the irony is not lost on anyone: a consultant who advises clients
   on worker classification frequently has a classification problem of their own. **ITA subsection
   125(7)** defines a personal services business as one where an incorporated employee performs the
   services and would reasonably be regarded as an officer or employee of the entity receiving them
   but for the existence of the corporation, where the corporation does not employ more than five
   full-time employees throughout the year. A safety consultant embedded full time on one client's
   site for eighteen months is the textbook fact pattern. The consequence is brutal: the small
   business deduction is denied, and **ITA paragraph 18(1)(p)** restricts deductions to little more
   than salary and a short list of specified amounts — no rent, no professional fees, no vehicle.
2. **WSIB on both sides of the engagement.** The consultant advises clients on classification,
   premium rates, claims and clearance certificates, and simultaneously has their own registration
   question: whether they are an independent operator who must register, and whether their own
   workers or subcontract trainers create a premium exposure. The clearance certificate matters in
   both directions, because a client who hires an unregistered contractor can be left carrying their
   premiums. No other consultant page on the site deals with this.
3. **Advice that carries statutory consequence.** An opinion that proves wrong has consequences
   under occupational health and safety legislation, so professional liability and errors and
   omissions cover is not optional. Premiums are deductible; the harder question is the outstanding
   claim, where **ITA paragraph 18(1)(e)** bars a deduction for a reserve or contingent amount, so an
   accrual that is sound for accounting purposes is not deductible for tax until the obligation is
   real.
4. **A second revenue stream with completely different accounting.** Most of these practices also
   deliver certification training, and course revenue behaves nothing like consulting fees: prepaid
   seat blocks and multi-session programmes are deferred across delivery, per-certificate fees are
   paid out to an accrediting body, and whether the course is an **exempt educational supply** under
   **Part III of Schedule V** to the Excise Tax Act decides whether the input tax credits on that
   side of the business exist at all.

Deliberately **not** this page: engineering or environmental practice (53519, 53518), general
corporate training delivery (53955), placing workers with clients (49811).
