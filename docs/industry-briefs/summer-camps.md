## The angle — the business that earns a year's revenue in eight weeks and houses its own staff

Three existing pages sit near this one and none of them covers what actually defines a camp.
**Campgrounds** (52199) sells sites and accommodation with no programming and no children in its
care. **Adventure Tourism Businesses** (52170) runs guided outdoor programmes, but for paying
adults, on trips rather than sessions. **After-School Programs** (57815) has the same children and
the same child care characterisation, but runs through the school term, in somebody else's
building, with no residential staff and no idle site. **Ski Resorts** (52250) is the mirror image
on seasonality and is worth cross-linking for that reason alone.

So this page is scoped to the two things only a summer camp has:

1. **The compression.** Registrations are taken from January to May for eight to ten weeks of
   delivery in July and August. Cash arrives five months before the revenue is earned, the earning
   window is a single summer, and the site carries cost for the other forty-two weeks. Every
   consequence on the page follows from that: deferred revenue, instalments set against tax rather
   than cash, a year end chosen deliberately, and capital property that is idle ten months a year.
2. **The two obligations about other people.** A camp issues **child care expense receipts** that
   parents rely on to claim a deduction under **ITA section 63** — a compliance obligation about
   the parents' returns rather than the camp's, and the direct analogue of Form T2202 on the
   career colleges page. And a residential camp **houses and feeds its own staff**, which puts
   board and lodging squarely into taxable-benefit territory under **ITA paragraph 6(6)(a)**.

The sharpest single fact on the page, and the one most camps get wrong: **a day camp and an
overnight camp are not the same thing in a parent's hands.** Day camp fees are ordinary child care
expenses; amounts for an overnight camp are subject to a separate **periodic (weekly) limit** per
child. A camp that runs both and issues one undifferentiated receipt has handed every one of its
families a problem.

The second sharpest, because it is money the camp is currently getting wrong in its own favour or
against it: **CPP contributions are not withheld from an employee under 18.** Pensionable
employment begins the month after the employee turns 18. A camp whose counsellor roster is largely
sixteen and seventeen year olds has a payroll setup question that no generic preparer will raise.

| 1 | **Camp fees paid for the care of an eligible child can be child care expenses** to the parent under **ITA section 63**, deductible generally by the lower-income supporting person, subject to annual limits by the child's age and eligibility. The camp is not the claimant; the camp is the issuer of the receipt the claim rests on. |
| 2 | Amounts paid for a child's attendance at an **overnight camp or boarding school** are subject to a **separate periodic limit per week** per eligible child, distinct from the annual limit that applies generally. **No amount, rate or weekly figure is stated on this page.** |
| 3 | A **child care receipt** identifies the camp, the child, the amount paid for care and the period it covers, and where the provider is an **individual** rather than a corporation it must show that individual's **social insurance number**. A receipt that bundles day and overnight attendance, or care with non-care items, is not usable as issued. |
| 4 | **Camp fees received before the session runs are a prepayment, not revenue.** Revenue is earned as the programme is delivered, so registrations and deposits taken in winter and spring sit as deferred revenue until the session they relate to. Cancellation and refund terms are a liability measured against that balance. |
| 5 | **CPP: pensionable employment begins the month after an employee turns 18.** No CPP contributions are withheld or matched for an employee under 18. **EI has no equivalent age exemption** — insurable employment does not turn on age — so a camp's under-18 staff are generally EI-insurable while not yet CPP-pensionable. |
| 6 | **Board and lodging provided to an employee is generally a taxable benefit.** **ITA paragraph 6(6)(a)** excludes the value of board and lodging at a **special work site** — where the duties required the employee to be away from their principal place of residence — or at a **remote work location**, for a period of not less than **36 hours**, subject to conditions including that the employee maintained a self-contained domestic establishment elsewhere as their principal place of residence. Whether a given camp and a given staff member fall inside it is a question of fact. |
| 7 | **Schedule V, Part VI, section 12** exempts a supply of a **recreational programme provided primarily to children 14 and under** where supplied by a **public sector body**, with conditions. The exemption turns on the **status of the supplier**, so it is generally unavailable to a camp carried on as a for-profit business, and potentially available to a non-profit or municipal camp. Whether any given camp qualifies is a question of fact and is not concluded on this page. |
| 8 | A supplier making **exempt** supplies **cannot claim input tax credits** on the related inputs. A camp with a mix of exempt and taxable supplies — tuck shop sales, merchandise, equipment rental, off-season facility rental — apportions its credits on a fair and reasonable basis applied consistently. |
| 9 | **GST/HST registration** is required once worldwide taxable supplies exceed **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Ontario is **13%**. Exempt supplies do not count toward the threshold. The **Quick Method** is capped at **$400,000** of annual taxable supplies. |
| 10 | **Capital cost allowance** recovers the cost of camp property over time rather than on purchase: cabins and dining halls, docks and waterfront structures, canoes and equipment, vehicles, and leasehold improvements where the site is leased. Different property falls in different classes. **The classes are named where relevant; no rate is stated on this page.** |
| 11 | **ITA paragraph 18(1)(b)** bars the deduction of a capital outlay. Off-season work that restores property to its prior condition is generally a current repair; work that betters the property or creates an asset of enduring value is capital. The distinction turns on the facts of each job, not on the invoice heading. |

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

- **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount.** The site-wide
  conflict is unresolved (page 53439 carries 11.2% / $1.275M while roughly twenty other pages carry
  12.2% / $1.25M). Until the firm confirms its current figures, this page states neither — which
  matters more here than on most pages, because selling a camp usually means selling land.
- **No child care expense amount of any kind** — not the annual limit per child, not the overnight
  camp weekly limit, not the earned-income percentage. The mechanism is explained; every figure is
  checked against the current year.
- **No capital cost allowance rate.** Classes are named where the characterisation is settled;
  rates are not published.
- **No conclusion on whether a given camp's supplies are exempt**, and no conclusion on whether a
  given camp is a public sector body for the purposes of Schedule V, Part VI, section 12.
- **No conclusion on whether ITA paragraph 6(6)(a) reaches a particular camp or staff member.** The
  special work site and remote work location tests are applied to facts, not asserted on a page.
- **No conclusion on any worker's employment status.** Counsellor, instructor, contractor — decided
  on the whole relationship, on the camp's facts.
- **No advice on charity or non-profit status.** Many camps are registered charities or non-profit
  organisations with an entirely different filing regime (T3010 or T1044 rather than a T2). This
  page is written for camps carried on as a business; the structure question is scoped at the
  consultation rather than answered here.
- **No non-accounting advice**: no camp licensing or regulatory requirements, no health and safety
  or supervision ratios, no accreditation standards, no insurance product recommendation, no advice
  on police record checks or screening obligations.
- **No foreign rules.** Camps hiring international staff or taking US campers raise cross-border
  questions that are scoped separately.
- **No fee beyond the published pricing block.**
