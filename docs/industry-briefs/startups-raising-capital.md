## The angle — the round itself, and what it can quietly cost

The site already carries **Accounting for Startups** (16096), which is day-to-day startup
bookkeeping and compliance, and the investor-side pages **Angel Investors** (52666), **Venture
Capital Firms** (52683) and **Private Equity Firms** (52682). This page is none of those. It is the
company during a financing, where the structural consequences land on the company and its founders
rather than on the people writing the cheque.

Four subjects are specific to a company raising capital and appear on no page on the site:

1. **The round can cost you CCPC status, and almost nobody models it.** A Canadian-controlled private
   corporation is defined in **ITA subsection 125(7)** as a private corporation resident in Canada
   that is not controlled, directly or indirectly in any manner whatever, by non-residents, by public
   corporations, or by any combination of them. A round led by a non-resident fund, or by a public
   company, can end that status. What goes with it is not small: the small business deduction, the
   enhanced refundable SR&ED investment tax credit, the deferral on CCPC stock option shares under
   **ITA subsection 7(1.1)**, and the founders' access to the lifetime capital gains exemption on
   qualified small business corporation shares.
2. **A change of control resets the loss pool.** Where control is acquired, **ITA subsection 249(4)**
   deems a tax year to end immediately before the acquisition, and **ITA subsection 111(5)** then
   restricts the non-capital losses carried forward so that they are deductible only against income
   from the same or a similar business carried on for profit. For a company that has spent four years
   accumulating losses as its principal asset, that is the most expensive line in the term sheet, and
   it is never in the term sheet.
3. **Money raised is not revenue, and the costs of raising it have their own rule.** A share issuance
   is a capital transaction: the money is share capital and contributed surplus, not income, and the
   paid-up capital it creates is a different number from the shareholder's adjusted cost base.
   Meanwhile **ITA paragraph 20(1)(e)** lets the costs of issuing shares be deducted over five years
   rather than expensed in the year they are incurred.
4. **The data room is a tax file.** Diligence on a priced round looks at option grants, contractor
   classification, payroll remittances, shareholder loans and unfiled returns, and what it finds
   becomes a representation, an indemnity or a price adjustment. Everything on that list is cheaper
   to fix before the term sheet than during the closing.

Deliberately **not** this page: ordinary startup bookkeeping and compliance (16096), the investor's
own tax position (52666, 52682, 52683), and securities law in any form — see Open items.

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

1. **No securities law content of any kind** — no prospectus exemption, no offering memorandum
   requirement, no accredited investor test, no filing obligation and no view on whether a given
   raise is compliant. That is counsel's work, and this page says so.
2. **No SR&ED rate, expenditure limit, taxable capital threshold or grind figure.** The mechanism is
   named and the CCPC condition is named; the amounts are not, because they have moved recently and
   a wrong number here would be worse than no number.
3. No conclusion that a particular round does or does not cause an **acquisition of control**, or
   does or does not end **CCPC status**. Both turn on the actual cap table, the share terms and the
   shareholders' agreement.
4. No conclusion on whether a **SAFE or convertible note is debt or equity** for tax purposes. It
   turns on the instrument's terms.
5. **No valuation.** We do not set or opine on the fair market value of shares for an option grant or
   for anything else, and no multiple or benchmark appears on this page.
6. **No legal advice on the subscription agreement, the shareholders' agreement or the term sheet** —
   no view on liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, drag-along or vesting terms.
7. No treaty rate stated for any particular investor's country of residence, and no conclusion on
   treaty entitlement.
8. **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount**, for the same
   unresolved-conflict reason as on 57627, 57645, 57751, 57752, 57768, 57776, 57836, 57843, 57973 and
   58918.
9. No Canadian Entrepreneurs' Incentive rate, limit or schedule.
10. No conclusion on whether a non-resident's shares are taxable Canadian property, and no
    section 116 clearance outcome predicted.
11. No CCA class or rate stated for equipment or software.
12. No statement about another country's treatment of the company or its investors; we advise on
    Canadian tax and coordinate where another jurisdiction is involved.
13. No fee quoted beyond the published pricing chrome.
