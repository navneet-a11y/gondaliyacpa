This page inherits `docs/industry-briefs/pet-care-spine.md` in full. All twelve locked facts in the
spine apply here: the occupation is not provincially licensed while the activity is municipally

supply at 13% in Ontario with the $30,000 registration threshold measured over four consecutive

audit exposure on the Form RC4110 factors, the vehicle regime runs through Class 10 and Class 10.1,
and the incorporation and compliance baseline is as the spine states it. **Nothing in the spine's

conclusion, not an Employment Standards entitlement, not an insurance term, not a gift-certificate
or forfeited-deposit characterisation, not a Class 10.1 dollar limit, not a liability conclusion,
not a home-office conclusion, not SR&ED, and not a single industry statistic.

Three siblings are being built in the same category block and this page is deliberately distinct
from all three. **Pet grooming** is a page about a premises with a table, a tub and a retail shelf,
where inventory under ITA section 10 and a Class 13 leasehold carry the file. **Pet boarding** is a
page about a facility sold by the night, where occupancy, holiday deposits and a built-out building
carry the file. **Pet sitting** is the closest neighbour and the one this page works hardest to
avoid: pet sitting is about working inside a client's home, bookings made far ahead around the
client's travel, and the duties a sitter performs while the household is empty. **This page is none
of that.** Its spine is a business made of short visits where the map is the margin and the walkers
are the audit risk: route density and unpaid travel time as the thing that decides margin, group
walks as the capacity-constrained source of that margin, walker classification as the single
largest exposure, a vehicle and a mileage logbook instead of any premises at all, and prepaid walk
packages against the $30,000 line. This page does not discuss overnight stays, key custody as a
centrepiece, or house-sitting duties. The deliberate pivot away from Pet Sitting is that a sitter's
economics are set by the hours spent in one home, while a walker's economics are set by the
distance between the next two doors.

## Step 1 research lock — 12 rows

| # | Item | Locked answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is dog walking a licensed occupation in Ontario? | **No.** There is no provincial college, protected title or licensing statute for dog walkers in Ontario, and this page may not imply otherwise. What exists instead is **municipal business licensing and municipal animal-related bylaws**, which differ by municipality. **Open item:** no municipal licensing requirement, fee, permit or threshold is asserted for any municipality. |
| 2 | What decides margin in a dog walking business, and where does it appear in the books? | **Route density** — how close the next client is — and it appears nowhere on a standard income statement. Two businesses with identical revenue and identical hourly pay can have completely different margins because one spends its day walking and the other spends it driving. The costing unit is therefore the **route**, not the month: visits, drive legs and walker hours are tracked per route so travel time between paid visits is costed instead of absorbed invisibly. No per-visit rate, walk price or margin figure is asserted. |
| 3 | How do group walks change the economics, and what constrains them? | A walker taking several dogs on one outing earns multiple fees for one hour of labour, which is where the margin in this business actually comes from. It is **capacity-constrained per walker** and may also be constrained by **municipal animal bylaws** governing how many animals one person may walk or have under control. **Open item:** that bylaw question is real and variable by municipality; **no limit, number or requirement is asserted**, and it is confirmed with the municipality. |
| 4 | Are the walkers employees or independent contractors? | **A question of fact, never a label.** Engaging walkers as subcontractors is the norm in this trade, and the determination runs on the **Form RC4110** factors — who sets the schedule, who holds the client relationship, who supplies the leads and the vehicle, whether the walker can profit or lose, and how integrated the work is — not on the invoice or on what the arrangement was called. Genuine contractors are reported on **T4A**. **Open item:** no conclusion is asserted for any walker, and **no personal services business conclusion** under ITA subsection 125(7) is asserted either. |
| 5 | What follows if a contractor roster fails the test? | **Back source deductions, interest and penalties on the business's largest cost line.** Compliance baseline where there are employees: **WSIB** from the first hire; source deductions remitted on the **PD7A** by the 15th of the following month with CRA's late-remittance penalty reaching **10%** (graduated); **T4** slips and Summary by the last day of February; Ontario **Employer Health Tax** past the **$1,000,000** exemption. |
| 6 | What about Employment Standards? | **Employment Standards Act, 2000** entitlements are real and are employment counsel's to quantify. The statute and the Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development are named. **Open item:** no overtime rate, public holiday pay calculation, vacation rate, termination notice or severance figure is asserted. |
| 7 | How is the vehicle treated, and what is the evidence? | A walker's car sits in **Class 10**, or in **Class 10.1** where it meets the **ITA subsection 248(1)** definition of an automobile and cost more than the prescribed limit, in which case each such vehicle sits alone in its own class and **neither recapture nor terminal loss arises on disposal**. A **mileage logbook is the evidence and the single most valuable record this business keeps**, because driving is most of the working day. **ITA section 67.1** limits the food and beverage portion of expenses to 50%. **Open item:** whether any given vehicle is an "automobile" is not asserted, the prescribed Class 10.1 cost limit is **not stated**, and **no per-kilometre rate or allowance figure is stated**. |
| 8 | What are the other CCA classes on an asset-light file? | **Class 8 at 20%** for leads, harnesses, crates, carriers and equipment; **Class 50 at 55%** for computers and phones; **Class 12 at 100%** for scheduling and routing software; **Class 14.1 at 5%** for goodwill and an **acquired client list**, which is what one walking business actually buys from another. Pools are reported on **Schedule 8**. There is no premises, so no Class 13 leasehold and no Class 1 building on a typical file. |
| 9 | Where does a workspace in the home sit? | It is **reviewed on the facts, never asserted.** Whether a workspace in the home is deductible for a particular claimant, and whether a home-based operation is permitted at a given address under municipal bylaws, are both **open items**. The page names them as real questions and claims no conclusion for anyone. |
| 10 | How are prepaid walk packages taxed? | A block of ten or twenty walks sold up front is **included in income under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a)** and **reserved out under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m)** to the extent the walks have not yet been performed. An owner who treats a sold package as earned revenue is paying tax on walks still owed. **ETA section 181.2** governs gift certificates and **ETA section 182** deals with a forfeited deposit where an agreement is breached. **Open item:** no package is characterised as a gift certificate and no cancellation charge as a forfeited deposit. Bad debt on an unpaid client invoice is deductible under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(p)**. |
| 11 | What is the HST position? | Dog walking is a **taxable supply at 13% in Ontario** — there is no animal health care exemption. Registration is required once taxable supplies pass **$30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters, a line a solo walker with a full book of short visits crosses quietly. Returns are filed on **GST34**; place of supply follows the recipient's address. |
| 12 | Where is the hard legal boundary, and what is the corporate baseline? | Practising veterinary medicine is restricted to licensed veterinarians under the **Veterinarians Act**, regulated by the **College of Veterinarians of Ontario**; the **Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019**, enforced by **Animal Welfare Services**, applies to anyone with custody of an animal. **Open item:** where either line falls is the client's question for their own counsel and veterinarian, and this page gives **no animal-health, behaviour, training, handling, leash, breed or welfare guidance of any kind**. Corporate baseline per the spine: roughly **12.2%** Ontario combined on the first **$500,000** of active income under **ITA section 125** against a top personal rate of **53.53%**; **$1.25M** Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under **ITA 110.6**; section **85** rollover on **Form T2057**; records six years under **ITA section 230**; late filing **5%** plus **1%** per month to twelve months; VDP on **Form RC199** cancelling penalties with roughly **50%** interest relief; Notice of Objection within **90 days**; taxpayer relief on **Form RC4288**; a **CSRS 4200** compilation across two fiscal years. |
