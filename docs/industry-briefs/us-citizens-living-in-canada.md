## The angle — what makes this page different from its three siblings

This is the **citizenship-based taxation** page: the person lives in Canada, intends to keep
living here, and will file in both countries for as long as they hold the citizenship. The
subject is the permanent double-filing life and the specifically Canadian assets that make it
expensive.

- **Row 607** is corporate and outbound. Nothing here is about expansion structuring, transfer
  pricing or permanent establishment.
- **Row 609** (green card holders) shares the filing regime but is centred on **residency
  abandonment and long-term-resident expatriation**. This page therefore does **not** make
  expatriation its subject; where giving up status comes up it is named and sent to that page's
  territory and to counsel.
- **Row 610** (Canadians working in the US) is about **employment income crossing the border** —
  Article XV, the substantial presence test, FICA and totalization. This page is about a person
  resident in Canada, not commuting.

The four things this page owns:
1. **The obligation does not end at the border.** A US citizen files a Form 1040 reporting
   worldwide income regardless of where they live, with Form 1116 or Form 2555 for relief.
2. **The Canadian tax-efficient account is the US problem.** Whether a **TFSA**, **RESP**, FHSA or
   RDSP is a foreign trust engaging **Form 3520** and **Form 3520-A**, and whether a Canadian
   mutual fund or ETF is a passive foreign investment company engaging **Form 8621**, are the
   questions that go unanswered until a notice arrives. **RRSPs** are addressed by the treaty.
3. **Owning a Canadian corporation is the single most expensive thing a US person here can do.**
   A CCPC that is entirely ordinary on the Canadian side sits inside the **Form 5471** regime,
   and the anti-deferral rules behind it can tax undistributed corporate income in the
   shareholder's hands.
4. **Account reporting is separate from tax.** **FinCEN Form 114** (FBAR) and **Form 8938** are
   information reporting with their own regimes, and Canadian institutions report under FATCA.
