## The angle — the company that stopped trading and was never closed

The site already carries **Businesses Winding Down** (56800), which is about ending a business:
final returns, dissolution, the decisions a departing owner makes. This page is the opposite
situation and a far more common one. The business stopped. Nobody closed the corporation. It has now
been sitting there for two years, or five, or nine, and the owner's honest belief is that because
nothing is happening, nothing is due.

Four subjects are specific to the dormant shell and appear on no page on the site:

1. **A nil return is still a return.** Under **ITA subsection 150(1)** every corporation resident in
   Canada must file a T2 for each tax year, within six months of the end of that year, whether or not
   it carried on business and whether or not any tax is payable. Dormancy is not an exemption; it is
   simply a return with zeros on it.
2. **The penalty arithmetic is the opposite of what people assume.** The familiar late filing penalty
   is **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month, which on a nil balance is nil — and that is
   exactly why unfiled dormant years are so common. But failing to file still exposes the corporation
   to the penalty in **ITA subsection 162(7)**, the greater of **$100** and **$25** for each day of
   default to a maximum of **100** days, and a demand to file under **subsection 150(2)** changes the
   position again.
3. **Ontario's annual return is no longer part of the T2, and default can dissolve you.** Since 2021
   the **Ontario Annual Return** is filed separately through the **Ontario Business Registry** rather
   than with the corporate tax return, which is why thousands of corporations stopped filing it
   without noticing. A corporation in default of its obligations under the **Ontario Business
   Corporations Act** can be dissolved by the Director for that default, which is how an owner
   discovers that the company holding their property no longer exists.
4. **The accounts stay open, and so does the directors' exposure.** A GST/HST registration keeps
   generating filing obligations for every reporting period until the account is actually closed, and
   the same is true of a payroll account. Meanwhile **ITA section 227.1** and **ETA section 323** make
   directors personally liable for unremitted source deductions and net tax, with the two-year
   limitation running from when the director last ceased to hold office — so keeping a dormant
   company alive is also keeping that clock from starting.

Deliberately **not** this page: winding a business down as a decision (56800), selling it (58918),
or holding investments through a corporation that is actually active (26666).

| 1 | **ITA subsection 150(1)** requires every corporation resident in Canada to file a return of income for each tax year, **within six months of the end of the year**, whether or not it carried on a business and whether or not tax is payable. There is no exemption for inactivity. |
| 2 | The **late filing penalty** under **ITA subsection 162(1)** is **5%** of the unpaid tax at the filing due date plus **1%** of that amount for each complete month the return is late, to a maximum of **twelve** months. Where no tax is owing, that penalty computes to nil. |
| 3 | **ITA subsection 162(7)** imposes, where no other penalty is specified, a penalty equal to the **greater of $100 and $25 for each day of default**, to a maximum of **100** days. **Subsection 150(2)** allows the Minister to demand a return by notice. |
| 4 | The **Ontario Annual Return** is a separate filing from the T2. Since **2021** it is filed through the **Ontario Business Registry** and is no longer submitted with the corporate income tax return. |
| 5 | Under the **Ontario Business Corporations Act**, a corporation that is in default of its filing obligations may be **dissolved** by the Director for that default. A dissolved Ontario corporation may apply to be **revived**. |
| 6 | On dissolution, property of the corporation that has not been distributed may **forfeit to the Crown**, and dealing with it afterwards requires the corporation to be revived or the property to be recovered. |
| 7 | A **GST/HST registrant** must file a return for **every reporting period**, including periods with no activity, until the registration is cancelled. A person who has ceased commercial activity generally must request cancellation of the registration rather than simply stop filing, and cancellation has consequences for property the corporation still holds. |
| 8 | A **payroll (RP) account** likewise remains open until it is closed, with the associated reporting obligations, and T4 information returns are due for any year in which remuneration was paid. |
| 9 | **ITA section 227.1** makes directors jointly and severally liable for amounts a corporation failed to deduct, withhold or remit, and **ETA section 323** does the same for net tax. In each case an assessment of a director cannot be made more than **two years** after the person last ceased to be a director. |
| 10 | **Non-capital losses** carry forward **20** years; **net capital losses** carry forward indefinitely. Where control of the corporation is acquired, **ITA subsection 249(4)** deems a tax year to end and **subsection 111(5)** restricts the non-capital losses to income from the same or a similar business. |
| 11 | A dormant corporation can still be **associated** with other corporations, and associated corporations must share the **$500,000** business limit for the small business deduction. The group's taxable capital also affects that limit. |
| 12 | **Records** must be kept six years under **ITA section 230**, and the records of a corporation that is **dissolved** must be retained for **two years after the date of dissolution**. Canadian baseline: Notice of Objection within **90 days**; relief on **Form RC4288**; **Form RC199** for the Voluntary Disclosures Program. |

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

1. No statement of what the ITA subsection 162(7) penalty would be in a given case, and no prediction
   of whether the CRA will assess it on a particular set of unfiled years.
2. **No Ontario Business Registry fee, and no corporate registry fee of any kind.**
3. No legal advice on dissolution, revival or the articles that effect either. Those are legal
   filings; we prepare and file the tax returns and coordinate with the reader's counsel.
4. No conclusion on whether a particular corporation should be dissolved or kept dormant. It depends
   on the loss pool, what the corporation holds, whether the name or the entity has future value, and
   the owner's own plans.
5. No conclusion that a particular corporation is or is not **associated** with another, which turns
   on the actual share ownership and control.
6. No statement about the corporate law of a province other than Ontario, or of Canada federally,
   beyond noting that a federally incorporated company and a company incorporated elsewhere have
   their own registry obligations.
7. **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount**, for the same
   unresolved-conflict reason as on 57627, 57645, 57751, 57752, 57768, 57776, 57836, 57843, 57973,
   58918 and 58923.
8. No combined small business tax rate.
9. No CCA class or rate for property still held in the shell.
10. No conclusion on the GST/HST consequences of cancelling a registration where the corporation
    still holds property; the mechanism is named and the calculation is done on the file.
11. No statement that a director has or has not ceased to hold office, and no conclusion on whether
    the two-year limitation has begun or expired in a given case.
12. No fee quoted beyond the published pricing chrome.
