## The angle — why this is not row 608 with a different noun

Rows 608 and 609 share a filing regime, and that is exactly why this page must be built on what
is *different* rather than on what overlaps. The difference is one sentence:

> For a US citizen, no tax filing decision can cost them their citizenship. For a green card
> holder, tax filing decisions and immigration status touch each other.

That single fact reorganises the whole page. **Status is the asset here**, and the page is about
the interaction between a tax position and that asset:

1. **A lawful permanent resident is taxed as a US resident** on worldwide income, the same as a
   citizen. Living in Canada does not change that by itself.
2. **Both countries can treat the person as resident at once** — Canada under **ITA subsection
   2(1)** on residential ties, the United States on status — which makes the **Article IV**
   tie-breaker a live question rather than a theoretical one.
3. **Claiming treaty residence in Canada is not a free move.** A treaty-based position is
   disclosed on **Form 8833**, and the relationship between asserting non-US residence for tax
   and holding permanent residence for immigration is precisely the question this page raises and
   refers to immigration counsel. **The page gives no immigration advice and states no immigration
   consequence** — but it refuses to pretend the interaction does not exist, which is the single
   most useful thing it can do for this reader.
4. **Letting the status go has its own tax regime.** Expatriation, **Form 8854**, the long-term
   resident concept, and **Form I-407** as the immigration instrument. Row 608 deliberately
   deferred all of this to here.
5. **If they then leave Canada, Canada charges its own departure tax** — **ITA subsection
   128.1(4)** deemed disposition, **Form T1161**, **Form T1243**, and the **subsection 220(4.5)**
   deferral elected on **Form T1244**. This is the page where both exits are on the table at once.

**Depth discipline against row 608:** the TFSA/RESP foreign-trust question, the PFIC question and
Form 5471 are **named once each and cross-linked, not developed**. Row 608 owns those. This page
spends its depth on status, the tie-breaker, expatriation and departure.
