## The angle — investor, not operator

`/accountant-for-farms/` (51850) is live and covers the **operating** farm: crops, livestock, machinery,
AgriStability. A farmland *investor* does not farm. They own land and collect cash rent or a crop
share, or they hold it for appreciation. That difference drives four things page 51850 does not
touch, and they are the spine of this page:

1. **Cash rent is usually property income, not farming income.** That single reclassification
   changes which deductions are available, whether the land can still be qualified farm or fishing
   property, and whether anything is active business income at all.
2. **The restricted farm loss rule in ITA section 31.** Where farming is not the taxpayer's chief
   source of income, farm losses are restricted rather than fully deductible. Investors who do some
   farming on the side walk into this constantly and operators rarely do.
3. **Land is not depreciable.** No capital cost allowance is available on the land itself, while tile
   drainage, fencing and farm buildings are depreciable. Investors routinely assume the opposite.
4. **The exit.** Qualified farm or fishing property and the ITA section 110.6 capital gains
   exemption, the intergenerational rollover to a child under ITA subsections 70(9) and 73(3), and
   the capital-versus-income question on a sale.

Deliberately **not** this page: operating a farm (51850), flipping houses (48320), syndicated
real estate (52658).

| 1 | A Canadian resident is taxed on worldwide income under **ITA subsection 2(1)**; a non-resident on Canadian-source income under **ITA subsection 2(3)**. Farmland in Ontario is taxable Canadian property either way. |
| 2 | **Cash rent from farmland is generally property income**, reported on **Form T776**, not farming income on **Form T2042**. A crop share can fall on either side depending on the risk the owner bears. The characterisation is a question of fact and it is the first thing we settle. |
| 3 | **Land is not depreciable property.** No capital cost allowance on the land. Tile drainage, fencing, farm buildings and other improvements are depreciable, each in its own class, and we allocate the purchase price between land and those assets at acquisition rather than years later. |
| 4 | **ITA section 31 restricts farm losses** where farming is not the chief source of income. The restricted portion is not lost — it carries forward — but it is not deductible against other income in the year. |
| 5 | **Qualified farm or fishing property** can access the lifetime capital gains exemption under **ITA section 110.6**. Eligibility turns on ownership period and a "used principally in the business of farming" test, and it is determined on the facts of the particular parcel. |
| 6 | **Intergenerational rollover:** farm property can transfer to a child at an elected amount under **ITA subsection 73(3)** during life and **ITA subsection 70(9)** on death, deferring the gain. Conditions attach and are checked parcel by parcel. |
| 7 | **Capital versus income on sale.** Land bought to hold and rent is normally on capital account; land bought, severed and resold can be an adventure in the nature of trade taxed as business income. Intention at acquisition, and the evidence for it, decide this. |
