# Shared pet care services research lock

Applies to category 12 pages where the client is paid to **look after someone else's animal**:
pet grooming, pet boarding, dog walking, pet sitting, and future pages in the same block.
The old `/veterinary/` page predates this spine and is not governed by it.

Three things define this block. First, **nobody licenses the occupation provincially** — a
groomer, walker, sitter or boarding operator is not a regulated profession in Ontario the way
a nurse or an engineer is — but **municipalities regulate the activity**, and animal welfare
legislation applies to anyone who has custody of an animal. Second, **there is a hard legal
boundary these businesses sit right beside**: practising veterinary medicine is restricted to
licensed veterinarians, so where care ends and treatment begins is a live question for the
client and never a question this firm answers. Third, **the money usually arrives before the
service does** — packages, prepaid blocks, holiday deposits — which makes revenue timing the
recurring accounting problem across all four.

| # | Locked fact |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The occupation is not provincially licensed; the activity is municipally regulated.** There is no provincial college or protected title for grooming, walking, sitting or boarding in Ontario, and no page in this block may imply otherwise. What does apply is **municipal business licensing and animal-related bylaws** — kennel and boarding licences, limits on the number of animals kept, home-business permission and zoning — which differ by municipality. Those requirements are confirmed with the municipality and are **not asserted**. |
| 2 | **Animal welfare legislation applies to anyone with custody of an animal.** In Ontario the **Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019** is the governing statute, enforced by **Animal Welfare Services**. Standards of care exist and compliance has a real cost. No standard, requirement, inspection power, penalty or threshold is asserted. |
| 3 | **The veterinary boundary is the most important line on these pages.** Practising veterinary medicine is restricted to licensed veterinarians under the **Veterinarians Act**, with the **College of Veterinarians of Ontario** as the regulator. Administering medication, treating, diagnosing or advising on an animal's health is on the far side of that line. These pages name the boundary as real and as the client's question to settle with their own counsel and veterinarian, and they give **no animal-health, medication, behaviour or welfare guidance of any kind**. |
| 4 | **Pet care is a taxable supply at 13% in Ontario.** There is no health care exemption for animals: grooming, boarding, walking and sitting are ordinary taxable services. Registration is required once taxable supplies pass **$30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters — the single event most owner-operators in this block are unprepared for, because many start well below it and cross it without noticing. Place of supply follows the recipient's address. |
| 5 | **Money received before the service is performed is income first and reserved second.** Prepaid packages of walks or grooms, boarding booked months ahead, memberships and holiday deposits are included in income under **ITA paragraph 12(1)(a)** and reserved out under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(m)** to the extent the service has not yet been rendered. **ETA section 181.2** governs gift certificates and **ETA section 182** deals with a forfeited deposit where an agreement is breached — cancellations are endemic in this block, so both are reviewed rather than assumed. |
| 6 | **Supplies used in a service and goods sold over the counter are not the same account.** Shampoo, conditioner, blades, bedding and cleaning product consumed in performing the service are supplies expensed as used. Retail product sold to the client is **inventory under ITA section 10**, valued consistently. A business that runs both through one account can neither value its stock nor cost its service. |
| 7 | **The people doing the work are the largest audit exposure.** Groomers, walkers and sitters engaged as subcontractors rather than employees is the norm in this block and is tested on the **Form RC4110** factors — control, tools, chance of profit and risk of loss, integration — never on the invoice or on what the parties called it. Genuine contractors are reported on **T4A**. **WSIB** from the first hire; source deductions on the **PD7A** by the 15th of the following month with CRA's late-remittance penalty reaching **10%** (graduated); **T4** by the last day of February; Ontario **EHT** past the **$1,000,000** exemption. |
| 8 | **Vehicles matter more here than premises do for some of these businesses.** A mobile grooming van, a walker's car or a sitter's vehicle sits in **Class 10**, and a vehicle meeting the **ITA subsection 248(1)** definition of an automobile and costing more than the prescribed limit falls into **Class 10.1**, where each vehicle sits in its own class and neither recapture nor terminal loss arises on disposal. The prescribed limit is **not asserted**. A mileage logbook is the evidence, and **ITA section 67.1** limits the food and beverage portion of expenses to 50%. |
| 9 | CCA otherwise: **Class 8 at 20%** for tables, tubs, dryers, clippers, crates, runs, fencing, play equipment and office fittings; **Class 13** for a leasehold improvement, which on a built-out facility is often the largest single asset; **Class 1** for an owned building; **Class 50 at 55%** for computers; **Class 12 at 100%** for booking and application software; **Class 14.1 at 5%** for goodwill and an acquired client list. |
| 10 | **Insurance is a condition of being trusted with an animal, not just an overhead.** General liability, care-custody-and-control cover for animals in the business's charge, and bonding where staff hold client keys are commonly required before a client or a facility will engage the business. Premiums are deductible. **No limit, deductible, exclusion, retroactive date or run-off period is asserted.** |
| 11 | **Incorporation.** Roughly **12.2%** Ontario combined on the first **$500,000** of active income under **ITA section 125**, against a top personal rate of **53.53%**, and only where the corporation is not a **personal services business** under **ITA subsection 125(7)**. **$1.25M** Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (**ITA 110.6**). Section **85** rollover on **Form T2057**. Bad debt on an unpaid client invoice deductible under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(p)**. Records six years under **ITA section 230**. |
| 12 | Compliance and lender baseline: late filing costs **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month up to twelve months. A Voluntary Disclosures Program application on **Form RC199** accepted under the general program cancels penalties in full and gives roughly **50%** interest relief. Notice of Objection within **90 days**; taxpayer relief on **Form RC4288**. A **CSRS 4200** Notice to Reader compilation across two fiscal years, with deferred revenue shown as the liability it is, is what supports a lease, an equipment loan or a sale. |

## Open items &mdash; NOT asserted on any pet care page until confirmed

1. **Municipal licensing and bylaw requirements** — business licence, kennel or boarding licence, animal number limits, home-business permission, zoning. Named as real, variable by municipality, and confirmed with the municipality. No requirement, fee, limit or threshold asserted for any municipality.
2. **Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019 standards of care.** The statute and Animal Welfare Services are named. No standard, requirement, inspection power, penalty or threshold is asserted.
3. **Where the Veterinarians Act boundary falls for any given activity**, including administering medication. The restriction and the College of Veterinarians of Ontario are named. No conclusion is asserted, and **no page in this block gives animal-health, medication, behaviour, breed, nutrition or welfare guidance at all**.
4. **Whether a groomer, walker or sitter is an employee or an independent contractor** in a given case, and **whether any arrangement is a personal services business**. The Form RC4110 factors and the ITA 125(7) test are stated; the conclusions are questions of fact.
5. **Employment Standards Act, 2000 entitlements** — overtime, public holiday pay, vacation pay, termination and severance. Named as real and as employment counsel's domain. No rate, multiplier, threshold or number of weeks asserted.
6. **Insurance and bonding terms**, including what care-custody-and-control cover responds to. Named as commercially decisive. No limit, deductible, exclusion or period asserted.
7. **Whether a prepayment is a gift certificate under ETA 181.2, or a forfeited deposit under ETA 182** on cancellation. Both provisions are named; no prepayment or cancellation charge is characterised.
8. **Whether a given vehicle is an "automobile"** under ITA 248(1), and the prescribed Class 10.1 cost limit. The regime is stated; no vehicle is characterised and no dollar limit asserted.
9. **Liability for injury to an animal, to a person, or to a client's property**, including anything arising from a dog bite. Named as a real exposure and as counsel's domain. No legal conclusion asserted.
10. **Whether a home-based operation is permitted at a given address**, and the tax treatment of the workspace in the home for a particular claimant. Named as a real question reviewed on the facts.
11. **SR&ED.** Not claimed anywhere in this block.
12. **No industry statistic** — market size, growth, pricing, day rate, occupancy rate or margin — is asserted on any page in this block.
