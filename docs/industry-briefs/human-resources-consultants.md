## The angle — one service, one consultant, four rates of tax

Nothing on the site covers human resources consulting. The nearest neighbours are Staffing Agencies
(49811), Employment Agencies (57966, built by the same other session), Corporate Trainers (53955),
Health and Safety Consultants (57768) and Professional Service Consultants. None of them is this
practice: an adviser who sells judgement by the hour, the retainer or the project, to employers in
more than one province.

Four subjects are specific to this consultant and appear on no page on the site:

1. **The place of supply, which is the whole page.** A policy manual for a manufacturer in Moncton, a
   compensation study for a hospital group in Halifax, a misconduct investigation for a retailer in
   Charlottetown and a fractional engagement with a software firm in Calgary are one service sold
   four times at four different rates. Advisory fees are a taxable supply, the **$30,000** small
   supplier test is crossed on a single fractional retainer, and from that point the rate follows the
   client: **13%** where the supply is made in Ontario, **15%** in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and
   Labrador and Prince Edward Island, **14%** in Nova Scotia since **1 April 2025**, and **5%** GST
   everywhere else in Canada.
2. **Nova Scotia is the number that is wrong in the template.** The rate came down on 1 April 2025 and
   the figure carried in most heads, and in most invoicing templates set up before that date, is the
   one that stopped applying. Over-collecting from a client and under-collecting from a client are
   both errors, and they do not cost the same to put right.
3. **The retainer, which is taxed and earned on two different dates.** Tax on a retainer is accounted
   for at the earlier of payment and the day the consideration becomes due, while the revenue is
   earned only as the work is performed. A deposit on a fixed-fee investigation sits in the practice
   as a liability and in the HST return as a reported supply in the same quarter, and a practice that
   reports those two things off the same number gets one of them wrong.
4. **The associate brought in for one file.** An investigation with two complainants needs two
   interviewers in the same week, so these practices subcontract. With the practice as the payer the
   classification question is theirs to answer, a genuine independent associate takes a **T4A** for
   fees for services, and an arrangement that is employment in substance is employment whatever the
   engagement letter calls it.

Deliberately **not** this page: placing or supplying workers (49811, 57966), training delivery as the
business (53955), occupational health and safety advice (57768). Employment law itself is never
touched — see Open items.

| 1 | **GST/HST registration** is required once worldwide taxable supplies exceed **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over the four previous consecutive calendar quarters. Consulting and advisory fees are a taxable supply, not an exempt one, so every dollar of fee income counts toward the test. |
| 2 | The **place of supply** rules for a service generally look to the address of the recipient obtained in the ordinary course of business, the one most closely connected with the supply, and set the rate by that province rather than by where the consultant works or lives. |
| 3 | Current rates: **13%** HST in Ontario; **15%** in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island; **14%** in Nova Scotia; **5%** GST in Alberta, British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Quebec and the three territories. |
| 4 | **Nova Scotia's** HST rate was reduced to **14%** effective **1 April 2025**, the provincial part coming down by one percentage point. Supplies made before that date carried the former rate. |
| 5 | **Section 169** of the Excise Tax Act allows an input tax credit to the extent that property or a service is acquired for consumption, use or supply in the course of a commercial activity. A registrant making taxable supplies of advisory services is in commercial activity, so the tax on data licences, software subscriptions, equipment and business travel is recoverable on that basis. |
| 6 | **Section 168** sets when tax is payable: the earlier of the day the consideration is paid and the day it becomes due. A retainer or deposit therefore enters the GST/HST return for the period in which it is received, whatever stage the work has reached. |
| 7 | **Fees received in advance are not revenue.** An unearned retainer or project deposit is a liability until the service is performed, and is earned as the work is delivered rather than when the money arrives. |
| 8 | **ITA subsection 125(7)** defines a **personal services business**: an individual (the incorporated employee) performs services on behalf of the corporation, that individual or a related person is a specified shareholder, and the individual would reasonably be regarded as an officer or employee of the person receiving the services but for the existence of the corporation — unless the corporation employs more than five full-time employees throughout the year or supplies an associated corporation. Where it applies, the small business deduction is denied and **ITA paragraph 18(1)(p)** restricts deductions to the incorporated employee's salary and benefits and a short list of specified amounts. |
| 9 | Whether a relationship is employment or an independent contract is decided on the whole relationship — control, ownership of tools, chance of profit and risk of loss, and integration into the payer's business. It is a question of fact, assessed on the facts rather than on what the contract calls the parties. |
| 10 | A payer must report **fees for services** paid to a subcontractor on a **T4A** information slip. No CPP or EI is withheld from a genuine independent contractor; where the relationship is employment, source deductions and a T4 apply instead. |
| 11 | The **Quick Method** is available only where annual taxable supplies do not exceed **$400,000**, the remittance rate turns on the province and the type of business, and the method bars credits on operating inputs while leaving credits on capital property available. Whether it beats the regular method is arithmetic on the practice's own figures. |
| 12 | Canadian baseline: top personal rate **53.53%** in Ontario; the small business deduction under **ITA section 125** against the **$500,000** limit; business-use-of-home under **ITA subsection 18(12)**, deductible only against the income of that business with the excess carried forward; vehicle costs on the business portion supported by a log; records six years under **ITA section 230**; late filing **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month to twelve months; Notice of Objection within **90 days**; relief on **Form RC4288**; **Form RC199** for the Voluntary Disclosures Program; instalments where nothing is withheld at source. |

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

1. **No combined small business tax rate.** The published draft's hero closed on "11.2% (from July 1,
   2026)". The site carries two conflicting combined rates — 11.2% on page 53439 against 12.2% on
   seven others — and that conflict is still unresolved, so the clause was rewritten to name the
   small business deduction and the **$500,000** limit without stating a rate. The four pages built
   immediately before this one (57768, 57776, 57836, 57843) state no combined rate either.
2. No conclusion that any particular engagement is or is not a personal services business. It turns
   on the facts of the engagement and the page says so.
3. **No employment law advice of any kind** — no view on an employer's obligations, on a termination
   entitlement, on whether a policy complies with any statute, or on the conduct or outcome of a
   workplace investigation. We are accountants, and the reader is the expert on that.
4. No conclusion about whether a given associate is an employee or an independent contractor. The
   factors are named; the answer is a question of fact on the actual arrangement.
5. No Quick Method remittance rate quoted, and no conclusion that the Quick Method is better. It is
   run against the regular method on the reader's figures.
6. No provincial sales tax rate or registration conclusion for Quebec, British Columbia,
   Saskatchewan or Manitoba beyond flagging that a separate registration can arise.
7. No statement on whether a particular supply is zero-rated when the client is a non-resident; the
   mechanism is named and the conditions are a question of fact on the engagement.
8. **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount**, for the same
   unresolved-conflict reason as items above and as on 57627, 57645, 57751, 57752, 57768, 57776,
   57836 and 57843.
9. No CCA class or rate stated for a computer, vehicle or office equipment.
10. No professional body named, and no membership, designation or continuing-education requirement
    of any such body stated.
11. No advice on whether to incorporate stated as a rule; it is modelled on the reader's numbers.
12. No insurance product, coverage level or insurer recommended, and no view on whether an indemnity
    or limitation-of-liability clause in a consulting agreement is enforceable.
13. No fee quoted beyond the published pricing chrome.
14. We advise on Canadian tax and coordinate where another jurisdiction is involved.
