## Inheritance and positioning

This page inherits `docs/industry-briefs/pet-care-spine.md` in full. All twelve locked facts in
that spine apply here — the occupation is not provincially licensed while the activity is
municipally regulated, animal welfare legislation applies to anyone with custody of an animal,
the Veterinarians Act boundary is named and never crossed, boarding is a taxable supply at 13%
in Ontario with registration once taxable supplies pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar
quarters, money received before the service is performed is income under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a)
and reserved under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m), retail stock is inventory under ITA section 10 while
product consumed in care is a supply, the people doing the work are the largest audit exposure,
and the CCA, insurance, incorporation and compliance baselines carry across unchanged. **Nothing
in the spine's twelve open items is asserted on this page, in either direction** — not municipal
or kennel licensing, not a Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019 standard, not where the
Veterinarians Act boundary falls, not an employee-versus-contractor or personal services business
conclusion, not an Employment Standards entitlement, not an insurance term, not the
characterisation of a deposit under ETA 181.2 or 182, not whether a vehicle is an automobile or
the Class 10.1 limit, not a liability conclusion, not a home-based permission, not SR&ED, and no
industry statistic of any kind.

**Distinctness from the three sibling pages in this block.** The spine is shared; the spine of
*this* page is **a fixed number of runs, sold by the night, booked around the holidays**. Pet
boarding is the only page in the block with **capacity**, and capacity is what makes the
accounting different. Revenue is occupancy against a fixed run count, so the governing figure is
revenue per available run-night rather than revenue in total; costs barely move with occupancy
because the building, the heat, the overnight cover and the insurance are there whether the runs
are full or empty, so the operating leverage cuts both ways and hard. The year is not flat —
Christmas, March break and summer carry it — and holiday bookings are taken months ahead with
deposits, which puts a December year-end next to a fully-deposited Christmas and makes the ITA
20(1)(m) reserve the single most expensive line on the file. The premises is an owned or leased
building with runs in it, which raises a real property structuring question no sibling page has.
And overnight cover is a payroll shape nothing else in this block carries: someone is on site or
on call when the animals are. By contrast, **Pet Grooming** is a premises with a table and a
retail shelf plus a mobile van; **Dog Walking** is route density and walker classification; **Pet
Sitting** is work inside clients' homes with no premises at all. None of those three has a
capacity ceiling, an occupancy metric, a holiday deposit book or an owned building.

## Step 1 research lock

| # | Item | Locked answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is pet boarding a licensed occupation in Ontario? | No. There is no provincial college, protected title or occupational licence for operating a boarding kennel in Ontario, and this page does not imply otherwise. What is real and decisive is **municipal** regulation — business licensing, kennel or boarding licences, limits on the number of animals kept, home-business permission and zoning — which matters more on a boarding page than anywhere else in this block, because boarding is the activity municipalities most often license. **Open item: every municipal requirement, fee, limit and threshold. Confirmed with the municipality; none asserted here.** |
| 2 | Animal welfare legislation | The **Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019** is the governing Ontario statute and **Animal Welfare Services** is the enforcement body. Both are named as real, and compliance has a real cost that belongs in the ledger. **Open item: every standard, requirement, inspection power, penalty and threshold under the Act.** |
| 3 | The veterinary boundary | Practising veterinary medicine is restricted to licensed veterinarians under the **Veterinarians Act**, regulated by the **College of Veterinarians of Ontario**. Where care of a boarded animal ends and treatment begins is the client's question for their own counsel and veterinarian. **This page gives no animal-health, medication, vaccination, behaviour, breed, nutrition, handling or welfare guidance of any kind. Open item: where the boundary falls for any given activity.** |
| 4 | Sales tax on boarding | Boarding, daycare days and add-on services are ordinary **taxable supplies at 13%** in Ontario — there is no health care exemption for animals. Registration is required once taxable supplies pass **$30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters, which is the event most owner-operators are unprepared for. Place of supply follows the recipient's address. Returns are filed on **GST34**. |
| 5 | Money taken before the stay | Amounts received before the boarding is provided are included in income under **ITA paragraph 12(1)(a)** and reserved out under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(m)** to the extent the service has not yet been rendered. **ETA section 181.2** governs gift certificates and **ETA section 182** deals with a forfeited deposit where an agreement is breached; cancellation terms are the norm here, so both provisions are reviewed. **Open item: whether a particular deposit is a gift certificate under 181.2, or a cancellation charge a forfeited deposit under 182.** |
| 6 | The measurement that governs profit | Capacity is fixed, so the figure that governs profit is **revenue per available run-night** — kennel-nights sold against run-nights available — not revenue in total. This is a management-accounting method, not a market figure: it is computed from the facility's own booking and ledger data. **Open item: no occupancy rate, nightly rate, market size, growth or margin statistic is asserted anywhere on this page.** |
| 7 | Capital cost allowance on the facility | An owned kennel building is **Class 1**. Runs, fencing, kennel panels, play structures, heating and ventilation equipment and office fittings are **Class 8 at 20%**. Improvements to a leased facility are **Class 13**, amortised over the term of the lease. Computers are **Class 50 at 55%**, booking and application software **Class 12 at 100%**, goodwill and an acquired client base **Class 14.1 at 5%**. Claimed on **Schedule 8**. |
| 8 | Vehicles | A transport vehicle sits in **Class 10**. A vehicle meeting the **ITA subsection 248(1)** definition of an automobile and costing more than the prescribed limit falls into **Class 10.1**, where each vehicle sits in its own class and neither recapture nor terminal loss arises on disposal. A mileage logbook is the evidence. **ITA section 67.1** limits the food and beverage portion of expenses to 50%. **Open item: whether any given vehicle is an "automobile", and the prescribed Class 10.1 cost limit — neither asserted.** |
| 9 | Overnight and weekend payroll | Coverage when the animals are on site is structural rather than exceptional and is accrued as earned. Classification is tested on the **Form RC4110** factors — control, tools, chance of profit and risk of loss, integration — never on the invoice. Genuine contractors are reported on **T4A**. **WSIB** from the first hire. Source deductions on the **PD7A** by the 15th of the following month, with CRA's late-remittance penalty reaching **10%** (graduated). **T4** and T4 Summary by the last day of February. Ontario **EHT** past the **$1,000,000** exemption. **Open items: whether any worker is an employee or a contractor; whether any arrangement is a personal services business; every Employment Standards Act, 2000 entitlement.** |
| 10 | Retail and supplies | Product sold to the client at pickup is **inventory under ITA section 10**, valued consistently. Bedding, cleaning product and consumables used in caring for a boarded animal are supplies expensed as used. A business running both through one account can neither value its stock nor cost a kennel-night. A bad debt on an unpaid boarding invoice is deductible under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(p)**. |
| 11 | How the real property is held | Where land and building are owned, whether they sit in the operating company or in a separate holding company is a genuine structuring question with real consequences on a future sale, financing and creditor exposure. It is **modelled on the client's facts, never assumed**. **Open items: that any holding-company structure is appropriate for anyone; and every land transfer tax, HST-on-real-property and rebate rate or amount — none asserted.** |
| 12 | Incorporation, compliance and the lender baseline | Roughly **12.2%** Ontario combined on the first **$500,000** of active income under **ITA section 125** against a top personal rate of **53.53%**, and only where the corporation is not a **personal services business** under **ITA subsection 125(7)**. **$1.25M** Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under **ITA 110.6**. Section **85** rollover on **Form T2057**. Records six years under **ITA section 230**. Late filing costs **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month up to twelve months. A Voluntary Disclosures Program application on **Form RC199** accepted under the general program cancels penalties in full and gives roughly **50%** interest relief. Notice of Objection within **90 days**; taxpayer relief on **Form RC4288**. A **CSRS 4200** Notice to Reader compilation across two fiscal years, with deferred deposits shown as the liability they are, is what finances an expansion. |
