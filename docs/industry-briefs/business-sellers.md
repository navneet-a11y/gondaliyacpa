## The angle — the vendor's side of the price, which is a tax number

The site already carries **Business Buyers** (48189) and **Businesses Winding Down** (56800). This
page is neither. The buyer's page is about acquiring; the winding-down page is about ending a
business that is not being sold. This page is the seller at the table, where almost every negotiating
position has a tax number behind it.

Four subjects are specific to the vendor and appear on no page on the site:

1. **Share sale against asset sale is the whole negotiation.** The vendor generally wants to sell
   shares: one level of tax, a capital gain, and the lifetime capital gains exemption if the shares
   qualify. The buyer generally wants to buy assets: a cost step-up, chosen liabilities, and none of
   the corporation's history. The gap between the two prices is largely the tax gap, and it is worth
   quantifying before the letter of intent rather than after.
2. **Qualification for the exemption is tested on dates that have already passed.** Under **ITA
   section 110.6** the shares must be shares of a small business corporation at the time of sale, with
   substantially all of the fair market value of the assets used in an active business carried on
   primarily in Canada, and throughout the 24 months before the sale more than half of that value
   must have been so used, with the shares not owned by anyone other than the seller or a related
   person in that period. A corporation holding surplus cash, a portfolio or a rental property can
   fail the test, and purification takes planning two years ahead of a sale that has not been
   contemplated yet.
3. **Selling to a corporation you are connected to can turn the gain into a dividend.** **ITA section
   84.1** applies where an individual resident in Canada sells shares to a corporation with which
   they do not deal at arm's length, and it can reduce paid-up capital or deem a dividend instead of
   a capital gain. It is the trap in the family sale and in the sale to a corporation owned by the
   buyer's children, and it is not something the share purchase agreement will flag for you.
4. **What the agreement calls the money changes what it is.** Consideration allocated to a
   **restrictive covenant** — a non-compete or a non-solicit — is caught by **ITA section 56.4** and
   can be fully taxable income rather than a capital gain unless an exception or election applies. An
   earnout changes when proceeds are recognised. A holdback does not. And on an asset sale the
   allocation across inventory, depreciable property, goodwill and receivables decides how much is
   recapture taxed in full and how much is a capital gain.

| 1 | A **share sale** by an individual gives rise to a capital gain on the shares. An **asset sale** is made by the corporation, so the gain, recapture and income arise inside the corporation and a second step is needed to get the money to the shareholder. The two routes are not equivalent and the difference is usually the largest single number in the deal. |
| 2 | **ITA section 110.6** gives an individual resident in Canada a lifetime capital gains exemption on a disposition of **qualified small business corporation shares**. The amount of the exemption is not stated on this page. |
| 3 | For the shares to be QSBC shares: at the time of disposition the corporation must be a **small business corporation** (all or substantially all of the fair market value of its assets used principally in an active business carried on primarily in Canada, or shares or debt of connected small business corporations); **throughout the 24 months** before the disposition more than **50%** of that value must have been so used; and in that period the shares must not have been owned by anyone other than the individual or a person or partnership related to them. |
| 4 | **ITA section 84.1** applies where an individual resident in Canada disposes of shares of a Canadian corporation to another corporation with which the individual does not deal at arm's length, and the two corporations are connected. It can grind paid-up capital and deem a dividend to the vendor in place of a capital gain. |
| 5 | **ITA section 56.4** governs amounts received for a **restrictive covenant**. Subject to its exceptions and elections, such an amount is included in income rather than treated as proceeds of disposition of capital property. |
| 6 | **ITA subparagraph 40(1)(a)(iii)** allows a **capital gains reserve** where part of the proceeds is not due until a later year, so the gain can be brought into income over a maximum of **five** tax years including the year of sale. A longer reserve period applies to certain transfers of farm or fishing property and small business corporation shares to a child. |
| 7 | On an **asset sale** the price must be allocated among the assets sold. Proceeds on depreciable property above the undepreciated capital cost give rise to **recapture**, which is fully taxable, up to the original capital cost, with any excess a capital gain. Goodwill and other intangibles fall in **Class 14.1**. |
| 8 | A **sale of shares** is a supply of a financial instrument and is **exempt** for GST/HST purposes. A **sale of assets** is generally taxable, but **ETA section 167** permits a joint election where a supplier sells a business or part of a business and the recipient acquires ownership, possession or use of all or substantially all of the property reasonably necessary to carry it on; the election is made on **Form GST44** and filed with the recipient's return. |
| 9 | Real property transferred on an asset sale has its own GST/HST treatment, and the section 167 election does not apply to a supply of real property to a recipient who is not a registrant. |
| 10 | After an asset sale the corporation still holds the proceeds. Distributing them is a separate question with its own tax consequences, and the corporation's final return and dissolution are separate steps again. |
| 11 | **Records must be kept six years** under **ITA section 230**, and a sale does not end that obligation: the years in which the business operated remain open to review on the normal reassessment periods. |
| 12 | Canadian baseline: top personal rate **53.53%** in Ontario; the small business deduction under **ITA section 125** against the **$500,000** limit; late filing **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month to twelve months; Notice of Objection within **90 days**; relief on **Form RC4288**; **Form RC199** for the Voluntary Disclosures Program; instalments where nothing is withheld at source. |

   it does or does not apply to a given sale.

   the preceding two years.
