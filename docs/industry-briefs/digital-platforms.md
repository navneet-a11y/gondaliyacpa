## The angle — the platform as intermediary, not as a product seller

This was the riskiest row on the list. "Digital Platforms" is the broadest title in the whole
keyword set, and the site already carries Digital Product Businesses (53437), E-Learning Platforms
(51049), Online Course Creators (53439), Cloud Service Providers (54831), Managed Service Providers
(54828), Software Resellers (56803) and the older Technology Startups & SaaS Companies page (32317).
Written loosely this page would be a vague duplicate of several of them.

The scope that makes it genuinely distinct is **the business that facilitates transactions between
other parties** rather than selling its own goods or services. That single decision gives the page
machinery that none of those pages has, and it makes the page the exact mirror of the Online
Resellers page (57645) — the same rules seen from the platform's side rather than the seller's.

Four subjects are specific to an intermediary and appear on no page on the site:

1. **Agent or principal, which decides whether GST/HST applies to the whole transaction or only to
   your take.** If the platform supplies the underlying service to the customer as principal, it
   charges tax on the gross value. If it acts as agent for the vendor, its own taxable supply is the
   intermediation service and tax applies to the commission. **ETA section 177** governs supplies
   made on behalf of another person and provides a billing agent election letting the agent account
   for tax on the principal's supply. The answer sits in the contracts and the terms of service, not
   in how the business describes itself, and getting it wrong is wrong by the whole gross-to-net
   difference — in the tax and in the reported revenue.
2. **The platform can be made the tax collector for other people's sales.** Where it facilitates
   supplies by third-party vendors who are not registered, the **distribution platform operator**
   rules in the Excise Tax Act can put the obligation to collect and remit GST/HST on those supplies
   onto the platform. A twelve-person software company becomes responsible for the sales tax on
   thousands of other businesses' transactions. Separately, the simplified registration regime
   reaches non-resident platforms making specified supplies to Canadian consumers.
3. **Part XX of the Income Tax Act makes the platform an information reporter.** A reportable
   platform operator must apply due diligence procedures to identify reportable sellers, then file an
   annual return naming each one and the total consideration paid to them, due by **31 January**
   following the calendar year. Most platform founders have never heard of it. It is the obligation
   that sits behind the Online Resellers page from the other direction.
4. **Money that passes through you is not revenue.** Pending payouts, wallet balances, escrow and
   security deposits are liabilities, and the gross settlement figure from a payment processor is not
   income. Booking it as revenue overstates the business to its own investors; spending it is worse.
   Chargebacks and refunds flow back through the platform, and a processor's held reserve is an asset
   the platform still owns.

Deliberately **not** this page: selling one's own digital product (53437), course and membership
products (53439, 51049), infrastructure and managed IT (54831, 54828), reselling someone else's
licences (56803), and the seller's side of the marketplace (57645, cross-linked instead).

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

1. No conclusion that the reader's platform is an agent or a principal. It is a question of fact
   decided on the agreements, and the page says so rather than asserting either.
2. No conclusion that any platform — the reader's or any named one — is or is not a distribution
   platform operator, or is or is not a reportable platform operator under Part XX.
3. **No Part XX de minimis threshold.** The excluded-seller thresholds are euro-denominated and are
   verified against the rules on a given file rather than published here.
4. No penalty amount stated for a failure to file the Part XX return. The exposure is named; the
   figure is not.
5. **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount.** The site
   carries two conflicting figures (page 53439 against roughly twenty others) and that conflict is
   still unresolved. Same discipline as the farmland (57627) and online resellers (57645) pages.
6. **No SR&ED credit rate and no expenditure limit figure.** The mechanism is named without any
   percentage or dollar amount.
7. No foreign tax rule: no United States state sales tax rate, economic nexus test or marketplace
   facilitator statute, and nothing on digital services taxes in other countries.
8. No advice on whether holding user funds makes the platform a money services business, requires
   FINTRAC registration, or needs a payment licence. That is a regulatory and legal question and the
   page says to take it to counsel.
9. No CCA class or rate stated.
10. No conclusion on whether a specific contract term achieves agency; terms are reviewed, not
    assumed, and we do not draft them.
11. No statement about provincial sales tax in Quebec, British Columbia, Saskatchewan or Manitoba
    beyond flagging that separate registrations can arise.
12. No advice on whether to incorporate stated as a rule; it is modelled on the reader's numbers.
13. No platform fee percentage, take rate or payout schedule for any named platform.
14. No fee quoted beyond the published pricing chrome.
15. We advise on Canadian tax and coordinate where another jurisdiction is involved.
