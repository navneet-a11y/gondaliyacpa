## The angle — the stock came from somewhere with no invoice

Three "reseller" pages already exist and none of them is this one. **Electronics Resellers** (49609)
and **Software Resellers** (56803) are single product verticals buying from distributors with real
invoices. **Wholesale E-Commerce Sellers** (53789) buys wholesale and sells in bulk. **Pawn Shops**
(52059) buys used goods from the public but also lends against them, which is its own file.
**Print-on-Demand** (57633) owns no inventory at all and is the explicit opposite case.

What none of them has is the file of someone sourcing existing goods — thrift stores, garage sales,
estate sales, auctions, Facebook Marketplace, liquidation pallets, a sneaker drop — and reselling
them on marketplaces. Four subjects are specific to that person and appear on no page on the site:

1. **The closet-or-business line, which is a real statutory regime and not a judgement call.**
   Selling your own belongings engages the **personal-use property** rules in **ITA section 54**:
   **ITA subsection 46(1)** deems both the adjusted cost base and the proceeds to be not less than
   **$1,000**, so a gain on an ordinary item disappears; and a loss on personal-use property is
   denied outright by **ITA subparagraph 40(2)(g)(iii)**. **Listed personal property** — art,
   jewellery, rare books, stamps, coins — is a subset whose losses **ITA section 41** confines to
   LPP gains. A reseller of collectibles can be inside this regime for part of their activity and
   outside it for the rest, in the same year.
2. **Income or capital, decided on the badges of trade.** Business income under **ITA section 9** is
   fully taxable; a capital gain is not. The analytical frame is the same one the house-flippers page
   (48320) applies to real estate — intention at acquisition, frequency, length of ownership, nature
   of the asset, relation to the taxpayer's other work — and nobody has applied it to goods here.
3. **Cost of goods sold with no invoices.** The practical heart of the file. Where the activity is a
   business, the goods are **inventory**, valued under **ITA subsection 10(1)** at the lower of cost
   and fair market value — which is what lets a reseller write down stock that will not sell, and is
   the exact machinery the print-on-demand page says does not apply to it. But none of it works
   without a cost base, and a cash purchase at a garage sale has no receipt. **ITA section 230**
   requires six years of records; without a supportable cost, the CRA can assess on gross proceeds.
4. **The platform now reports you, and the pallet is taxed at the border.** **Part XX of the Income
   Tax Act** requires a reportable platform operator to report each reportable seller's identity and
   total consideration to the CRA annually. And a reseller importing stock pays **Division III GST**
   under **Excise Tax Act section 212** at the border — goods, not the imported *intangibles*
   self-assessment the print-on-demand page deals with. Two different import regimes, deliberately
   contrasted.

Deliberately **not** this page: a product vertical with distributor invoices (49609, 56803), bulk
wholesale (53789), lending against goods (52059), stockless fulfilment (57633), real estate
flipping (48320).

## Step 1 research lock — 12 rows

| # | Locked for this page |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Business income or capital gain** is the threshold question. Profit from an **adventure or concern in the nature of trade** is business income under **ITA section 9**, fully taxable. The test is applied on the badges of trade: intention at acquisition, the frequency and number of transactions, the length of ownership, the nature of the asset, the work done on it, and the taxpayer's relation to the trade. |
| 2 | Where the activity is a business, goods held for resale are **inventory**, valued under **ITA subsection 10(1)** at the lower of cost and fair market value. This is the provision that lets a reseller write down stock that will not sell at what it cost. |
| 3 | **Personal-use property** is defined in **ITA section 54** as property used primarily for the personal use or enjoyment of the taxpayer or related persons. **ITA subsection 46(1)** deems both the adjusted cost base and the proceeds of disposition to be not less than **$1,000**, so no gain arises on an item that cost and sold for less than that. |
| 4 | A **loss** on the disposition of personal-use property is denied by **ITA subparagraph 40(2)(g)(iii)**. Selling your own belongings at less than you paid produces nothing deductible. |
| 5 | **Listed personal property** is a subset of personal-use property under **ITA section 54**: prints, etchings, drawings, paintings, sculptures and similar works of art; jewellery; rare folios, manuscripts and books; stamps; and coins. **ITA section 41** allows an LPP loss to be applied only against LPP gains, carried back three years and forward seven. |
| 6 | **Books and records** must be kept for six years under **ITA section 230**. For a reseller this is the whole file: without a supportable cost base, there is nothing to deduct from the proceeds the platform reported, and the assessment can proceed on gross. |
| 7 | **Inventory costing** uses specific identification where items are unique, which is the normal case for secondhand and collectible stock. Where a single lot or pallet price covers many units, the cost is allocated across them on a reasonable and consistently applied basis. |
| 8 | **GST/HST registration** is required once worldwide taxable supplies exceed **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Ontario is **13%**. The place of supply rules set the rate by the customer's province. Exports are generally zero-rated under **Schedule VI Part V** of the Excise Tax Act but still count toward the threshold. |
| 9 | **Used goods are not exempt.** A sale of used goods by a registrant in the course of a commercial activity is a taxable supply at the ordinary rate; there is no general exemption for secondhand items. |
| 10 | **Division III GST** on goods imported into Canada is payable by the importer under **Excise Tax Act section 212** at the time of importation, and is generally recoverable as an input tax credit where the goods are for use in a commercial activity. Customs duty and the value for duty are separate determinations from the GST. |
| 11 | **Distribution platform operator** rules can make a marketplace, rather than the seller, responsible for collecting and remitting GST/HST on a sale made through it. Separately, **Part XX of the Income Tax Act** requires a reportable platform operator to collect and report each reportable seller's identifying information and the total consideration paid to them to the CRA annually, with the return due by **31 January** following the calendar year reported. There is a de minimis exclusion for the smallest sellers of goods; its thresholds are checked against the rules rather than asserted here. |
| 12 | Canadian baseline: top personal rate **53.53%** in Ontario; small business deduction under **ITA section 125** against the **$500,000** limit; business-use-of-home under **ITA subsection 18(12)** limited to the income from the business with the excess carried forward; Quick Method capped at **$400,000** of annual taxable supplies; late filing **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month to twelve months; Notice of Objection within **90 days**; relief on **Form RC4288**; **Form RC199** disclosure cancels penalties in full with roughly **50%** interest relief; instalments where nothing is withheld at source. |
