The page is built on **a premises with a table and a shelf — and a second business on wheels**. Five
things carry it: consumables and retail stock are two accounts and never one, with product consumed in
performing a groom expensed as used while the same product sold at the counter is inventory under ITA
section 10; the salon build-out is usually the largest asset on the balance sheet, split across Class 8,
Class 13, Class 1, Class 50, Class 12 and Class 14.1; mobile grooming is a different business in the

and prepaid packages, no-shows and the $30,000 registration line drive revenue timing.

table**, where the **leasehold fit-out and the equipment pools are the balance sheet**, and where a
**fitted-out van runs as a second cost centre inside one corporation**. Where the siblings ask how a
night or a visit is priced, this page asks what a single groom costs and which of the shop and the van

## Step 1 research lock

| # | Item | Locked answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Consumables versus retail stock | Shampoo, conditioner, blades, cologne, towels and cleaning product **consumed in performing a groom** are supplies expensed as used. The same or similar product **sold to the client at the counter** is **inventory under ITA section 10**, valued consistently from year to year. Running both through one "products" account is the most common bookkeeping error in the trade: the business can neither value its stock nor cost a groom. Two accounts, never one. |
| 2 | Salon equipment CCA | Grooming tubs, hydraulic tables, dryers, clippers, cages, runs, fencing, reception and retail fixtures sit in **Class 8 at 20%** on **Schedule 8**. On most grooming files this pool plus the leasehold is the balance sheet. |
| 3 | Fit-out of a leased unit | Plumbing, drainage, flooring, wash-bay and ventilation improvements made to a **leased** unit are a leasehold improvement in **Class 13**, amortised over the term of the lease plus permitted renewals. An **owned** building is **Class 1**. A shop that expenses its fit-out understates its assets to a lender and overstates its loss to CRA. |
| 4 | Other pools | **Class 50 at 55%** for computers, tablets and point-of-sale hardware; **Class 12 at 100%** for booking and application software; **Class 14.1 at 5%** for goodwill and an acquired client list. The error we inherit most often is everything pooled into Class 8. |
| 5 | Mobile grooming vehicle | A grooming van is **Class 10**. Where the vehicle meets the **ITA subsection 248(1)** definition of an automobile *and* cost more than the prescribed limit it falls into **Class 10.1**, where each vehicle sits alone in its own class and **neither recapture nor terminal loss arises on disposal**. **Open item:** whether a given vehicle is an "automobile", and the prescribed cost limit — neither is asserted. |
| 6 | Van fit-out and mileage | The grooming equipment, generator, water and heating system fitted into a van are **capital**, not repairs, and belong in the appropriate pool rather than in vehicle expense. Business use is evidenced by a **mileage logbook**. A business running both a shop and a van must cost them separately or it cannot tell which one earns. |
| 7 | Groomer engagement | Groomers paid a commission split, renting a station, or engaged as subcontractors is the norm. Employee versus independent contractor is tested on the **Form RC4110** factors — control, tools, chance of profit and risk of loss, integration — never on what the parties called it. Genuine contractors are reported on **T4A**. **Open item:** the conclusion in any given case, and whether any arrangement is a personal services business under **ITA subsection 125(7)** — neither is asserted. |
| 8 | Payroll compliance baseline | **WSIB** registration from the first hire. Source deductions reported on the **PD7A** and remitted by the **15th of the following month**, with CRA's late-remittance penalty on source deductions reaching **10%** (graduated). **T4** slips and T4 Summary by the **last day of February**. Ontario **Employer Health Tax** past the **$1,000,000** exemption. **Open item:** every **Employment Standards Act, 2000** entitlement — no rate, multiplier or threshold asserted. |
| 9 | Station rent is a different supply | Where a groomer genuinely rents a station, the business's revenue is **rent** rather than a service fee, which is a different supply with a different ledger treatment and a different HST analysis. **Open item:** whether any particular arrangement is a rental or an employment or contractor relationship — not asserted. |
| 10 | Prepaid packages and cancellations | Prepaid groom packages, punch cards and memberships are included in income under **ITA paragraph 12(1)(a)** and reserved out under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(m)** to the extent the groom has not yet been performed. **ETA section 181.2** governs gift certificates and **ETA section 182** deals with a forfeited deposit where an agreement is breached; cancellation and no-show fees are endemic in grooming. **Open item:** whether a given prepayment is a gift certificate, or a given cancellation charge a forfeited deposit — both provisions are named, neither characterisation is asserted. |
| 11 | HST and the revenue figure | Grooming is a **taxable supply at 13% in Ontario** — there is no health care exemption for animals. Registration is required once taxable supplies pass **$30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters, and many shops cross that line without noticing. Card and booking-platform fees are deducted before the deposit lands, so **net is not revenue**: books are built from gross service and retail sales with the fees on their own line. No platform, processor or supplier is named and no fee or rate is stated. |
| 12 | Structure, records and the authorities at the edge | Roughly **12.2%** Ontario combined on the first **$500,000** of active income under **ITA section 125** against a top personal rate of **53.53%**; **$1.25M** Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under **ITA 110.6**; a section **85** rollover on **Form T2057**; bad debt on an unpaid client invoice under **ITA paragraph 20(1)(p)**; records six years under **ITA section 230**. Late filing costs **5%** of the balance owing plus **1%** per month to twelve months; a Voluntary Disclosures Program application on **Form RC199** accepted under the general program cancels penalties in full with roughly **50%** interest relief; Notice of Objection within **90 days**; taxpayer relief on **Form RC4288**; a **CSRS 4200** Notice to Reader compilation across two fiscal years supports a lease or an equipment loan. At the edges of the file sit three authorities named and never interpreted: the **Veterinarians Act** restriction with the **College of Veterinarians of Ontario** as regulator, **municipal** business and animal licensing, and the **Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019** enforced by **Animal Welfare Services**. Groomers are **not** a provincially licensed or regulated profession in Ontario and the page says so. |
