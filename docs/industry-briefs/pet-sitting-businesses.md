## Inheritance and positioning

This page inherits `docs/industry-briefs/pet-care-spine.md` in full. All twelve locked facts in the
spine apply here — the occupation is not provincially licensed while the activity is municipally
regulated, animal welfare legislation reaches anyone with custody of an animal, the Veterinarians
Act boundary sits immediately beside the work, pet care is a taxable supply at 13% in Ontario with
registration at the $30,000 threshold, money received before the service is performed is income
first and reserved second, the people doing the work are the largest audit exposure, and the
compliance and lender baseline is the spine's. **Nothing in the spine's twelve open items is
asserted on this page in either direction**, and the page adds no assertion of its own on any of
them: not a municipal requirement, not a PAWS Act standard, not a worker-status or personal
services business conclusion, not an Employment Standards Act entitlement, not an insurance or
bonding term, not a liability conclusion, not a characterisation of a deposit under ETA 181.2 or a
cancellation charge under ETA 182, not whether a vehicle is an automobile, not the prescribed
Class 10.1 limit, not a workspace-in-the-home conclusion, no SR&ED claim and no industry statistic.

**The spine of this page is a business that operates inside other people's homes, booked months
ahead around their travel.** Four things follow and they are what make the page distinct. First,
**there is no premises at all** — no shop, no kennel, no leasehold — so there is no Class 13
leasehold story and no occupancy story, the cost base is people, vehicles and insurance, the
balance sheet is close to empty, the business has almost nothing to pledge, and a lender therefore
underwrites the contract book and the receivable ledger rather than assets, which a CSRS 4200
Notice to Reader compilation across two fiscal years is what makes legible. Class 14.1 at 5% for
goodwill and an acquired client list is in practice the only substantial intangible, and it is what
one sitting business buys from another. Second, **holding a client's keys and entering an empty
home** changes what insurance has to cover and what a sitter must be; the exposure runs to the
property and its contents as well as the animal. Third, **revenue is booked around travel, so the
deposits arrive long before the work** — concentrated in holidays and school breaks, with a
cancellation policy behind every one — and a year-end that lands beside a fully-deposited holiday
season is where this business overstates its year. Fourth, **a visit, a set of daily drop-ins and a
live-in overnight stay are three products at three rates**, and a business that prices them as one
cannot tell which earns.

Distinction from the three siblings building in parallel:

- **Pet Grooming** is a premises with a grooming table and a retail shelf — leasehold improvements,
  inventory under ITA section 10, product versus supplies. This page has no premises and sells no
  product, so neither Class 13 nor retail inventory appears here at all.
- **Pet Boarding** is a facility sold by the night with capacity and occupancy — kennel runs,
  Class 1 or Class 13, beds filled against beds available. This page has no facility and no
  capacity, so occupancy is never the metric; the constraint is sitter availability over a holiday
  window, not a number of runs.
- **Dog Walking** is the closest neighbour, and this page deliberately stays off its spine. There is
  **no route density, no group-walk economics and no per-walk or per-visit unit-margin analysis**
  anywhere on this page. Where this page discusses a vehicle it is travel between client homes as
  the reason a vehicle is the main capital item, not route planning. Where it discusses the billing
  unit it is the overnight and multi-day stay against the single visit, which is a product-mix
  question a walking business does not have. The money problem here is the advance holiday deposit
  and the empty balance sheet, neither of which is a dog walking story.
