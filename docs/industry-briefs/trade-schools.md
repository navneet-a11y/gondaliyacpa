## The angle — the school with a shop in it

The education cluster on the site currently holds E-Learning Platforms (51049), Online Course
Creators (53439), Corporate Trainers (53955), Driving Schools (50134) and Martial Arts Schools
(51650). None of them is a capital-intensive school teaching a regulated trade, and none of them
confronts the problem that defines this file.

**Row 483 and row 484 are nearly the same business in Ontario** — a trade school teaching a
vocational programme is generally a career college registered under the same statute, with the same
tuition and refund obligations. Two pages are defensible for search but only with deliberately
different emphases, so the split is:

- **This page (483) is the school as a capital-intensive operation.** The shop, the equipment, the
  consumables, the instructors who are working journeypersons, the employer and apprenticeship
  relationships — and the exempt-supply problem, which bites hardest precisely because this school
  has a quarter of a million dollars of welding bays and lifts in it.
- **Career Colleges (484) is the school as custodian of other people's money under a regulator.**
  Registration and programme approval, tuition held in trust, the prescribed refund policy as a real
  liability, third-party and student aid funding as a prepayment.

Each page names the statute and the exemption once and cross-links to the other.

Four subjects are specific to this school and appear on no page on the site:

1. **Exempt supplies and the input tax credit you cannot have.** **Part III of Schedule V** to the
   Excise Tax Act exempts instruction in courses leading to a certificate, diploma or licence
   attesting to competence to practise a trade or vocation, where supplied by a vocational school as
   defined. Exemption sounds like a benefit and for a capital-heavy school it is the opposite: an
   exempt supplier **claims no input tax credits**, so the HST on the welding bays, the hoists, the
   shop fit-out, the consumables and the utilities is a permanent cost rather than a recovery. On a
   school fitting out a new shop that is a very large number, and it changes how the build is
   financed and timed.
2. **Mixed supplies, which almost every trade school has.** Tool and kit sales to students are
   generally taxable. So is non-vocational or hobby instruction. A school with both exempt tuition
   and taxable sales has to apportion its credits on a fair and reasonable basis applied
   consistently — which is where the recoverable share of a shop build actually gets decided.
3. **The shop itself.** Capital cost allowance on equipment and trainer rigs, leasehold improvements
   to industrial space, consumables that are genuinely inventory under **ITA subsection 10(1)**
   rather than an expense, scrap and waste, and the disposal of obsolete trainers when a curriculum
   changes. This is a manufacturing-shaped balance sheet attached to an education business, and
   nobody in the education cluster has one.
4. **Instructors who are working tradespeople.** A journeyperson teaching two evenings a week while
   running their own contracting business is a genuinely hard classification call, and trade schools
   run on exactly that staffing model. Getting it wrong is assessed against the school.

Deliberately **not** this page: registration, trust accounting and refunds in depth (484, which
cross-links), online course products (53439, 51049), corporate training delivery (53955), driver
training (50134).

## Open items — NOT asserted anywhere on this page

1. No conclusion that the reader is a "vocational school" as defined, or that any particular course
   is an exempt educational supply. Both are questions of fact and the page says so.
2. No registration threshold, financial security amount, bond percentage, prescribed refund
   percentage or statutory deadline under the career colleges legislation. The mechanisms are named;
   the figures are checked against the regulation on a given file.
3. No CCA class or rate stated for shop equipment, trainer rigs, vehicles or leasehold improvements.
   Class 14.1 is named only as where an eligible intangible generally falls.
4. **No capital gains inclusion rate and no lifetime capital gains exemption amount.** The site
   carries two conflicting figures (page 53439 against roughly twenty others) and that conflict is
   still unresolved.
5. No conclusion that a given instructor is an employee or a contractor.
6. No apprenticeship programme, grant or funding amount stated, and no eligibility rule for any
   government training funding.
7. No advice on curriculum, accreditation standards, trade certification requirements or regulatory
   compliance. Those are not accounting questions.
8. No statement about whether a regulator requires audited, reviewed or compiled statements in any
   particular case; the page says the requirement is checked rather than assumed.
9. No statement about education regimes outside Ontario beyond flagging that another province has
   its own regulator.
10. No statement about provincial sales tax in Quebec, British Columbia, Saskatchewan or Manitoba
    beyond flagging that separate registrations can arise.
11. No advice on whether to incorporate stated as a rule; it is modelled on the reader's numbers.
12. No fee quoted beyond the published pricing chrome.
13. We advise on Canadian tax and coordinate where another jurisdiction is involved.
