RRSP Contribution Limit 2026

The RRSP contribution limit 2026 is the lesser of 18% of your 2025 earned income and $33,810, minus any pension adjustment, plus unused room carried forward from earlier years. You need $187,833.33 of earned income in 2025 to reach the full dollar limit. CRA has already published the 2027 limit of $35,390. You can go over your limit by $2,000 without penalty; anything more is taxed at 1% a month. Contributions for 2026 are due by March 1, 2027. At a glance

The 2026 dollar limit. The RRSP dollar limit for 2026 is $33,810, up $1,320 from $32,490 in 2025. It is a ceiling: your own limit is often much lower.

Your personal limit. Your 2026 RRSP deduction limit is the lesser of 18% of your 2025 earned income and $33,810, minus your pension adjustment and any past service pension adjustment, plus any pension adjustment reversal and unused room from earlier years.

Income needed for the maximum. You reach the full $33,810 only with $187,833.33 or more of earned income in 2025 ($33,810 / 18%) and no pension adjustment.

The 2027 limit is already published. CRA has set the 2027 RRSP dollar limit at $35,390. It applies to 2026 earned income, so you need $196,611.11 of earned income in 2026 to reach it.

Over-contribution. You can exceed your limit by $2,000 without penalty if you were 18 or older at any time in the previous year. Any excess above that is taxed at 1% a month and reported on Form T1-OVP.

Deadline and where to look. Contributions for the 2026 tax year are due by March 1, 2027 (the 60th day of 2027), a date CRA confirms each year. Your exact limit is on the RRSP deduction limit statement in your latest notice of assessment.

1. What the RRSP Contribution Limit 2026 Means

Contribution limit or deduction limit. People say "contribution limit", but the legal term is the RRSP deduction limit: the most you can deduct for the year. Nothing physically stops you from contributing more. The limit matters because amounts above it, beyond a $2,000 buffer, are taxed at 1% a month, and because amounts you cannot deduct give no tax saving while they sit in the plan.

Two numbers, often confused. The RRSP dollar limit of $33,810 is the same for everyone in 2026. Your RRSP deduction limit is personal and is built from your own earned income, your pension adjustments and your history. Most people's personal limit for 2026 is well below $33,810, while someone who has carried room forward for years can have a limit far above it.

The building blocks. CRA starts with 18% of your 2025 earned income and caps it at $33,810. It subtracts your 2025 pension adjustment (PA) and any past service pension adjustment (PSPA). It then adds any pension adjustment reversal (PAR) and all unused deduction room carried forward from earlier years. Each part is explained in the sections below.

What "room" means in practice. Your available room is your deduction limit minus any contributions you have already made but not yet deducted. If your 2026 limit is $20,000 and you have $3,000 of unused contributions from earlier years, you can contribute $17,000 more before you reach the limit ($20,000 - $3,000).

Shared with other plans. Contributions to your own RRSP, to a spousal RRSP for your spouse or partner, to a group RRSP through work and to a pooled registered pension plan all count against the same personal limit. Please add them together before you contribute. For how the RRSP fits with the other registered plans, see our overview of investment accounts in Canada.

How the dollar limit is set. The RRSP dollar limit for a year equals the money purchase limit for registered pension plans for the year before. CRA confirms that the 2027 RRSP limit of $35,390 equals the 2026 money purchase limit. The money purchase limit is indexed to growth in the average wage, which is why the RRSP limit rises by a different amount each year.

Why the 2027 figure is known early. Because the RRSP limit trails the pension limit by a year, the next year's RRSP figure is published well before that year starts. That lets high earners plan 2027 contributions now: the 18% test for 2027 uses 2026 earned income, and $196,611.11 of 2026 earned income reaches the full $35,390.

Older years. Earlier dollar limits matter only for understanding a long carry-forward history. You never need to add them up yourself, because CRA tracks your unused room year by year and shows the running total on your notice of assessment.

Do not confuse it with the TFSA limit. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000 for everyone 18 or older who is resident in Canada, regardless of income. The RRSP limit depends on what you earned. 3. The 18% Rule and What Counts as Earned Income

The rule. New RRSP room for 2026 is 18% of your 2025 earned income, up to $33,810. Someone with $50,000 of earned income gets $9,000 ($50,000 x 18%); someone with $120,000 gets $21,600 ($120,000 x 18%). Earned income is a tax term with its own definition, and it is not the same as total income on your return.

Business owners. The salary versus dividend choice in a small corporation changes RRSP room directly. Salary from your company is earned income and builds room at 18%. Dividends do not. An owner who switches from salary to dividends keeps the room already built but stops adding to it from that year on.

Landlords and the self-employed. Net rental income and net self-employment income count, but so do losses. A rental property that shows a loss after interest and capital cost allowance lowers your earned income and so your new room. Please check the net figure, not the gross rent or sales.

Students and young workers. Part-time and summer jobs create room even when no tax is owing. Our guide to how to invest in Canada covers where a first-time saver can start. A student who files a return on $12,000 of employment income banks $2,160 of room ($12,000 x 18%) that can be used years later at a much higher tax rate.

4. Pension Adjustment, PSPA and PAR

Pension adjustment (PA). If you belong to a registered pension plan or a deferred profit sharing plan (DPSP), your employer reports a pension adjustment on your T4 each year. It is the value of the pension benefits you earned that year with that employer. It reduces your RRSP room for the next year and can be zero, but never negative. The idea is fairness: people with workplace pensions should not get the full RRSP room on top.

How a PA is measured. In a defined contribution pension plan, the PA is generally the total contributed by you and your employer for the year. In a defined benefit plan, it is a formula based on the pension you earned that year, so it can be much larger than what you paid in. Employer contributions to a DPSP also create a PA. A group RRSP does not, because its contributions are ordinary RRSP contributions.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. Lina earned $70,000 in 2025 and belongs to a defined contribution pension plan where she and her employer each put in 5% of pay. Her 2025 PA is $7,000 ($70,000 x 5% x 2). Her new RRSP room for 2026 is $12,600 ($70,000 x 18%) minus $7,000, which is $5,600.

Past service pension adjustment (PSPA). A PSPA arises when a defined benefit plan improves the pension for past years of service, or when you buy back past service. It reduces your RRSP room, sometimes by a large amount in a single year. Some PSPAs need CRA certification before they take effect.

Pension adjustment reversal (PAR). If you leave a registered pension plan or a DPSP before retirement and the benefit you take away is worth less than the PAs that were reported for you, the plan reports a PAR. It adds RRSP room back for the year you leave. People who change jobs after a few years in a defined benefit plan are the main group who get one.

Where these show up. Your PA is in box 52 of your T4. CRA applies PA, PSPA and PAR automatically when it calculates the RRSP deduction limit on your notice of assessment, so you do not need to adjust the number yourself, only to understand why it may be lower than 18% of your pay.

5. Carry-Forward: Unused Room and Undeducted Contributions

Unused room. Room you do not use in a year is carried forward and added to the next year's deduction limit. There is no expiry date before the year you turn 71. A person who has earned income for 15 years but never contributed can have a 2026 deduction limit far above $33,810.

Undeducted contributions. Separately, contributions you make but choose not to deduct are carried forward as unused RRSP contributions. They still count against your room, because they are in the plan. They can be deducted in any later year, up to your deduction limit for that year.

Why the difference matters. Unused room is space you have not filled. Undeducted contributions are money already in the plan whose deduction you have saved for later. Your notice of assessment shows both. If you confuse them, you can either leave room unused or over-contribute.

Using carry-forward well. A large carry-forward is most valuable in a year of unusually high income. This example is hypothetical: in Ontario, a $5,000 deduction saves $1,102.50 at $50,000 of income and $2,170.48 at $150,000, using 2026 brackets. Holding room for a high-income year, such as a year with a large bonus, a severance payment or a big capital gain, can almost double the value of the same deduction.

What carry-forward cannot do. Room does not move between spouses, and it does not survive past the end of the year you turn 71 for your own plan. It also does not exist until you file: CRA only adds room for a year once it has assessed a return that reports your earned income. Please file any missing returns before relying on a carry-forward figure.

6. The 60-Day Window and the 2026 Deadline

The contribution year. RRSP contributions follow a contribution year that runs from the 61st day of one year to the 60th day of the next. The deadline for the 2026 tax year is March 1, 2027, the 60th day of 2027. CRA confirms the date each year and moves it to the next business day when the 60th day falls on a weekend. That is why the 2025 deadline was March 2, 2026.

Which return a contribution goes on. A contribution made from January 1, 2026 to March 1, 2027 can be deducted on your 2026 return if it was not already deducted on your 2025 return. A contribution made in the first 60 days of 2027 can be deducted for 2026 or carried to 2027 or later. Either way, it must be listed on Schedule 7 of your 2026 return.

The double-count trap. Contributions made in January and February 2026 that you already deducted on your 2025 return have used 2025 room. Do not count them again against your 2026 limit. Your notice of assessment for 2025 already reflects them.

7. RRSP Over-Contribution: The $2,000 Buffer and the 1% Tax

The buffer. CRA lets you have up to $2,000 more in your RRSPs than your deduction limit allows without any penalty tax, as long as you were 18 or older at any time in the previous year. A teenager who was under 18 for the whole previous year has no buffer at all, so even $1 over is an excess.

The tax above the buffer. Any cumulative excess above your limit plus $2,000 is taxed at 1% for each month it remains. It is not a one-time charge: it keeps running every month until you withdraw the excess or new room absorbs it.

Filing and paying. The tax is reported on Form T1-OVP, which must be filed within 90 days after the end of the calendar year in which the excess existed. Interest and late-filing penalties apply if you miss it. If the excess came from a reasonable error and you took steps to remove it, you can ask CRA to waive the tax on Form RC2503.

Removing the excess. You can withdraw the excess. Normally tax is withheld on any RRSP withdrawal, but Form T3012A lets you ask CRA to approve a withdrawal of undeducted contributions without withholding. If you never deducted the excess, you can usually claim an offsetting deduction so the withdrawal is not taxed twice. Alternatively, if January 1 brings enough new room, the excess is absorbed from that month on.

Do not use the buffer as extra room. The $2,000 is not deductible while it is over the limit. It earns tax-deferred returns, but there is no deduction until future room covers it. If you have savings beyond your RRSP room, a tax-free savings account or a high-interest savings account is the usual place for them. The buffer's real job is a safety margin for small mistakes, such as an extra payroll contribution or a missed receipt.

8. Group RRSPs and Employer Contributions

What a group RRSP is. A group RRSP is a set of individual RRSPs administered through your employer. You contribute by payroll deduction, and many employers add a matching contribution. Each plan belongs to you and counts against your own deduction limit.

Employer money uses your room. Employer contributions to a group RRSP are added to your employment income on your T4 and then appear on your RRSP receipt, so you deduct them like your own contributions. This example is hypothetical: Ravi earns $80,000, contributes 5% of pay ($80,000 x 5% = $4,000) and his employer matches it with another $4,000. That is $8,000 of his room used in the year ($4,000 + $4,000). If his 2026 limit is $14,400, he has $6,400 left for personal contributions ($14,400 - $8,000).

Tax relief at source. Employers usually reduce the income tax withheld from each pay to reflect group RRSP contributions, so the tax saving arrives on each paycheque rather than as a refund. That is useful, but it also means the group plan contributions are easy to forget when you add up your own contributions.

Personal and group plans together. Many people hold a group RRSP for the match and a personal self-directed RRSP for everything else. Both draw on one limit. If you are choosing where to hold the personal plan, our ranking of the best RRSP accounts compares costs, and the guide on transferring an investment account explains how to move a group plan when you leave the employer.

Group RRSP versus DPSP. Some employers pay their match into a deferred profit sharing plan instead of the group RRSP. DPSP contributions do not appear on your RRSP receipt; they create a pension adjustment that lowers next year's RRSP room. Check your T4 for a PA if your employer runs a DPSP.

The most common over-contribution. Payroll contributions keep going after you have used your limit, especially if you also made a large personal contribution at the deadline. Please compare year-to-date group contributions with your limit each autumn, and ask payroll to pause or lower contributions if needed. Stopping payroll contributions can also stop the employer match, which is an immediate return that usually outweighs a small, fixable excess, and the $2,000 buffer covers most of these slips.

9. Spousal RRSP Contributions and Your Limit

Your room, their plan. Contributions to a spousal RRSP use your deduction limit, not your spouse's. If your 2026 limit is $18,000, the total you put into your own RRSP and a spousal RRSP together cannot exceed $18,000 (plus the buffer) without an excess. Your spouse's own limit is untouched and can be used for their own plan.

Who claims the deduction. You, the contributor, claim the deduction on your return. The receipt is issued in your name, with your spouse named as the annuitant. That is why spousal contributions make sense when the contributor has the higher tax rate today.

Withdrawals can come back to you. If your spouse withdraws in the year of a spousal contribution or either of the two years after, the withdrawal is generally taxed to you. This does not change your limit, but it affects how long the money should stay in the plan. Our RRSP guide walks through the timing.

After 71. You lose the ability to contribute to your own RRSP after the year you turn 71, but you can still use your room for a spousal RRSP until the end of the year your spouse turns 71. This lets older workers with earned income keep using their room, as long as they have a younger spouse or partner.

Common-law partners. The rules apply to married spouses and common-law partners in the same way. When a relationship ends, the existing spousal plan stays with the annuitant, and the contributor's unused room is unaffected.

10. Where to Find Your RRSP Deduction Limit

Your notice of assessment. After CRA processes your return, your notice of assessment includes an RRSP deduction limit statement. For the 2025 return, it shows your RRSP deduction limit for 2026, any unused RRSP contributions you have not yet deducted, and the room still available. This is the number to work from, because CRA has already applied your PA, PSPA, PAR and carry-forward.

Online and by phone. The same statement is available in your online CRA account, or by calling CRA with your social insurance number and details from your last return. Your employer, bank and broker do not know your limit and cannot tell you.

Adjust for what happened after you filed. The statement only knows about contributions reported on the return it assessed. Anything contributed after that, including group RRSP payroll deductions, is not reflected. Please subtract every contribution made since the assessed return before you add more.

If the number looks wrong. Common reasons are a PA you forgot about, a late PSPA, a return that has not been assessed yet, or a missing year of returns. A notice of reassessment replaces the earlier figure. If you have not filed for several years, your limit will not include room from those years until the missing returns are filed and assessed.

11. Special Cases: Age 71, Newcomers, Repayments and Transfers

The year you turn 71. December 31 of the year you turn 71 is the last day you can contribute to your own RRSP. Room you have not used by then can only go to a spousal RRSP, if your spouse is younger. Your own plan then becomes a RRIF or annuity; the RRIF minimum withdrawal table shows what must come out each year, and the RRIF calculator projects it. If you have earned income in your 71st year, it creates room for the next year; some people contribute that amount in December before the plan closes and accept one month of the 1% tax on the part above the $2,000 buffer.

Newcomers. RRSP room comes from earned income in the previous year while you were resident. Someone who became resident in 2026 usually has no RRSP room until 2027, based on the Canadian earned income reported on a 2026 return. Earned income from before you arrived does not count.

Non-residents. If you leave Canada, room you built while resident stays available, and Canadian employment income earned while non-resident can still build room. A deduction is only useful if you have Canadian income to claim it against.

HBP and LLP repayments. Repayments to the Home Buyers' Plan and Lifelong Learning Plan are RRSP contributions that you designate as repayments on Schedule 7. They do not use your deduction limit and are not deducted. A $60,000 HBP balance repaid at the minimum of $4,000 a year ($60,000 / 15) leaves all of your room free for regular contributions. Our Home Buyers' Plan guide covers the repayment schedule.

Transfers that do not use room. A direct transfer from a First Home Savings Account to an RRSP does not reduce your RRSP room, provided you have no excess FHSA amount. Direct transfers between your own RRSPs (see how to transfer an RRSP), and transfers on separation under a court order or written agreement, do not use room either.

Transfers that do use room. An RESP accumulated income payment can be moved to your RRSP, up to $50,000 over your lifetime, but only if you have the room for it. The rest of an accumulated income payment is taxed with an extra 20% tax (12% in Quebec).

13. Common RRSP Limit Mistakes

Treating $33,810 as your limit. The dollar limit is the ceiling for people with very high income and no pension. Contributing $33,810 on a $70,000 salary with no carry-forward puts you far over the limit and into the 1% monthly tax.

Reading the wrong year. The statement on your 2025 notice of assessment gives your 2026 limit. Using an older notice can overstate or understate your room by thousands of dollars.

Thinking a spousal plan uses your spouse's room. A spousal contribution draws on the contributor's limit. A couple where one person contributes to both their own plan and a spousal plan can go over that one person's limit while the other spouse's room sits unused.

Deducting more than the limit on your return. Claiming a deduction above your limit does not create room. CRA reassesses, denies the extra deduction and charges interest on the tax that results. Claim only up to the limit and carry the rest forward as unused contributions.

Assuming dividends build room. Owner-managers who take only dividends build no new RRSP room. If you plan to contribute, the pay mix has to create earned income.

Not filing returns. Room only appears once CRA assesses a return reporting your earned income. Students, newcomers and people with low income lose years of room by not filing.

What You Need to Check Your RRSP Limit and What It Gives You

What you need

  • Your latest notice of assessment or reassessment
  • Your 2025 T4 slips, including box 52 for any pension adjustment
  • Receipts for every RRSP contribution since your last assessed return
  • Year-to-date group RRSP contributions from your pay stub
  • Any spousal RRSP contributions you made
  • Any PSPA or PAR notice from a pension plan
  • Your 2026 HBP or LLP repayment amount, if you have one

What knowing your limit gives you

  • A contribution plan that stays clear of the 1% monthly tax
  • The full deduction your income allows
  • Room you can save for a higher-income year
  • Correct payroll settings for a group RRSP
  • A clear split between your own plan and a spousal plan
  • Confidence about using the $2,000 buffer only as a safety margin
  • A forecast of 2027 room against the published $35,390 limit

How to Work Out Your RRSP Contribution Limit 2026

  1. Take 18% of your 2025 earned income. Use the earned income definition, not total income.
  2. Cap it at $33,810. If 18% is higher, the dollar limit applies.
  3. Subtract your 2025 PA and any PSPA. Your PA is in box 52 of your T4.
  4. Add any PAR and your unused room. These appear on your notice of assessment.
  5. Subtract unused contributions and anything contributed since your last assessed return. This gives the room you can still use.
  6. Compare with your notice of assessment. CRA's figure governs. If yours differs, find out why before contributing.

8 Strategies to Make the Most of Your RRSP Room

1. File every year

Room only exists once CRA assesses a return that reports earned income. Students and low earners who file build room they can use decades later, when each deduction is worth much more.

2. Hold room for high-income years

A deduction is worth your marginal rate. If you expect a bonus, a severance or a big capital gain, keeping carry-forward room for that year can nearly double the tax saved per dollar.

3. Contribute now, deduct later

You do not have to wait to invest. Contributing early starts tax-deferred growth, which the compound interest calculator can project, and you can still hold the deduction for a later return.

4. Reconcile the group plan each autumn

Add year-to-date payroll and employer contributions to your personal ones in October or November, so you have time to adjust before year-end and the deadline.

5. Watch for a PAR when you leave a pension

Leaving a defined benefit plan or DPSP early can restore room through a PAR. Ask the plan administrator, and check that the restored room appears on your next notice of assessment.

6. Use room for a younger spouse after 71

Earned income after 71 still creates room. A spousal RRSP lets you use it until the end of the year your spouse turns 71.

7. Plan salary for room in a small business

For incorporated owners, salary builds RRSP room and dividends do not. $187,833.33 of 2025 salary reached the full 2026 limit; less salary means proportionally less room.

8. Keep the buffer empty

The $2,000 buffer earns no deduction while it is over your limit. Keeping it unused means a stray payroll contribution or a late receipt never costs you the 1% monthly tax.

Illustrative Examples: Three 2026 RRSP Limits

This example is hypothetical. Three people work out their 2026 RRSP deduction limits from their 2025 income.

  1. Aisha, employee with no pension. 2025 earned income of $80,000 gives new room of $14,400 ($80,000 x 18%), below the $33,810 cap. Her notice of assessment shows $6,000 of unused room carried forward, so her 2026 limit is $20,400 ($14,400 + $6,000). She contributed $2,500 in April 2026, after filing, so she can add $17,900 more ($20,400 - $2,500) before reaching her limit.
  2. Daniel, defined benefit pension member. 2025 earned income of $100,000 gives $18,000 ($100,000 x 18%). His 2025 T4 shows a pension adjustment of $9,500, so his 2026 limit is $8,500 ($18,000 - $9,500), with no carry-forward.
  3. Mei, high earner with no pension. 2025 earned income of $250,000 gives $45,000 ($250,000 x 18%), which is above the cap, so her new room is the 2026 dollar limit of $33,810. With 2026 earned income at the same level, her 2027 new room will be the published $35,390, because $250,000 x 18% = $45,000 is again above the cap.

Your personal limit is the lesser of 18% of your 2025 earned income and $33,810, minus your pension adjustment and any past service pension adjustment, plus any pension adjustment reversal and unused room from earlier years. Your exact figure is on the RRSP deduction limit statement in your latest notice of assessment.

What is the RRSP limit for 2027?

CRA has published the 2027 RRSP dollar limit of $35,390, which equals the 2026 money purchase pension limit. It applies to 2026 earned income, so you need $196,611.11 of earned income in 2026, and no pension adjustment, to reach the full amount. Your personal 2027 limit will appear on the notice of assessment for your 2026 return.

How is my RRSP deduction limit calculated?

CRA takes 18% of your earned income from the previous year and caps it at that year's dollar limit. It subtracts your pension adjustment and any past service pension adjustment, then adds any pension adjustment reversal and all unused room carried forward. Earned income mainly means employment, self-employment and net rental income, not investment income or dividends.

What income do I need to reach the maximum RRSP contribution in 2026?

You need $187,833.33 or more of earned income in 2025, because 18% of that amount equals the $33,810 dollar limit. A pension adjustment lowers the result, so a pension plan member may never reach the maximum. Carry-forward room can push your total limit above $33,810 even with lower income.

Does unused RRSP room carry forward?

Yes. Unused RRSP deduction room carries forward with no expiry until the end of the year you turn 71. Contributions you make but do not deduct also carry forward and can be deducted in a later year. Your notice of assessment shows both the running room total and any undeducted contributions.

What is the $2,000 RRSP over-contribution buffer?

CRA lets you exceed your deduction limit by up to $2,000 without the 1% monthly tax, as long as you were 18 or older at any time in the previous year. The $2,000 is not deductible while it is over your limit, but it becomes deductible once new room covers it. Treat it as a safety margin for small mistakes.

How is an RRSP over-contribution taxed?

Any excess above your deduction limit plus $2,000 is taxed at 1% for each month it stays in the plan. You report it on Form T1-OVP within 90 days after the end of the year. You can remove the excess by withdrawing it, and you can ask CRA to waive the tax on Form RC2503 if it came from a reasonable error.

How does a pension adjustment affect my RRSP limit?

A pension adjustment is the value of pension benefits you earned in a year through a registered pension plan or DPSP. It is reported in box 52 of your T4 and reduces your RRSP room for the following year. Defined benefit members often have large PAs. If you leave a plan early, a pension adjustment reversal can restore some room.

Do employer contributions to a group RRSP count toward my limit?

Yes. Employer contributions to a group RRSP are added to your employment income and appear on your RRSP receipt, so they use your deduction room just like your own contributions. Employer contributions to a deferred profit sharing plan work differently: they create a pension adjustment that reduces next year's RRSP room instead.

Where can I find my RRSP deduction limit?

Your notice of assessment includes an RRSP deduction limit statement showing next year's limit, unused contributions and available room. It is also in your online CRA account or by phone from CRA. Subtract any contributions made after the assessed return was filed, including group RRSP payroll deductions, to get the room you can still use.

When is the deadline to use my 2026 RRSP room?

Contributions for the 2026 tax year must be made by March 1, 2027, the 60th day of 2027. CRA confirms the date each year. Room you do not use by then is not lost: it carries forward to 2027 and later years, until the end of the year you turn 71.

Do HBP repayments or FHSA transfers use RRSP room?

No. Home Buyers' Plan and Lifelong Learning Plan repayments are designated on Schedule 7 and do not use your deduction limit. A direct transfer from an FHSA to an RRSP also does not reduce your RRSP room, as long as you have no excess FHSA amount. An RESP accumulated income payment moved to an RRSP, by contrast, does need room.
