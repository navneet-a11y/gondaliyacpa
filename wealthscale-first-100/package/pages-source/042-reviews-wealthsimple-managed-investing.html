2. Wealthsimple Managed Fees by Tier

One fee, set by your total assets. Wealthsimple charges a yearly management fee on your managed balance. The rate depends on your membership tier, and your tier depends on everything you hold with Wealthsimple, not only the managed account. Core starts at $1, Premium at $100,000 and Generation at $500,000. The fee pays Wealthsimple to build, rebalance and run the portfolio. The cost of the ETFs inside the portfolio, the MER, is separate and is taken inside each fund.

What the fee costs in dollars. On $50,000 at Core, the management fee is $250 a year ($50,000 x 0.5%) and Classic fund costs add up to $75 ($50,000 x 0.15%), for $325. On $150,000 at Premium, the fee is $600 ($150,000 x 0.4%) and fund costs add up to $225, for $825. On $500,000 at Generation, the fee is $2,000 and fund costs add up to $750, for $2,750. The Generation fee steps down toward 0.2% as assets grow, and Wealthsimple tells Generation clients to confirm their exact rate with its advisor team.

The tier rule that saves money. Your tier is the higher of your net deposits or your total portfolio value across Wealthsimple. A client with $30,000 in managed investing and $70,000 in a self-directed TFSA, chequing or crypto reaches Premium, and the 0.4% rate then applies to the managed $30,000. Combined household assets also count toward Premium or Generation when two or more clients who share a home address form a household. Upgrades are automatic, take at least one business day, and new deposits can take up to 72 hours to count.

How you can lose a tier. Status drops only if both your net deposits and your total assets fall and stay below the threshold. Market declines do not reduce net deposits, so a falling market alone will not push you back to Core. Withdrawals, transfers out and currency conversions that carry an FX fee do reduce net deposits.

Other products, other fees. Automated Investing costs 0.25% a year, capped at $250 per account per year once the balance reaches $100,000. Summit's private-market portion carries a 1.00% management fee. The Private Market Fund charges a 1% Wealthsimple fee on top of the underlying fund's own management and performance fees. The advisor-led Wealth Management service charges 0.8%, falling to 0.5% at $10,000,000 or more, excluding fund costs. Our guide to every Wealthsimple fee lists the rest, such as the $20 paper statement fee.

Classic. This is the standard Wealthsimple robo advisor portfolio and the one most clients use. It holds 8 to 10 ETFs in a mix set by your risk profile. Wealthsimple rebalances it when you deposit, withdraw or change your goal, and reinvests dividends into the ETFs that have fallen below target.

Summit. Summit adds a private-markets slice to a public ETF portfolio. You need $10,000 in investable assets to open one and $3,500 before the private portion is switched on, and Wealthsimple asks for a horizon of at least four years. Private assets can be harder to sell quickly, so treat Summit money as long-term money.

Income portfolios. Wealthsimple lists Vanguard Income with a 5.2% target and a 4.7% yield, Core Bond with a 3.7% yield and Money Market with a 2.5% yield. Private Market Fund. This fund needs a $10,000 minimum and $50,000 in investable assets. On top of Wealthsimple's 1% fee, the underlying fund charges about 1.25% to 1.50% in management fees and about 15% in performance fees. Liquidity comes in monthly windows, and redemptions may be paused, so this is the least liquid option on the menu. Wealthsimple names the RRSP, TFSA and non-registered accounts for it.

4. Fund Costs (MER) by Wealthsimple Portfolio

What the MER is. The management expense ratio is the yearly cost of running each ETF, expressed as a percentage of what you hold in it. You never see it as a separate charge: it is taken inside the fund and shows up as a slightly lower return. Your true cost at any robo-advisor is the management fee plus the MER, which is why our score adds the two.

6. Rebalancing, Cash and How Deposits Are Invested

Automatic rebalancing. Wealthsimple rebalances each managed portfolio based on your deposits, withdrawals and goal changes. New money goes first to the ETFs that sit below their target weight, and dividends are reinvested the same way. This keeps the mix close to target without forcing a sale, which matters in a taxable account where selling can trigger a capital gain.

The cash buffer. Wealthsimple keeps about 0.3% to 0.5% of each managed account in cash. On $50,000 that is roughly $150 to $250 not invested in the portfolio. The buffer is small, so its drag on returns is minor, but it explains why your account never shows 100% invested.

How fast deposits are invested. Wealthsimple invests deposits within five business days. During that window the money is not exposed to the market. If you are moving a large sum, check the activity screen after a week to confirm the trades went through. Instant deposits let you move up to $50,000 on Core and up to $250,000 on Premium and Generation before the bank transfer settles.

Goal changes and risk levels. Changing your goal or risk level prompts a rebalance toward the new mix. In a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA that has no tax effect. In a non-registered account it may realize gains, so make large risk changes with your tax bill in mind, ideally early in a year with lower income.

Regular deposits. A fixed monthly deposit buys more units when prices fall and fewer when they rise, and it removes the temptation to time the market. Pair it with your payday so the money moves before you spend it.

7. Tax-Loss Harvesting, Asset Location and Custom Portfolios

Tax-loss harvesting from Premium. Wealthsimple includes tax-loss harvesting for Premium and Generation clients. Core clients do not get it. Tax-loss harvesting generally works like this: when an ETF in a non-registered account falls below what you paid, the system sells it to realize a capital loss and buy a similar, not identical, ETF so you stay invested. The realized loss can offset capital gains on your tax return.

Where it helps and where it does nothing. Harvesting only matters in a non-registered account. Gains and losses inside a TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, RESP or RRIF are not reported each year, so there is nothing to harvest. If all your managed money sits in registered accounts, the feature adds no value to you.

The superficial loss rule. The Canada Revenue Agency denies a capital loss if you, or a person affiliated with you such as a spouse, buy identical property within 30 calendar days before or after the sale and still hold it 30 days after. A harvesting system can only control trades in the account it manages. Buying the same ETF in your self-directed account or your spouse's account within that window can cancel the loss. Unused net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely. Our capital gains tax calculator shows how a loss offsets a gain.

Asset location on Generation. Generation clients get asset location, which places each type of investment in the account where it is taxed least across the household. For example, holdings that pay fully taxable income can sit in an RRSP, while growth holdings can sit in a TFSA. Wealthsimple does not publish the rules its system uses, so ask the advisor team how it applies to your accounts.

Custom portfolios on Generation. Generation clients can also get custom portfolios. Automated Investing offers a different tax tool: direct indexing of US stocks in non-registered accounts, with tax-loss harvesting included, at 0.25% a year capped at $250 per account. Inside its direct indexing sleeves there is no FX fee, while conversions outside them carry the standard 1.5%.

10. Safety: Who Holds Your Money, CIRO and CIPF

The companies involved. Wealthsimple Inc. is the portfolio manager that runs the managed portfolios. Wealthsimple Investments Inc. is an investment dealer and a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO), and it holds client accounts for managed investing, self-directed trading and crypto. Wealthsimple Financial Corp. is the parent of Wealthsimple Investments Inc. As of December 18, 2023, a majority of Wealthsimple Financial Corp.'s voting securities were controlled by Power Corporation of Canada subsidiaries and affiliates.

CIPF protection. Because Wealthsimple Investments Inc. is a CIRO member, eligible accounts are protected by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund if the dealer becomes insolvent. CIPF covers up to $1,000,000 in each of three categories: general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA combined), retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF combined) and RESPs.

What CIPF does not cover. CIPF protects against the loss of your property if a member firm fails. It does not cover market losses: if your ETFs fall 20%, CIPF pays nothing. It also does not cover crypto assets. Managed ETF portfolios are securities, so they fall inside CIPF's scope.

Size and security. Wealthsimple reported $155.6 billion in assets under administration at June 30, 2026. Clients who sign in with a passkey are reimbursed for all direct losses from unauthorized account activity, and Wealthsimple states it is SOC 1 and SOC 2 compliant. Your own safeguards. Use a passkey, keep your contact details current, and review the activity screen monthly.

11. Opening an Account and Transferring In

Opening. You open a managed account in the Wealthsimple app or on the web. As at any Canadian robo-advisor, you answer know-your-client questions on your goal, time horizon and risk tolerance, which set the portfolio's risk level. No minimum is stated for Classic, and the Core tier starts at $1. Summit needs $10,000 in investable assets, and the Private Market Fund needs a $10,000 minimum and $50,000 in investable assets.

Transfer fees covered from $25,000. Most institutions charge to transfer an account out. Wealthsimple reimburses that fee when you transfer in $25,000 or more, up to $150 per account, and the account must stay funded for 90 days. The threshold was $15,000 before April 10, 2025. Wealthsimple's own pages differ on whether you must send proof of the fee or whether the refund is automatic, so keep your old statement showing the charge.

The 1% transfer match. Wealthsimple's 1% Match pays 1% on the first $2,000,000 transferred in, up to $20,000, when you move at least $25,000 within 30 days of registering. Managed accounts qualify, including the TFSA, RRSP, FHSA and non-registered accounts. The match is paid in 24 equal monthly payments to chequing, and a 20% withdrawal buffer applies for 730 days. On a $50,000 transfer, the match is $500, paid at about $20.83 a month ($500 ÷ 24). The terms took effect May 4, 2026, with no end date stated.

10 Strategies to Cut Your Wealthsimple Managed Fees

1. Count every account toward your tier

Chequing, crypto, self-directed and managed balances all count toward Premium. Holding $40,000 managed and $60,000 elsewhere at Wealthsimple reaches $100,000 and drops the managed fee from 0.5% to 0.4%, worth $40 a year on the $40,000.

2. Form a household

Two or more clients at the same home address can pool assets for tier purposes. A couple with $50,000 each reaches Premium together, and each person's managed fee falls to 0.4%. Members choose what to share and cannot move each other's money.

3. Put taxable money where harvesting works

Tax-loss harvesting only helps in a non-registered account and only from Premium. If you hold taxable money elsewhere, consider whether moving it to Wealthsimple would both reach Premium and give the harvesting feature something to work on.

4. Respect the 30-day superficial loss window

Do not buy the same ETF in your own self-directed account, or let your spouse buy it, within 30 calendar days of a harvested sale. A superficial loss is denied, which undoes the tax benefit the harvesting created.

5. Start with Classic

Classic has the lowest published fund cost, up to 0.15%. A portfolio at 0.23% costs $40 more a year on $50,000. Choose a higher-cost portfolio only when its feature, such as values screening or income focus, matters to you.

6. Fill registered room first

The fee is the same in every account type, but tax is not. Using your 2026 TFSA room of $7,000 and your FHSA room of $8,000 before investing in a taxable account shelters growth from tax and leaves less for harvesting to fix.

7. Transfer $25,000 or more in one move

Wealthsimple covers another firm's transfer-out fee, up to $150 per account, only from $25,000. The 1% Match also needs $25,000 within 30 days of registering. Consolidating small accounts in one transfer can meet both thresholds.

8. Park short-term cash without a fee

The Money Market Income portfolio carries no management fee and showed a 2.5% yield. Money needed within a year or two does not need a growth portfolio paying 0.5% plus fund costs.

9. Test Automated Investing on large taxable balances

Automated Investing charges 0.25% capped at $250 per account a year. On $250,000, the cap means an effective 0.1% ($250 ÷ $250,000). It includes tax-loss harvesting through direct indexing in non-registered accounts.

10. Avoid moves that cut your net deposits

Withdrawals, transfers out and currency conversions that carry an FX fee reduce net deposits, which can drop your tier if your assets also fall. Plan large withdrawals so you stay above $100,000 or $500,000 if the lower fee matters to you.

Wealthsimple Managed Investing Review FAQ

How much does Wealthsimple managed investing cost?

The management fee is 0.5% a year on Core, 0.4% on Premium ($100,000 or more in assets) and 0.4% on Generation ($500,000 or more), falling to 0.2% at $10,000,000. Fund costs are extra: up to 0.15% in the Classic portfolios. On $50,000 at Core, that is $250 in fees plus up to $75 in fund costs, or $325 a year, as of October 22, 2026.

Is Wealthsimple a robo-advisor?

Yes. Wealthsimple managed investing is a robo-advisor: you answer questions about your goal and risk tolerance, and Wealthsimple Inc., a portfolio manager, builds and rebalances an ETF portfolio for you. It also offers self-directed trading, chequing, crypto and tax software, and every one of those balances counts toward the tier that sets your managed fee.

What are the Wealthsimple portfolios?

The managed menu lists Classic, Summit, Income, the Private Market Fund, Automated Investing and advisor-led Wealth Management. Classic holds between eight and ten ETFs. Summit adds a 20%, 25% or 30% private-markets slice. Income offers Vanguard Income, Core Bond and Money Market. Automated Investing uses direct indexing in non-registered accounts at 0.25%, capped at $250 per account a year.

Does Wealthsimple offer tax-loss harvesting?

Yes, on Premium and Generation, which start at $100,000 and $500,000 in assets. Core clients do not get it. Harvesting only helps in a non-registered account, because registered accounts do not report yearly gains or losses. Watch the superficial loss rule: buying an identical ETF within 30 calendar days before or after a harvested sale, in any account you or your spouse control, can deny the loss.

Is there a minimum to start with Wealthsimple managed investing?

Wealthsimple does not state a minimum for its Classic portfolios, and the Core tier starts at $1 in assets. Some options have entry rules: Summit needs $10,000 in investable assets and $3,500 before its private-markets slice switches on, and the Private Market Fund needs a $10,000 minimum and $50,000 in investable assets.

Can I talk to a human advisor at Wealthsimple?

Yes. Wealthsimple offers access to advisors, with priority phone support on Premium and priority phone and email on Generation. From $1,000,000, clients get a dedicated advisor team. Households with $1,000,000 or more can also choose Wealth Management, run by CFP and CIM advisors who act as fiduciaries, for a fee of 0.8% falling to 0.5% at $10,000,000.

Which accounts can I hold in Wealthsimple managed investing?

The managed investing page lists the TFSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, FHSA, non-registered account, RRIF, RESP and LIRA, and the business page adds corporate accounts. A LIF is listed for Automated Investing but is not confirmed for the managed portfolios. Crypto cannot be held in a managed or registered account, but crypto balances count toward your tier.

Does Wealthsimple still offer socially responsible and Halal portfolios?

Wealthsimple's help centre describes a socially responsible portfolio with fund costs of 0.21% to 0.23%, but it is not shown on the managed investing product menu, so please confirm in the app that you can choose it. The help centre also still describes a Halal portfolio, while Wealthsimple's product pages no longer list one, so availability is being confirmed.

How does Wealthsimple rebalance my portfolio?

Wealthsimple rebalances automatically when you deposit, withdraw or change your goal. New money and dividends go to the ETFs that sit below their target weight, which limits selling and the capital gains it can trigger in a taxable account. About 0.3% to 0.5% of the account is held in cash, and deposits are invested within five business days.

Is Wealthsimple managed investing safe?

Your managed account is held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., an investment dealer and CIRO member, so eligible accounts are protected by CIPF up to $1,000,000 in each of the general, retirement and RESP categories if the firm fails. CIPF does not cover market losses. Clients who use a passkey are reimbursed for direct losses from unauthorized account activity.

Is Wealthsimple cheaper than Questwealth?

No. On $50,000, Questwealth's 0.25% fee plus fund costs up to 0.12% comes to 0.37%, or $185 a year, against 0.65%, or $325, at Wealthsimple Core. Questwealth stays cheaper at higher balances too. Wealthsimple scores slightly higher overall, 4.2 / 5 against 4.1 / 5, because of its wider portfolio menu, advice access and published tax-loss harvesting.

How do I reach Premium pricing at Wealthsimple?

Hold $100,000 or more across Wealthsimple, measured as the higher of your net deposits or total portfolio value. The TFSA, RRSP, RESP, LIRA, managed and self-directed non-registered accounts, crypto, Save and chequing all count, and household members at the same address can combine assets. The upgrade is automatic and takes at least one business day; new deposits can take up to 72 hours to count.
