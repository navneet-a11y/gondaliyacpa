1. Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank: Quick Verdict

For a salaried saver who banks by app. EQ Bank wins if you can direct deposit your pay. Its 2.75% beats every Wealthsimple chequing rate, and it charges no monthly fee for it. That is the main reason it ranks first in our chequing ranking at 4.6 / 5.

2. Wealthsimple Chequing vs EQ Bank: Rates and Rules

Wealthsimple's rate depends on your tier. Wealthsimple Chequing pays 1.25% for Core clients, 1.75% for Premium clients and 2.25% for Generation clients. Premium starts at $100,000 in assets with Wealthsimple and Generation at $500,000. Status is measured as the higher of your net deposits or your total portfolio value, and household members who share an address can combine assets toward a tier.

Wealthsimple's direct-deposit boost. Core and Premium clients get an extra 0.50% with $2,000 or more of direct deposits within a 30-day period: Core rises to 1.75% and Premium to 2.25%. Generation clients already earn 2.25% and are not eligible for the boost. Interest is calculated daily and paid monthly.

EQ's rate depends only on your pay. EQ Bank pays 1.00% on the whole Personal Account balance, or 2.75% (1.00% plus a 1.75% bonus) when you direct deposit your pay. There are no tiers. The minimum pay amount for the bonus was not part of the figures we checked, so please confirm it with EQ.

4. Fees Compared

Monthly fees. Both everyday accounts charge $0 a month with no minimum balance, which is why both score 5.0 on fees in our ranking. Neither ties the $0 fee to a balance, unlike big-bank unlimited accounts, which charge $15.95 to $17.95 a month and mostly waive it only above a $4,000 or $4,500 balance.

Everyday transactions. EQ lists unlimited transactions and Interac e-Transfers at no charge. Wealthsimple lists $0 e-Transfers, $0 minimum balance and no Wealthsimple ATM fee, and does not state a transaction count or limit.

Where Wealthsimple can charge. An outgoing domestic wire costs $15, waived for Generation clients; incoming wires are free. Overdraft has no fee, but interest is charged from 3.95%. Incoming foreign-currency wires are converted with a standard FX fee that Wealthsimple does not state as a figure.

Where EQ can charge. International transfers from the US Dollar Account go through Wise, which charges its own fee. Conversions between Canadian and US dollars use EQ's exchange rate on the transfer date. EQ's charges for items such as returned payments were not part of the figures we checked.

The credit card fee. Wealthsimple's credit cards cost $20 a month ($240 a year) unless you hold $100,000 or more in individual assets or direct deposit $4,000 or more a month; Premium and Generation clients pay $0. The cards pay 2% cash back on net purchases, so a Core client paying the fee needs $12,000 a year of spending just to cover it ($240 ÷ 2%). We do not cover an EQ credit card in this comparison.

6. US Dollar Accounts and Currency

EQ's US Dollar Account. EQ pays 2.50% on US dollar balances (effective May 15, 2026), with no everyday banking fees. On US$10,000 for a year, that is US$250.00. Conversions use EQ's exchange rate on the transfer date, and international transfers go through Wise, which charges its own fee. It is the highest US dollar savings rate in our register.

Wealthsimple's US dollar savings. Wealthsimple offers up to eight US dollar savings accounts with no monthly fee and no minimum. Direct deposit into US dollars is not yet available. Two Wealthsimple pages showed different US dollar savings rates on October 22, 2026, so we do not print a rate until it is confirmed. No Wealthsimple page we checked states whether CIPF or CDIC covers US dollar savings.

Currency conversion. Wealthsimple's prepaid card charges 0% on foreign purchases, which suits travellers spending in other currencies without holding them. For investing, Wealthsimple charges 1.5% to convert currency when you trade US securities from a Canadian-dollar account, and a tiered fee from 1.5% down to 0% on conversions in its US dollar investing account, depending on transaction size. EQ does not publish its conversion spread as a percentage, so compare EQ's quoted rate with the market rate on the same day.

Deposit insurance on US dollars. CDIC rules cover eligible foreign-currency deposits at member institutions, and Equitable Bank is a member. EQ does not state the coverage of its US Dollar Account in the figures we checked, so please confirm it with EQ before holding a large balance. Interest on US dollar balances is taxable in Canada and is reported in Canadian dollars.

Which to use. For holding US dollars you already have, EQ's confirmed 2.50% is the clear choice today. For spending abroad, Wealthsimple's 0% foreign transaction fee card is the confirmed option. See our USD savings accounts comparison and Wealthsimple USD accounts review.

8. Deposit Protection: CDIC vs CIPF

EQ Bank: a CDIC member. EQ Bank is a trade name of Equitable Bank, a member of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation. CDIC insures eligible deposits up to $100,000 per insured category at each member, including interest. Deposits in your own name, joint deposits, TFSAs, RRSPs, RRIFs, RESPs, RDSPs and FHSAs are separate categories, so one person can have several $100,000 limits at EQ.

Wealthsimple Chequing: CDIC through partner banks. Wealthsimple is not a CDIC member. Chequing cash is held in trust at CDIC member institutions, for up to $1,000,000 of combined cover, at $100,000 per beneficiary per member institution. The cover is automatic and costs nothing extra. Wealthsimple does not disclose which partner banks it uses, for contractual reasons.

Wealthsimple Savings: CIPF, not CDIC. The Savings account is covered by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund up to $1,000,000, not by CDIC. CIPF protects accounts at member dealers such as Wealthsimple Investments Inc., a CIRO dealer member. It protects property held by a member firm if the firm fails; it does not cover market losses, and it covers cash in the general category with margin, TFSA and FHSA accounts up to $1,000,000 combined. Wealthsimple says its registered savings accounts are covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per beneficiary per category, not by CIPF.

10. Who Should Choose Wealthsimple

People who cannot move their pay. Self-employed workers, contractors and anyone whose pay must land elsewhere earn more at Wealthsimple: 1.25% on Core chequing against EQ's 1.00%, and 2.5% in Savings with no notice.

Premium and Generation clients. If you already hold $100,000 or more at Wealthsimple, chequing pays 1.75% before any pay deposit, or 2.25% at Generation, the credit card fee is $0, and tier perks such as priority phone support apply.

Travellers. The prepaid card charges 0% on foreign purchases, and ATM operator fees are reimbursed worldwide with no cap stated. EQ's card features abroad were not confirmed.

Cheque writers and cash users. Wealthsimple provides a chequebook with a pay direct deposit and takes cash deposits at more than 5,300 Canada Post locations. EQ does not offer cheque writing, and cash deposits were not part of the figures we checked.

Investors who want one app. Wealthsimple combines chequing and savings with self-directed investing, managed portfolios and registered accounts, so money can move between banking and investing in one place. See our Wealthsimple review and Core vs Premium vs Generation comparison.

Large cash balances. Chequing cover of up to $1,000,000 through partner banks suits someone holding well above $100,000 in cash who wants it insured in one account.

11. Who Should Choose EQ Bank

Salaried people at any wealth level. If your pay can be direct deposited, EQ's 2.75% beats every Wealthsimple chequing rate, with no tier to reach. A Core client with $10,000 earns $100.00 more a year at EQ (2.75% against 1.75%).

Savers who can plan withdrawals. EQ's 30 Day Notice Savings at 2.75% pays more than Wealthsimple Savings at 2.5% for money you will not need on short notice, such as the second layer of an emergency fund.

People holding US dollars. EQ's 2.50% US Dollar Account is a confirmed rate with no everyday banking fees. Wealthsimple's US dollar rates were not confirmed when we checked.

GIC buyers with small amounts. EQ sells GICs from $100, in regular, TFSA, RRSP and FHSA versions, once its rates are confirmed.

People who want a bank. EQ is a CDIC member in its own right, with $100,000 of cover per insured category and no trust structure or investment-dealer entity in between. If that simplicity matters to you, EQ provides it. Our EQ Bank review covers every account.

12. Using Wealthsimple and EQ Bank Together

A common split. Many people use one provider for everyday banking and the other for a specific job. For example, pay goes to EQ for 2.75%, while the Wealthsimple prepaid card handles foreign purchases at 0% and Wealthsimple holds investments. Interac e-Transfers between the two cost nothing at either end.

Watch the direct-deposit rules. Both bonuses depend on pay arriving by direct deposit. Wealthsimple's boost needs $2,000 or more within 30 days, and its credit card fee waiver needs $4,000 or more a month. EQ's minimum for its bonus was not part of the figures we checked. If you split your pay, confirm that each portion still qualifies, or you may lose both bonuses at once.

Split savings by timeline. Keep instant-access money in Wealthsimple Savings at 2.5% and money you can give 30 days' notice on in EQ's 30 Day Notice Savings at 2.75%. On $10,000 in each for a year, that is $250.00 and $275.00.

Spread insurance. Holding money at both spreads it across CDIC cover at EQ and CDIC or CIPF cover at Wealthsimple, which helps if your cash is well above $100,000.

Keep it simple. Two providers mean two apps, two sets of tax slips and more to track. If one provider covers what you need, one may be enough. Our bank accounts hub and savings hub list every alternative, and our comparison hub has other head-to-heads.

Wealthsimple vs EQ Bank FAQ

Is EQ Bank or Wealthsimple better?

It depends on your pay. If you can direct deposit your pay, EQ Bank pays 2.75%, more than any Wealthsimple chequing rate, and it ranks first in our chequing ranking at 4.6 / 5. If you cannot, travel often or need cheques, Wealthsimple fits better: 1.25% or more on chequing, 2.5% savings with no notice and a 0% foreign transaction fee card. Wealthsimple scores 4.4 / 5.

Which pays more interest, Wealthsimple or EQ Bank?

With a pay direct deposit, EQ Bank pays more: 2.75% against 1.75% for Wealthsimple Core and 2.25% for Premium and Generation. Without one, Wealthsimple pays more: Core chequing 1.25% and Savings 2.5%, against EQ's 1.00%. EQ's 30 Day Notice Savings pays 2.75% without a pay deposit if you can wait 30 days for withdrawals.

Is Wealthsimple Chequing CDIC insured like EQ Bank?

Both are covered by CDIC, in different ways. EQ Bank is a CDIC member, so eligible deposits are insured up to $100,000 per insured category. Wealthsimple is not a member; its chequing cash is held in trust at CDIC member institutions for up to $1,000,000 of combined cover. Wealthsimple does not name its partner banks.

Is Wealthsimple Savings covered by CDIC?

No. Wealthsimple Savings is covered by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) up to $1,000,000, not by CDIC. CIPF protects property held at a member firm if the firm fails, and it does not cover market losses. EQ Bank's savings and notice accounts are covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per insured category.

Do I need to direct deposit my pay to get the best rate?

At EQ Bank, yes: the Personal Account pays 2.75% with a pay direct deposit and 1.00% without. Which is better for travel, Wealthsimple or EQ Bank?

Wealthsimple, based on confirmed figures. Its Visa Platinum Prepaid card has no fee and a 0% foreign transaction fee, and it reimburses ATM operator fees worldwide with no cap stated. Which has the better US dollar account?

EQ Bank, on confirmed figures. Its US Dollar Account pays 2.50% with no everyday banking fees, or US$250.00 a year on US$10,000. Can I use Wealthsimple and EQ Bank together?
