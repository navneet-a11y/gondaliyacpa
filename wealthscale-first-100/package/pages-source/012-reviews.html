9. Safety Checks in Every Review: CIRO, CIPF and CDIC

Investments and deposits are protected differently. Brokers are investment dealers regulated by the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO). The Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) protects client property if a member firm fails, up to $1,000,000 for general accounts, $1,000,000 for retirement accounts and $1,000,000 for RESPs. Bank deposits are covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) up to $100,000 per insured category. Neither covers a fall in the value of your investments.

10. How to Use a Review to Choose

Start from your needs, not the score. A high score means a provider does well across our weighted factors. It does not mean it is the best fit for you. Please use the reviews this way:

  1. List what you need. Write down the accounts you will hold, how often you trade, whether you buy US shares and whether you want a managed portfolio.
  2. Shortlist by score. Pick the top three or four on the list that matches you: overall, US stocks, beginners, options, a single account type or robo-advisors.
  3. Read the cost section. Check commissions, the conversion fee and any account fee against your own pattern, and run the numbers in our cost calculator.
  4. Read the cons. Each review lists what the provider does not offer or does not publish. A missing RESP or an unpublished conversion rate can rule a provider out.
  5. Compare two head to head. Use a versus page to see your final two on every row, then open the account and request a direct transfer.

Online Broker Reviews: FAQ

Which online broker has the highest score in Canada?

Interactive Brokers Canada has the highest overall broker score at 4.4 / 5 as of October 22, 2026. It leads on cost, with a US$2 minimum per currency conversion and a 3.675% CAD margin rate. Questrade and Wealthsimple follow at 4.0 / 5. Questrade leads the US stocks score and Wealthsimple leads the beginners score.

Is Questrade or Wealthsimple better?

Both score 4.0 / 5 overall, and Questrade ranks just ahead on its unrounded score, 3.979 against 3.954. Questrade includes US dollars in registered accounts at no cost and has an advanced platform. Wealthsimple has a lower margin rate, $0 transfer-out fees and the higher beginners score. Which is better depends on how you invest.

Is EQ Bank a good bank?

EQ Bank's Personal Account has the highest chequing score we publish, 4.6 / 5, rank 1 of 20. It charges no monthly fee, pays 1.00% or 2.75% with pay direct deposit, reimburses ATM fees in Canada and is covered by CDIC through Equitable Bank. It does not offer cheque writing.

Do providers pay for reviews or higher scores?

No. Scores come from published prices and features run through the same rules for every provider. No provider can pay for a review, a score or a position. WealthScale has no paid or affiliate links today, and any future partner links will be labelled and will not change scores or order.

Why are some brokers scored but not reviewed?

We score every broker we track from its published prices, so all 13 appear in our rankings. Full reviews take longer, and five brokers do not have one yet: Desjardins Online Brokerage, National Bank Direct Brokerage, BMO InvestorLine, CIBC Investor's Edge and Scotia iTRADE. They appear in our tables without a review link.

How often are reviews updated?

A review is updated when a provider changes a price, rate or feature that feeds its score, and the score is recalculated at the same time. Every review shows the date its figures were checked. The reviews listed here use figures checked on October 22, 2026.

Do your reviewers open accounts at each provider?

Our reviews are built from what each provider publishes on its own pricing, account and help pages and from regulators' member lists, not from personal accounts. We do not claim to have tested an account, and we do not publish testimonials. Anything a provider does not publish is marked as not published.

What does a review score out of 5 mean?

It is a weighted average of factor scores from 0 to 5, built from published prices and features. For brokers the factors are cost, account types, US investing, cash and margin rates, platform and safety. Scores are shown to one decimal and compare providers within the same list only.

Are WealthScale reviews financial advice?

No. Reviews are general information about published terms. They cannot take account of your goals, tax position or circumstances, and they never tell you which investments to buy. Please use them to build a shortlist, then check the details that matter to you before you open an account.
