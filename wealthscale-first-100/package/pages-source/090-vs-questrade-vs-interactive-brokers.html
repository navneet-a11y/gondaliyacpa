1. Questrade vs Interactive Brokers: Quick Verdict

Three prices decide it. The gap between these two is not in stock commissions, where Questrade's $0 beats IBKR's $1 minimum by very little, but in what happens around the trade. Converting currency costs 1.5% at Questrade and a US$2 minimum at IBKR. Borrowing costs 8.70% at Questrade and 3.675% at IBKR. Idle cash earns effectively nothing at Questrade and interest above set balances at IBKR. Each of those can outweigh a year of IBKR commissions.

Where Questrade fights back. Questrade lets a TFSA, RRSP or other registered account hold US dollars at no extra cost, which is why it edges IBKR for first place in our US stocks ranking. It offers every account type we score, including the RESP, LIRA and LIF, publishes its transfer fees and rebates, runs a robo-advisor, and charges $0 a contract on US equity options.

The bottom line. IBKR is the cheaper broker for anyone who converts currency, borrows, holds cash or trades often in size. Questrade is the better fit for a buy-and-hold investor who wants $0 trades, US dollars held in registered accounts, a family RESP or a locked-in account at one firm.

2. Commissions: $0 vs Per Share

Questrade: $0 commission. Questrade charges nothing to buy or sell Canadian-listed or US-listed stocks and ETFs online, and no ECN fees on Canadian securities. On US orders, ECN fees apply only when you route an order directly to a market (for example US$0.003 to US$0.004 a share when the order removes liquidity), overnight-session fills cost US$0.003 a share, and US sales pay the SEC fee of 0.0000206 times the trade value. It ranks first in our commission-free trading ranking.

IBKR: fixed or tiered per-share pricing. Fixed pricing is CAD 0.01 a share on Canadian stocks and ETFs (minimum CAD 1, maximum 0.5% of the trade value) and USD 0.005 a share on US ones (minimum USD 1, maximum 1%). Tiered pricing is CAD 0.008 a share (minimum CAD 1) and USD 0.0035 a share (minimum USD 0.35), plus exchange and regulatory fees. Most orders under 100 Canadian shares or 200 US shares pay the $1 minimum under fixed pricing.

What a year of IBKR commissions buys back. Questrade's commission advantage is real but small: $1 a trade on most orders. Its 1.5% conversion fee is not. The table shows how many $1-minimum IBKR trades one year's conversion saving would pay for.

12. Using Both, and Switching Between Them

Questrade vs Interactive Brokers can be both. One possible split keeps the RESP, LIRA and a Questwealth portfolio at Questrade, where IBKR does not describe those accounts, and runs a non-registered margin account at IBKR for borrowing, US trading and currency conversion at its US$2 minimum.

Moving from IBKR to Questrade. IBKR does not publish a fee for Canadian transfers out; Canada-US transfers cost USD 10 per security. Questrade rebates the other firm's transfer fee, up to $150 per account, on eligible transfers.

  1. Check that the receiving broker offers the same account type, especially an RESP, LIRA or LIF.
  2. Check how the receiving account will hold any US dollars or US-listed securities.
  3. Find out the sending broker's transfer-out fee and whether the receiving one rebates it.
  4. Open the new account and request a direct transfer, in kind where possible.
  5. Keep enough cash in the old account to cover its fee so no debit balance builds up.
  6. Check the first statement at the new broker against the last one at the old.

Never withdraw to move. Is Questrade or Interactive Brokers better?

Interactive Brokers scores higher: 4.4 / 5 and first of 13 in our ranking, against 4.0 / 5 and second for Questrade, as of October 22, 2026. Questrade is better for $0 trades, US dollars in registered accounts, the RESP, LIRA and LIF, and a built-in robo-advisor.

Is Interactive Brokers cheaper than Questrade?

For anyone who converts currency or borrows, yes. Converting $10,000 costs US$2 at IBKR against $150 at Questrade, and CAD margin is 3.675% against 8.70%. For small Canadian buys with no conversion, Questrade is cheaper: $0 against IBKR's CAD 1 minimum, or $12 a year on 12 buys. On our two scoring profiles, IBKR averages $28 a year and Questrade $45.

What are the currency conversion fees at Questrade and IBKR?

Questrade charges 1.5% on USD/CAD conversion, or $150 on $10,000. IBKR adds no markup to the exchange rate and charges 0.20 basis points, with a minimum of US$2 per conversion, so any amount up to $100,000 costs US$2 and $250,000 costs US$5.

Can I hold US dollars in a TFSA or RRSP at Questrade or IBKR?

Which has lower margin rates, Questrade or IBKR?

Interactive Brokers, by a wide margin. IBKR charges 3.675% on CAD up to CAD 130,000 and 5.38% on USD up to USD 100,000. Questrade charges 8.70% on CAD and 11.50% on USD under $100,000. Borrowing $20,000 for a year costs $735 at IBKR and $1,740 at Questrade, as of October 22, 2026.

Does Questrade or IBKR pay interest on cash?

IBKR does: 1.675% on CAD balances above CAD 13,000 and 3.38% on USD balances above USD 10,000, with full rates for accounts above USD 100,000 in net assets. Questrade's published formula, prime minus 8.50% for CAD and prime minus 10.00% for USD, works out negative, so it pays effectively 0%.
