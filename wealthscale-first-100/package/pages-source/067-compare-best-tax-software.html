1. How We Ranked the Best Tax Software in Canada

Who is included. Only products on the Canada Revenue Agency's list of NETFILE-certified software for the 2025 tax year are ranked. A product that cannot file your return electronically is not a fair comparison, so certification is the entry ticket, not a scoring factor.

The weights. Each product gets four sub-scores from 0 to 5: price (35%), features (35%), support (15%) and Quebec filing (15%). Price and features carry the most weight because together they decide whether you can file your own return for little or nothing. Support and Quebec filing matter a lot to some filers and not at all to others.

The points rules. Price: 5 for a full-featured free tier, 3.5 when the free tier covers only simple returns, 3 when the price is not published. Features: 5 when all income types are handled, including self-employment and investment income, 4 for most, 3 for basic or not confirmed. Support: 5 when expert review or full service is available, 4 for an expert consultation on a paid tier, 3 for phone support, 1 for none. Quebec: 5 when the product files Quebec returns, 2.5 when not confirmed.

12. Common Tax Software Mistakes

Starting in a free tier that cannot finish. TurboTax Free excludes investment income, rental income, self-employment, donations and medical expenses. Check your slips and receipts against the free tier's limits before you begin, so you do not discover the upgrade at the end.

Using last year's software for this year's return. NETFILE certification is by tax year. Desktop users need the current year's version, and a provider's page may lag behind the Canada Revenue Agency's list.

Missing a slip. Investment slips can arrive late or only online. Auto-fill helps where it is offered, but compare the imported slips against your own records.

Forgetting carryforwards. Unused RRSP deductions, FHSA deductions and net capital losses can carry forward. If you switch software, have last year's return and notice of assessment to hand so nothing is lost.

Treating the return as only about tax. Your return also supplies the income figures the Canada Revenue Agency uses for benefit payments such as the GST/HST credit. See every payment date on our CRA benefit payment dates page.

Assuming Quebec support. CloudTax, GenuTax and StudioTax do not confirm Quebec filing for 2025, and Wealthsimple Tax Pro and TurboTax Expert Full Service are not available in Quebec.

Paying for help you do not use. A tier with expert help costs more. If your return is simple, the free or lowest tier may be enough; if it is not, help can be worth it. Decide before you start, not at checkout.

10 Ways to Pay Less to File Your Return

These are general, rule-based ways to keep filing costs down. None depends on a particular product being right for you.

1. Start with the free tier that truly fits

Check your slips against each free tier's limits before you begin. TurboTax Free excludes investment income, rental income, self-employment, donations and medical expenses; Wealthsimple Tax Basic has no income-type limits. Starting in the right tier avoids a surprise upgrade at the end.

2. Count your household's returns

Wealthsimple Tax Basic and CloudTax Free cover up to two returns, and Wealthsimple Tax Plus covers up to eight. Counting everyone who files before you buy tells you whether one purchase can cover the whole family.

3. Check UFile's free groups

UFile files free for post-secondary students, first-time filers, families with total income under $20,000 and simple returns with one T4 or one government pension. A student or a newcomer in your home may file for nothing.

4. Use perks you already have

Wealthsimple Premium clients ($100,000 or more, including household assets) get Tax Plus included, and Generation clients ($500,000 or more) get Pro. If you already qualify, there is no reason to pay for those tiers.

5. Prepare first, pay when you file

TurboTax's paid Do It Yourself line and UFile charge only when you file. Enter everything first, then decide whether the result is worth the price or whether a different product would cost less.

6. Buy only the help you need

Choose the lightest level of help that covers your return. TurboTax Expert Assist gives unlimited questions and a final review, while Expert Full Service has an expert do the return, and Full Service prices rise after April 15.

7. File early for full-service pricing

Because TurboTax Expert Full Service prices rise after April 15, booking help early in the season costs less. Gathering your slips in March gives you time to compare products before prices change.

8. Import your slips where you can

Wealthsimple Tax imports slips from the Canada Revenue Agency and Revenu Québec on every plan. Importing cuts typing errors and the risk of missing a slip, which can lead to a reassessment and interest later.

9. Keep last year's return

Unused RRSP and FHSA deductions and net capital losses carry forward. Having last year's return and notice of assessment to hand means you claim them in full, whichever software you use this year.

10. In Quebec, choose one product for both returns

Wealthsimple Tax Basic and Plus, TurboTax Do It Yourself, Expert Assist and Desktop, H&R Block and UFile file both returns. Using one product for both saves entering the same information twice.

Illustrative Example: Filing for a Family of Four

This example is hypothetical. Anita and Marc live in Ontario with two adult children who are full-time post-secondary students. Anita has T4 employment income and dividends from a non-registered account. Marc is self-employed. Both children have part-time T4 income. All four will file for the 2025 tax year.

  1. What the parents' returns need. Anita's return has investment income and Marc's has self-employment income, so TurboTax Free cannot handle either. TurboTax's paid prices are not confirmed by tier, so we cannot price that route here.
  2. Option A: one Wealthsimple Tax Plus purchase. Plus covers up to eight returns for $40 plus tax. Before tax, that is $40 / 4 = $10.00 a return.
  3. Option B: Basic for the parents, UFile for the students. Wealthsimple Tax Basic covers two returns with no income-type limits at a price they choose; if they choose $0, the parents file for $0. UFile files free for post-secondary students, so the two children file for $0. Total: $0 + $0 = $0.
  4. Option C: use a membership perk. If the household holds $100,000 or more with Wealthsimple, counting both parents' accounts together, Plus is included. All four returns could then be filed in one account for $0.
  5. Over five years. Option A costs $40 x 5 = $200 plus tax. Options B and C cost $0 if the family's situation and the products' terms stay the same.
  6. If they lived in Quebec. Wealthsimple Tax Basic and Plus file Quebec returns, so options A, B (for the parents) and C still work. UFile also files with Revenu Québec.

What it shows. The cheapest route depends on what each return contains and how many returns the household files, not on the headline price of one product. Run each family member through the tax software picker, and check the TFSA vs RRSP comparison before deciding where next year's savings go.
