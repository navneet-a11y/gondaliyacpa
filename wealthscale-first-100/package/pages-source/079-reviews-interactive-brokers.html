1. Interactive Brokers Canada Review: Our Verdict

The short version. Interactive Brokers Canada scores 4.4 / 5 and ranks first of the 13 online brokers we score, ahead of Questrade and Wealthsimple at 4.0 / 5. It wins by being cheap where other brokers are expensive: currency conversion, margin and idle cash. It is the only broker we score that earns the full 5.0 on both cash and margin and platform.

Where it earns its score. IBKR's cost score is 4.65 of 5, the highest of the 13 brokers. Our active profile, which converts $6,000 a year, costs $44.00 at IBKR against $90.00 at Questrade and Wealthsimple, because four conversions cost US$8 rather than $90. Its 3.675% CAD margin rate and 1.675% cash rate earn every available point on the cash and margin factor. Fractional shares, DRIP, options, an advanced desktop platform and a mobile app give it full marks on platform.

Where it loses points. IBKR scores 4.0 of 5 on account types because RESP and LIRA or LIF accounts are not described on the pages we checked. It scores 3.25 of 5 on US investing, despite the cheapest conversion we track, because it charges per-share commissions on US trades and does not state whether registered accounts can hold US dollars.

The trade-off with $0-commission brokers. Per-share pricing means a buy-and-hold investor pays something on every trade. Twelve Canadian purchases of 40 shares a year cost $12.00 at IBKR and $0.00 at Questrade or Wealthsimple. That gap is small, and it reverses the moment you convert currency: one $10,000 conversion costs US$2 at IBKR and $150 at a 1.5% broker.

Who should pick it. IBKR fits investors who buy US shares with Canadian dollars, who borrow on margin, who keep more than CAD 13,000 in cash at the broker, or who trade options and want low margin rates beside them. Families who need an RESP, and anyone with locked-in pension money, will need a second broker. See where it sits on our best online brokers in Canada ranking.

Small conversions. On $1,000 the US$2 minimum is 0.20% of the amount, still far below 1.5%. Batching small conversions into one larger one keeps the cost at a single US$2. Differences in the table count US-dollar fees at par with Canadian dollars, as our methodology does.

US dollars in registered accounts. Whether a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA at IBKR can hold US dollars is not stated on the pages we checked, which is why IBKR scores 0.5 of 2 on that part of our US investing factor. If you plan to hold US shares in an RRSP or TFSA without converting on every trade, please ask IBKR how its registered accounts handle US dollars before you transfer. Questrade, by contrast, states that its registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost.

US dividend withholding. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, US dividends paid into an RRSP, RRIF, LIRA or LIF are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA, FHSA, RESP or non-registered account, 15% is withheld with a Form W-8BEN on file, or 30% without one. In a non-registered account you can usually claim a foreign tax credit for the 15%; in a TFSA you cannot. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA explains why, and our guide on how to buy US stocks in Canada covers the whole process.

Do you still need Norbert's Gambit? At a 1.5% broker, Norbert's Gambit can save a lot on a large conversion. At IBKR, a direct conversion of up to $100,000 costs US$2, so the extra steps rarely pay. Check the numbers for your amount with our currency conversion fee calculator.

5. IBKR Platforms and Tools

Four ways to trade. IBKR names four platforms: Trader Workstation (TWS), its desktop trading platform; IBKR Desktop; IBKR Mobile; and the Client Portal, which you reach through the web portal login. Having an advanced desktop platform and a mobile app earns IBKR both of those platform points; Wealthsimple, for example, does not get the advanced-platform point.

Fractional shares. IBKR offers fractional trading in TSX and Cboe Canada stocks and ETFs (since August 1, 2023) and in US stocks. Fractional trades follow the whole-share commission schedule, so under fixed pricing each fractional order is subject to the same per-share rate, the same CAD 1.00 or USD 1.00 minimum and the same 0.5% or 1% maximum of trade value as a whole-share order.

Dividend reinvestment. IBKR's DRIP reinvests cash dividends from US- and Canada-listed common and preferred stocks for clients of IB Canada. Standard commissions apply to each reinvestment, unlike Questrade and Wealthsimple, which list no reinvestment fee. To reinvest into fractions, your account needs fractional trading permissions.

Account structures for active traders. Individual accounts come as cash, Reg T margin or portfolio margin. A cash account cannot borrow. A margin account lets you borrow against your holdings and is needed to sell short or write most options strategies. Portfolio margin sets the loan limit by the overall risk of the portfolio rather than position by position. Margin accounts can magnify losses as well as gains, so please make sure you understand the margin call rules before you borrow.

What we did not score. Our platform factor counts features, not screen design or speed, and we do not describe tools we could not confirm on IBKR's own pages. IBKR ranks second of 10 on our best investing apps list, which uses the beginners weighting.

What it pays. In an account worth more than USD 100,000, CAD 50,000 of idle cash earns interest on the CAD 37,000 above the threshold: (CAD 37,000 x 1.675% = CAD 619.75) a year if the rate holds. 9. Is Interactive Brokers Canada Safe? Safety and Regulation

Regulation. Interactive Brokers Canada Inc. states that it is a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO), which oversees investment dealers in Canada. Its site also says it is a member of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund, and CIPF's current member list includes Interactive Brokers Canada Inc. Because both memberships are stated by the broker, IBKR scores 4.0 of 5 on our safety factor.

CIPF coverage. CIPF protects client property, meaning cash and securities, that is missing when a member firm becomes insolvent. Coverage is set by account group at each member firm.

CIPF coverage limits per member firm (as of October 22, 2026)

Retirement accountsRRSP, RRIF, LIF, combined$1,000,000

What is not covered. CIPF does not cover a fall in the value of your investments, unsuitable advice, the failure of a company whose shares you own, or crypto assets. Margin losses are market losses: if a position bought on margin falls and the broker sells to cover the loan, CIPF does not replace the money.

Ownership. Our rubric adds a point for brokers owned by a bank or a financial cooperative group. IBKR is not bank-owned, so it does not get that point, and it ties with Wealthsimple, moomoo, Webull and Qtrade at 4.0. Our guide to CIPF and CDIC protection explains how the two schemes differ.

10. Opening an IBKR Account and Transfers

Opening. IBKR states there are no account minimums, so you can open and fund an account with any amount. Like any Canadian broker, it needs your photo ID and social insurance number, because investment income is reported to the CRA. Choose cash or margin at the start; a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA is a separate account.

Transfers in. IBKR's transfer-in rebate policy is not published on the pages we checked, and we found no Canadian sign-up offer. If your current broker charges $150 to transfer out, assume you pay it. Ask for a direct transfer: a TFSA moved directly between institutions does not affect your contribution room, while withdrawing and re-depositing it yourself counts as a new contribution and can cause an over-contribution. Our guide on how to transfer an investment account covers the forms.

Transfers out. The fee for moving a Canadian account out of IBKR through the usual Canadian transfer system (ATON) is not published on the pages we checked. Transfers through the US system (ACATS) are free, and northbound or southbound transfers between Canada and the US cost USD 10 per security (ISIN). Moving five holdings that way costs (5 x USD 10 = USD 50).

11. IBKR Customer Support

What this review covers. Support hours and contact channels for Canadian clients are not part of the figures we checked for this review, so we do not list them. We print only support details we can confirm in our research, and we will add them when we can.

Questions worth asking early. Three answers shape your costs at IBKR and are not on the pages we checked: whether your registered accounts can hold US dollars, what it charges to transfer an account out through ATON, and whether it rebates the fee your current broker charges. Ask all three before you move money, and keep the replies.

Have these ready. Your account number, the account type (TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, RRIF or non-registered), the name of the institution you are moving from and a recent statement. For US shares, confirm that a Form W-8BEN is on file for your TFSA and non-registered accounts, because US dividends can be withheld at 30% instead of the 15% treaty rate without it.

Keep a record. Note the date, who you dealt with and what was agreed, and save chats and emails. A dated record makes a stalled transfer or a disputed fee much easier to resolve.

10 Strategies to Keep IBKR Costs Low

1. Batch your conversions

Each conversion of up to $100,000 costs the US$2 minimum. Converting $24,000 in four orders costs US$8; in one order it costs US$2. A conversion once or twice a year is usually enough for a regular US purchase plan.

2. Make fewer, larger orders

The CAD 1.00 minimum covers up to 100 Canadian shares. Twelve monthly purchases of 40 shares cost CAD 12.00 a year; four quarterly purchases of 120 shares cost (4 x CAD 1.20 = CAD 4.80). Fewer orders mean fewer minimums.

3. Compare fixed and tiered pricing

Tiered pricing charges USD 0.0035 a share with a USD 0.35 minimum on US trades, against USD 1.00 under fixed pricing, but it adds exchange and regulatory fees. For small US orders tiered can cost less; check your own trade confirmations after a month.

4. Mind the CAD 13,000 cash threshold

Canadian cash earns 1.675% only above CAD 13,000 at full rates. Below that, idle cash earns nothing, so invest it or hold it in a savings account until you are ready.

5. Use no more than two withdrawals a month

The first two withdrawals each calendar month are free. After that, an EFT costs CAD 2.00 and a wire CAD 12.00. Combine withdrawals where you can.

6. Put options contracts in one order

The US options minimum is USD 1.00 per order. Two single-contract orders cost (2 x USD 1.00 = USD 2.00); one two-contract order costs (2 x USD 0.65 = USD 1.30).

7. Know the margin tiers

CAD margin costs 3.675% up to CAD 130,000 and 3.175% above that, up to CAD 1,300,000. Interest is charged on what you borrow, so repaying a margin balance quickly is the surest saving.

8. Place US holdings by account

US dividends in an RRSP or RRIF are exempt from US withholding once documented; in a TFSA 15% is lost. Confirm how IBKR's registered accounts hold US dollars before you build your plan around them.

9. Keep an RESP or LIRA where it is

IBKR does not describe RESP or locked-in accounts. Rather than move everything, keep those at a broker that offers them and move only the accounts IBKR supports.

10. Re-run your costs each year

Your trading pattern changes. Once a year, enter your real trades and conversions into the broker cost calculator above and check that IBKR is still the cheapest fit.

Illustrative Example: A Year at IBKR for a US-Focused Investor

This example is hypothetical. Anika, 52, has a TFSA, an RRSP and a non-registered margin account at IBKR worth more than USD 100,000 in total. She uses fixed pricing, buys US shares in her non-registered account, keeps Canadian ETFs in her TFSA, and holds cash in the non-registered account. Rates are assumed to stay the same all year.

  1. She converts CAD 40,000 to US dollars in two conversions of CAD 20,000. Each costs the minimum: ($20,000 x 0.002% = $0.40, below US$2), so (2 x US$2.00 = US$4.00). At a 1.5% broker the same conversion costs ($40,000 x 1.5% = $600.00).
  2. She buys 300 US shares each quarter: (300 x USD 0.005 = USD 1.50) an order, or (4 x USD 1.50 = USD 6.00) a year.
  3. She buys 120 Canadian ETF shares each month in her TFSA: (120 x CAD 0.01 = CAD 1.20) an order, or (12 x CAD 1.20 = CAD 14.40) a year.

  5. Her US shares pay US$800 of dividends. With a W-8BEN on file, (US$800 x 15% = US$120) is withheld, which she can usually claim as a foreign tax credit because the account is non-registered.
  6. Total costs, counting US dollars at par: ($4.00 + $6.00 + $14.40 = $24.40). Interest earned: $201.00. Net: ($201.00 - $24.40 = $176.60) ahead.
