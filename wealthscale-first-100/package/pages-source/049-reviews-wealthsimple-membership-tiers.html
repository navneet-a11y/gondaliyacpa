At a glance

Thresholds. Wealthsimple Core starts at $1 in assets, Wealthsimple Premium at $100,000 and Wealthsimple Generation at $500,000. No tier charges a membership fee, and upgrades happen automatically.

How your tier is measured. Wealthsimple uses the higher of your net deposits or your total portfolio value. You drop a tier only if both fall and stay below the threshold, so a market decline alone does not demote you.

Households. Two or more Wealthsimple clients who share a home address can form a household and combine assets toward Premium or Generation. No size limit or relationship rule is stated.

What Premium adds. A 0.4% managed fee instead of 0.5%, tax-loss harvesting, the USD account included, Wealthsimple Tax Plus included, 1.75% chequing (2.25% with direct deposit), 4.45% CAD margin, a $0 credit card fee, priority phone support and instant deposits up to $250,000.

What Generation adds. 2.25% chequing without a direct deposit, 3.95% CAD margin, Tax Pro included, a 15% staking share instead of 30%, the domestic wire fee waived, asset location, custom portfolios and priority email support. The managed fee stays at 0.4% until much larger balances, falling to 0.2% at $10,000,000.

What never changes. Commissions ($0), the 1.5% FX fee on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account, conversion rates inside a USD account, the 0.5% crypto trading fee and the 2.5% savings rate are the same on every tier.

1. Wealthsimple Tiers Explained: Core, Premium and Generation

Three tiers, no fee. Every Wealthsimple client is placed in one of three membership tiers based on the assets they hold with Wealthsimple. Core starts at $1, Premium at $100,000 and Generation at $500,000. You do not apply or pay: Wealthsimple moves you up automatically when you cross a threshold. The only paid add-on on Wealthsimple's pricing is the USD account for Core clients, at $10 plus tax a month.

Why tiers matter. The tiers do not change what you pay to trade stocks, ETFs or options; those commissions are $0 on every tier. They change the costs that grow with your balance or your borrowing: the managed investing fee, margin and line of credit rates, the USD account fee and the credit card fee. They also change what you earn on chequing and which tax software plan you get.

At $10,000,000, the managed fee for Generation falls to 0.2%.

2. How Wealthsimple Measures Your Tier

The higher of two numbers. Wealthsimple looks at both your net deposits and your total portfolio value and uses whichever is higher. Net deposits are the money you have put in, through deposits and transfers into managed, trading, crypto and chequing accounts, minus what you have taken out. Portfolio value is what everything is worth today.

Why this protects you in a downturn. Market declines do not reduce net deposits. If you deposit $100,000 and the market falls so your accounts are worth $90,000, your net deposits are still $100,000, so you stay Premium. You move down only if both measures fall and stay below the threshold.

How net deposits and portfolio value set your tier (hypothetical cases)

Deposited $80,000; market rises$80,000$104,000$104,000Premium

What lowers net deposits. Withdrawals and transfers out reduce net deposits. So do currency conversions that carry an FX fee, which is easy to miss. If you sit close to a threshold, a large withdrawal or a big conversion can tip you below it once your portfolio value is also under the line.

Timing. Upgrades are automatic but take at least one business day, and new deposits can take up to 72 hours to count. If you move money to reach Premium before a planned conversion or a margin loan, please allow a few days and check that your tier has changed first.

A practical test. Look at your current net deposit total, not just today's market value. A client who thinks they are on Core because markets fell may still be Premium on net deposits. The tier tool above uses the figure you enter, so enter the higher of the two.

4. Households: Combining Assets for Premium or Generation

Who can form one. Any two or more Wealthsimple clients who share a residential address can form a household. Wealthsimple states no maximum size and no relationship rule, so the published rules do not limit a household to partners or family. Combined household assets count toward Premium or Generation for every member.

What members see. Members choose what to share. Others in the household can see shared balances, but they cannot move money in or out of another member's accounts. Each person keeps control of their own accounts.

A worked threshold. If one partner has $70,000 and the other $35,000, each alone is Core. Together they hold $105,000 ($70,000 + $35,000), so as a household both reach Premium. A family of four at one address with $130,000, $200,000, $90,000 and $85,000 holds $505,000 ($130,000 + $200,000 + $90,000 + $85,000) and reaches Generation together.

The credit card caution. Premium and Generation clients pay $0 for the Wealthsimple credit card, but the waiver is written as $100,000 or more in individual assets, or $4,000 or more a month in direct deposits. If you reach Premium only through a household, please confirm the card fee is waived for you before relying on it. The metal card is also tied to $100,000 or more in individual assets.

When a household changes. Because a household is built on a shared address, a member who moves out may no longer qualify, and the combined total would fall. Please check each member's tier after any move, separation or change of address, because the managed fee, margin rate and USD account fee follow the tier.

6. Wealthsimple Core: What You Get From $1

The full platform at no cost. Core is not a stripped-down plan. A Core client gets $0 commissions on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs, $0 per contract on equity and ETF options, fractional shares from $1, every account type, 24-hour weekday trading in US-listed securities and $0 to transfer out. For a self-directed investor who buys Canadian-listed ETFs, Core costs exactly what Premium costs: nothing. Our Wealthsimple Trade review covers the self-directed platform.

Banking on Core. Chequing pays 1.25%, rising to 1.75% with a direct deposit of $2,000 or more in 30 days, with a $0 monthly fee and ATM operator fees reimbursed. Savings pays 2.5%, the same as every other tier. On $10,000 of chequing cash, Core earns $125.00 a year, or $175.00 with the direct-deposit boost.

Where Core costs more. Four charges are higher on Core: the managed fee (0.5%), the USD account ($10 plus tax a month), the margin rates (4.95% CAD, 7.5% USD) and the credit card ($20 a month unless you direct deposit $4,000 or more a month). Core also pays for Wealthsimple Tax Plus ($40 plus tax) if you want more than Basic, and lacks tax-loss harvesting on managed accounts.

Who Core suits. Self-directed investors in Canadian-listed funds, savers who direct deposit their pay, and beginners building toward their first $100,000 lose little on Core. The cost of being on Core shows up mainly for managed investors, borrowers, card users without a large direct deposit, and anyone holding US dollars.

Getting the most from Core. Direct deposit lifts chequing to 1.75%, Automated Investing caps its fee at $250 per account, and Basic tax filing is pay what you want. Our Wealthsimple Chequing review and Wealthsimple Tax review cover both in detail.

7. Wealthsimple Premium: What Changes at $100,000

Lower ongoing costs. At $100,000, the managed investing fee falls from 0.5% to 0.4%, the USD account becomes included, the CAD margin and line of credit rate falls from 4.95% to 4.45% (prime), and USD margin falls from 7.5% to 7%. The credit card fee drops to $0, subject to the individual-assets wording noted in section 4.

Better rates and service. Chequing rises to 1.75%, or 2.25% with a direct deposit of $2,000 or more in 30 days, which matches Generation's rate. Instant deposits rise from $50,000 to $250,000. Support moves to priority phone. Managed accounts add tax-loss harvesting, which can lower tax in non-registered accounts but does nothing inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA.

Extras. Premium includes Wealthsimple Tax Plus (normally $40 plus tax), up to three partner rewards, 20% off a Willful online will, 30% off Medcan Year-Round Care, 20% off a Medcan Annual Health Assessment and 15% off Medcan Dedicated Care and M Care.

What stays the same. Commissions, the 1.5% FX fee on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account, the tiered conversion rates inside a USD account, the 0.5% crypto trading fee, the 30% staking share and the $15 domestic wire fee do not change at Premium.

The direct-deposit sweet spot. A Premium client who direct deposits $2,000 or more to chequing in each 30-day period earns 2.25%, the same rate a Generation client earns without one. On $25,000 of chequing cash, that is $562.50 a year at Premium with direct deposit against $437.50 without it.

8. Wealthsimple Generation: What Changes at $500,000

Rates. Wealthsimple Generation pays 2.25% on chequing without any direct deposit; it is not eligible for the boost because it already earns the top rate. CAD margin and the line of credit fall to 3.95% (prime minus 0.5%), and USD margin to 6.5%. On a $50,000 CAD margin balance for a year, that is $1,975.00 at Generation against $2,225.00 at Premium.

Managed investing. The managed fee stays at 0.4% at $500,000 and falls to 0.2% at $10,000,000 or more. Wealthsimple does not publish the rate between those points and tells Generation clients to confirm theirs with the advisor team. Generation adds asset location and custom portfolios, and from $1,000,000 a dedicated advisor team. Households of $1,000,000 or more can also use Wealth Management at 0.8%, falling to 0.5% at $10,000,000, before fund costs.

Tax and crypto. Wealthsimple Tax Pro, normally $80 plus tax, is included, with a 30-minute consultation with a tax expert; Pro is not offered in Quebec or in French. Wealthsimple keeps 15% of staking rewards instead of 30%, and shows staking yields of up to 5% instead of up to 3.8%. Our Wealthsimple Crypto review explains how staking works.

Service and extras. Support moves to priority phone and priority email, with 24/7 chat also listed for Generation. The $15 domestic wire fee is waived. Medcan discounts rise to 40% off Year-Round Care and 30% off the Annual Health Assessment, and Generation clients get three partner rewards.

What Generation does not add. There is no lounge access, concierge or lower FX fee as a tier perk, and the crypto trading fee stays at 0.5% plus spread. The clearest published dollar gains at Generation are the margin rate, the chequing rate without direct deposit and Tax Pro. Our Wealthsimple Managed Investing review covers the advisor services.

Tax-loss harvesting. Premium also adds tax-loss harvesting to managed accounts. It sells holdings at a loss to offset capital gains elsewhere, which can lower your tax in a non-registered account. It has no effect inside a TFSA, RRSP, FHSA or other registered account, so its value depends entirely on how much you hold outside them. Generation and the managed fee. Between $500,000 and $10,000,000, Wealthsimple does not publish a rate below 0.4%, so we cannot show a confirmed saving over Premium. At $10,000,000 the published floor of 0.2% would cost $20,000 a year against $40,000 at 0.4%.

On a $250,000 account it costs $250.00 against $1,000.00 for a managed portfolio at the Premium rate. It is a different product, so compare features, but it shows that tier is not the only way to cut the fee. 10. Upgrade Math: Currency Costs and the USD Account

What Premium does not do. The 1.5% FX fee on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account is the same on Core, Premium and Generation. The conversion rates inside a USD account (1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000 per transaction) also do not depend on your tier. Reaching Premium does not make any single conversion cheaper. You can price your own conversions in the currency conversion fee calculator.

What it does do. Premium includes the USD account, which costs $10 plus tax a month ($120 a year before tax) on Core. That changes the math for anyone who holds US securities, because the USD account lets you keep sale proceeds and US dividends in US dollars instead of paying 1.5% to bring them back.

Reading the table. In a Canadian-dollar account, selling a US stock and buying another means two more conversions at 1.5% each, so the second row costs three conversions of $75.00. On Core, the USD account pays off for large conversions or repeated round trips, because its $120 fee wipes out the saving on a single small conversion. On Premium, the fee disappears, so the USD account never costs more than the Canadian-dollar route and often costs less. For a US investor, the true FX value of Premium is the $120 a year plus every round trip and dividend conversion you avoid.

Which accounts qualify. Wealthsimple's help centre says a USD account can be added to a TFSA, FHSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, LIRA, RESP or non-registered account, but not to a RRIF, spousal RRIF or LIF, so these savings do not apply to a RRIF or LIF. 13. Should You Move Assets to Reach Wealthsimple Premium?

Add up what you would use. Premium's value depends on which products you use. A self-directed investor in Canadian-listed ETFs who never borrows, converts or uses the card gains only the chequing rate and Tax Plus. A managed investor with US holdings, a card and a margin balance can gain several hundred dollars a year. Use the tables above to total only the lines that apply to you.

Count the cost of moving. Your current institution may charge a transfer-out fee, often $150. On transfers of $25,000 or more, Wealthsimple covers that fee up to $150 per account if the account stays funded for 90 days. Registered accounts must move by direct transfer, never by withdrawal and redeposit, or you can use contribution room or trigger withholding tax. Our guide to transferring an investment account covers the steps.

The 1% match. If you are eligible, Wealthsimple's 1% transfer match pays 1% of qualifying transfers of $25,000 or more made within 30 days of registering, up to $20,000, in 24 monthly payments into chequing. A $40,000 transfer would earn $400.00, or $16.67 a month. It has run since May 4, 2026, with no end date.

Protection limits. CIPF covers up to $1,000,000 in each of three categories at Wealthsimple Investments Inc.: general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA), retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF) and RESPs. Chequing is covered by CDIC through partner banks up to $1,000,000 combined. If consolidating would take you above a limit in one category, weigh that before you move. Our Is Wealthsimple safe? guide explains each layer.

Pros of moving up to Premium

  • Managed fee falls from 0.5% to 0.4%
  • USD account included, worth $120 a year before tax
  • Credit card fee of $240 a year waived
  • Chequing 1.75%, or 2.25% with direct deposit
  • CAD margin falls from 4.95% to 4.45%
  • Tax Plus and priority phone support included

Cons and limits

  • No change to the 1.5% FX fee or conversion rates
  • Little gain for self-directed investors in Canadian-listed funds
  • Tax-loss harvesting helps only non-registered accounts
  • Card waiver wording uses individual, not household, assets
  • Transfer fees above the $150 coverage, or on transfers under $25,000, are yours to pay
  • More of your money at one institution

Do not let the tier drive investment choices. Moving money only to reach a threshold makes sense when the products you already use get cheaper. It does not justify buying investments you would not otherwise hold or borrowing to inflate your balance.

How to Reach Wealthsimple Premium or Generation

  1. Find your number. Add up the assets that count at Wealthsimple and compare your net deposits with your portfolio value. Use the higher of the two.
  2. Check your household. List other Wealthsimple clients at your address. If together you clear $100,000 or $500,000, set up a household and choose what to share.
  3. Decide what to move. Identify accounts at other institutions that you would hold at Wealthsimple anyway. Moving money you would keep elsewhere only for a tier rarely pays.
  4. Transfer, do not withdraw. Request direct transfers for registered accounts. On transfers of $25,000 or more, Wealthsimple covers the old institution's fee up to $150 per account if the account stays funded 90 days.
  5. Wait for the upgrade. Allow up to 72 hours for deposits to count and at least one business day for the tier change.
  6. Switch on what you paid for before. Open the included USD account, use the included tax plan at filing time and confirm the card fee shows $0.
  7. Stay above the line. Plan large withdrawals and fee-bearing conversions so your net deposits and portfolio value do not both fall below the threshold.

10 Ways to Get More From Your Wealthsimple Tier

1. Form a household early

Two clients at one address with $70,000 and $35,000 reach Premium together at $105,000. Setting up the household costs nothing and lowers the managed fee, includes the USD account and adds Tax Plus for both members.

2. Know your net deposits

Your tier uses the higher of net deposits or portfolio value. After a market drop, check your net deposits before assuming you have fallen to Core, because deposits are not reduced by market losses.

3. Plan withdrawals near a threshold

Withdrawals, transfers out and fee-bearing conversions all reduce net deposits. If you sit just above $100,000, a large withdrawal can drop you to Core once your portfolio value is also below the line.

4. Pair Premium with direct deposit

Premium plus a direct deposit of $2,000 or more in 30 days earns 2.25% on chequing, the same as Generation. On $25,000, that is $562.50 a year against $437.50 without the deposit.

5. Open the USD account once included

On Premium the USD account costs nothing extra, so it never costs more than converting through a Canadian-dollar account. Holding US dollars there avoids 1.5% on every sale proceed and US dividend you keep in US dollars.

6. Put tax-loss harvesting where it works

Tax-loss harvesting on Premium and Generation helps only in non-registered managed accounts. If all your managed money is in a TFSA or RRSP, do not count it as part of the upgrade's value.

7. Use the included tax plan

Premium includes Tax Plus ($40 plus tax) and Generation includes Tax Pro ($80 plus tax). Choose them at filing time; Pro is not offered in Quebec or in French.

8. Confirm the card waiver

The card waiver is written for $100,000 in individual assets or $4,000 a month in direct deposits. If you reached Premium through a household, check your card statement shows $0 before relying on the waiver.

9. Cross the line with one transfer

A single transfer of $25,000 or more gets your old institution's fee covered, up to $150 per account. If eligible, the 1% transfer match adds 1% of the amount, paid over 24 months.

This example is hypothetical. Priya and Sam share an address. Priya holds $60,000 in a managed RRSP and $10,000 in chequing, with no direct deposit. Sam holds $35,000 in a managed TFSA and pays $10 a month for a USD account. Each buys Wealthsimple Tax Plus for their own return. Each alone is Core; together they hold ($70,000 + $35,000 = $105,000), so as a household both become Premium. We assume rates stay the same and leave out sales tax.

  1. Priya's managed fee falls from ($60,000 x 0.5% = $300.00) to ($60,000 x 0.4% = $240.00), saving $60.00.
  2. Sam's managed fee falls from ($35,000 x 0.5% = $175.00) to ($35,000 x 0.4% = $140.00), saving $35.00.
  3. Priya's chequing interest rises from ($10,000 x 1.25% = $125.00) to ($10,000 x 1.75% = $175.00), or $50.00 more.
  4. Sam's USD account becomes included, saving ($10 x 12 = $120.00).
  5. Tax Plus becomes included for both, saving ($40 x 2 = $80.00).
  6. Yearly gain: ($60.00 + $35.00 + $50.00 + $120.00 + $80.00 = $345.00). Tax-loss harvesting adds nothing here, because both managed accounts are registered.

Ravi, a separate client, has $470,000 and is deciding whether to transfer a $40,000 RRSP from his bank to reach Generation. He keeps $20,000 in chequing without a direct deposit, carries a $30,000 CAD margin balance, would buy Tax Pro anyway and sends four domestic wires a year.

  1. Chequing: ($20,000 x 1.75% = $350.00) on Premium against ($20,000 x 2.25% = $450.00) on Generation, or $100.00 more.

  5. Because the transfer is $25,000 or more, Wealthsimple would cover his bank's transfer-out fee up to $150 if the account stays funded 90 days. The 1% transfer match covers only transfers made within 30 days of registering, so it applies only if Ravi opened his account within that window; it would then add ($40,000 x 1% = $400.00), paid at ($400.00 ÷ 24 = $16.67) a month.

Wealthsimple Premium and Generation FAQ

What is Wealthsimple Premium?

Wealthsimple Premium is the middle membership tier, for clients or households with $100,000 or more at Wealthsimple. It has no fee. Compared with Core, it lowers the managed fee to 0.4%, adds tax-loss harvesting, includes the USD account and Wealthsimple Tax Plus, raises chequing to 1.75%, cuts CAD margin to 4.45% and waives the credit card fee.

How much do I need for Wealthsimple Premium?

You need $100,000 in Wealthsimple assets, measured as the higher of your net deposits or your total portfolio value. Assets held by other Wealthsimple clients at your home address can count if you form a household. Upgrades are automatic, take at least one business day, and new deposits can take up to 72 hours to count.

How much do I need for Wealthsimple Generation?

Generation starts at $500,000 in Wealthsimple assets, on the same measure of net deposits or portfolio value, whichever is higher. Households can combine assets to reach it. Generation adds 2.25% chequing without direct deposit, 3.95% CAD margin, Tax Pro, a 15% staking share, a waived domestic wire fee, asset location and custom portfolios.

Does Wealthsimple Premium cost anything?

No. Core, Premium and Generation have no membership fee; you qualify by the assets you hold. Wealthsimple lists no paid membership plan. The only paid add-on on its pricing is the USD account for Core clients, at $10 plus tax a month, which becomes included once you reach Premium.

Is Wealthsimple Premium worth it?

It depends on what you use. On $100,000 of managed assets, the lower fee saves $100 a year. The included USD account saves $120 before tax, Tax Plus is worth $40 and the card fee waiver $240 if you would pay it. A self-directed investor in Canadian-listed ETFs who uses none of these gains little beyond the chequing rate.

Can my spouse and I combine assets for Wealthsimple Premium?

Yes. Two or more Wealthsimple clients who share a home address can form a household, and combined assets count toward Premium or Generation for every member. No size limit or relationship rule is stated. Members choose what to share and cannot move each other's money. The card fee waiver is written for individual assets, so please confirm it applies.

Will I lose Wealthsimple Premium if the market falls?

Not from a market fall alone. Wealthsimple uses the higher of net deposits or portfolio value, and market declines do not reduce net deposits. You move down only if both measures fall and stay below $100,000. Withdrawals, transfers out and fee-bearing currency conversions do reduce net deposits, so plan those carefully near the threshold.

Does Wealthsimple Premium lower the FX fee?

No. The 1.5% FX fee on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account is the same on Core, Premium and Generation, and conversion rates inside a USD account depend on transaction size, not tier. Premium's currency benefit is the included USD account, worth $120 a year before tax, which lets you keep US dollars and avoid repeat conversions.

What does Wealthsimple Generation include?

Everything in Premium, plus 2.25% chequing without a direct deposit, 3.95% CAD and 6.5% USD margin, Wealthsimple Tax Pro, a 15% staking share with yields shown up to 5%, the $15 domestic wire fee waived, asset location, custom portfolios, priority email support and larger Medcan discounts. The managed fee falls to 0.2% at $10,000,000.

Which accounts count toward Wealthsimple Premium?

Wealthsimple names the TFSA, RESP, RRSP, GRSP, LIRA, non-registered accounts (managed or self-directed), Crypto, Save and chequing, and says almost all accounts count. The FHSA, RRIF and LIF are not named in that list, so please confirm them with Wealthsimple if you are relying on those balances to reach a tier.

How long does a Wealthsimple tier upgrade take?

Upgrades are automatic and take at least one business day after you cross the threshold. New deposits can take up to 72 hours to count toward your tier. If you are moving money to reach Premium before a conversion, margin loan or tax filing, allow a few days and confirm your new tier first.

Is Wealthsimple Core good enough?

For many investors, yes. Core includes $0 commissions on listed stocks and ETFs, $0 per options contract, fractional shares, every account type, 2.5% savings and chequing at 1.25%, or 1.75% with direct deposit. Core costs more mainly for managed portfolios, margin borrowing, the USD account and the credit card.
