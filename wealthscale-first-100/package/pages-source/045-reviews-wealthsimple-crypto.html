What the spread is. The spread is the gap between the price you pay and the market's mid price. It is built into your quote rather than shown as a separate line, and its size is not stated in the fee schedule. That makes the true cost of a market order higher than 0.5% by an amount you cannot see in advance. A limit order removes the spread: you name your price, and you still pay the 0.5% fee.

Recurring buys. Recurring or automated crypto buys carry no fee and no spread. For someone adding a fixed amount every week or month, that is the lowest-cost way to buy at Wealthsimple. The catch is control: a recurring buy fills at the scheduled time, not at a price you choose.

US-dollar trades. Wealthsimple's crypto page and its fee schedule describe the cost of US-dollar crypto trades differently, so we do not state a figure. If you plan to trade in US dollars, please confirm the conversion cost in the app before you place the order.

Compared with the rest of Wealthsimple. Listed stocks and ETFs trade at $0 commission at Wealthsimple, so crypto costs more to trade there than shares do. Our commission-free trading platforms ranking covers share trading costs. Our full list of Wealthsimple fees covers every product.

Why small orders cost more. The $1 surcharge on orders under $100 is a fixed amount, so it weighs heavily on small trades. A $50 market order pays $0.25 in fees ($50 x 0.5%) plus the $1 surcharge, for $1.25, or 2.5% of the order. At $100 the surcharge stops and the cost falls to $0.50, or 0.5%.

The practical rule. If you buy in small amounts, either use a recurring buy, which carries no fee or spread, or wait until you have $100 or more for a single order. Buying $50 a week by hand costs $65 a year in published fees ($1.25 x 52), before spreads. The same $2,600 bought through a weekly recurring buy costs $0 in fees and spreads.

Round trips add up. You pay the fee on the way in and again on the way out. Buying and later selling $1,000 of a coin costs $10.00 in published fees ($5.00 x 2), plus spreads if you use market orders. A coin needs to rise by more than 1% before a market-order round trip breaks even.

Large orders. Above $100, the fee is a flat 0.5% at every size for clients who are not Active Traders. There is no volume discount for one large trade. A $10,000 market order costs $50.00 in fees plus the spread, while the same order as a limit order costs $50.00 with no spread.

6. Wealthsimple Staking: Coins, Yields and the Share Wealthsimple Keeps

What staking is. Proof-of-stake networks pay rewards to holders who lock coins to help validate transactions. Wealthsimple stakes on your behalf and passes on part of the reward. You can stake ETH, SOL, ADA and DOT.

How the yield works. The yields Wealthsimple shows are up-to figures, set by each network and shown net of its fees. They change with network conditions and are not guaranteed. At a steady 3.8%, $10,000 of a staked coin would earn rewards worth $380 in a year, measured at a constant coin price ($10,000 x 3.8%). At 5%, the same holding would earn $500. Price swings in the coin itself will usually matter far more than the yield.

The share Wealthsimple keeps. Wealthsimple's fee schedule takes 30% of staking rewards on Core and Premium and 15% on Generation. On $100 of rewards, a Core client receives $70 and a Generation client $85. A validator's commission is separate: for ETH, it is about 5%. Premium clients pay the same 30% as Core clients, so reaching $500,000 is what lowers the staking fee.

How the share compares. NDAX keeps a 20% administration fee on rewards. Newton keeps a fee from rewards; its share was not stated in the terms we checked.

Risks to know. Staked coins may face waiting periods when you stop staking, depending on the network. Rewards are paid in the coin, so their dollar value moves with its price. Staking rewards are also taxable, as section 11 explains.

8. The $30,000 Purchase Limit for Most Investors

The rule. Canadian securities regulators cap how much crypto most retail investors can buy on a registered platform. At Wealthsimple, an investor who is not an eligible crypto investor can buy up to $30,000 in net acquisition cost in any 12-month period. An eligible crypto investor can buy up to $100,000, and an accredited investor has no limit. These limits come from Wealthsimple's exemptive relief decision of December 2025.

What net acquisition cost means. The limit counts what you pay to buy crypto, less what you receive from selling it, over the 12 months. Selling coins frees up room under the cap.

Where it applies. The limit does not apply in every province. The December 2025 decision sets out where it applies. Eligible and accredited status depend on income and asset tests set by securities law.

Why the limit exists. The cap is an investor-protection rule for retail clients buying a volatile asset through a registered platform. If you find yourself close to it, that is a good moment to review how much of your savings sits in one asset class.

Where recurring buys fit. Recurring buys count toward the limit in the same way as manual buys. A $500 weekly recurring buy adds $26,000 over a year ($500 x 52), close to the $30,000 cap, so check the total before you set a large schedule.

9. Is Wealthsimple Crypto Safe? Regulation, Custody and CIPF

Regulated as a dealer. Wealthsimple Crypto is offered by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., an investment dealer and a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO). It has been a registered crypto trading platform since January 1, 2024, when Wealthsimple Digital Assets Inc., formerly a restricted dealer, was amalgamated into it. Wealthsimple Investments Inc. is on the CSA list of authorized crypto trading platforms (the CSA page was last refreshed September 18, 2025) and the Ontario Securities Commission's list of registered crypto trading platforms. Its OSC exemptive relief was renewed in December 2025 for four years.

Where the coins are held. Most client crypto is kept in offline cold storage with regulated custodians, which Wealthsimple does not name, and it is held in trust for clients. Wealthsimple says each custody partner carries $75 million or more in cold-storage coverage. The CIPF gap. CIPF protects eligible property held by a member firm if the firm becomes insolvent. It does not cover crypto assets at any platform, including Wealthsimple. Cash sitting in your crypto account is covered, because it is not a crypto asset. If you hold a large crypto balance, the trust arrangement and custodian coverage are what stand between you and a platform failure, not CIPF.

The firm behind it. Wealthsimple reported $155.6 billion in assets under administration at June 30, 2026. 11. Tax on Crypto in Canada: What Wealthsimple Users Need to Know

Crypto is not tax-free money. For tax purposes, the Canada Revenue Agency treats crypto as property, not as currency. Every disposition has to be reported. A disposition includes selling a coin for dollars, trading one coin for another, swapping, using crypto to pay for something and giving it away.

Capital gain or business income. If you hold crypto as an investment, a gain is usually a capital gain. If your activity looks like a business, for example frequent short-term trading with the intent to profit from price moves, the gain can be business income, which is fully taxable. The answer depends on your facts, not on the platform you use. For the tax years 2001 to 2025, half of a capital gain was included in income.

Your cost base. Your adjusted cost base (ACB) for a coin is the average cost of all identical coins you hold, including the fees you paid to buy them. Fees paid when you sell reduce your proceeds. With a 0.5% fee on each trade at Wealthsimple, both adjustments are worth recording: on a $1,000 buy, the $5.00 fee raises your cost base to $1,005.

Losses. A capital loss on crypto can offset capital gains. Net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely. The superficial loss rule can also apply: if you, or a person affiliated with you, buy the identical coin within 30 calendar days before or after selling at a loss and still hold it 30 days after, the loss is denied.

Keep records. Record the date, the Canadian-dollar value, the fee and the purpose of every trade, swap, reward and transfer. If you use more than one platform or wallet, your ACB pools across all of them for identical coins. 10 Strategies to Cut Your Wealthsimple Crypto Fees

1. Buy through recurring buys

Recurring buys carry no fee and no spread. Buying $50 a week this way costs $0 in trading costs over a year, against $65 in published fees for the same 52 purchases placed by hand, before spreads.

2. Use limit orders for one-off trades

A limit order pays the 0.5% fee but no spread. On a market order, the spread adds a cost you cannot see in advance. Set your limit close to the current price if you want the order to fill quickly.

3. Never buy under $100 by hand

The $1 surcharge turns a $50 order into a 2.5% cost. Either set the amount up as a recurring buy or wait until you have $100 or more for one manual order.

4. Price in the round trip

Every manual trade pays 0.5% on the way in and 0.5% on the way out. A coin bought and sold by market order must rise by more than 1% before you break even, so trade less, not more.

5. Skip the instant withdrawal fee

Moving cash out instantly to a debit card costs 2.5%, or $25.00 on $1,000. Plan withdrawals ahead and check the cost and timing of a standard bank withdrawal in the app.

6. Count your whole tier

All your Wealthsimple accounts count toward Generation at $500,000, where the staking share Wealthsimple keeps falls from 30% to 15%. On $1,000 of staking rewards, that is $150 more for you.

7. Test every outside transfer

Send a small amount first and confirm it arrives before you move the rest. Wealthsimple lists no transfer fee, but each send pays a blockchain network fee, and a transfer on the wrong network can be lost.

8. Track your cost base from day one

Add each purchase fee to your cost base and subtract each selling fee from proceeds. At 0.5% a trade, those adjustments lower your taxable gain, but only if you can show them.

9. Mind the 30-day loss window

If you sell a coin at a loss and buy the identical coin back within 30 calendar days, the superficial loss rule can deny the loss. Wait out the window or accept the gain or loss as it stands.

10. Watch the $30,000 limit

Most investors can buy $30,000 in net acquisition cost in any 12 months. Large recurring buys reach it quickly: $500 a week is $26,000 a year. Check your total before you raise a schedule.
