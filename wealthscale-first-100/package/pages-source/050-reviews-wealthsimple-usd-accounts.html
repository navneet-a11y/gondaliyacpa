WealthScale is independent and not affiliated with Wealthsimple.

A Wealthsimple USD account lets you hold US dollars and trade US-listed stocks and ETFs with no conversion fee on each trade, instead of paying 1.5% every time you buy or sell from a Canadian-dollar account. It costs $10 a month plus tax on the Core tier after a 30-day trial, and it is included at no extra charge on Premium ($100,000+) and Generation ($500,000+). You can add one to a TFSA, FHSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, LIRA, RESP, non-registered, margin, corporate or crypto account, but not to a RRIF, spousal RRIF or LIF. Converting money inside the USD account costs 1.5% under $10,000, falling to 0% at $100,000 or more.

1. Wealthsimple USD Account Review: Our Verdict and Score

The short version. Wealthsimple charges $0 commission on US-listed stocks and ETFs, but every US trade from a Canadian-dollar account pays a 1.5% currency conversion fee on the way in and again on the way out. The USD account removes that fee from each trade by letting you hold US dollars. You pay to convert once, at a rate that falls as the conversion gets bigger, and then trade in US dollars as often as you like.

Who wins. The account is a clear gain for Premium and Generation clients, who get it at no extra charge. It also pays for itself on Core for anyone who converts in large single amounts, because the fee inside the USD account drops to 0.5% at $25,000 and 0% at $100,000. Active traders gain the most, since they stop paying 1.5% on every round trip.

Who loses. A Core client who buys a few thousand dollars of US stocks a year and never sells pays $120 a year plus tax for little benefit, because small conversions inside the USD account still cost 1.5%. Retirees with a RRIF or LIF cannot use it at all.

How we score it. In our ranking of the best brokers for US stocks, Wealthsimple scores 3.7 / 5, rank 5 of 13. The US-stocks score puts 40% of the weight on the US investing sub-score, where Wealthsimple earns 3.0 out of 5: 1 point for USD in registered accounts with a fee on Core, 1 point for a 1.5% conversion fee and 1 point for $0 US commissions. Overall, Wealthsimple scores 4.0 / 5.

2. What a Wealthsimple USD Account Is

An add-on, not a separate login. A Wealthsimple USD account is a US-dollar side added to an investing account you already hold, such as a TFSA or RRSP. Once it is on, the account can hold US-dollar cash and US-listed securities in US dollars. Your Canadian-dollar side stays as it was. You move money between the two sides by converting, at the fee rates in section 5.

What changes when you trade. Without a USD account, buying a US-listed stock in a TFSA converts your Canadian dollars at Wealthsimple's corporate exchange rate plus 1.5%, and selling converts the proceeds back with another 1.5%. With a USD account, you buy with US dollars already in the account, so there is no conversion on the trade. When you sell, the proceeds stay in US dollars until you choose to convert them.

Why that matters. The 1.5% fee is the largest cost of US investing at Wealthsimple, since commissions are $0. A $10,000 round trip from a CAD account, buying and later selling, costs about $300 in conversion fees ($10,000 x 1.5% x 2 = $300), before any change in the share price. Inside a USD account the same round trip costs nothing in conversion fees once the money is in US dollars.

USD savings is separate. Wealthsimple also offers USD savings accounts, up to 8 of them, with no monthly fee and no minimum balance. These hold US-dollar cash to earn interest rather than to trade. Section 7 covers them. Wealthsimple's pricing page lists the $10 Core fee against the USD account, and its USD page lists USD savings with no monthly fee, so please confirm in the app what your tier pays before you open one.

What it does not do. The USD account does not change the 1.5% fee on US trades placed from a Canadian-dollar account, which applies on every tier. It does not change US withholding tax, which depends on the type of account, not its currency. And it cannot be added to a RRIF, spousal RRIF or LIF. For the wider picture of buying US stocks from Canada, see our guide to buying US stocks in Canada.

5. Currency Conversion Fees Explained

Two different fee schedules. Wealthsimple charges currency conversion in two ways. A US trade placed from a Canadian-dollar account pays 1.5% on top of Wealthsimple's corporate exchange rate, on every tier and every trade size. A conversion inside a USD account, where you move Canadian dollars into US dollars or back, pays a fee that falls with the size of each conversion. The size is per transaction, not per year, and it has nothing to do with your membership tier.

6. When the $10 Fee Pays for Itself

The break-even rule. On Core, the USD account costs $120 a year before tax. It pays for itself when it saves more than $120 of conversion fees in a year. At the 1.5% rate charged on US trades from a CAD account, $120 equals the fee on $8,000 of conversions ($120 ÷ 1.5% = $8,000). If you would otherwise convert more than $8,000 a year through US trades in a CAD account, by buying, selling and rebuying, the USD account is likely to pay off. If you convert less, it may not.

Small conversions do not get cheaper. Inside the USD account, conversions under $10,000 still cost 1.5%. So a Core client who adds $1,500 a quarter to US holdings pays the same 1.5% either way, plus $120 for the USD account. The account helps that investor only if they later trade within US dollars or collect US dividends without converting them.

A tier side effect. Currency conversions that carry a fee reduce your net deposits, one of the two measures Wealthsimple uses to set your tier. If you are close to the $100,000 Premium threshold on net deposits alone, frequent paid conversions can push you below it. Your total portfolio value still counts, since Wealthsimple uses the higher of the two.

The simplest answer. On Premium or Generation, turn the USD account on: it costs nothing extra and never makes a conversion more expensive. On Core, run your own pattern through the break-even rule. Our Wealthsimple fees guide lists every other charge that could affect the decision.

8. US Withholding Tax by Account Type

Currency does not change the tax. A USD account changes how you pay for US investments. It does not change how the US taxes the dividends. US withholding tax depends on the type of account the security sits in, not on whether the account holds Canadian or US dollars.

The treaty rate. Under Article X of the Canada-US tax treaty, US tax on dividends paid to Canadian residents is capped at 15%, provided the broker has a valid Form W-8BEN on file for you. Without it, the US statutory rate of 30% applies. Wealthsimple, like every broker, needs that form to apply the 15% rate.

Retirement accounts are exempt. Article XXI(2) of the treaty exempts dividends and interest earned by arrangements operated to provide retirement benefits that are generally tax-exempt in Canada. RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs are such arrangements, so US-listed securities held directly in them are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. A spousal RRSP is an RRSP and gets the same treatment.

TFSA, FHSA and RESP are not. These accounts are not retirement arrangements under the treaty, so 15% is withheld on US dividends. Because the account pays no Canadian tax, there is nothing to claim a foreign tax credit against, and the 15% is a permanent cost. In a non-registered account, 15% is also withheld, but you report the gross dividend and can claim a foreign tax credit equal to the lesser of the foreign tax paid and the Canadian tax on that income, on Form T2209 (line 40500), with Form T2036 for the provincial part outside Quebec.

9. Norbert's Gambit at Wealthsimple

What it is. Norbert's Gambit is a way to convert Canadian dollars to US dollars at close to the market exchange rate, using a security that trades in both currencies. You buy the Canadian-dollar version, have the position moved to the US-dollar version, then sell it for US dollars. Wealthsimple states that Norbert's Gambit is available for CAD to USD conversion at near-market rates.

What it costs at Wealthsimple. Wealthsimple charges $0 commission on listed Canadian and US securities, so the trades themselves carry no commission. What remains is the market spread on the security, the price movement while the position is moved, and the time it takes. Because the gambit ends with US-dollar proceeds, you need somewhere to hold them, which in practice means a USD account.

When it beats the USD account's own fee. Inside a USD account, a $100,000 conversion already costs 0%, so the gambit adds little at that size. It is most useful for conversions under $100,000, where the USD account charges 1.5%, 1.0% or 0.5%. On a $20,000 conversion, for example, the USD account fee is $200 ($20,000 x 1.0%); the gambit avoids that fee, though not the spread and timing costs.

The trade-off. The gambit takes more steps than a one-tap conversion and exposes you to price movement while the trade settles. It is a mechanism, not a recommendation of any security. Our step-by-step guide to Norbert's Gambit explains each stage, and the conversion cost calculator in section 5 shows what you save against each broker's standard fee.

Who uses it. Investors who convert $10,000 to $99,999 at a time and are comfortable with a few extra steps get the most from it. For amounts under $10,000, the dollar savings are smaller, and for $100,000 or more the Wealthsimple USD account already charges nothing.

10. Trading in US Dollars: Dividends, DRIP, Options, Margin and Hours

Dividends and DRIP. Wealthsimple offers optional dividend reinvestment, switched on or off for a whole account rather than per security. No reinvestment fee is listed, but the conversion fee applies when US-dollar dividends are reinvested in an account without a USD side. With a USD account, US dividends can be paid and reinvested in US dollars.

Recurring investments and fractional shares. You can set recurring investments daily, weekly, every two weeks or monthly in stocks, ETFs and crypto. They are not available in joint non-registered accounts that have USD turned on. Fractional shares are available in thousands of Canadian and US stocks and ETFs from $1, in real time during market hours, with $0 commission, though US fractional trades from a CAD account still pay the 1.5% conversion fee. Fractional shares cannot be traded in extended hours and cannot be transferred to another institution.

24/5 trading. US-listed stocks and ETFs trade 24 hours a day, five days a week at Wealthsimple, from Sunday 8 pm ET to Friday 8 pm ET: pre-market 4:00 am to 9:30 am, regular hours 9:30 am to 4:00 pm, post-market 4:00 pm to 8:00 pm and overnight 8:00 pm to 4:00 am ET. Extended-hours orders must be limit orders. Canadian securities are not part of 24-hour trading.

Options. Equity and ETF options cost $0 commission and US$0 per contract on every tier, since October 2025. Index options carry per-contract fees: SPX, SPXW, VIX and NDX US$1, RUT and RUTW US$0.50, XSP and DJX US$0.25. In a registered account such as a TFSA or RRSP, you can buy calls and puts, sell cash-secured puts and sell covered calls; all other strategies need a margin account.

USD margin. USD margin costs 7.5% on Core, 7% on Premium and 6.5% on Generation, against a USD prime rate of 7% as of September 18, 2026. Borrowing US$10,000 for a year costs US$750.00 on Core, US$700.00 on Premium and US$650.00 on Generation. Compare rates in our lowest margin rates ranking.

Pass-through fees. Wealthsimple's $0 US commission does not cover regulatory and exchange pass-through fees, such as ECN, TAF or ORF charges, which may apply to some US orders and appear on the trade confirmation. Our Wealthsimple Trade review covers the trading platform in full.

11. Is Money in a Wealthsimple USD Account Protected?

Investing accounts. US-dollar cash and US-listed securities inside a Wealthsimple investing account, such as a USD TFSA or USD RRSP, are held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., a CIRO dealer member and CIPF member. CIPF covers cash and securities held by a member firm if the firm fails and client property is missing. The limits are $1,000,000 for general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA combined), $1,000,000 for retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF) and $1,000,000 for RESPs.

USD savings. Wealthsimple has not published the coverage for its USD savings accounts. No page we checked on October 22, 2026 says whether they are covered by CIPF, by CDIC or by neither. CDIC can insure foreign-currency deposits at its member institutions, but that does not tell us how Wealthsimple's USD savings is set up. Until Wealthsimple states it, treat USD savings coverage as unconfirmed.

What no plan covers. Neither CIPF nor CDIC covers a fall in the value of your investments, and neither covers changes in the exchange rate. If the US dollar weakens against the Canadian dollar, the Canadian-dollar value of your US holdings falls, and that is market risk you carry yourself.

Exchange rate risk in plain terms. Holding US dollars is a bet, in part, on the exchange rate. A USD account removes the conversion fee on each trade, but it leaves you holding a currency that moves. If you will spend the money in Canada, the rate on the day you convert back matters as much as the fee.

10 Strategies to Cut Currency Costs at Wealthsimple

1. Reach the next fee threshold

Converting $10,000 costs $100.00, while $9,999.99 costs $150.00. Likewise $25,000 costs $125.00 against $250.00 for $24,999.99. Where you can, round a conversion up to the next threshold rather than splitting it.

2. Batch instead of drip

Two $12,500 conversions cost $250.00 at 1.0%, while one $25,000 conversion costs $125.00 at 0.5%. If you add to US holdings over the year, consider saving in Canadian dollars and converting in one larger block.

3. Use the household to reach Premium

Household assets count together. Two partners at the same address with a combined $100,000 both reach Premium, which makes the USD account included and saves $120 a year before tax for each partner who would otherwise pay for it.

4. Keep sale proceeds in USD

Every conversion back to Canadian dollars and out again costs a fee. If you plan to buy more US securities, leave sale proceeds in the USD account until then.

5. Hold US dividend payers in the RRSP

RRSP dividends from US-listed securities are exempt from US withholding once documented. In a TFSA or FHSA, 15% is lost on each dividend, a cost no USD account can avoid.

6. File your W-8BEN

Without a valid W-8BEN, US dividends in taxable and TFSA-type accounts are withheld at 30% instead of 15%. On US$1,000 of dividends that is US$300.00 instead of US$150.00.

7. Consider Norbert's Gambit for mid-size amounts

For conversions under $100,000, where the USD account still charges a fee, Norbert's Gambit converts at near-market rates. Weigh the extra steps and timing risk against the fee you avoid.

8. Turn DRIP on only with USD enabled

Reinvesting US dividends in an account without a USD side triggers the conversion fee on each dividend. With USD enabled, dividends reinvest in US dollars.

9. Consider Canadian-listed ETFs

If you do not need US-listed securities, Canadian-listed ETFs trade in Canadian dollars with $0 commission and no conversion at all. This is a general option, not a product pick.

10. Check your tier before you pay

If your assets are near $100,000, one more deposit may lift you to Premium, where the USD account is included. Upgrades take at least one business day and new deposits up to 72 hours to count, so check your tier before paying for another month on Core.

Illustrative Example: One Year of US Investing in a Wealthsimple RRSP

This example is hypothetical. Marc is on the Core tier with an RRSP at Wealthsimple. Each year he invests $30,000 in US-listed stocks and ETFs, and twice a year he rebalances by selling $10,000 of one US holding and buying $10,000 of another. We compare one year with and without a USD account, using Wealthsimple's fees as of October 22, 2026, and leave out sales tax and exchange-rate movement.

  1. Without a USD account, buying $30,000 of US securities from the CAD side costs ($30,000 x 1.5% = $450.00).
  2. Each rebalance converts $10,000 back to CAD on the sale and $10,000 into USD on the purchase: ($10,000 x 1.5% x 2 = $300.00) per rebalance, or ($300.00 x 2 = $600.00) for the year.
  3. Total conversion cost without a USD account: ($450.00 + $600.00 = $1,050.00).
  4. With a USD account, Marc converts the $30,000 in one block at 0.5%: ($30,000 x 0.5% = $150.00).
  5. Rebalancing happens inside the USD side, so it costs $0.00 in conversion fees.
  6. The USD account fee on Core is ($10 x 12 = $120.00) before tax.
  7. Total cost with a USD account: ($150.00 + $120.00 = $270.00).
  8. Saving for the year: ($1,050.00 − $270.00 = $780.00). On Premium the fee is included, so his cost would be $150.00.

Had Marc split the $30,000 into three $10,000 conversions, the fee inside the USD account would be (3 x $10,000 x 1.0% = $300.00) instead of $150.00. Because the account is an RRSP, US dividends on US-listed securities he holds directly are also exempt from US withholding once the account is documented.

Wealthsimple USD Account FAQ

What is a Wealthsimple USD account?

It is a US-dollar side added to a Wealthsimple investing account, such as a TFSA or RRSP. It holds US dollars and US-listed securities, so you can buy and sell US stocks and ETFs without paying the 1.5% conversion fee on each trade. You pay a fee only when you convert money between Canadian and US dollars.

How much does a Wealthsimple USD account cost?

On Core it costs $10 a month plus tax after a 30-day trial, or $120 a year before tax, with no pro-rated refund if you cancel. It is included at no extra charge on Premium ($100,000+ in assets) and Generation ($500,000+), though you still need to opt in. Conversion fees are separate.

Is the Wealthsimple USD account free on Premium?

The account itself is included on Premium and Generation, with no monthly fee. Conversion fees still apply when you move money between currencies: 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000 inside the USD account. Trades of US-listed securities from the USD side carry no conversion fee.

Can I hold USD in a Wealthsimple TFSA?

Yes. Wealthsimple allows a USD account in a TFSA, priced at $10 a month on Core and included on Premium and Generation. It saves conversion fees, but US dividends in a TFSA are still withheld at 15% with a W-8BEN on file, and that tax cannot be recovered.

Can I hold USD in a Wealthsimple RRSP?

Yes, in an RRSP and a spousal RRSP. The RRSP is the strongest home for US dividend payers, because the Canada-US tax treaty exempts US dividends paid to retirement arrangements once the account is documented. A USD RRSP avoids both the conversion fee on trades and US withholding on US-listed securities held directly.

Can I hold USD in a Wealthsimple FHSA?

Yes. Wealthsimple allows a USD account in an FHSA, which several brokers, including National Bank Direct Brokerage, Qtrade and TD Easy Trade, do not. US dividends in an FHSA are withheld at 15%, as in a TFSA, and the tax cannot be recovered.

Can I hold USD in a Wealthsimple RRIF?

No. Wealthsimple does not offer a USD account in a RRIF, spousal RRIF or LIF. If you plan to convert an RRSP to a RRIF at Wealthsimple, expect US holdings in the RRIF to trade through the Canadian-dollar side, with the 1.5% conversion fee on each US trade.

What is Wealthsimple's currency conversion fee?

US trades from a Canadian-dollar account cost 1.5% on top of Wealthsimple's corporate exchange rate, on every tier. Inside a USD account, each conversion costs 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000. The fee depends on the size of each conversion, not on your membership tier.

What is the Wealthsimple USD savings rate?

We are confirming it. Wealthsimple's USD page and its help article give different USD savings rates for each tier, so we do not print either until they match. What is confirmed: up to 8 USD savings accounts, no monthly fee, no minimum balance and no direct deposit into USD yet.

Does a USD account avoid US withholding tax?

No. Withholding depends on the account type, not its currency. RRSPs, spousal RRSPs and LIRAs are exempt under the Canada-US tax treaty once documented. TFSAs, FHSAs and RESPs have 15% withheld with no way to recover it. Non-registered accounts have 15% withheld, which you can claim as a foreign tax credit.

Can I do Norbert's Gambit at Wealthsimple?

Yes. Wealthsimple states that Norbert's Gambit is available for converting Canadian dollars to US dollars at near-market rates. With $0 commissions on listed securities, the main costs are the market spread and price movement while the trade completes. You need a USD side to hold the US-dollar proceeds.

Is the Wealthsimple USD account worth it?

On Premium or Generation, yes, because it costs nothing extra. On Core, it pays off when it saves more than $120 a year in conversion fees, which equals 1.5% on $8,000 of conversions. Large single conversions and frequent US trading make it worthwhile; small, occasional purchases often do not.

Is money in a Wealthsimple USD account protected?

USD cash and US securities in a Wealthsimple investing account are held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., a CIPF member, so CIPF limits apply if the firm fails and property is missing. Wealthsimple has not published the coverage for USD savings accounts. No plan covers market losses or exchange-rate changes.
