1. Broker Rating Methodology: The Rules Behind Every Score

Published figures only. Every input in the model is a price, rate or yes-or-no feature that the provider publishes on its own pricing, account or help pages, or an entry on a regulator's list: the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) member list, the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) dealer directory, the CSA list of authorized crypto trading platforms and the Canada Revenue Agency's NETFILE certified-software list. All of them were checked on October 22, 2026. We do not use estimates, sales calls or figures recalled from earlier years.

One model for every provider. Each broker is priced with the same two investor profiles and given points by the same rules. There are no hand adjustments, editor's picks or bonus points. A rank is the result of arithmetic you can repeat yourself from the tables on this page, and our calculators use the same published prices.

Unknowns are not guessed. When a provider does not publish something, we do not fill the gap. A feature its own pages do not mention scores zero until it is confirmed. A fee that is not stated earns the lower "not stated" points. A missing currency conversion rate is priced at the median published rate of 1.5%, so a broker cannot gain from keeping its rate private. A provider can always raise its score by publishing a better price.

What we never use. Scores ignore whether a provider pays WealthScale, through an affiliate link, advertising, sponsorship or any other partnership. They also ignore sign-up bonuses and limited-time promotions, which end, and app-store ratings or social media popularity, which we cannot verify. WealthScale has no paid or affiliate links today. Partner applications are in progress, and if partner links are added they will be labelled and will never change inclusion, scores or order. Our page on how WealthScale makes money explains this in full.

General information, not advice. A score compares the published terms of a provider. It does not say which investments to buy, and it cannot know your balance, trading habits or tax position. Use the scores to build a short list, then read the review and check the costs for your own pattern. Our editorial standards explain who writes and reviews each page and how figures are sourced.

3. How We Measure Cost: Two Investor Profiles, Step by Step

Why profiles, not headline prices. A "$0 commission" broker can still cost more than a per-share broker once currency conversion is added, and a per-share broker can cost more for someone who trades small orders often. Pricing a realistic pattern of trades at every broker shows what a year actually costs. Both profiles are hypothetical investors; the prices are each broker's published standard prices for a self-directed account as of October 22, 2026.

The active profile. A TFSA holding $20,000. Each month the investor places two Canadian stock or ETF trades and one US trade, which is 36 trades a year. Over the year they convert $6,000 from Canadian to US dollars in four conversions of $1,500. Yearly cost = 12 x (2 x the Canadian commission + 1 x the US commission) + the conversion cost on $6,000 + any recurring account fee that cannot be avoided at this balance.

The buy-and-hold profile. The same $20,000 TFSA, with one Canadian trade a month and no currency conversion. Yearly cost = 12 x the Canadian commission + any recurring account fee. This profile rewards brokers that charge nothing to buy Canadian-listed ETFs and nothing to hold an account.

Assumptions we hold fixed. Every trade is a $2,000 order of 40 shares, which matters for brokers that charge per share: Interactive Brokers Canada's CAD 0.01 a share comes to $0.40 on 40 shares, so its CAD 1 minimum applies. US-dollar fees are counted at par, one US dollar as one Canadian dollar. We leave out ECN, exchange and regulatory pass-through fees, promotional pricing, free-trade allowances tied to age or a banking package, and active-trader discounts.

The formula. Cost score = 5 minus (the average of the two yearly costs divided by $80), kept between 0 and 5. Each $80 of average yearly cost takes one point off, so an average of $400 or more scores 0. The table shows every broker's arithmetic in full.

4. US Investing, Cash and Margin Points

US investing: three parts, up to 5 points. First, US dollars in registered accounts: 2 points when a broker offers USD in its TFSA, RRSP or other registered accounts with no fee, 1 point when a fee applies, and 0.5 when it is not stated. Second, the conversion fee: 2 points at 0.1% or less, 1.25 at up to 1.3%, 1 at 1.5%, 0.75 above 1.5% and 0.5 when no rate is published. Third, the US commission on our 40-share order: 1 point at $0, 0.75 at $2 or less and 0 above $2.

Why USD accounts earn the most. Holding US dollars inside a registered account means you convert once and can sell a US stock and buy another without paying the conversion fee twice. On $10,000, a 1.5% fee each way is $150 in and $150 out. A broker that lets you keep the dollars removes the second charge. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA explains the withholding tax that applies on top, and Norbert's Gambit shows the other common way to convert.

5. Platform, Beginner, Safety and Options Points

Platform: one point per feature. A broker earns one point each for fractional shares, a dividend reinvestment plan (DRIP), options trading, an advanced trading platform and a mobile app, up to 5. It earns half a point where a feature is only partly offered or not named, for example a mobile app reached only through a bank's banking app, or advanced platforms the broker does not name. A feature the broker's own pages do not mention scores 0.

Beginner platform: a separate sub-score. Used only in the beginners score, it gives 1.5 points for fractional shares, 1.5 for a mobile app (0.75 where the app is only partly confirmed), 1 for a DRIP and 1 for no account minimum (0.5 where the minimum is not stated). Fractional shares and an app carry the most weight because they let a new investor start with small amounts from a phone.

Safety: membership first, then ownership. A broker earns 4 points when its own pages state that it is a member of both CIRO and CIPF. It earns 3.5 when CIPF's member list confirms its membership and CIRO membership follows from that, because CIPF covers CIRO dealer members, but the broker's pages we checked do not say so. One more point is added when the broker is owned by a bank or a financial co-operative group, up to a maximum of 5. CIPF protects client property held by an insolvent member firm, up to $1,000,000 for general accounts, $1,000,000 for retirement accounts and $1,000,000 for RESPs. It does not cover market losses.

8. Robo-Advisor Ratings: Factors, Weights and Current Scores

Five factors. Robo-advisors are scored on fees (45%), account types (15%), portfolio choice (15%), minimum to start (10%) and access to advice (15%). Fees carry almost half the weight because a robo-advisor's fee is charged every year on your whole balance.

Fees: 45%. We add the management fee to the highest published fund cost (management expense ratio, or MER) of the core portfolios, at a $50,000 balance. Fees score = 5 minus (that total in percent minus 0.35) x 5, kept between 0 and 5. A total of 0.35% scores 5, and every 0.2 percentage point above it takes 1 point off. At $50,000, every robo-advisor we rank is in its first fee tier.

The other four factors. Accounts: half a point for each of 10 account types. Portfolios: one portfolio family scores 2, two score 3, three score 4, and four or more score 5. Minimum: the stated minimum, or where none is stated, the lowest balance in the published fee schedule (Wealthsimple Core starts at $1; Questwealth's lowest fee tier starts at $250). $100 or less scores 5, up to $500 scores 4.5, and more than $500 scores 4. Advice: planners or advisors available to every client score 5, advisors by phone or email score 4.5, and no client advice described scores 2.

10. Keeping the Broker Rating Methodology Current: Ties, Updates and Challenges

How ties are broken. Scores are shown to one decimal. When two providers show the same score, the higher unrounded score ranks first, and if the unrounded scores are also equal, the providers are ordered by name. Questrade (3.979) ranks ahead of Wealthsimple (3.954) on the overall list although both show 4.0 / 5, and Simplii No Fee Chequing Account ranks ahead of Tangerine No-Fee Daily Chequing Account because both score exactly 3.85.

When scores change. A score is recalculated whenever a provider changes a price, rate or feature that feeds the model, such as a commission, a conversion fee, a margin rate or a new account type. Every page that shows a score shows the date its figures were checked. Our editorial standards set how often we re-check: rates weekly and fees monthly. A change in one provider's score can move other providers up or down a list without any change to their own scores.

Partnerships never change scores. Inclusion, score, rank and the look of a provider's listing are the same whether or not that provider pays WealthScale. A provider cannot buy a review, a faster re-check or a higher position. If WealthScale adds partner links in future, they will be labelled "Partner link" and will not touch the model on this page.

How to challenge a score. If you think a figure is wrong, please write to us through our contact page with the page, the figure and where the provider publishes the correct one. We re-check the figure against the provider's own published pricing. If it has changed, we update the input, recalculate every affected score and list, and change the checked date. We aim to reply within two business days. Providers use the same route and get the same treatment as readers.

Changes to the model itself. If we change a weight or a points rule, we publish the new rule on this page and recalculate every score at once, so no provider is scored under old rules while another is scored under new ones.

Broker Rating Methodology FAQ

Why does cost carry 35% of a broker's score?

Cost is the one factor every investor pays every year, whatever they hold. Commissions and currency conversion come straight off your returns, so a broker that is $80 a year cheaper for the same trades gains a full cost point. Account types, US investing and the platform each count 15%, so a cheap broker with few accounts or a weak platform can still rank below a slightly dearer one.

Do partnerships or affiliate links change a score?

No. Scores come only from published prices and features run through the same rules for every provider. Whether a provider pays WealthScale has no part in its score, rank or listing. WealthScale has no paid or affiliate links today. If partner links are added later, they will be labelled, and the model on this page will stay the same.

How do you score a broker that does not publish its currency conversion fee?

For the cost profiles we count its conversion cost at 1.5%, the median of the nine rates that brokers do publish. On the US investing factor it gets 0.5 points for the fee, less than the 0.75 given to a published fee above 1.5%. On the lowest-FX list it is shown after the ranked brokers as not published. This applies to moomoo Canada, Qtrade Direct Investing, Scotia iTRADE and Desjardins Online Brokerage.

Why are some robo-advisors not ranked?

A robo-advisor is ranked only when both its management fee schedule and its fund costs are published in text we can verify, because fees are 45% of the score. BMO SmartFolio, Justwealth, TD Automated Investing, Nest Wealth and Qtrade Guided Portfolios are listed but not ranked, either because a fee figure is missing or because we could not confirm the service is open to new clients.

Why are savings and GIC lists sorted by rate instead of score?

Someone choosing a savings account or a GIC is mostly choosing on the interest rate, so those tables are sorted by the rate, highest first, with the date checked and the deposit insurance shown. The currency conversion and margin lists work the same way. Chequing accounts are scored because fees, ATM access and features matter as much as the rate.

How are ties between brokers broken?

Scores are shown to one decimal. When two providers show the same score, the one with the higher unrounded score ranks first. If the unrounded scores are equal too, they are ordered by name. On lists sorted by a single figure, such as conversion cost or margin rate, providers with the same figure are tied.

How often are broker scores updated?

A score is recalculated whenever a provider changes a price, rate or feature that feeds the model. We re-check rates weekly and fees monthly, and every page shows the date its figures were checked. The scores on this page use figures checked on October 22, 2026.

Can I challenge a score?

Yes. Please send the page, the figure and where the provider publishes the correct one through our contact page. We re-check against the provider's own published pricing and, if the figure has changed, update the input and recalculate every affected score and list. Providers can challenge a score the same way but cannot pay for a review or a faster re-check.
