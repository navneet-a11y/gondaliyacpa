1. Webull Canada Review: Our Verdict

The short version. Webull Canada scores 3.0 / 5 and ranks ninth of the 13 online brokers we score, and last of the seven $0-commission brokers on our best commission-free brokers list. Its trading is cheap, but the account range is the narrowest we score, its margin rate is high, and a monthly fee applies to every account.

Where it earns points. Webull's cost score is 4.33 of 5. Stocks and ETFs cost $0 in commission, so our buy-and-hold profile pays only the $8.40 yearly account fee. Webull states that it is regulated by CIRO and is a CIPF member, which earns 4.0 of 5 on safety. A desktop platform for Mac, Windows and Linux Ubuntu, a mobile app and options trading earn 3 of the 5 platform points.

Where it loses points. Account types score 1.5 of 5, the lowest of the 13 brokers, because Webull lists only cash, margin, TFSA and RRSP accounts. Cash and margin scores 0.5 of 5: the CAD margin rate of prime + 3% works out to 7.45%, and no cash interest rate is shown. US investing scores 2.5 of 5, because conversion costs 1.5% and Webull does not state whether registered accounts can hold US dollars.

The account fee. $0.70 a month sounds small, and on a large account it is. On a $20,000 TFSA it is 0.042% a year. On a $1,000 starter TFSA it is 0.84% a year ($8.40 ÷ $1,000). Three accounts cost (3 x $8.40 = $25.20) a year.

Who should pick it. Webull may suit an investor who trades Canadian and US shares from a phone in a TFSA, RRSP or margin account, with a balance large enough that $8.40 a year does not matter, and who wants the funding offer of up to $250. Most other investors will find a broader $0-commission broker with no account fee. See the full best online brokers in Canada ranking.

US dollars in a TFSA or RRSP. Webull does not state whether its TFSA or RRSP can hold US dollars, which is why it scores 0.5 of 2 on that part of our US investing factor. If an account cannot hold US dollars, every US purchase and every US sale pays the 1.5% markup, and a round trip costs about 3%. Please ask Webull how its registered accounts handle US dollars before you plan a US strategy around them. Questrade states that its registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost.

US dividend withholding. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, US dividends paid into an RRSP are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA or a cash or margin account, 15% is withheld with a Form W-8BEN on file, or 30% without one, and in a TFSA it cannot be recovered. In a cash or margin account you can usually claim a foreign tax credit. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA explains the rules.

Ways to cut the cost. Convert less often and in larger amounts, and keep US holdings in US dollars where the account allows. Norbert's Gambit converts through an interlisted ETF and pays trading costs rather than a percentage; at Webull those trades cost $0 in commission, but please confirm how Webull handles the journal step first. 9. Is Webull Safe in Canada? Safety and Regulation

Regulation. Webull states that Webull Securities (Canada) Limited and Webull Canada Crypto Limited are each regulated by CIRO, the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization, and are members of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund. Webull Securities (Canada) Limited is on CIPF's current member list. With both memberships stated, Webull scores 4.0 of 5 on our safety factor.

CIPF coverage. CIPF protects client property, meaning cash and securities, that is missing when a member firm becomes insolvent. Coverage is set by account group at each member firm.

10. Opening a Webull Account, Transfers and Current Offers

Opening. No general minimum to open is stated. Like any Canadian broker, Webull needs your photo ID and social insurance number, because investment income is reported to the CRA. Each account you open adds $0.70 a month, so open only the accounts you will use.

Transfers in. Webull advertises a transfer bonus of $150 CAD when you transfer assets to Webull, but its terms were not shown on the pages we checked. Ask for a direct transfer: a TFSA moved directly between institutions does not affect your contribution room, while withdrawing and re-depositing it yourself counts as a new contribution. Our guide on how to transfer an investment account covers the forms.

Transfers out. A partial transfer out costs $50 and a full transfer $150, plus tax, charged by CI Investment Services. Moving two accounts out in full costs (2 x $150 = $300) plus tax. Some receiving brokers rebate transfer fees, for example Questrade up to $150 per account.

The cashback offer. Webull pays $50 cashback on an initial deposit or transfer of $500 or more, plus $200 once net qualifying funding reaches $20,000 and is held for 90 days, for up to $250. Eligible accounts are cash, margin, TFSA and RRSP. No end date is stated. At exactly $20,000, $250 is 1.25% of the funding.

14. Who Webull Suits and Who Should Look Elsewhere

App-first traders with a TFSA, RRSP or margin account. If those are the only accounts you need and you trade Canadian shares from your phone, Webull's $0 commissions keep trading costs at the $8.40 yearly account fee.

New investors who can fund $20,000. The up-to-$250 cashback offer is worth 1.25% of $20,000. If you were going to hold that much in a TFSA, RRSP or margin account for at least 90 days anyway, the reward can cover many years of account fees.

New options traders. Three months of $0 per contract lets a new options user learn without commissions, though exercise and assignment still cost $1 per line item, and US$0.99 a contract applies afterward.

Who should look elsewhere. First-time buyers who need an FHSA, parents who need an RESP, retirees who need a RRIF, couples and business owners will need another broker. Small accounts pay a high share of their balance in fees: $8.40 is 0.84% of $1,000. Margin borrowers pay 7.45%, against 3.675% at Interactive Brokers Canada. Investors who want to hold US dollars in a TFSA or RRSP should choose a broker that states it allows that. Our best brokers for US stocks and best TFSA accounts lists rank the alternatives, and our broker reviews hub links every review.

A two-broker setup. Some investors keep an FHSA or RESP at a full-range broker and use Webull for a TFSA. That works, but each broker adds its own exit fees, and Webull's account fee applies to each account you hold there. Count both before you split.

10 Strategies to Keep Webull Costs Low

1. Open only the accounts you need

The $0.70 monthly fee applies to every account. A TFSA, an RRSP and an unused margin account cost (3 x $8.40 = $25.20) a year. Close or never open accounts you will not fund.

2. Watch the fee on small balances

$8.40 a year is 0.84% of a $1,000 account and 0.042% of a $20,000 account. If your Webull balance is small, a broker with no account fee keeps more of your return.

3. Convert less often, in larger amounts

Every conversion costs 1.5%, which is $150 on $10,000. Plan US purchases so you convert once for several of them, and hold US dollars where the account allows.

4. Hold US dividend payers in the RRSP

US dividends in an RRSP are exempt from US withholding once documented; in a TFSA or cash account 15% is withheld. Placing US dividend payers in the RRSP can save that 15%.

5. Take the cashback only on money you would hold anyway

The $200 needs $20,000 of net qualifying funding held for 90 days. If you would move that money anyway, the $250 total is a real gain; moving money only for the reward can cost more in exit fees later.

6. Use the free options period well

New options users pay $0 per contract for three months. After that, US$0.99 a contract applies. If you only trade options occasionally, a broker charging $0 a contract costs less over time.

7. Borrow little, and never go negative in a registered account

Margin at 7.45% costs $745 a year on $10,000. A negative balance in a TFSA or RRSP costs prime + 7.5%, or 11.95% at a 4.45% prime. Keep registered accounts in credit.

8. Reinvest dividends by hand

No DRIP is described, so dividends wait as cash with no published interest rate. Reinvesting costs $0 in commission at Webull; set a reminder to do it after each payout.

9. Use a partial transfer when you can

Moving part of an account out costs $50; moving all of it costs $150, both plus tax. If you only need to move some holdings, a partial transfer saves $100.

10. Keep FHSA and RESP money elsewhere

Webull does not list the FHSA or RESP. Do not skip those accounts: the FHSA gives $8,000 of room a year and RESP grants are paid only into an RESP.
