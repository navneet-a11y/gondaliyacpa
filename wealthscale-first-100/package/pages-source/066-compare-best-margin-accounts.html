The lowest margin rates Canada brokers publish in October 2026 start at 3.675% at Interactive Brokers Canada, on balances up to CAD 130,000. moomoo Canada follows from 3.85%, then Wealthsimple at 4.95% on its Core tier (3.95% on Generation). 1. How We Sorted Margin Rates

The stated metric. This page orders brokers by one number: the published CAD margin rate for the lowest tier under $100,000. That is the rate a new borrower with a smaller balance starts at, and it is the figure this page compares when it looks for the lowest margin interest rate in Canada. Lower is better. It is a sort on a published figure, not a WealthScale score.

Why the overall score is shown too. A broker with a low margin rate can still cost more in commissions, currency conversion or account fees. Our overall score weighs cost 35%, account types 15%, US investing 15%, platform 15%, cash and margin rates 10% and safety 10%. We show it beside each rate so you can see the whole broker. Questrade, for example, has the highest margin rate but an overall score of 4.0 / 5.

"From" rates and tiers by assets. moomoo publishes only a starting rate ("from 3.85%"), so we list it at that rate and flag that yours may be higher. Wealthsimple's rate depends on your total assets, so we list its Core rate (4.95%), which a new client with under $100,000 pays. Even at its Generation rate of 3.95%, Wealthsimple would stay third.

Ties and gaps. National Bank Direct Brokerage and CIBC Investor's Edge both publish 6.25%; their order does not mean one is cheaper. TD Direct Investing, RBC Direct Investing and BMO InvestorLine offer margin accounts but did not publish rates on the pages we could open, so they are listed separately rather than guessed.

Dates matter. Every rate was checked on October 22, 2026. Interactive Brokers' rate table is undated, moomoo's rate is dated November 3, 2025, and Qtrade's schedule took effect October 30, 2025. 2. Prime-Based Pricing: How Margin Rates in Canada Are Set

What prime is. Prime is the base rate a bank or dealer uses to price variable-rate loans. RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC, National Bank, Desjardins and Manulife Bank all have a prime rate of 4.45%. The big banks cut prime to 4.45% effective October 30, 2025, the day the Bank of Canada's cut of its policy rate to 2.25% took effect. The gap between the two is 2.20 percentage points.

What could move it. The Bank of Canada has held its policy rate at 2.25% at every decision since: December 10, 2025, and January 28, March 18, April 29, June 10, July 15 and September 2, 2026. Its remaining 2026 announcements are on Wednesday, October 28 and Wednesday, December 9, at 9:45 a.m. ET. When prime moves, every prime-linked margin rate below moves with it.

7. Margin Account Basics: How Borrowing Against Investments Works

What a margin account is. A margin account is a non-registered investing account that lets you borrow from the broker, using the investments in the account as security. You can use it like a cash account and never borrow; interest applies only to the amount you owe. Many brokers also use the margin account for short selling and for options strategies that are not allowed in registered accounts.

Loan value and buying power. Each broker decides how much it will lend against each security, under rules overseen by the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO). The total it will lend, added to your cash, is your buying power. Some securities may have little or no loan value, so two accounts of the same size can have very different buying power.

The margin requirement. Your own equity (account value minus the loan) must stay above the broker's requirement. If prices fall, your equity falls faster than the account value, because the loan stays the same. When equity drops below the requirement, you get a margin call: add cash, add securities, or sell.

Account limits and restrictions. Wealthsimple lets you hold up to two personal margin accounts, one joint margin account (in beta) and one corporate margin account per corporation. Some of its clients get a restricted margin account with a $1,000 borrowing limit. Questrade offers individual, joint and corporate margin accounts, and Desjardins offers a separate "margin option" account and a short-selling account.

How interest is charged. Interest is calculated on the debit balance, and at Wealthsimple it accrues daily and is charged monthly. Rates float with prime or the broker's benchmark. Read your monthly statement to see the interest charged, and compare it with what you expected from the rate tables on this page. 8. Margin Calls and Forced Sales: The Risks

Borrowing magnifies both directions. This example is hypothetical. You invest $20,000 of your own money and borrow $10,000, buying $30,000 of investments. If they fall 20%, they are worth $24,000 ($30,000 x 0.80). You still owe $10,000, so your equity is $14,000 ($24,000 - $10,000), a 30% loss on your own money. Without borrowing, the same fall costs 20% ($20,000 to $16,000).

The same $20,000 with and without a $10,000 margin loan (hypothetical, one year at 6.00%)
Market moveWithout marginWith margin, before interestWith margin, after $600 of interest
Fall of 20%$16,000 (down 20%)$14,000 (down 30%)$13,400 (down 33%)
Rise of 20%$24,000 (up 20%)$26,000 (up 30%)$25,400 (up 27%)

Interest works against you both ways. At 6.00%, the $10,000 loan costs $600.00 a year. In the falling case your loss grows from 30% to 33% ($13,400 is $6,600 below $20,000). In the rising case your gain shrinks from 30% to 27%. If the investments simply stay flat, you lose the $600.

Margin calls and forced sales. If your equity falls below the broker's requirement, the broker can ask for more cash or securities, and it can sell positions to cover the shortfall. A forced sale can happen at a low price and at a time you did not choose, and you remain responsible for any debt left after the sale. A sharp overnight drop can leave you owing more than the account is worth.

Rates can rise while you are borrowed. Prime-linked rates move when prime moves. A 0.25-point rise adds $25 a year for every $10,000 borrowed. Borrowing that works at today's 4.45% prime may not work at a higher one.

This page does not recommend borrowing to invest. It compares published rates and explains how margin works. Whether borrowing suits you depends on your income, your other debts and how much loss you can absorb, and nothing here is personal advice. 9. Is Margin Interest Tax-Deductible?

The general rule. In general terms, Canadian tax law allows a deduction for interest on money borrowed to earn income from a business or from property, such as interest or dividends, when the borrowed money is used in a non-registered account. What decides it is the use of the borrowed money, not the fact that it came from a margin account. This section describes the rule in general terms only; it is not tax advice for your situation.

What usually does not qualify. Interest on money borrowed to contribute to a TFSA or an RRSP is not deductible. Interest on money you withdraw from a margin account and spend on personal items does not qualify either, even though the loan is secured by investments. If part of a loan funds investments and part funds personal spending, only the investing part can qualify.

Keep the trail. A deduction depends on being able to show what the borrowed money bought. Keep your monthly statements showing the interest charged, and avoid mixing borrowed money used for investing with money used for anything else. A separate margin account used only for investing makes the record much simpler.

What a deduction is worth. If the interest qualifies, the deduction lowers its after-tax cost by your marginal tax rate. A deduction does not turn a losing investment into a winning one: you still pay the interest, and the investments still have to earn more than its after-tax cost.

Where to go next. The details, including how capital gains and losses on margin-funded investments are reported, depend on your full return. Please speak with a tax professional before relying on a deduction. 11. Safety: CIRO, CIPF and Margin Accounts

Who regulates margin lending. The Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) oversees all investment dealers in Canada and trading on Canada's debt and equity marketplaces. Every member of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) is a CIRO member. All 13 brokers on this page are CIPF members or, for National Bank Direct Brokerage and TD Direct Investing, whose own pages do not name the legal entity, operate under a bank-owned dealer on CIPF's member list (National Bank Financial Inc. and TD Waterhouse Canada Inc.).

What CIPF covers. If a member firm becomes insolvent, CIPF covers missing cash and securities held for you, within those limits. A margin account shares the $1,000,000 general limit with your cash, TFSA and FHSA accounts at the same firm.

What it does not cover. CIPF excludes market losses, unsuitable advice, an issuer's insolvency and crypto assets. A loss from a margin call or a forced sale is a market loss, so CIPF does not cover it, and the loan you owe the broker remains a debt.

13. Common Margin Mistakes

Choosing a broker on commissions alone. A $0-commission broker with an 8.70% margin rate costs $870.00 a year on $10,000 borrowed. A broker charging a small commission at 3.675% costs $367.50. If you borrow, compare the rate first.

Taking a "from" rate at face value. moomoo lists margin "from 3.85%" without publishing who qualifies. Ask what rate your account will pay before you rely on it.

Leaving a debit in a registered account. National Bank charges 21.00% on registered and cash-account debits, and Questrade 12.55% in CAD. A forgotten fee or an unfunded US purchase can sit there for weeks.

Using all your buying power. The more of your buying power you use, the smaller the drop that triggers a margin call. Borrowing well below the maximum leaves room for prices to move.

Expecting a deduction that does not apply. Interest on money borrowed for a TFSA or RRSP contribution, or for personal spending, is not deductible. Keep borrowed money for investing separate from everything else.

Forgetting that prime moves. A borrowing plan built on a 4.45% prime should still work if prime rises. Recheck your costs after each Bank of Canada decision.

10 Ways to Pay Less Margin Interest

These are cost-control habits, not investment advice. None of them suggests borrowing more or buying any security.

1. Compare the spread over prime

Most rates are prime plus a spread, so the spread is what really differs between brokers. Prime + 1.25% at Desjardins and prime + 4.25% at Questrade will stay 3 points apart whatever prime does.

2. Use the tiers you already qualify for

Wealthsimple's rate falls 0.5 points at $100,000 and again at $500,000 in total assets, and a household can combine its assets. Qtrade's Investor Plus status cuts its spread by 0.30 points under $100,000.

3. Move a long-term balance to a lower rate

On $25,000 carried all year, 8.70% costs $2,175.00 and 3.675% costs $918.75. A $150 transfer fee is small next to a $1,256.25 yearly difference.

4. Pay the interest each month

Unpaid interest adds to the loan and earns interest itself. Paying it from cash or dividends each month keeps the balance, and next month's charge, from growing.

5. Never leave a registered account negative

Debit rates in registered accounts reach 21.00% at National Bank and 12.55% at Questrade. Keep a small cash buffer for fees and fund trades before they settle.

6. Fund the USD side before buying US shares

Where an account holds US dollars, a USD purchase with no USD cash can create a USD debit at a high rate. Convert first, or use a broker that converts automatically at a known fee.

7. Pay down the loan with new money

Directing new deposits, dividends or sale proceeds to the loan cuts the balance the rate applies to. Each $1,000 repaid at 8.70% saves $87.00 a year.

8. Borrow well below your buying power

A smaller loan means a larger fall is needed before a margin call. Avoiding a forced sale at a bad price is often worth more than the interest itself.

9. Keep investment borrowing separate

Using one margin account only for investing makes it easier to show what the borrowed money bought, which matters if you plan to claim the interest. Do not withdraw borrowed money for personal spending.

10. Recheck after each rate decision

The Bank of Canada announces on October 28 and December 9, 2026. When prime changes, every prime-linked rate on this page changes, so recheck the tables and your plan.

Can I use margin in a TFSA or RRSP?

Not in the usual sense. A TFSA or RRSP is not a margin account, so it does not give you a borrowing limit. Registered accounts can still go negative, for example when a fee is charged with no cash, and brokers charge a debit rate on that: 7.00% at CIBC, 9.45% at Scotia, 12.55% at Questrade and 21.00% at National Bank. Wealthsimple lets you link a TFSA to a margin account for buying power.

What is a margin call?

A margin call happens when your equity, the account value minus the loan, falls below the broker's requirement. You must add cash or securities, or the broker can sell positions to cover the shortfall. A forced sale can happen at a low price and at a time you did not choose, and you still owe any debt left afterward.

What happens to my margin rate if the Bank of Canada changes rates?

If your broker prices margin as prime plus a spread, your rate moves when prime moves. Each 0.25-point change adds or saves $25 a year for every $10,000 borrowed. Wealthsimple, Desjardins, Qtrade, Scotia, Webull and Questrade all use prime-based formulas. Interactive Brokers follows its own benchmark.

Are USD margin rates higher than CAD rates?

Usually, because USD prime is 7.00% against a CAD prime of 4.45%. Interactive Brokers charges 5.380% on USD up to USD 100,000, Wealthsimple 7.5% on Core, National Bank and CIBC 8.75%, and Questrade USD prime + 4.50% (11.50%). Borrowing in US dollars avoids a conversion but adds exchange-rate risk.

Is a margin account protected by CIPF?

Yes, as property held at a member firm. A margin account falls in CIPF's general category, which covers up to $1,000,000 combined with your cash, TFSA and FHSA accounts at the same firm if it becomes insolvent. CIPF does not cover market losses, so losses from a margin call or forced sale are not protected.

How can I get a lower margin rate?

Reach a tier or move brokers. Most brokers cut the spread at $100,000, Desjardins charges prime at $500,000, CIBC charges 4.75% from $1,000,000, and Wealthsimple charges 3.95% once you hold $500,000, counting household assets. Moving a long-term balance from 8.70% to 3.675% saves $502.50 a year per $10,000.
