2. No Fee Bank Account Options

Eight accounts charge $0 a month. EQ Bank, Wealthsimple, Manulife Bank, KOHO Essential, Simplii, Tangerine, PC Money and Neo. A no fee bank account removes the monthly charge, but each has its own conditions or remaining charges.

No strings. EQ Bank, Wealthsimple, Simplii, Tangerine and PC Money charge no monthly fee at any balance. Of these, EQ Bank and Wealthsimple publish interest rates; Simplii, Tangerine and PC Money did not show a chequing rate when we checked.

With conditions. Manulife charges $1.00 per debit purchase, bill payment or e-Transfer sent, and $1.50 per ATM withdrawal in Canada, whenever your balance is below $1,000. KOHO Essential costs $7 a month unless you set up direct deposit or deposit $1,000 a month.

Changing in 2027. From January 1, 2027, Neo charges clients without a membership $1 per e-Transfer sent, $1.50 per cash withdrawal and $4.99 for non-sufficient funds. Neo's Essentials membership has no fee and removes all three.

Charges to watch. Tangerine and PC Money charge $1.50 at ATMs outside their networks in Canada. PC Money charges $3.50 to cancel a sent e-Transfer and $10 for non-sufficient funds. Simplii's optional overdraft costs $4.97 in a month you use it, plus 19% interest.

$0 at a big bank, for some people. National Bank The Minimalist is $0 for ages 0 to 24, Scotiabank's Basic Bank Account is $0 for seniors and several eligible groups, and TD waives its Minimum Chequing Account for certain groups, including newcomers in their first year. 6. Deposit Insurance: CDIC, Provincial Plans and Trust Coverage

CDIC. The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation insures eligible deposits at member institutions up to $100,000, principal and interest combined, per insured category at each member. The categories are separate: deposits in one name, joint deposits, deposits in trust, and deposits inside an RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RESP, RDSP or FHSA. CDIC covers deposits in Canadian or foreign currency, GICs and term deposits. It does not cover stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds or crypto.

Provincial plans for credit unions. Provincially regulated credit unions are covered by provincial plans instead. Saven Financial states its deposits are insured up to $250,000 by DICO, Ontario's credit union insurer. Outlook Financial states that 100% of deposits are guaranteed by the Deposit Guarantee Corporation of Manitoba, which also covers Hubert Financial.

Trust coverage at fintechs. Wealthsimple places chequing cash in trust at partner CDIC members, for up to $1,000,000 combined ($100,000 per beneficiary per member institution), and does not name the partners. KOHO makes up to $100,000 eligible only after you opt in to earn interest. Neo says its chequing funds are eligible up to $100,000 through a member it does not name.

CIPF is different. Wealthsimple's savings account is protected by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund up to $1,000,000, not by CDIC. CIPF covers cash and securities held at a failed investment dealer, not market losses.

Enter your balances by institution and category to see how much CDIC would cover.

Trade names share a limit. Simplii is a trade name of CIBC, so Simplii and CIBC deposits in the same name and category share one $100,000 limit. For a fintech's structure in detail, read is Wealthsimple safe.

8. How to Switch Banks in Canada

Open first, close last. Open the new account and test it with a small transfer and an e-Transfer before you move anything important. Keep the old account open until every payment has moved.

Move your pay. Give your employer the new account's institution, transit and account numbers, usually on a direct deposit form from the new bank. Expect one more pay to land in the old account. Update any government benefit deposits too.

Move automatic payments one by one. List every pre-authorized debit from your last two statements, such as rent, insurance, phone and loan payments. Update each with the payee and confirm the first payment comes from the new account.

Watch the old fee rules. If your old account has a balance waiver, moving money out mid-month can trigger the full monthly fee. Move the balance right after a statement date. A payment that bounces after you empty the account can bring a non-sufficient-funds charge of $10 at TD, Scotiabank or PC Money.

Downgrade rather than close. If you need cheques or a branch now and then, move the old account to its lowest-fee plan ($3.95 or $4.00 a month at the big banks) instead of closing it.

Investment accounts move by transfer. Registered accounts such as a TFSA or RRSP move between institutions by a direct transfer, not a withdrawal. Please read how to transfer an investment account before you move registered money.

How to Choose the Best Bank Account in Canada

Use these steps to narrow 20 chequing accounts and the savings options above to the one or two that fit.

  1. Decide what the account is for. Spending money belongs in chequing; money you will not touch for months belongs in savings or a GIC.
  2. Check your lowest daily balance. Below $4,000, a big-bank unlimited package will charge you $15.95 to $17.95 in those months.
  3. Use your pay. Direct deposit lifts EQ Bank to 2.75%, adds 0.50% at Wealthsimple Core and Premium, and keeps KOHO Essential at $0.
  4. Count cash and transactions. Heavy ATM users should look for reimbursement; anyone over 12 or 18 transactions a month should avoid capped accounts.
  5. Confirm the protection. Choose between a CDIC member, a provincial credit union plan, or a fintech's trust structure.
  6. Compare the yearly total. Run your numbers in the chequing cost calculator and check any welcome offer's end date.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best bank account in Canada?

For a chequing account, the EQ Bank Personal Account has our highest score at 4.6 / 5. It has no monthly fee, unlimited transactions and e-Transfers, ATM fees reimbursed across Canada, and 2.75% interest with pay direct deposit. The best account for you also depends on whether you need branches, cheques or a 0% foreign exchange card.

Which Canadian banks have no-fee chequing accounts?

EQ Bank, Wealthsimple, Manulife Bank, Simplii, Tangerine, PC Money and Neo charge no monthly chequing fee, and KOHO Essential is $0 with direct deposit or $1,000 a month in deposits. The six big banks charge $3.95 or more, except for certain groups such as ages 0 to 24 at National Bank or seniors at Scotiabank.

Are online banks in Canada safe?

EQ Bank, Manulife Bank, Tangerine Bank and President's Choice Bank are CDIC members, so eligible deposits are insured up to $100,000 per category, and Simplii is covered through CIBC. Fintechs such as Wealthsimple, KOHO and Neo are not members. Wealthsimple and KOHO place funds in trust at member institutions, and Neo says funds are eligible through a member it does not name.

Do big banks pay interest on chequing?

Not on the accounts we rank. TD posts 0.000%, Scotiabank states no interest is paid on its two accounts, and RBC states the same for Signature No Limit. BMO, CIBC, National Bank and RBC (for Day to Day Banking) did not publish a chequing rate when we checked on October 22, 2026. Online banks and fintechs pay up to 2.75% without a paid plan.

How much does a big-bank chequing account cost?

Capped accounts cost $3.95 or $4.00 a month, or $47.40 to $48.00 a year. Unlimited packages cost $15.95 to $17.95 a month, or $191.40 to $215.40 a year, unless you keep $4,000 or $4,500 in the account. RBC Signature No Limit has no balance waiver.

What is the highest savings rate in Canada right now?

Among everyday rates we verified on October 22, 2026, KOHO's Everything plan pays 3.5% but costs $14.75 to $22 a month. Without a plan fee, Saven pays 2.85% (insured by DICO) and Oaken pays 2.80% (CDIC). Promotional rates go higher for a few months, such as 4.60% at Simplii and RBC for new clients who open by October 31 and October 27, 2026.

How much does CDIC cover?

CDIC covers eligible deposits up to $100,000, including principal and interest, per insured category at each member institution. Deposits in one name, joint deposits, trust deposits, and deposits in an RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RESP, RDSP or FHSA are separate categories. Stocks, ETFs, mutual funds and crypto are not covered.

How long does it take to switch banks?

Opening an online account can take minutes, but moving your pay and pre-authorized payments usually takes one or two pay cycles, because each employer and payee must update their records. Keep the old account funded until every payment has come from the new account.

Should I keep money in chequing or savings?

Keep about a month of spending in chequing and the rest in savings or a GIC. Even the best chequing rate (2.75% at EQ Bank with direct deposit) is beaten by Saven's 2.85% and Oaken's 2.80% savings rates and by every 1-year GIC in our table, which pay 3.35% to 3.85%.
