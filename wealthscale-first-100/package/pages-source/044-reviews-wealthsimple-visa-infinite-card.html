1. Wealthsimple Credit Card Review: Our Verdict

The short version. The Wealthsimple Visa Infinite+ is a simple card: 2% back on everything, no categories to track, no foreign transaction fee and a monthly fee that disappears for many Wealthsimple clients. Its value depends almost entirely on whether you pay the $20 monthly fee.

If your fee is waived. With $100,000 or more in individual assets at Wealthsimple, or $4,000 or more a month in direct deposits, you pay $0 and keep the full 2%. On $24,000 of yearly spending, that is $480 in cash back ($24,000 x 2%) with no fee to subtract.

If you pay the fee. At $240 a year, the first $12,000 of spending only covers the fee ($240 ÷ 2% = $12,000). On the same $24,000, you keep $240 ($480 - $240). Below $1,000 a month in spending, the card costs more than it returns.

Why no score. WealthScale scores brokers, robo-advisors, crypto platforms, tax software and chequing accounts with published rubrics. We do not yet score credit cards, so this review gives a verdict without a number rather than invent one. For the same reason, we do not compare the card with other banks' cards on this page: we compare only the two Wealthsimple cards, whose terms we checked on October 22, 2026. Our rating methodology page lists what we score today.

The main limits. The cards are available in limited quantities, Wealthsimple matches you to one of the two, and you must pay the balance from a Wealthsimple chequing account. Insurance amounts for Visa Infinite+ travel coverage are not stated, so travellers who rely on card insurance should read the certificate before a trip.

2. Wealthsimple Cash Back Card: 2% on Everything

One flat rate. Both Wealthsimple cards pay 2% cash back on all net purchases. There are no bonus categories to track. Groceries, gas, travel and online shopping all earn the same 2%.

What does not earn cash back. Cash-like transactions, refunds and fees are excluded. Cash back is paid on net purchases, so a refund reduces the purchases that earn it. Cash advances are cash-like, so they do not earn cash back, and they carry interest at 22.99%.

Waiver one: $100,000 in individual assets. The card page words the asset test as $100,000 or more in individual assets. Wealthsimple's membership tiers can also be reached through combined household assets. If you reach Premium only by pooling with a household member, please confirm in the app that your card fee is waived, because the card terms refer to your individual assets.

Waiver two: $4,000 a month in direct deposits. Since July 10, 2025, the fee is waived when $4,000 or more a month arrives in your Wealthsimple account by direct deposit. What the waiver is worth. A waived fee saves $240 a year. For someone spending $1,500 a month, that turns $120 of net cash back into $360. For many Core clients whose pay is under $4,000 a month and whose assets are under $100,000, the fee will apply.

Watch for changes in status. Tier status falls only if both net deposits and total assets fall and stay below the threshold. If you change jobs and your direct deposits stop, or you withdraw a large amount, check whether the card fee returns. Our Core vs Premium vs Generation guide explains how status is measured.

4. Break-Even Math: When the Wealthsimple Credit Card Fee Pays for Itself

The formula. Break-even spending is the yearly fee divided by the cash back rate. At $240 a year and 2%, that is $12,000 a year ($240 ÷ 0.02), or $1,000 a month ($12,000 ÷ 12). Every dollar of spending above that line adds 2 cents of net cash back.

Below the line. At $500 a month ($6,000 a year), you earn $120 in cash back and pay $240 in fees, a net loss of $120. At $750 a month, the loss is $60. A card with a fee can still be worth holding below break-even for its insurance or travel perks, but on cash back alone it is a cost.

Above the line. At $2,000 a month, you net $240 after the fee. At $3,000 a month, you net $480. If the fee is waived, you keep the full 2% from the first dollar.

Enter your own monthly spending in the cash back calculator below. It shows your yearly cash back, the fee and the monthly spending needed to cover it, using the Wealthsimple card's 2% rate and $20 monthly fee by default.

Include foreign spending. Because the card charges a 0% foreign transaction fee, purchases in other currencies count toward break-even at the same 2% rate. For people who travel or shop online in US dollars, that makes the card earn more than its domestic numbers suggest.

Do not spend to reach break-even. Spending an extra $1,000 to earn $20 of cash back costs $980. The break-even line tells you whether the card fits your normal spending, not a target to hit.

5. 0% Foreign Transaction Fee

No conversion fee from Wealthsimple. The Wealthsimple credit card charges a 0% foreign transaction fee. When you pay in US dollars, euros or any other currency, Wealthsimple adds no percentage fee for the conversion, and the purchase earns 2% cash back like any other.

What it is worth on a trip. On $3,000 of spending abroad, you earn $60 in cash back ($3,000 x 2%) and pay no Wealthsimple foreign transaction fee. The exchange rate itself still applies, as it does on any card.

10. Interest Rates and Paying Your Balance

The rates. Wealthsimple charges 20.99% a year on purchases and 22.99% on cash advances. After two missed payments, penalty rates of 25.99% and 27.99% apply.

Balance typeAnnual rate

Penalty rates after 2 missed payments25.99% and 27.99%

Why carrying a balance erases the cash back. The purchase rate of 20.99% is about 10.5 times the 2% cash back rate (20.99 ÷ 2 = 10.495). Carrying $2,000 for one month costs about $34.98 in interest ($2,000 x 20.99% ÷ 12). That wipes out the cash back on about $1,749 of spending ($34.98 ÷ 0.02 = $1,749). The card only makes sense if you pay the full balance every month.

How you pay. You can pay the card only from a Wealthsimple chequing account. Autopay is available. Set autopay for the full statement balance, and keep enough in chequing before each due date. If your pay goes to another bank, you will need to move money into Wealthsimple chequing first.

Missed payments. Two missed payments trigger the penalty rates. A missed payment can also be reported to the credit bureaus and lower your credit score, which affects future borrowing, including a mortgage. Autopay from a funded chequing account is the simplest protection.

Cash advances. Cash advances do not earn cash back and carry 22.99% interest. If you need cash, use your Wealthsimple chequing card at an ATM instead: Wealthsimple charges no ATM fee and reimburses eligible ATM operator fees.

13. Who the Wealthsimple Credit Card Suits and Who Should Look Elsewhere

Clients with $100,000 or more at Wealthsimple. The fee is waived, so the full 2% is yours from the first dollar. This is the group that gets the most from the card.

People paid $4,000 or more a month by direct deposit. Moving your pay to Wealthsimple chequing waives the card fee and also lifts Core chequing interest from 1.25% to 1.75% through the direct-deposit boost.

Travellers and online shoppers who pay in other currencies. The 0% foreign transaction fee plus 2% cash back makes every foreign purchase cheaper than on a card that charges a conversion fee.

People who want simplicity. One rate on everything means no categories to track.

Look elsewhere if you pay the fee and spend little. Below $1,000 a month, the $240 fee outweighs the cash back. The Wealthsimple Chequing prepaid card gives 0% FX with no fee, though without the 2% cash back or insurance. Our no-fee chequing accounts ranking lists other options.

Look elsewhere if you carry a balance. At 20.99%, interest costs far more than any cash back. Paying down the balance comes first. Our compound interest calculator shows how interest builds over time.

Look elsewhere if you need a card now. With limited quantities and a matched card, you may wait. If you need specific insurance for an upcoming trip, confirm the coverage on the card you are offered before you rely on it.

How to Apply for the Wealthsimple Credit Card

  1. Open Wealthsimple Chequing. An active chequing account is required, and it is the only account you can pay the card from.
  2. Check the income test. Confirm you meet $80,000 personal or $150,000 household income for the Visa Infinite+, or one of the Privilege routes.
  3. Apply in the app. Wealthsimple runs a full credit check and matches you to the Visa Infinite+ or the Visa Infinite Privilege. Cards are available in limited quantities.
  4. Set up the fee waiver. Move your pay by direct deposit if it is $4,000 or more a month, or check whether your individual assets reach $100,000.
  5. Turn on autopay. Set autopay for the full statement balance from chequing so you never pay 20.99% interest.
  6. Choose where cash back goes. Send it to chequing to earn your tier's rate, or to an investing account such as a Wealthsimple TFSA if you have room.
  7. Read your insurance certificate. Before your first trip, check the coverage limits and provincial conditions for the card you received.

10 Strategies to Earn More With the Wealthsimple Credit Card

1. Get the fee waived first

A waived fee is worth $240 a year, more than the cash back on $12,000 of spending. Check whether your individual assets at Wealthsimple reach $100,000 or your direct deposits reach $4,000 a month before you start spending on the card.

2. Route your pay to Wealthsimple

Direct deposits of $4,000 or more a month waive the card fee, and $2,000 or more in 30 days lifts Core chequing interest from 1.25% to 1.75%. One change to your payroll form can do both.

3. Put everyday spending on one card

With a flat 2% and no categories, every purchase earns the same. Moving recurring bills such as phone, internet and subscriptions to the card raises your total cash back with no extra effort.

4. Use it for foreign purchases

The 0% foreign transaction fee means purchases abroad and online in other currencies earn 2% with no Wealthsimple conversion fee. On $3,000 of travel spending, that is $60 in cash back.

5. Pay in full with autopay

At 20.99%, one month of interest on $2,000 is about $34.98, the cash back on about $1,749 of spending. Autopay for the full balance from chequing keeps every dollar of cash back.

6. Never take a cash advance

Cash advances earn no cash back and carry 22.99% interest. Use the Wealthsimple chequing card at an ATM instead, where Wealthsimple charges no ATM fee and reimburses eligible operator fees.

7. Send cash back where it works hardest

Cash back in chequing earns 1.25% to 2.25% by tier. If you have unused TFSA room, sending it to your TFSA lets future growth go untaxed. Check your TFSA contribution room before you redirect it.

8. Keep receipts for insurance

Purchase protection, phone insurance and extended warranty claims are easiest when the item was paid with the card and you have the receipt. Save digital copies in one folder.

9. Know which card you have

The Privilege card publishes travel medical up to $2,000,000 and trip cancellation up to $5,000 per trip; the Infinite+ does not state its amounts. Confirm your card's cover before you decline other insurance for a trip.

10. Recheck the fee after life changes

A new job, a large withdrawal or a move can end a waiver. If your direct deposits drop below $4,000 a month and your assets are under $100,000, the $20 monthly fee can return.

Wealthsimple Credit Card FAQ

How much is the Wealthsimple credit card fee?

The Wealthsimple credit card costs $20 a month, or $240 a year, as of October 22, 2026. Quebec residents are charged the fee annually. The fee is $0 for Premium and Generation clients, and it is waived for clients with $100,000 or more in individual assets at Wealthsimple or $4,000 or more a month in direct deposits.

How do I get the Wealthsimple credit card fee waived?

Hold $100,000 or more in individual assets at Wealthsimple, or receive $4,000 or more a month in direct deposits into your Wealthsimple account. The direct-deposit rule has applied since July 10, 2025. If you reach Premium only through combined household assets, confirm in the app that the waiver applies, because the card terms refer to individual assets.

What cash back does the Wealthsimple credit card pay?

Both Wealthsimple cards pay 2% cash back on all net purchases, with no categories. Cash-like transactions, refunds and fees do not earn cash back. You can send cash back to almost any Wealthsimple account; in chequing it earns 1.25% on Core, 1.75% on Premium and 2.25% on Generation. On $24,000 of yearly spending, 2% is $480.

Does the Wealthsimple credit card charge foreign transaction fees?

No. The Wealthsimple credit card charges a 0% foreign transaction fee, so purchases in other currencies carry no Wealthsimple conversion fee and still earn 2% cash back. The exchange rate itself still applies. On $3,000 of spending abroad, you would earn $60 in cash back with no foreign transaction fee from Wealthsimple.

What income do I need for the Wealthsimple Visa Infinite+?

You need $80,000 in personal income or $150,000 in household income. You must also be a Canadian resident who has reached the age of majority, hold an active Wealthsimple chequing account and pass a full credit check. Wealthsimple matches applicants to the Visa Infinite+ or the Visa Infinite Privilege, and both are available in limited quantities.

What is the difference between Visa Infinite+ and Visa Infinite Privilege?

Both pay 2% cash back with a 0% foreign transaction fee and the same $20 monthly fee. The Privilege card needs $150,000 personal or $200,000 household income, $50,000 in yearly spending or $400,000 in Wealthsimple assets. It adds travel medical up to $2,000,000, trip cancellation up to $5,000 per trip and access to 1,200+ airport lounges.

How much do I need to spend to break even on the Wealthsimple credit card?

If you pay the fee, you need $12,000 a year in spending, or $1,000 a month, because $240 divided by 2% is $12,000. Below that, the fee is larger than the cash back. Above it, each extra dollar adds 2 cents. If your fee is waived, there is no break-even point: you keep the full 2% from the first dollar.

What insurance comes with the Wealthsimple credit card?

The Visa Infinite+ includes 90-day purchase protection, phone insurance up to $1,000, an extended warranty that doubles the manufacturer's period, and travel medical, cancellation and baggage cover with amounts not stated. The Privilege card states travel medical up to $2,000,000, trip cancellation or interruption up to $5,000 per trip, rental car and flight delay cover, underwritten by Chubb.

Does the Wealthsimple credit card include lounge access?

The Visa Infinite Privilege gives access to 1,200+ airport lounges, though Wealthsimple does not name the network or state a visit limit on the Privilege card page. Lounge access is not stated for the Visa Infinite+. Lounge access is a card benefit, not a Premium or Generation membership perk, so reaching a higher tier does not add it.

What interest rate does the Wealthsimple credit card charge?

Purchases are charged 20.99% a year and cash advances 22.99%. After two missed payments, penalty rates of 25.99% and 27.99% apply. Carrying $2,000 for one month at 20.99% costs about $34.98, which wipes out the cash back on about $1,749 of spending, so pay the full balance every month.

How do I pay my Wealthsimple credit card bill?

You can pay only from a Wealthsimple chequing account, and autopay is available. Set autopay for the full statement balance and keep enough in chequing before each due date. If your pay goes to another bank, move money into Wealthsimple chequing first, or switch your direct deposit, which can also waive the card fee at $4,000 a month.

Who issues the Wealthsimple credit card?

The cards are issued by Wealthsimple Payments Inc., which is registered with FINTRAC as a money services business and is a payment service provider under the Bank of Canada's retail payments regime. It also issues the Visa Platinum Prepaid card for Wealthsimple Chequing. Wealthsimple Payments Inc. is not a CDIC member.
