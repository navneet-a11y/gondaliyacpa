3. How Interest Is Calculated and Paid

Daily interest, monthly payment. Savings accounts in Canada usually work out interest on each day's closing balance and pay it once a month. The daily formula is balance x annual rate / 365. At 2.85%, $10,000 earns $0.78 a day ($10,000 x 0.0285 / 365 = $0.7808), or $23.42 over a 30-day month ($0.7808 x 30). Wealthsimple states that its Chequing interest is calculated daily and paid monthly; for other accounts, the account agreement sets the method, so please read it before you compare two close rates.

Compounding adds a little. When interest is paid monthly, next month's interest is earned on a slightly larger balance. A 2.85% rate paid and compounded monthly works out to an effective yearly yield of 2.8875% ((1 + 0.0285 / 12)^12 minus 1). On $10,000 that is $288.75 instead of $285.00, a difference of $3.75. Compounding matters far less than the rate itself, so compare the posted rates first. Our compound interest calculator shows the effect over many years.

Single rates versus tiers. Saven, Oaken, Hubert, Outlook and Alterna publish a single rate with no balance tiers, Wealthsimple states "no tiers", and KOHO pays its plan rate on the entire balance once you opt in. Other accounts use tiers. Simplii pays 0.30% on balances up to $50,000 and 1.00% only above $1,000,000. Scotiabank's High Interest Savings Account sets the rate by your Total Relationship Balance: 0.00% under $10,000, 1.50% from $10,000 and 2.20% only from $500,000. TD's ePremium Savings pays 0.000% under $10,000 and 0.450% from $10,000.

Notice accounts. A notice account pays more in return for advance warning before a withdrawal. EQ Bank pays 2.75% on its 30 Day Notice Savings Account and 2.35% on its 10 Day Notice Savings Account, against 1.00% on its Personal Account without direct deposit. The trade-off is time: money for a surprise car repair should not sit behind a 30-day notice.

Rates are variable. Everyday savings rates are not fixed. The provider can change them at any time, and changes often follow Bank of Canada decisions (section 11). EQ Bank's current rates, for example, have been in effect since May 15, 2026. If you want a rate that cannot fall for a set term, compare the best GIC rates in Canada, where a 1-year GIC paid up to 3.85% on October 22, 2026.

Registered accounts pay differently. The same provider often pays a different rate inside a TFSA or RRSP. EQ Bank pays 1.50% on TFSA, RRSP and FHSA cash savings, below its 2.75% Personal Account rate with direct deposit, while Saven, Hubert and Outlook pay the same rate in registered and non-registered accounts. 4. Promotional Rates Explained

How promotions are built. Most promotions add a bonus to the regular rate for a set time. RBC's 4.60% is its 0.55% regular rate plus a 4.05% bonus for 90 days. Manulife's 3.00% is its 1.50% regular rate plus 1.50% for 730 days. Simplii advertises 4.60% "total" for 5 months (153 days). When the promotion ends, the bonus stops and only the regular rate continues.

Who qualifies. Read the eligibility line first. Simplii's offer is for new clients opening their first HISA from August 1 to October 31, 2026, within 60 days of joining. RBC's is for first-time primary owners opening by 3:00 p.m. Eastern time on October 27, 2026. Manulife's applies to new personal non-registered Canadian-dollar accounts opened from July 2 to October 30, 2026, and only to net new deposits. National Bank's 3% on its Cash Advantage Solution applies to new deposits from October 19, 2026 to February 28, 2027.

Caps. Simplii pays its promotional rate on balances up to $200,000, Manulife on up to $500,000 of net new deposits, RBC on up to $1,000,000 and National Bank on up to $999,999.99. Money above the cap earns only the regular rate.

The first-year yield. A short promotion looks large but pays for only part of the year. On $10,000 held for 12 months, RBC's offer earns $154.86, an effective 1.55% for the year. Simplii's earns $210.24, or 2.10%. Saven's everyday 2.85% earns $285.00 with no deadline and no switching. 5. Deposit Insurance: CDIC, Provincial Plans and CIPF

CDIC basics. The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation insures eligible deposits at member banks and trust companies up to $100,000 per insured category at each member institution, and that limit includes both principal and interest. The categories are deposits in one name, joint deposits, deposits held in trust, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, RESPs, RDSPs and FHSAs, and each is insured separately. CDIC covers savings and chequing deposits in Canadian or foreign currency, GICs and term deposits. It does not cover mutual funds, stocks, bonds, ETFs or crypto.

How the categories add up. A couple banking at one CDIC member could hold $100,000 in each person's own name, $100,000 in a joint account and $100,000 in each person's TFSA, for $500,000 of coverage at that one institution ($100,000 x 2 + $100,000 + $100,000 x 2). RRSP, RRIF and FHSA deposits each add another category for each spouse.

Coverage is per institution, not per brand. Simplii Financial is a trade name of CIBC, so Simplii and CIBC deposits in the same category share one $100,000 limit. EQ Bank is the trade name of Equitable Bank. Oaken deposits are held at Home Bank or Home Trust Company, which are two separate members. Tangerine Bank is a CDIC member in its own right.

Credit unions use provincial insurers. Provincially regulated credit unions are covered by provincial deposit insurance plans instead of CDIC. Saven Financial's page states that its deposits are insured up to $250,000 by DICO. Hubert Financial (Sunova Credit Union) deposits are guaranteed by the Deposit Guarantee Corporation of Manitoba. Outlook Financial (Assiniboine Credit Union) states that 100% of deposits are guaranteed by the same corporation, and Achieva Financial (Cambrian Credit Union) states that all deposits are guaranteed without limit.

Fintech accounts. KOHO is not a CDIC member. When you opt in to interest, KOHO holds funds in trust at CDIC member institutions it does not name, and up to $100,000 is eligible for CDIC protection. Neo is not a CDIC member either. Its chequing funds are eligible for CDIC insurance up to $100,000 through a member it does not name, and its savings page did not state an insurer when we checked.

Wealthsimple Savings is CIPF, not CDIC. Wealthsimple Savings is protected by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund up to $1,000,000. CIPF covers client property that goes missing if a member investment dealer fails; it does not cover market losses. Wealthsimple Chequing is different: its cash is held in trust at CDIC members for up to $1,000,000 of combined coverage. Our guide is Wealthsimple safe explains both.

6. Online Banks vs Big Banks: The Savings Rate Gap

The gap. RBC pays 0.550% on its High Interest eSavings account and National Bank pays 0.55% on its HISA. TD's ePremium pays nothing under $10,000 and 0.450% above it. Scotiabank's High Interest Savings Account pays 0.00% under a $10,000 relationship balance and 1.50% above it. CIBC's and BMO's everyday savings rates did not appear on their pages. Online banks and credit-union divisions pay several times more: Saven 2.85%, Oaken 2.80% and EQ Bank 2.75% with direct deposit.

What the gap costs. On $25,000 held for a year, 2.85% instead of RBC's 0.55% is $575.00 more interest ($25,000 x 2.30%). Against TD's 0.45% it is $600.00 ($25,000 x 2.40%), and against Simplii's 0.30% it is $637.50 ($25,000 x 2.55%). 7. Tax on Savings Interest in 2026

Interest is fully taxable. Interest from a savings account outside a registered plan is income in the year you receive it, taxed at your marginal rate in the same way as salary. There is no lower rate for interest, unlike Canadian dividends or capital gains. Your bank or credit union issues a T5 slip for interest it reports, and you include all interest on your return even if no slip arrives.

What you keep in Ontario at $80,000. At $80,000 of taxable income in Ontario in 2026, the combined marginal rate on extra interest is 29.65% (20.5% federal plus 9.15% Ontario). $285.00 of interest on $10,000 at 2.85% costs $84.50 in tax ($285.00 x 29.65%), leaving $200.50, an after-tax yield of 2.005% ($200.50 / $10,000). Inside a TFSA, the same $285.00 is not taxed at all.

8. TFSA, FHSA and RRSP Savings Accounts

A savings account can sit inside a registered plan. Money, GICs and deposits are qualified TFSA investments, and the same is generally true of RRSPs. Interest inside a TFSA is not taxed. Inside an RRSP, RRIF or FHSA, it is not taxed while it stays in the account. A high-interest TFSA can suit savings you might need within a few years, where you want the tax shelter without investment risk. Our TFSA guide covers the full rules.

9. How to Choose the Best High Interest Savings Account in Canada

Start with the everyday rate, not the promotion. The everyday rate is what you earn after the first few months, so it decides most of your interest over time. Sort by it first, then treat a promotion as a short extra on top.

Read the condition behind the rate. Ask what you must do to earn it: pay a plan fee (KOHO, Neo), direct deposit your pay (EQ Bank's 2.75%), hold a large relationship balance (Scotiabank's 2.20% needs $500,000) or give notice before withdrawing (EQ's notice accounts). If you will not meet the condition, use the fallback rate in your comparison.

Count the fees on the way out. A $5 debit fee on one withdrawal a month costs $60 a year, which is the full year's interest on $10,000 at 0.60%. Check how many withdrawals and Interac e-Transfers are included before you choose an account for money you will move often.

Check the insurer and the limit. CDIC for banks and trust companies, a provincial insurer for credit unions, CIPF for Wealthsimple Savings, and trust arrangements for KOHO. If you hold more than $100,000 in one category, the insurer and its limit can matter more than a small rate difference.

Check where you live. Saven's account is not offered in Quebec. Some credit-union divisions limit who can join, so confirm eligibility for your province before you plan around a rate.

Match the account to the job. An emergency fund needs fast access with no notice and no withdrawal fees. Money for a goal a year or more away can sit in a notice account or a GIC. Savings you will not touch for years may belong in an investment account; our guide on how to invest in Canada covers that step. 10. How to Open an Account and Move Money

What you will be asked for. Expect to give your legal name, date of birth, Canadian address and Social Insurance Number, because the institution reports your interest to the CRA. You will also verify your identity, usually with government photo ID, and link an existing chequing account for transfers in and out.

Credit-union divisions ask you to join. Saven requires a Saven membership, and other credit-union divisions work in a similar way: you become a member of the credit union as part of opening the account. Read the membership terms before you apply.

Funding the account. Most online accounts are funded by a transfer pulled from your linked chequing account or by an Interac e-Transfer you send. Check the fee on each side. Sending an e-Transfer out of RBC's High Interest eSavings costs $1.00, out of National Bank's HISA $1.25, and out of Manulife's Advantage Account $1.00 when the balance is under $1,000.

Time deposits around promotions. A promotion counts only the deposits that meet its terms. Simplii's offer requires you to open your first HISA within 60 days of joining. Manulife counts only net new deposits. Move the money inside the window and keep a record of the date and amount you moved.

Moving a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA. Open the new registered account first, then ask the new institution to request a direct transfer from the old one. Do not withdraw and redeposit TFSA money yourself if you have no spare room for the year (section 8).

Closing the old account. Once the money has arrived and any pre-authorized payments have moved, close the old account so you do not pay a monthly fee on an empty balance. Keep your last statement, since you will need the interest figures at tax time.

12. Common Mistakes With High-Interest Savings Accounts

Counting on a rate you do not qualify for. EQ Bank's 2.75% falls to 1.00% if your pay is not direct deposited. Simplii's 1.00% applies only above $1,000,000. Fix: compare accounts at the rate you will actually earn, based on your balance and your pay arrangements.

Paying a plan fee that costs more than the extra interest. Below about $27,231, KOHO's Everything plan billed yearly leaves you with less interest than a no-fee 2.85% account (section 2). Fix: work out the break-even balance before you subscribe for the rate.

Assuming every account is CDIC-insured. Wealthsimple Savings is CIPF-protected, credit-union savings use provincial insurers, and KOHO and Neo are not CDIC members. Fix: check the protection column in our table, and keep each balance within the limit that applies.

Forgetting that interest counts toward the CDIC limit. A $100,000 deposit plus a year of interest is over $100,000 in one category. Fix: keep a margin below the limit, or use another category or another member institution.

Relying on an undated rate. Achieva's 1.8% has no effective date on its page, and Saven's, Hubert's and Peoples Trust's pages show no "as of" date. Fix: confirm the current rate at sign-up and look at your first monthly statement.

Leaving interest off your tax return. All interest earned outside registered plans is taxable, whether or not a T5 slip arrives. Fix: download your yearly interest summary from each institution in February and add the totals to your return.

Missing a promotion's eligibility window. Opening the account inside the window is not always enough; Simplii's offer also needs the HISA opened within 60 days of joining. Fix: read the full eligibility wording before you open anything, and ask the provider to confirm in writing if the terms are unclear.

10 Strategies to Earn More on Your Savings

1. Give each dollar a job

Split savings by when you need them: an emergency fund in a no-notice savings account, money for the next year in a notice account or short GIC, and money for later in a GIC ladder or investments. Each job has a different best account, and one account rarely wins all three.

2. Fill the TFSA before a taxable account

At $80,000 of income in Ontario, 29.65% of taxable interest goes to tax. The same savings inside a TFSA keep every dollar of interest. If you have unused room, a TFSA savings account at 2.85% can beat a taxable account at a higher rate.

3. Use a notice account for money you will not touch

EQ Bank's 30 Day Notice Savings pays 2.75% without the direct-deposit condition, against 1.00% on its Personal Account without direct deposit. On $20,000 kept for a year, that is $550 instead of $200.

4. Keep a small buffer at your main bank

Leave enough in chequing for bills due in the next few days and keep the rest in high-interest savings. A transfer that takes a day or two will not cause an overdraft or a missed payment, and the bulk of your money still earns the higher rate.

5. Spread large balances across insurers

Above $100,000 in one CDIC category, split the money across member institutions. $250,000 spread over three CDIC members is $83,333.33 at each, within every limit even after a year of interest at 2.85%.

6. Automate a transfer on payday

Set a recurring transfer from chequing to savings for the day after your pay arrives. Money that moves before you see it in your spending account is easier to keep, and the savings goal calculator above shows the monthly amount a target needs.

7. Ladder money you will not need for a year

A 1-year GIC paid up to 3.85% on October 22, 2026, a full point above the best no-fee savings rate. Moving savings you will not need for a year into a ladder of GICs fixes part of your return. Our GIC rates page shows how.

8. Let each spouse save their own income

Interest is taxed in the hands of the person whose money earned it. When the lower-income spouse saves part of their own pay in their own account, the interest on that money is taxed at their lower rate, without running into attribution rules.

9. Batch your withdrawals

Accounts that charge $5 per debit (National Bank, RBC over the limit, CIBC eAdvantage, BMO Savings Builder after one) reward fewer, larger withdrawals. Move money to chequing once a month instead of four times and keep the fees off your interest.

10. Put a date on every rate

Write down the rate and the date you opened each account, and the end date of any promotion. Look again after each Bank of Canada decision. A rate that was best in October may not be best in December.

Illustrative Example: Moving $40,000 From a Big-Bank Account

This example is hypothetical. Priya lives in Ontario, has $80,000 of taxable income in 2026, unused TFSA room of at least $7,000, and $40,000 in a big-bank savings account at 0.55%. Her combined marginal rate on interest is 29.65%.

  1. Today. Her $40,000 earns $220.00 a year ($40,000 x 0.55%). Tax takes $65.23 ($220.00 x 29.65%), so she keeps $154.77 ($220.00 minus $65.23).
  2. TFSA first. She moves $7,000 into a TFSA savings account at 2.85%. It earns $199.50 a year ($7,000 x 2.85%), with no tax.
  3. The rest to a no-fee 2.85% account. The other $33,000 goes to a non-registered savings account at 2.85% with no monthly fee. It earns $940.50 ($33,000 x 2.85%). Tax takes $278.86 ($940.50 x 29.65%), so she keeps $661.64 ($940.50 minus $278.86).
  4. The total. After tax she keeps $861.14 a year ($199.50 + $661.64), which is $706.37 more than before ($861.14 minus $154.77).
  5. The insurance check. Each balance, even with a year of interest added, stays well inside the published limit of the insurer behind it. If one account were at a CDIC member, the TFSA and the regular account would also be separate $100,000 categories.
  6. What could change it. The 2.85% rate is variable. If it dropped by 0.25 points, Priya's yearly interest on the $40,000 would fall by $100 before tax ($40,000 x 0.25%), and she would still be far ahead of 0.55%.

Is my savings account covered by CDIC?

Savings at a CDIC member bank or trust company are covered up to $100,000 per insured category at each member, including interest. Separate categories include single-name, joint, TFSA, RRSP, RRIF, FHSA, RESP, RDSP and trust deposits. Credit union savings, such as Saven, Hubert and Outlook, are covered by provincial insurers instead, and Wealthsimple Savings is protected by CIPF.

Is Wealthsimple Savings insured by CDIC?

No. Wealthsimple Savings is protected by CIPF up to $1,000,000, not by CDIC. CIPF covers client property that goes missing if a member investment dealer fails, not market losses. Wealthsimple Chequing is different: its cash is held in trust at CDIC member institutions for up to $1,000,000 of combined coverage. Wealthsimple Savings paid 2.5% with data as of August 19, 2026.

Are KOHO and Neo savings balances insured?

Neither KOHO nor Neo is a CDIC member. KOHO holds funds in trust at CDIC member institutions it does not name once you opt in to interest, and up to $100,000 is eligible for CDIC protection. Neo states its chequing funds are eligible for CDIC insurance up to $100,000, but its savings page did not name an insurer when we checked on October 22, 2026.

How is interest from a savings account taxed?

Interest earned outside a registered plan is fully taxable as income in the year you receive it, at your marginal rate. At $80,000 of taxable income in Ontario in 2026, that rate is 29.65%, so $285 of interest costs $84.50 in tax. Interest earned inside a TFSA is not taxed, and interest inside an RRSP or FHSA is not taxed while it stays in the plan.

Can I hold a high-interest savings account inside a TFSA?

Yes. Money and deposits are qualified TFSA investments, and many providers offer a TFSA savings account. On October 22, 2026, Saven paid 2.85%, Hubert 2.50%, Outlook 1.95% and EQ Bank 1.50% on TFSA savings. The 2026 TFSA dollar limit is $7,000. Move TFSA money between institutions by direct transfer so you do not over-contribute.

How often do savings account rates change?

Everyday savings rates are variable, so a provider can change them at any time. Changes often follow Bank of Canada decisions. The policy rate has been 2.25% since October 30, 2025, and the last two decisions of 2026 are on October 28 and December 9. Promotional rates are fixed for their stated period, then revert to the everyday rate.

Is a high-interest savings account better than a GIC?

They do different jobs. A savings account lets you withdraw at any time, at a variable rate of up to 2.85% with no fee on October 22, 2026. A non-redeemable GIC locks your money for its term in return for a fixed rate, up to 3.85% for one year and 4.50% for five years on the same date. Many savers keep an emergency fund in savings and longer-term money in GICs.

How much interest will $10,000 earn in a savings account?

A notice savings account pays a higher rate in return for advance notice before each withdrawal.
