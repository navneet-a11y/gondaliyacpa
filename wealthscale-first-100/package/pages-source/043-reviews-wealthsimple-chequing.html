2. Wealthsimple Chequing Interest Rate by Tier

The rate depends on your tier. Wealthsimple sorts clients into three tiers by the assets they hold with it. Your chequing rate follows your tier, and Core and Premium clients can add 0.50% with direct deposit.

The direct-deposit rule. Core and Premium clients earn an extra 0.50% when they receive $2,000 or more in eligible direct deposits within a 30-day period. The boosted rates do not stack beyond 2.25%, and Generation clients are not eligible because they already earn 2.25%. On $5,000, the boost is worth $25.00 a year at Core ($87.50 against $62.50 at 1.25%).

How your tier is measured. Wealthsimple uses the higher of your net deposits or your total portfolio value. Net deposits count deposits and transfers into managed, trading, crypto and chequing accounts. Market falls do not reduce them, but withdrawals, transfers out and currency conversions that carry a foreign exchange fee do. You only drop a tier if both net deposits and total assets fall and stay below the threshold. Upgrades are automatic and take at least 1 business day; new deposits take up to 72 hours to count.

Households. Two or more Wealthsimple clients who share a residential address can form a household, and combined household assets count toward Premium or Generation. Members choose what to share and cannot move each other's money.

How interest is paid. Interest is calculated daily and paid monthly, and the rate can change. Wealthsimple cut its standard chequing rate from 1.50% to 1.25% on November 3, 2025. Interest outside a registered account is taxable income in the year you earn it. For a deeper look at each tier's other benefits, see Wealthsimple Core vs Premium vs Generation.

4. The Visa Platinum Prepaid Card

What you get. Each chequing account comes with one Visa Platinum Prepaid card, in digital and physical form, with no card fee and 0% foreign exchange on purchases. It draws on your chequing balance, so it works like a debit card for spending, but it runs on the Visa network.

Who issues it. The card is issued under licence by Wealthsimple Payments Inc. That company is registered with FINTRAC as a money services business and with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act. It is not a CDIC member; the CDIC coverage on your chequing cash comes through the trust arrangement described in section 9.

Where it does not work. The card does not work in Costco Canada warehouses, which accept Mastercard only. If you shop at Costco, you will need another payment method there.

Foreign spending. A 0% foreign exchange charge is uncommon among the chequing accounts we rank; KOHO, for example, charges a foreign transaction fee on its Essential plan. Combined with worldwide ATM operator fee reimbursement, this is the main reason Wealthsimple ranks as the best chequing account we score for travel.

Credit card versus prepaid card. Wealthsimple also offers credit cards (Visa Infinite+ and Visa Infinite Privilege) that pay 2% cash back, with a $20 monthly fee waived for clients with $100,000 or more in individual assets or $4,000 or more a month in direct deposits. Those cards are paid only from a Wealthsimple chequing account. See our Wealthsimple credit card review for the full terms.

5. ATM Fee Reimbursement and Cash Deposits

No network, fees reimbursed. Wealthsimple has no ATM network of its own. Instead, it charges no ATM fee itself and reimburses eligible ATM operator fees anywhere in the world, with no cap stated. Wealthsimple cites a typical operator fee of about $3.00.

What that is worth. If you take cash out twice a month at machines that charge $3.00 each, that is $72.00 a year (2 x $3.00 x 12) that Wealthsimple pays back. At an account that charges $1.50 of its own at out-of-network ATMs and does not reimburse the operator, the same habit would cost $36.00 in bank fees plus whatever the machine owner charges.

How it compares. EQ Bank reimburses ATM fees anywhere in Canada. Wealthsimple's reimbursement covers machines abroad too, which matters if you travel. Big banks give you their own network: TD Unlimited charges no TD fee at other ATMs in Canada, while TD Minimum charges $2.

Depositing cash. You can deposit cash into Wealthsimple Chequing at more than 5,300 Canada Post locations. Wealthsimple's current chequing page does not state a daily cash deposit limit, so please confirm the limit in the app before you rely on large cash deposits.

Getting cash out abroad. Because the card charges 0% foreign exchange and operator fees are reimbursed worldwide, a withdrawal in another currency avoids both a conversion charge on the card and the machine's fee, as long as the operator fee is an eligible one.

6. e-Transfers, Bill Pay, Cheques, Wires and Bank Drafts

Interac e-Transfers. Sending an e-Transfer costs nothing, and eligible clients can send up to $50,000 a day, the highest published limit among the accounts we rank. That covers rent, a car deposit or a large bill in one go.

Bill payments. Bill pay is available. Wealthsimple states no fee and no limit for it.

Cheques. You can deposit a cheque by taking a photo in the app; funds take about 6 business days to process, and instant partial access is described as coming soon. If your pay is direct deposited, Wealthsimple sends you a chequebook at no charge, so you can write cheques to a landlord or contractor.

Direct deposit and early pay. For direct deposit and pre-authorized payments, Wealthsimple's institution number is 703 and its transit number is 00001; your account number is in the app. With direct deposit, paycheques can arrive up to 1 day early.

Wires. Incoming wires carry no fee, and international SWIFT wires are available. An outgoing domestic wire costs $15, waived for Generation clients. Foreign-currency wires coming in are converted with a standard FX fee whose amount Wealthsimple does not state on the chequing page.

Bank drafts. You can order a bank draft in the app, which is useful for a home closing or a large purchase that needs certified funds. Wealthsimple announced same-day delivery of bank drafts in the second quarter of 2026.

What we could not confirm. International transfers through a partner service are not described on the current chequing page, so we do not list them as a feature.

7. Overdraft and Borrowing

The published terms. Wealthsimple Chequing offers overdraft with no fee, and interest from 3.95%. That is a low rate for an overdraft. For comparison, Simplii charges $4.97 in any month you use its overdraft service plus 19% interest, and PC Financial charges 19% on overdraft protection.

What it costs in practice. Going $200 into overdraft for 10 days at 3.95% costs $0.22 ($200 x 3.95% x 10 / 365). At Simplii, the same overdraft costs $6.01: $4.97 plus $1.04 in interest at 19%.

What is not published. Wealthsimple does not explain on its chequing or line-of-credit pages how the overdraft limit is set, how it links to other borrowing, or what decides whether you pay 3.95% or more. Please check your overdraft terms in the app before relying on them.

Portfolio line of credit. Separately, Wealthsimple offers a portfolio line of credit secured by your investments, at 4.95% (Core), 4.45% (Premium) or 3.95% (Generation). It lends up to 35% of eligible collateral, needs at least $1,000 in collateral, has no credit check and no minimum payment, and accepts up to 5 non-registered accounts and TFSAs as collateral (not FHSAs, RRSPs, RESPs, LIRAs or joint accounts). A limited-time offer gives a first loan of up to $3,000 at 0% for 3 months; end date not stated.

A caution. Borrowing against investments carries the risk that a market fall reduces your collateral. A line of credit is not a substitute for a cash cushion in chequing.

8. Cards, Joint, Kids and Business Accounts

Which card you hold. The card on Wealthsimple Chequing today is the Visa Platinum Prepaid, issued by Wealthsimple Payments Inc. A legacy Wealthsimple Prepaid Mastercard is issued by KOHO Financial Inc.; if you still hold one, check which card is linked to your account.

How many accounts. You can hold up to 8 individual chequing accounts and up to 8 joint chequing accounts. Separate accounts can work as envelopes for bills, travel or a home down payment, all at no monthly fee.

Joint accounts and CDIC. For CDIC purposes, funds in a joint Wealthsimple chequing account count under the primary holder. So when coverage is worked out, the joint balance is attributed to the primary holder, which matters if that person also keeps a large individual balance.

Kids and Teens accounts. Wealthsimple offers a Kids and Teens account paying up to 2.25% (rates as of September 15, 2026), with $0 fees, 0% foreign exchange and ATM fees reimbursed worldwide. A parent can open accounts for up to 10 children. e-Transfers, direct deposit and higher limits for these accounts are described as coming soon.

Business chequing. Wealthsimple also offers business chequing, where CDIC coverage is up to $100,000 per business rather than the $1,000,000 available on personal chequing.

9. Wealthsimple Chequing CDIC Coverage

How the coverage works. Wealthsimple is not a CDIC member and does not appear on CDIC's member list. Instead, it places chequing cash in trust at partner institutions that are CDIC members. CDIC insures trust deposits up to $100,000 per beneficiary at each member institution, so spreading your cash across several partners gives up to $1,000,000 in combined coverage. The coverage is automatic and costs nothing.

The partners are not named. Wealthsimple says that for contractual reasons it cannot disclose which banks it uses. That means you cannot check whether money you hold directly at one of those banks overlaps with your Wealthsimple trust deposit there. If you keep large balances at several banks, bear this in mind.

How the CDIC rule applies. CDIC insures eligible deposits up to $100,000, principal and interest together, per insured category at each member institution. Deposits in trust are their own category, separate from deposits in your own name and from deposits in an RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RESP, RDSP or FHSA. CDIC does not cover stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds or crypto.

Savings is different. Wealthsimple's non-registered savings account is not CDIC-insured; it is protected by the Canadian Investor Protection Fund up to $1,000,000. Registered savings accounts held at Wealthsimple Investments Inc. are covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per beneficiary per category and are not CIPF-protected. Wealthsimple Investments Inc. is a CIRO dealer member.

Account security. Wealthsimple says clients who use a passkey are reimbursed for all direct losses from unauthorized activity, and that it is SOC 1 and SOC 2 compliant.

10. Opening an Account and Moving Your Pay

No minimum to start. Chequing has no minimum balance, and the Core tier begins at $1 in assets. You open the account in the Wealthsimple app.

Set up direct deposit early. The 0.50% boost needs $2,000 or more in eligible direct deposits within a 30-day period, and a chequebook at no charge needs your payroll direct deposited. Give your employer institution number 703, transit number 00001 and the account number shown in the app. Your first pay may still land in your old account.

Move automatic payments. Bill pay and pre-authorized payments can come from chequing. Move them one at a time and keep your old account funded until each one has come out of the new account.

Tier changes take time. If moving money in lifts you to Premium ($100,000) or Generation ($500,000), the upgrade is automatic but takes at least 1 business day, and new deposits can take up to 72 hours to count.

Moving investments too. If you transfer investment accounts to Wealthsimple, it reimburses transfer-out fees on transfers of $25,000 or more, up to $150 per account, and the account must stay funded for 90 days. Its 1% transfer match, effective May 4, 2026 with no end date stated, pays 1% on the first $2,000,000 transferred (maximum $20,000) with a $25,000 minimum within 30 days of registering. The match is paid in 24 equal monthly payments into chequing. Registered accounts should move by direct transfer; see how to transfer an investment account.

11. How It Works With Other Wealthsimple Accounts

Chequing counts toward your tier. Chequing balances count toward Core, Premium and Generation along with TFSA, RRSP, RESP, LIRA, non-registered, crypto and savings accounts. If you hold a Wealthsimple TFSA, it adds to the same total. Your tier then sets your chequing rate, your managed investing fee and other perks.

The credit card needs chequing. The Visa Infinite+ card requires an active Wealthsimple chequing account, and both Wealthsimple credit cards can only be paid from one. Cash back of 2% can be redeemed to almost any Wealthsimple account, and in chequing it earns your tier's rate (1.25%, 1.75% or 2.25%).

Savings sits beside chequing. The non-registered savings account pays 2.5% to all clients with no fees, no minimum and no withdrawal limits. It has no account or transit number, so your pay cannot go there directly; it lands in chequing first. Savings is covered by CIPF, not CDIC.

Investing from chequing. You can move money from chequing to self-directed investing, where Canadian and US listed stocks and ETFs trade at $0 commission and US trades from a Canadian-dollar account carry a 1.5% foreign exchange fee. See our Wealthsimple Trade review and the full Wealthsimple review.

One app, one point of failure. Keeping chequing, savings, investing and a credit card in one app is convenient. It also means one login controls everything, so a passkey matters: Wealthsimple reimburses passkey users for all direct losses from unauthorized activity.

14. Who It Suits and Who Should Look Elsewhere

Good fit: travellers and cross-border spenders. 0% foreign exchange on the card and ATM operator fees reimbursed worldwide remove the two most common travel charges.

Good fit: Wealthsimple investors. If you already hold $100,000 or more at Wealthsimple, Premium gives you 1.75%, or 2.25% with direct deposit, and chequing sits in the same app as your TFSA, RRSP and credit card.

Good fit: people who use other banks' ATMs. With no network of its own, Wealthsimple reimburses the machine owner's fee instead.

Good fit: anyone who needs a fast, large e-Transfer. The $50,000 daily limit for eligible clients is the highest published among the accounts we rank.

Look elsewhere: you want the highest rate on your pay as a Core client. EQ Bank pays 2.75% with pay direct deposit against Wealthsimple Core's 1.75%.

Look elsewhere: you want a bank that is a CDIC member itself, or branch service. EQ Bank, Manulife Bank and Tangerine are CDIC members with no monthly fee; the big banks have branches.

Look elsewhere: you shop mostly at Costco. The prepaid Visa does not work in Costco Canada warehouses.

8 Strategies to Earn More and Pay Less

1. Send at least $2,000 by direct deposit

The 0.50% boost needs $2,000 or more in eligible direct deposits within 30 days. If you split your pay, make sure this account receives at least that amount each month.

2. Combine assets as a household

Clients at the same address can combine assets toward Premium ($100,000) or Generation ($500,000). Reaching Premium with direct deposit lifts chequing from 1.75% to 2.25%.

3. Keep spending money in chequing, savings in savings

Wealthsimple's savings account pays 2.5% to every client, more than Core chequing even with the boost. Keep a month of spending in chequing and the rest in savings.

4. Pay by card when abroad

The prepaid Visa charges 0% foreign exchange on purchases, so paying by card in another currency adds no conversion charge from Wealthsimple. Pair it with reimbursed ATM operator fees when you need local cash.

5. Use any ATM, but check eligibility

Operator fees are reimbursed when eligible. Use the app to confirm reimbursements arrive, especially after a trip.

6. Order bank drafts in the app

For a home closing or a car purchase, order a bank draft in the app instead of visiting a branch, and allow time for delivery before your deadline.

7. Avoid domestic wires below Generation

An outgoing domestic wire costs $15 unless you are a Generation client. For amounts within the $50,000 daily limit, an e-Transfer costs nothing.

8. Use separate accounts as envelopes

Up to 8 individual chequing accounts let you set money aside for bills, travel or taxes without paying for extra accounts.

Illustrative Example: One Balance, Three Tiers

This example is hypothetical. Alex lives in Ontario, earns $80,000 a year and keeps an average of $8,000 in Wealthsimple Chequing. Alex starts on the Core tier and takes out cash twice a month at machines that charge a $3.00 operator fee.

  1. Core, no direct deposit. $8,000 x 1.25% = $100.00 a year in interest.
  2. Core, with $2,000 or more direct deposited every 30 days. $8,000 x 1.75% = $140.00 a year, which is $40.00 more ($140.00 - $100.00).
  3. Premium with direct deposit (after Alex's household reaches $100,000 in Wealthsimple assets). $8,000 x 2.25% = $180.00 a year.
  4. ATM fees reimbursed. 2 withdrawals x $3.00 x 12 months = $72.00 a year paid back, if the operator fees are eligible.
  5. Tax on the interest. At Alex's 2026 marginal rate of 29.65% (federal 20.5% plus Ontario 9.15% at $80,000), tax on $140.00 is $41.51 ($140.00 x 29.65%), leaving $98.49.
  6. Comparison with EQ Bank. With pay direct deposited, EQ Bank would pay $8,000 x 2.75% = $220.00, which is $80.00 more than Core with direct deposit ($220.00 - $140.00). EQ Bank reimburses ATM fees in Canada; it does not publish reimbursement for ATMs abroad or a 0% FX card. Alex's choice depends on how often Alex spends outside Canada.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wealthsimple Chequing a good account?

For many people who bank by app, yes. It scores 4.4 / 5 on our rubric, second of the 20 chequing accounts we rank. It has no monthly fee, pays 1.25% to 2.25% by tier, reimburses ATM operator fees worldwide and charges 0% foreign exchange on its card. EQ Bank scores higher at 4.6 / 5.

What is the Wealthsimple chequing interest rate?

As of October 22, 2026, Core clients earn 1.25%, Premium clients 1.75% and Generation clients 2.25%. Core and Premium add 0.50% with $2,000 or more in direct deposits within 30 days, to a maximum of 2.25%. Interest is calculated daily and paid monthly.

How does the Wealthsimple direct deposit boost work?

Core and Premium clients earn an extra 0.50% on chequing when they receive $2,000 or more in eligible direct deposits within a 30-day period. Core rises from 1.25% to 1.75% and Premium from 1.75% to 2.25%. Generation clients already earn 2.25% and are not eligible for the boost.

Is Wealthsimple Chequing covered by CDIC?

Yes, through trust deposits. Wealthsimple is not a CDIC member, but it holds chequing cash in trust at partner member institutions, giving up to $1,000,000 in combined coverage at $100,000 per beneficiary per member institution. Coverage is automatic. Wealthsimple does not name the partner banks.

Does Wealthsimple Chequing have any fees?

There is no monthly fee, minimum balance fee, e-Transfer fee or Wealthsimple ATM fee, and the card charges 0% foreign exchange. An outgoing domestic wire costs $15 (waived for Generation clients), and overdraft carries interest from 3.95%, with no overdraft fee.

Does Wealthsimple reimburse ATM fees?

Yes. Wealthsimple charges no ATM fee of its own and reimburses eligible ATM operator fees worldwide, with no cap stated. It cites a typical operator fee of about $3.00. It has no ATM network of its own. Two $3.00 operator fees a month add up to $72.00 a year that Wealthsimple would pay back.

Can I write cheques with Wealthsimple Chequing?

Yes, once your pay is direct deposited. Wealthsimple sends a chequebook at no charge to clients with payroll direct deposit. You can also deposit cheques by photo in the app, with funds taking about 6 business days to process. Wealthsimple describes instant partial access to deposited cheques as coming soon.

What are Wealthsimple's direct deposit numbers?

Wealthsimple's institution number is 703 and its transit number is 00001. Your own account number is shown in the app. Give all three to your employer to set up direct deposit, which can also bring your pay up to 1 day early.

Which card comes with Wealthsimple Chequing?

Each chequing account comes with one Visa Platinum Prepaid card, digital and physical, issued by Wealthsimple Payments Inc. It has no card fee and charges 0% foreign exchange on purchases. A legacy Wealthsimple Prepaid Mastercard is issued by KOHO Financial Inc. The Visa card does not work in Costco Canada warehouses, which accept Mastercard only.

Is Wealthsimple Chequing better than EQ Bank?

It depends on how you bank. EQ Bank scores 4.6 / 5 and pays 2.75% with pay direct deposit, against 1.75% at Wealthsimple Core. Wealthsimple scores 4.4 / 5 and wins for travel, with 0% foreign exchange on the card and ATM fees reimbursed worldwide, plus a chequebook with direct deposit.

Can I deposit cash into Wealthsimple Chequing?

Yes, at more than 5,300 Canada Post locations. Wealthsimple's current chequing page does not state a daily cash deposit limit, so check the limit in the app before depositing a large amount. Because Wealthsimple has no branches, Canada Post is the way to add physical cash.

Does Wealthsimple Chequing offer overdraft?

Yes. Wealthsimple lists overdraft with no fee and interest from 3.95%. It does not publish how the overdraft limit is set. A $200 overdraft for 10 days at 3.95% would cost $0.22 in interest. The same overdraft at Simplii would cost $6.01, including its $4.97 monthly service charge.
