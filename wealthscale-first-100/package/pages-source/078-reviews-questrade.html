1. Questrade Review: Our Verdict

The short version. Questrade scores 4.0 / 5 and ranks second of the 13 online brokers we score, behind Interactive Brokers Canada at 4.4 / 5 and just ahead of Wealthsimple, which also rounds to 4.0 / 5 (3.954 against Questrade's 3.979). It earns that place on three things: $0 commissions on stocks and ETFs, every account type a household is likely to need, and US dollars held in registered accounts at no extra cost.

Where it earns its score. Cost carries 35% of the score, and Questrade's buy-and-hold profile costs $0.00 a year. Its active profile costs $90.00, all of it currency conversion. It scores the full 5.0 on account types because it offers all 10 we count, including RESP, LIRA, joint and corporate accounts that moomoo and Webull do not list. It also scores 4.0 of 5 on US investing, the highest US investing sub-score of the 13 brokers.

Where it loses points. Cash and margin is Questrade's weakest factor at 0.5 of 5. Its published cash interest formula gives 0% at today's prime rate, and its 8.70% margin rate under $100,000 is the highest of the 10 brokers that publish one. Safety scores 3.5 of 5 under our rubric: Questrade, Inc. is on the CIPF member list, but CIRO membership is not stated on the pages we checked.

The currency question. The 1.5% conversion fee is Questrade's main running cost for anyone buying US shares. It is the same rate Wealthsimple and Webull charge and lower than the roughly 1.6% at several bank brokers, but far above Interactive Brokers Canada, which charges 0.20 basis points with a US$2 minimum. Questrade softens the blow by letting you keep US dollars in your TFSA and RRSP, so you convert once and not on every trade.

Who should pick it. Questrade fits long-term investors, families who want a family RESP next to their TFSA and RRSP, people with a LIRA from a former employer, and anyone who wants a robo-advisor and a self-directed account at the same firm. Active traders who borrow, or investors who convert large sums often, will usually pay less elsewhere. Compare it with our full best online brokers in Canada ranking before you decide.

2. Questrade Fees and Commissions

Stocks and ETFs. Questrade charges $0 to buy or sell Canadian-listed stocks and ETFs online, with no ECN fees on Canadian securities. US stocks and ETFs are also $0 commission. ECN fees apply only to US orders you route directly to a venue: for example, US$0.003 to US$0.004 a share on orders that remove liquidity. Fills in the overnight US session cost US$0.003 a share, and US sales carry the SEC fee of 0.0000206 times the trade value, or US$0.21 on a US$10,000 sale.

Fees to hold an account. There is no annual account fee. The inactivity fee is $0 whether your combined equity is over or under $1,000. Questrade Plus is an optional subscription at $19.99 a month; the one benefit we could confirm is a CA$0.05 per contract cashback on US equity options, so it only pays for itself at 400 or more contracts a month ($19.99 ÷ $0.05 = 399.8).

Fees to leave. A full or partial transfer to another institution costs $150. That is the same as TD Direct Investing, RBC Direct Investing, Scotia iTRADE, National Bank Direct Brokerage and Qtrade, but Wealthsimple charges $0 to transfer out. If you might switch again within a few years, count the $150 as part of the cost of owning the account.

3. Questrade FX Fee and USD Accounts

The rate. The Questrade FX fee is 1.5% on conversions between Canadian and US dollars, as of October 22, 2026. Converting $1,000 costs $15.00, $10,000 costs $150.00 and $50,000 costs $750.00. In our ranking of conversion costs on $10,000, Questrade sits fourth of nine brokers that publish a rate, tied with Wealthsimple and Webull at $150, behind Interactive Brokers Canada at US$2 and TD Direct Investing at $126.

USD in registered accounts. Questrade accounts are dual-currency enabled at no extra cost, including registered accounts, and Questrade does not force a conversion when you buy US stocks and ETFs. Once your RRSP or TFSA holds US dollars, you can buy, sell and collect US dividends without paying 1.5% each time. This is why Questrade scores the full 2 points for USD in registered accounts, while Wealthsimple Core charges $10 a month for a USD account and Qtrade charges US$15 a quarter per registered account unless you qualify for Investor Plus.

Ways to cut the conversion cost. Convert once in a larger amount rather than many small amounts, then leave the US dollars in the account. Some investors use Norbert's Gambit, which converts through an interlisted ETF and pays a trade cost rather than a percentage. Please confirm how Questrade handles the journal step before you try it, because a delay leaves you exposed to price moves. Our currency conversion fee calculator shows the break-even.

US dividend withholding. Where you hold US shares matters as much as how you convert. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, US dividends paid into an RRSP, RRIF, LIRA or LIF are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA, FHSA, RESP or non-registered account, 15% is withheld with a Form W-8BEN on file, or 30% without one. On US$1,000 of dividends in a TFSA, US$150 is lost and cannot be recovered as a foreign tax credit. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA covers this in full.

5. Questwealth Portfolios: Questrade's Robo-Advisor

It comes in two families: the standard Questwealth Portfolios and Questwealth SRI Portfolios. A halal portfolio is not mentioned on its pages.

What it costs. The management fee is 0.25% a year on balances of $250 to $99,999 and 0.20% at $100,000 or more. On top of that you pay the ETFs' own fund costs: up to 0.12% for the standard portfolios and up to 0.24% for the SRI portfolios. There are no opening fees, trading commissions or EFT fees. When the portfolio needs US dollars for an ETF, Questwealth adds 100 pips (0.01) to the exchange rate.

6. Questrade Platforms, Research and Education

Trading platforms. Questrade Pro, its active trading platform, is the one platform named on the trading-platforms page we checked. Other app names, including a mobile app, were not named on the pages we checked, so our platform factor gives half a point for mobile and Questrade scores 4.5 of 5. We do not describe screens or features we could not confirm from Questrade's own pages.

Fractional shares. Questrade lets you buy fractions of eligible Canadian and US stocks and ETFs in real time with $0 commission, though the 1.5% conversion fee applies when you pay for US shares with Canadian dollars. The minimum order size is not stated. Fractional buying helps when one share of a fund costs more than you want to invest that month, and it lets each monthly contribution go fully to work.

Dividend reinvestment. Questrade's DRIP covers almost every stock or ETF, with no transaction fees. When fractional shares are switched on, dividends reinvest into fractions, so a small dividend is not left sitting as cash. That matters at Questrade, where uninvested cash earns effectively nothing.

Research and data. Market data packages, research reports and screeners were not part of the figures we checked, and our score does not weigh research tools. 8. Questrade Cash Interest

The formula. Questrade publishes interest on uninvested cash as a formula: CAD prime minus 8.50% and USD prime minus 10.00%. With its CAD prime of 4.45%, the CAD result is minus 4.05%. With its USD prime of 7.00%, the USD result is minus 3.00%. Questrade's page states that a negative result means 0% interest, so cash in a Questrade account earns effectively 0% as of October 22, 2026.

What that means in dollars. $10,000 left uninvested for a year earns $0.00 at Questrade. The prime rate would have to rise above 8.50% before Canadian-dollar cash earned anything at all. For comparison, Interactive Brokers Canada pays 1.675% on Canadian-dollar balances above CAD 13,000, with its full rates needing a net asset value above USD 100,000.

Why it matters in a TFSA. TFSA room is limited, and the 2026 dollar limit is $7,000. Cash that earns nothing inside a TFSA wastes tax-free growth. If you are waiting to invest, keep the money in a high-interest savings account outside the broker until you are ready.

Effect on the score. Our cash and margin factor gives up to 2.5 points for cash interest of 1% or more and 1 point for any positive rate. Questrade gets 0 of 2.5 for cash and 0.5 of 2.5 for its margin rate, which is why it scores 0.5 of 5 on this factor. The factor carries 10% of the overall score.

The practical fix. Turn on DRIP so dividends reinvest, set up regular purchases so contributions do not sit, and move larger sums you will not invest soon to a savings product. Our savings hub compares savings accounts and GICs.

9. Is Questrade Safe? Safety and Regulation

CIPF membership. Questrade, Inc. is on the Canadian Investor Protection Fund's current member list. CIPF protects client property, meaning cash and securities, that is missing when a member firm becomes insolvent. CIRO membership is not stated on the pages we checked. Every CIPF member firm is a CIRO member, so CIRO membership follows from the CIPF listing, but our rubric gives 4.0 only when a broker states both, which is why Questrade scores 3.5 of 5 on safety.

How much CIPF covers. Coverage is set by account group, not by account. All your general accounts at one member firm share one limit, your retirement accounts share a separate limit, and RESPs where you are the subscriber have a third.

What CIPF does not cover. CIPF does not cover a fall in the value of your investments, unsuitable advice, the failure of a company whose shares you own, or crypto assets. If an ETF you hold drops 20%, that is market risk, and no protection fund pays it back.

Ownership. Our safety factor adds a point when a broker is owned by a bank or a financial cooperative group. Questrade is not, so it does not get that point; neither do Wealthsimple, Interactive Brokers Canada, moomoo, Webull or Qtrade. 10. Opening a Questrade Account, Transfers and the GET1000 Offer

Opening. Like any Canadian broker, Questrade needs your photo ID and social insurance number, because investment income is reported to the CRA. The minimum amount needed to open or start investing was not stated on the pages we checked. There is no fee to keep an account open and no inactivity fee, so a small starting balance costs nothing to hold.

Transfers in. When you move an account from another institution, Questrade rebates the transfer-out fee charged by that institution, up to $150 per account, and applies it automatically to eligible transfers. Ask for a direct transfer: a TFSA moved directly between institutions does not affect your contribution room, but withdrawing and re-depositing it yourself counts as a new contribution and can lead to an over-contribution. Our guide on how to transfer an investment account walks through the forms.

Transfers out. Moving a full or partial account out of Questrade costs $150. Some receiving brokers rebate that fee; Wealthsimple, for example, reimburses the other institution's fee, up to $150 per account, on transfers of $25,000 or more.

The GET1000 offer. From October 13 to November 2, 2026, Questrade pays a cash reward on new net funding, one tier only. Deposits must settle by November 2, 2026 and transfers must arrive by December 14, 2026. If the funding is held, rewards are paid around June 11, 2027 and December 10, 2027. A referral reward of $50 per friend also applies.

11. Questrade Customer Support

What this review covers. Support hours and contact channels are not part of the figures we checked for this review, so we do not list them. We print only support details we can confirm in our research, and we will add them when we do.

Have these ready when you call. Before you contact Questrade about a transfer, currency or a tax slip, have your account number, the name of the institution you are moving from, the account type (TFSA, RRSP, FHSA and so on), and a recent statement from the old account. For a transfer rebate, keep the statement or confirmation that shows the fee the other institution charged.

Check your W-8BEN status. If you hold US shares, confirm that a Form W-8BEN is on file for your non-registered and TFSA accounts. Without it, US dividends can be withheld at 30% instead of the 15% treaty rate. For an RRSP or RRIF, ask how the account is documented so US dividends are paid without US withholding.

Keep a record. Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and what was agreed, and save any chat or email. If a transfer stalls or a rebate does not arrive, a dated record makes the follow-up much faster.

10 Strategies to Keep Questrade Costs Low

1. Convert once, then hold US dollars

Every conversion costs 1.5%. Converting $12,000 once costs $180, the same as twelve $1,000 conversions, but holding the US dollars afterwards means sales, purchases and dividends need no new conversion. Keep US dollars in the registered account rather than converting back and forth.

2. Hold US dividend payers in an RRSP

US dividends paid into an RRSP or RRIF are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA, 15% is withheld and cannot be recovered. Placing US dividend payers in an RRSP and Canadian holdings in a TFSA can save that 15%.

3. Avoid direct-routing US orders

ECN fees apply only to US orders you route to a specific venue, at rates such as US$0.003 a share. On a 500-share order that is $1.50 or more. Standard routing keeps US trades at $0 commission.

4. Let the transfer rebate cover your exit fee

When you move to Questrade, it rebates the other institution's fee up to $150 per account. Moving three accounts that each carry a $150 fee can save $450. Keep the fee statement until the rebate posts.

5. Time a large move for the offer

GET1000 pays $100, $300 or $1,000 at $20,000, $50,000 and $100,000 of net funding. If you were moving anyway, finishing the transfer by December 14, 2026 can add a reward, provided you keep the funding in place.

6. Do not let cash sit

Questrade's cash interest is effectively 0%. Turn on DRIP, invest contributions soon after they land, and keep savings you will not invest for months in a savings account that pays interest.

7. Borrow briefly or not at all

At 8.70%, a $10,000 margin loan costs $870 a year. If you must borrow, keep it short, and know that the rate drops to 7.70% at $100,000. Never let a registered account go into debit at 12.55%.

8. Skip Questrade Plus unless you trade heavy options volume

Questrade Plus costs $19.99 a month. Its CA$0.05 per contract cashback on US equity options only covers that at 400 contracts a month. Below that volume the subscription costs more than it returns: at 100 contracts a month the cashback is (100 x CA$0.05 = CA$5.00) against the $19.99 fee.

9. Close options before expiry

Exercise costs $24.95, and a $45 fee applies if Questrade has to close an option on expiry day. Managing positions before the last day avoids both charges.

10. Compare Questwealth with your own costs

Questwealth costs 0.37% a year all-in on $50,000, or $185. A self-directed account with $0 commissions costs less in fees but needs your time. Pick the route that matches how hands-on you want to be.

Questrade Review FAQ

Is Questrade a good broker?

Questrade scores 4.0 / 5 overall, rank 2 of the 13 online brokers we score as of October 22, 2026. It charges $0 commission on stocks and ETFs, offers all 10 account types we count and lets you hold US dollars in registered accounts at no extra cost. Its weak spots are a 1.5% currency fee, effectively 0% cash interest and an 8.70% margin rate.

What are Questrade's fees?

Questrade charges $0 commission on Canadian and US stocks and ETFs, $0 on US equity options and $0 for DRIP. Currency conversion costs 1.5%. There is no annual or inactivity fee. Transferring an account out costs $150, exercising an option costs $24.95, and ECN fees apply only to US orders you route directly. Figures are as of October 22, 2026.

What is the Questrade FX fee?

Questrade charges 1.5% to convert between Canadian and US dollars, which is $150 on $10,000. That matches Wealthsimple and Webull but is far above Interactive Brokers Canada's 0.20 basis points with a US$2 minimum. Because Questrade lets you hold US dollars in registered accounts at no extra cost, you can convert once and avoid paying again on each trade.

Can I hold US dollars in a Questrade TFSA or RRSP?

Yes. Questrade accounts, including registered accounts, can hold US dollars directly at no extra cost, and US stocks and ETFs are not force-converted. This lets you pay the 1.5% conversion once. Remember that US dividends in a TFSA still lose 15% to US withholding, while an RRSP or RRIF is exempt once documented.

Is Questrade safe?

Questrade, Inc. is on the Canadian Investor Protection Fund's current member list. CIPF covers missing client property if a member firm becomes insolvent, up to $1,000,000 for general accounts combined, a separate $1,000,000 for retirement accounts and another for RESPs. It does not cover market losses. CIRO membership is not stated on the pages we checked.

Does Questrade pay interest on cash?

Effectively no. Questrade's published formula is CAD prime minus 8.50% and USD prime minus 10.00%. With its CAD prime at 4.45% and USD prime at 7.00%, both results are negative, and Questrade states that a negative result means 0% interest. $10,000 of idle cash earns $0.00 in a year as of October 22, 2026.

What is Questrade's margin rate?

Questrade charges CAD prime plus 4.25% on non-registered Canadian-dollar margin under $100,000, which is 8.70% with its 4.45% prime, and prime plus 3.25%, or 7.70%, at $100,000 or more. US-dollar margin costs 11.50% and 10.50%. That is the highest CAD rate of the 10 brokers that publish one.

How much does it cost to transfer out of Questrade?

Questrade charges $150 for a full or partial transfer to another institution. Some receiving brokers rebate this fee: Wealthsimple reimburses the other firm's fee, up to $150 per account, on transfers of $25,000 or more. Going the other way, Questrade rebates up to $150 per account of the fee your old broker charges you.

How much does Questwealth Portfolios cost?

Questwealth charges 0.25% a year on balances of $250 to $99,999 and 0.20% from $100,000, plus fund costs of up to 0.12% for standard portfolios and up to 0.24% for SRI portfolios. On $50,000 in a standard portfolio that is $185.00 a year, or 0.37%. It scores 4.1 / 5, rank 2 of 4 robo-advisors.

What is the Questrade GET1000 offer?

GET1000 runs October 13 to November 2, 2026. It pays $100 at $20,000 or more of net funding, $300 at $50,000 or more and $1,000 at $100,000 or more, one tier only. Deposits must settle by November 2, 2026 and transfers arrive by December 14, 2026. Rewards are paid around June 11, 2027 and December 10, 2027 if the funding is held.

Does Questrade offer an FHSA and an RESP?

Yes. Questrade offers the FHSA, individual and family RESPs, the TFSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, RRIF, LIRA, LIF and locked-in RRSP, plus cash, margin, joint, corporate, trust and estate accounts. It ranks first of nine brokers on our RESP list and second of 11 on our FHSA list, by overall score.
