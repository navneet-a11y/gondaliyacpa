1. moomoo Canada Review: Our Verdict

The short version. moomoo Canada scores 3.3 / 5 and ranks eighth of the 13 online brokers we score. It is cheap to borrow at, solidly regulated and built around one app, but it charges per share where many rivals charge $0, does not publish what its currency conversion really costs, and offers only four of the 10 account types we count.

Where it earns points. moomoo's CAD margin rate starts from 3.85% (as of November 3, 2025), second-lowest of the 10 brokers that publish one, which earns the full 2.5 margin points. TFSA and RRSP accounts can hold both Canadian and US dollars with no fee stated, worth the full 2 points for US dollars in registered accounts. moomoo states it is regulated by CIRO and is a CIPF member, for 4.0 of 5 on safety.

Where it loses points. Account types score 2.0 of 5 because there is no FHSA, RESP, joint or corporate account. Platform scores 3.0 of 5 because moomoo does not offer fractional shares or DRIP. Cost scores 3.95 of 5, below the $0-commission brokers, because every trade pays at least CA$1.49 or US$1.99, and because our methodology counts an undisclosed spread at the 1.5% median published rate.

The spread problem. A 0% fee sounds like the cheapest conversion in Canada, but the Moomoo Preferred Rate includes a spread whose size moomoo does not publish. We cannot tell you what $10,000 costs to convert at moomoo, and neither can anyone else using its published pricing. That uncertainty is the main reason moomoo's cost and US investing scores are not higher.

Who should pick it. moomoo may suit an app-first investor who trades US shares in a TFSA or RRSP, wants to hold US dollars in those accounts, and borrows on margin at times. It does not suit anyone who needs an FHSA or RESP, or a monthly investor who wants to buy fractions of a share. Compare it with the full best online brokers in Canada ranking before you decide.

3. moomoo Currency Conversion and US Dollars

A 0% fee is not a 0% cost. moomoo charges a 0% currency exchange fee, waived until further notice. Conversions use the Moomoo Preferred Rate, which includes a spread: the gap between the rate you get and the market rate. moomoo does not publish how big that spread is, so the cost of converting is real but unknown. For scoring, our methodology counts an unpublished spread at 1.5%, the median published rate, which is why moomoo's active profile is priced at $149.64 in the score.

How to measure it yourself. Before you convert, note the rate moomoo quotes and the mid-market rate at the same moment. Divide the difference by the mid-market rate to get the spread as a percentage. Do this on a small amount first, and keep a note of the result with the date.

US dollars in a TFSA or RRSP. moomoo says TFSA and RRSP holders can hold both Canadian and US dollars, with no fee stated. Holding US dollars lets you buy, sell and collect US dividends without converting each time. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, US dividends paid into an RRSP are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA or a cash or margin account, 15% is withheld with a Form W-8BEN on file, or 30% without one, and in a TFSA it cannot be recovered. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA explains why.

5. moomoo Platforms and Tools

One app, two screens. moomoo runs on the moomoo app, which comes in mobile and desktop versions. Having both a desktop platform and a mobile app earns moomoo two of the five points on our platform factor, and options trading earns a third.

No fractional shares. moomoo's own comparison article says it does not support fractional shares. Every purchase must be a whole number of shares, so a $100 monthly contribution cannot buy part of a share that costs more than that. Fractional shares are worth 1 point on our platform factor and 1.5 points on our beginners factor, and moomoo scores 0 on both.

No DRIP. moomoo states that DRIP is not available, though an auto-invest feature is. Dividends land as cash, and because the standard interest rate on cash is not published, small dividends can sit idle until you reinvest them by hand. Each manual reinvestment pays at least CA$1.49 or US$1.99, which is a large share of a small dividend: reinvesting a CA$30 dividend in one order costs about 5% of it (CA$1.49 ÷ CA$30).

Auto-invest instead. Auto-invest lets you schedule regular purchases. Remember that each scheduled order pays the per-share commission and platform fee, with the minimum. Twelve monthly orders cost at least (12 x CA$1.49 = CA$17.88) a year.

Where moomoo sits. moomoo ranks 8 of 10 on our best investing apps list, which uses the beginners weighting and only includes brokers whose mobile app is named on the pages we checked. The two missing features, fractional shares and DRIP, explain most of the gap to the apps above it.

8. moomoo Cash Interest

The Cash Sweep program. moomoo runs a Cash Sweep program for uninvested cash. Its welcome promotion cites 6% APY for 30 days after activation. The standard rate that applies after that period was not published on the pages we checked.

What the promotion is worth. A promotional rate for 30 days is worth far less than the headline suggests. On $5,000, 6% a year for 30 days is roughly ($5,000 x 6% x 30 ÷ 365 = $24.66) before compounding. After that, the account earns the unpublished standard rate.

How we score it. Our cash and margin factor gives 2.5 points for headline cash interest of 1% or more and 1 point for any positive rate. moomoo's standard rate is not published and we do not count a 30-day promotional rate, so moomoo gets 0 of 2.5 for cash. Its margin rate earns the full 2.5, for 2.5 of 5 on the factor.

How rivals compare. Interactive Brokers Canada pays 1.675% on Canadian-dollar cash above CAD 13,000, with full rates for accounts worth more than USD 100,000. Questrade's published formula gives 0% at today's prime rate. Webull's cash rate is not shown, and Wealthsimple does not state a rate for cash in its trading accounts.

What to do with idle cash. If you are holding cash for more than a few weeks, a high-interest savings account may pay a published rate. Inside a TFSA, where the 2026 dollar limit is $7,000, uninvested cash at an unknown rate wastes tax-free room. Our savings hub compares the options.

9. Is moomoo Safe in Canada? Safety and Regulation

Regulation. moomoo's Canadian business is Moomoo Financial Canada Inc. moomoo states that it is regulated by CIRO, the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization, which oversees investment dealers. Moomoo Financial Canada Inc. is on the Canadian Investor Protection Fund's current member list. With both memberships stated, moomoo scores 4.0 of 5 on our safety factor.

CIPF coverage. CIPF protects client property, meaning cash and securities, that is missing when a member firm becomes insolvent. Coverage is set by account group at each member firm.

11. moomoo Customer Support

What this review covers. Support hours and contact channels for Canadian clients are not part of the figures we checked for this review, so we do not list them. We print only support details we can confirm in our research.

Questions worth asking early. Three answers affect your costs at moomoo and are not fully published: the size of the spread in the Moomoo Preferred Rate, the formal terms of the Transfer Rewards reimbursement, and the standard Cash Sweep rate after the welcome period. Ask for each in writing before you move a large balance.

Have these ready. Your account number, the account type (TFSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, cash or margin), the institution you are moving from and a recent statement. For US shares, confirm a Form W-8BEN is on file for your TFSA, cash and margin accounts; without it, US dividends can be withheld at 30% instead of 15%.

Keep a record. Note the date, who you dealt with and what was agreed, and save chats and emails. A dated record is the fastest way to resolve a missing reward, a transfer delay or a disputed fee.

10 Strategies to Keep moomoo Costs Low

1. Measure the spread before you convert

moomoo's 0% fee hides a spread of undisclosed size. Compare its quoted rate with the mid-market rate at the same moment on a small conversion. If the gap is near 1.5%, a broker that publishes its rate may cost less.

2. Convert once and hold US dollars

Your TFSA and RRSP can hold US dollars with no fee stated. Converting once and keeping the US dollars means sales, purchases and dividends need no new conversion and no new spread.

3. Fill the minimum

The CA$1.49 minimum covers up to 100 Canadian shares (100 x CA$0.0149 = CA$1.49), and the US$1.99 minimum covers up to 200 US shares. Orders below those sizes pay the same as orders at them.

4. Buy quarterly, not monthly

Twelve monthly orders cost at least (12 x CA$1.49 = CA$17.88) a year; four quarterly orders cost at least (4 x CA$1.49 = CA$5.96). If you use auto-invest, a longer interval saves minimums.

5. Plan RRSP withdrawals

Each RRSP withdrawal costs CA$50 plus withholding tax. Two withdrawals cost CA$100 in fees; one larger withdrawal costs CA$50. Note that withholding rises to 20% above $5,000, so plan the size with your tax in mind.

6. Hold US dividend payers in the RRSP

US dividends in an RRSP are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. In a TFSA or cash account, 15% is withheld. Putting US dividend payers in the RRSP can save that 15%.

7. Put options contracts in one order

The US$1.00 minimum applies per order. Two single-contract orders cost (2 x US$1.00 = US$2.00); one two-contract order costs (2 x US$0.65 = US$1.30).

8. Count the exit fee per currency

Transfer-out fees are charged per currency. A full transfer of an account with CAD and USD assets costs ($75 + $75 = $150) plus tax. Know this before you split holdings across currencies.

9. Use coupons only for trades you planned

The $100 trading coupon comes as ten $10 coupons usable on transactions of $100 or more. Use them on purchases you would make anyway; extra trades to use a coupon still carry minimums and spread.

10. Keep FHSA and RESP money elsewhere

moomoo does not offer the FHSA or RESP. Do not skip those accounts because your main broker lacks them: the FHSA gives $8,000 of deductible room a year, and RESP grants are paid only into an RESP.

This example is hypothetical. Priya, 31, lives in Ontario and opens a moomoo TFSA and RRSP. In the TFSA she buys 60 shares of a Canadian ETF each month.   5. US dividends of US$120 in the RRSP: no US withholding once documented. In her TFSA they would lose (US$120 x 15% = US$18).
