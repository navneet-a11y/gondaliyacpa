1. TFSA Calculator: Room, Withdrawals and Over-Contributions

What it answers. The TFSA contribution room calculator adds up every annual limit you qualify for since 2009 and gives your room for 2026 and your new room on January 1. You enter your birth year, the year you became a resident of Canada if you were not always one, any years you were a non-resident, and your contributions and withdrawals.

The rules it applies. Room starts in the year you turn 18, even where the age of majority is 19 and you cannot open an account until then. The 2026 limit is $7,000, and someone who was 18 or older and resident every year since 2009 and never contributed has $109,000 of room. Newcomers start in the year they become resident and get that full year's limit, with no proration. No new room builds in a year you are a non-resident.

Withdrawals. A withdrawal is added back to your room on January 1 of the next year, not right away. That timing trips up many people who withdraw and redeposit in the same year. A direct transfer between two TFSAs does not affect room, but withdrawing and redepositing yourself counts as a new contribution.

Over-contributions. A second tool on the same page works out the tax on an excess: 1% a month on the highest excess in each month. Unlike the RRSP, the TFSA has no $2,000 cushion. If you have an excess, withdraw it as soon as possible and file the TFSA Return by June 30 of the following year.

2. RRSP Calculator: Tax Refund, Room and Withdrawal Tax

What it answers. The RRSP tax refund calculator works out how much tax a contribution saves in any province or territory using 2026 brackets. Two more tools on the page estimate your deduction limit and the tax on a withdrawal.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. An Ontario resident with $75,000 of taxable income who deducts a $5,000 RRSP contribution saves $1,632.50 of 2026 tax. That is $1,482.50 at the 29.65% combined rate ($5,000 x 29.65%) plus $150 of Ontario Health Premium, because the deduction moves income from $75,000 to $70,000 and the premium drops from $750 to $600. The same deduction saves $1,410.00 in British Columbia, $1,525.00 in Alberta and $1,805.88 in Quebec at the same income.

Room. Your RRSP deduction limit is 18% of your previous year's earned income up to the dollar limit ($33,810 for 2026 and $35,390 for 2027), minus any pension adjustment, plus unused room carried forward. You can go over by $2,000 without penalty if you were 18 or older in the previous year; above that, the excess is taxed at 1% a month.

Withdrawals and deadlines. A lump-sum withdrawal has tax withheld at 10% up to $5,000, 20% over $5,000 up to $15,000 and 30% over $15,000 outside Quebec. The withholding is a prepayment; your final tax depends on your marginal rate. The deadline for contributions that count for a tax year is the 60th day of the next year. For 2025 it was March 2, 2026; CRA has not published the date for the 2026 tax year yet.

3. FHSA and First Home Calculators

What it answers. The FHSA calculator page holds three tools: FHSA room by the year you opened your first account, a four-question eligibility check, and a first-home tool that adds FHSA savings to Home Buyers' Plan withdrawals for one buyer or a couple.

Room. FHSA room starts only when you open your first FHSA: $8,000 in that year and $8,000 each year after, up to $40,000 for life. Up to $8,000 of unused room carries forward, so the most you can contribute in one year is $16,000. The deadline is December 31, with no 60-day period after year-end. Contributions are deductible, and you can carry an unused deduction forward, even after the account is closed.

Eligibility. To open an FHSA you must be a resident of Canada, at least 18 (19 where that is the age of majority), no older than 71 at December 31 of the year you open it, and a first-time home buyer. That last test means you and your current spouse or common-law partner did not own and live in a home in the current year before opening or in the previous four calendar years.

FHSA plus the Home Buyers' Plan. You can use both for the same home. One buyer can put in up to $40,000 of FHSA contributions and withdraw up to $60,000 under the Home Buyers' Plan, or $100,000 before any FHSA growth ($40,000 + $60,000). A couple who both qualify can reach $200,000 ($100,000 x 2). The FHSA must close by December 31 of the year of the earliest of its 15th anniversary, your 71st birthday or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal.

4. RESP Grant Calculator: Grants and Growth to Age 17

What it answers. The RESP grant calculator projects every government grant a child can receive and the plan balance at 17, based on your contributions, family income, province and the child's age.

Federal grants. The basic Canada Education Savings Grant is 20% of the first $2,500 contributed each year, or $500. Unused grant room carries forward, so a child can get up to $1,000 in one year, and up to $7,200 over their lifetime. Families with adjusted family net income below $58,523 get an extra 20% on the first $500 each year ($100), and those from $58,523 up to $117,045 get an extra 10% ($50). The Canada Learning Bond pays lower-income families $500 in the first year and $100 a year after, up to $2,000, with no contribution needed.

Provincial grants. British Columbia pays a one-time $1,200 BC Training and Education Savings Grant. Quebec's basic incentive adds 10% of contributions, up to $250 a year and $3,600 for life. No other province currently has an RESP grant.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. At the basic 20% rate, a family that contributes $2,500 a year for 14 years and $1,000 in a 15th year collects the full $7,200 lifetime grant: $7,000 over the first 14 years ($500 x 14) plus $200 in the 15th ($1,000 x 20%). Its contributions total $36,000 ($2,500 x 14 + $1,000). Families who also receive the additional CESG reach the $7,200 cap sooner, because it counts toward the same lifetime maximum. Grants stop after the year the child turns 17, and grants at 16 and 17 depend on earlier contributions.

5. RRIF Minimum Withdrawal Calculator

What it answers. The RRIF minimum withdrawal calculator gives the minimum you must take this year and projects future minimums by age. You enter the balance on January 1, your age (or a younger spouse's age if you chose it when you set up the RRIF) and an assumed return.

The formula. The minimum is the fair market value on January 1 multiplied by a prescribed factor. At 70 or younger the factor is 1 divided by (90 minus your age), so 4.00% at 65. From 71 the factors come from a fixed chart: 5.28% at 71, 5.40% at 72, 5.53% at 73, 5.67% at 74 and 5.82% at 75, rising to 20% at 95 and over.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. A $500,000 RRIF at 71 must pay at least $26,400 that year ($500,000 x 5.28%). The same balance at 65 needs only $20,000 ($500,000 x 4.00%). No minimum is required in the year the RRIF is set up; the first one is due the following year.

Timing and tax. Your RRSP must become a RRIF, an annuity or a withdrawal by December 31 of the year you turn 71. A direct transfer to a RRIF has no tax withheld. Withdrawals above the minimum have tax withheld at the lump-sum rates of 10%, 20% or 30% outside Quebec. At 65 or older, RRIF income can qualify for the federal pension income amount of up to $2,000, and up to 50% of it can be split with a spouse or common-law partner.

7. Growth, Fee and Currency Calculators

Compound interest. The compound interest calculator shows how a lump sum and monthly savings grow, year by year, with yearly, semi-annual, quarterly, monthly or daily compounding. $10,000 at 5% compounded monthly grows to $16,470.09 in 10 years. 8. Benefit Calculators and Payment Dates

Payment dates. Our benefit payment dates hub counts down to the next payment and lists the full 2026 schedule. As of October 22, 2026, the next CPP and OAS payment is October 28, 2026, the next Canada Child Benefit payment is November 20, 2026, and the next Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit payment is January 5, 2027.

CPP and OAS start age. The calculator on our CPP and OAS payment dates page compares starting CPP at any age from 60 to 70 and OAS from 65 to 70. The maximum CPP retirement pension at 65 is $1,507.65 a month in 2026. Starting early cuts it by 0.6% a month (36% at 60) and waiting raises it by 0.7% a month (42% at 70). OAS rises 0.6% for each month you defer, up to 36% at 70.

OAS clawback. The OAS recovery tax takes 15% of net income above $95,323 for the 2026 income year. OAS is fully recovered at $155,320 for ages 65 to 74 and $161,320 for 75 and over. The clawback calculator shows how much of your pension you keep.

For July 2026 to June 2027 the maximum is $679 for a single person, $890 for a couple and $234 per child under 19, and the amount starts to fall when adjusted family net income passes $46,432. Our GST/HST credit page estimates your payment; CRA works out the exact amount from your return.

10. How Our Investment Calculators Work

Your numbers stay in your browser. Every calculator is code that runs on the page in your own browser. When you type a number, the result is worked out on your device. Nothing you enter is sent to or stored on our servers, and there is no account, email or sign-up.

What your browser remembers. To save you retyping, your browser keeps three entries on your device: your birth year, your province and your income. The next calculator you open fills them in. You can remove them at any time by clearing this site's data in your browser settings. If our site analytics are switched on, they record that a calculator was used and which one, never the values you entered.

One data set, dated. Every limit, rate and factor lives in a single data set with a checked date, currently October 22, 2026. Each tax, account and benefit figure comes from Canada Revenue Agency, Service Canada or provincial government publications and is entered only once it is confirmed. When a figure changes, we update the data set and every calculator on the site changes with it. Our editorial standards describe how figures are checked.

How the maths is done. Tax calculators apply each bracket in turn, subtract the basic personal amount credit at the lowest rate, apply dividend credits, and add the Ontario surtax and Health Premium or the Quebec abatement where they apply. Growth calculators convert an annual rate to an effective monthly rate and add deposits at the end of each month. The RRIF calculator multiplies the January 1 balance by the prescribed factor for the age. The TFSA calculator adds each annual limit from the year you turned 18 or became resident.

What they leave out. The calculators give general estimates. They do not include CPP and EI contributions, most non-refundable credits, or your personal history with CRA. For your exact TFSA or RRSP room, please check your latest notice of assessment. For a decision that depends on your whole situation, a professional who knows your file can account for what a calculator cannot.

How to choose the right calculator

  1. Start with the question: room, tax saved, growth, a withdrawal or a benefit amount.
  2. For how much you can contribute, open the TFSA, RRSP or FHSA calculator.
  3. For what a contribution or withdrawal does to your tax, use the RRSP refund tool or the income tax calculator above.
  4. For what your savings could become, use the compound interest calculator, then the fee tool.
  5. For a child's education or retirement income, use the RESP grant or RRIF minimum calculator.
  6. Re-run your numbers each January, when new limits take effect.
