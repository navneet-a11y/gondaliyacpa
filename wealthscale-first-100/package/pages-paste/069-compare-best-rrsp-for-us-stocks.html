The best RRSP for US stocks, on our US-stocks score, is at Questrade (4.0 / 5): its RRSP holds US dollars at no extra cost and US trades cost $0, though converting costs 1.5%. That matters more in an RRSP than in any other account. Under Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty, dividends on US-listed securities held directly in an RRSP are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented, while a TFSA loses 15%. Interactive Brokers ties at 4.0 / 5 with US$2 conversions. National Bank and Desjardins follow at 3.8 / 5, both with US-dollar RRSPs and RRIFs and no USD fee stated.

Top pick. Questrade scores 4.0 / 5 for US stocks. Its RRSP, spousal RRSP, RRIF, LIRA and LIF are dual-currency at no charge, so US-listed holdings can sit in US dollars with dividends paid in US dollars. US trades cost $0; converting the 2026 RRSP dollar limit of $33,810 costs $507.15 at 1.5%.

The treaty exemption. Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest earned by arrangements operated exclusively for pension or retirement benefits. RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs qualify. US-listed securities held directly in them have 0% US withholding once your broker has the account documented, against 15% in a TFSA.

What it is worth. On US$100,000 of US-listed holdings yielding 2% (hypothetical), the exemption keeps US$300 a year that a TFSA would lose, or US$3,000 over 10 years before growth.

US-dollar RRSPs with no fee stated. Questrade, National Bank Direct Brokerage, Desjardins, moomoo, Scotia iTRADE and TD Easy Trade. 1. How We Ranked the Best RRSP for US Stocks

All 13 brokers offer an RRSP. None drops out of this list, so it ranks the same 13 brokers by our US-stocks score: US investing 40%, cost 25%, account types 10%, platform 10%, safety 10% and cash and margin rates 5%. Our broker rating methodology sets out every rule.

Why US-dollar registered accounts carry so much weight here. The US sub-score gives up to 2 points for US dollars in registered accounts with no fee, 1 point if a fee applies, and 0.5 if the broker does not say. In an RRSP this is the measure that matters most, because the treaty exemption only pays off fully when US-listed holdings sit in the account in US dollars and their dividends stay in US dollars. The other parts are the conversion fee (up to 2 points) and the US commission (up to 1 point).

How this differs from our general RRSP list. Our list of the best RRSP accounts in Canada ranks the same brokers by the overall score, where US investing carries 15% and Interactive Brokers leads at 4.4 / 5. Here, brokers whose RRSPs hold US dollars at no cost, such as Questrade and National Bank, rank higher.

What the score does not measure. The treaty exemption is the same at every broker; the score cannot reward one broker for it. What the score captures is how cheaply each broker lets you use it: getting into US dollars, staying there, and trading. RRIF and locked-in account support is covered in the account-type sub-score and in the tables on this page. The same scores rank brokers across all account types in our list of the best brokers for US stocks.

2. The Treaty Exemption: Why US Dividends in an RRSP Are Not Taxed

The default US rate. The United States taxes dividends that US companies pay to foreign investors. For a Canadian resident, Article X(2)(b) of the Canada-US tax treaty caps that tax at 15%, provided the broker has a Form W-8BEN on file; without treaty documentation the rate is 30%. That is what a TFSA, FHSA, RESP or non-registered account pays.

The retirement exemption. Article XXI(2) of the same treaty exempts dividends and interest earned by a trust or other arrangement that is generally exempt from tax in its home country and is operated exclusively to provide pension, retirement or employee benefits. RRSPs, RRIFs and locked-in retirement accounts such as LIRAs and LIFs are such arrangements. A spousal RRSP is an RRSP, so it qualifies too.

What it means in practice. US-listed stocks and ETFs held directly in your RRSP pay their dividends with no US tax withheld, once your broker has the account documented. Your RRSP receives 100% of each dividend instead of 85%.

4. Hold US-Listed Securities Directly

Direct means listed in the United States. The exemption applies to US-listed securities that your RRSP owns itself. When your RRSP owns a US-listed stock or ETF, the RRSP is the shareholder, and its documented status as a retirement account is what switches off the withholding.

Canadian-listed funds that hold US shares. If your RRSP instead owns a Canadian-listed fund that holds US shares, the fund is the shareholder. The fund is not a retirement arrangement, so US tax can be withheld on the dividends it receives before anything reaches your RRSP, and your RRSP cannot recover it. Holding US exposure through a Canadian-listed fund in an RRSP therefore does not get the exemption that holding the US-listed security directly does. This is a general point about structure, not a view on any fund.

Why US dollars matter. US-listed securities trade in US dollars. If your RRSP can hold US dollars, you convert once, buy, and receive dividends in US dollars that can be reinvested without converting. If it cannot, each purchase converts from Canadian dollars and each dividend converts back, paying the spread twice. That is why US-dollar RRSPs with no fee carry so much weight in this ranking.

Moving existing US holdings. If you already own US-listed securities at another broker, an in-kind, direct RRSP transfer moves them without selling, so no conversion or trade happens. Check that the receiving RRSP can hold US dollars and US-listed securities first. See how to transfer an RRSP for the forms.

Which accounts to use for what. Because only retirement accounts get the exemption, a simple rule of thumb is: US-listed dividend payers in the RRSP or RRIF; low-dividend or Canadian holdings in the TFSA. Our ranking of the best TFSA for US stocks covers the TFSA side.

8. US Dividends in an RRSP: Checking the Exemption Works

Documentation comes first. The exemption applies once your broker has your RRSP documented. Please ask your broker to confirm that your RRSP, and any spousal RRSP, RRIF, LIRA or LIF, is set up for the treaty exemption before you buy US dividend payers.

Check the first dividend. Your activity statement or transaction history usually shows each US dividend with the gross amount and any tax withheld. In a documented RRSP holding a US-listed security directly, the tax line should be zero. If US tax was withheld, contact your broker promptly and ask why; the answer may be missing documentation on the account.

Compare with your other accounts. The same US-listed holding in your TFSA or non-registered account should show 15% withheld with a W-8BEN on file. If either shows 30%, the W-8BEN is missing. In a non-registered account you can claim a foreign tax credit for the 15%; in a TFSA you cannot.

Keep dividends in US dollars. The exemption gives you the full dividend; a Canadian-dollar RRSP then converts it at the broker's rate. RBC publishes a 60 basis point rate on dividends and interest (as of March 9, 2026), lower than its general spread; Wealthsimple applies its conversion fee when US dividends are reinvested in a non-USD account. A US-dollar RRSP avoids both.

What the exemption does not cover. It covers US-source dividends and interest on securities your RRSP holds directly. It does not change Canadian tax: RRSP withdrawals are taxed as income in Canada whatever the RRSP held. And it does not reach US tax withheld inside a Canadian-listed fund that holds US shares, as section 4 explains.

9. RRSP Rules That Shape a US Portfolio

How much you can contribute. Your deduction limit is the lesser of 18% of the previous year's earned income and the yearly dollar limit, minus pension adjustments, plus unused room carried forward. The dollar limit is $33,810 for 2026 and $35,390 for 2027. Unused room carries forward to later years. Contributions are made in Canadian dollars and converted inside the RRSP.

Deadlines. Contributions for a tax year can be made until the 60th day of the following year. The deadline for the 2025 tax year was March 2, 2026; the exact date for the 2026 tax year has not been published yet.

Over-contributions. You can go up to $2,000 over your limit without penalty if you were 18 or older at any time in the previous year. Anything above that is taxed at 1% a month.

Age limits and spousal plans. December 31 of the year you turn 71 is the last day to contribute to your own RRSP. You can contribute to a spousal RRSP until the end of the year your spouse or common-law partner turns 71. Withdrawals from a spousal RRSP can be taxed in your hands if you contributed in the year of withdrawal or either of the two preceding years.

Withdrawals. Lump-sum withdrawals have tax withheld at source: outside Quebec, 10% up to $5,000, 20% from $5,001 to $15,000 and 30% over $15,000. In Quebec, the federal part is 5%, 10% and 15%, plus Quebec tax. Non-residents generally have 25% withheld unless a treaty reduces it. Withholding is a prepayment; the withdrawal is added to your income for the year.

10. From RRSP to RRIF: Keeping US Holdings in Retirement

The exemption continues. A RRIF is a retirement arrangement under Article XXI(2) of the treaty, just like the RRSP it came from. US-listed securities held directly in a documented RRIF keep paying dividends with no US withholding.

Not every broker has a US-dollar RRIF. Questrade, National Bank, Desjardins and Scotia iTRADE state US-dollar RRIFs with no fee. Qtrade offers one for US$15 a quarter (none with Investor Plus). RBC accounts hold Canadian and US dollar balances. Wealthsimple offers a RRIF but no USD account for it, so US holdings there would trade and pay dividends through the Canadian-dollar side at 1.5%. moomoo, Webull and TD Easy Trade list no RRIF at all, so an RRSP there would need to move before it becomes a RRIF. Interactive Brokers launched its RRIF on February 3, 2026, but does not state US-dollar support.

Minimum withdrawals. From the year after you set up a RRIF, you must withdraw at least the minimum each year: the RRIF's fair market value at the start of the year times a prescribed factor for your age. The factor is 0.0528 at 71, 0.0540 at 72 and 0.0582 at 75. This example is hypothetical: on a $300,000 RRIF at 72, the minimum is $16,200 ($300,000 x 0.0540).

The conversion at payout. If your RRIF pays you in Canadian dollars, each withdrawal from US-dollar holdings converts at the broker's rate. Converting the $16,200 above would cost $243.00 at 1.5% or $204.12 at TD's 1.26%. Larger, less frequent payments can land in lower bands at tiered brokers.

Withholding on RRIF payments. The Canada Revenue Agency's payer guide treats the part of a RRIF payment above the minimum as a lump sum, with tax withheld at 10%, 20% or 30% (5%, 10% or 15% federal in Quebec). For factors at every age, see our RRIF minimum withdrawal table, the RRIF calculator and our ranking of the best RRIF accounts.

13. Common Mistakes With US Stocks in an RRSP

Holding US exposure through a Canadian-listed fund and expecting the exemption. The exemption reaches US-listed securities the RRSP owns directly. A Canadian-listed fund holding US shares is the shareholder, and US tax inside the fund is not recovered by your RRSP.

Not checking the first dividend. If your RRSP is not documented, US tax may be withheld. Look at the first US dividend and raise any withholding with your broker right away.

Using a Canadian-dollar RRSP for US holdings. Every purchase converts in and every dividend converts out. On US$2,000 of dividends a year, converting at 1.5% costs $30 a year (counting US dollars at par) that a US-dollar RRSP would avoid.

Converting a large balance at a flat 1.5%. $100,000 at 1.5% costs $1,500.00. The same conversion costs $460.00 at TD, US$2.00 at Interactive Brokers, $0.00 inside a Wealthsimple USD account, or $0 plus spreads by Norbert's Gambit at a broker with $0 ETF trades.

Ignoring the RRIF stage. moomoo, Webull and TD Easy Trade list no RRIF, and Wealthsimple has no US-dollar RRIF. If your RRSP is there at 71, plan the move or the conversion in advance.

Over-contributing. Beyond the $2,000 cushion, an excess costs 1% a month. Please check your notice of assessment for your deduction limit before you contribute.

Putting low-dividend US holdings in the RRSP and high-dividend ones in the TFSA. That arrangement loses the most to withholding. As a general rule, the RRSP is the better home for US-listed dividend payers.

Are US dividends in an RRSP taxed by the United States?

Not on US-listed securities the RRSP holds directly, once your broker has the account documented. Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest earned by arrangements operated exclusively for retirement benefits, which includes RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs. A TFSA pays 15% with a W-8BEN.

Why is an RRSP exempt from US withholding but a TFSA is not?

The treaty exemption covers arrangements operated exclusively to provide pension or retirement benefits. An RRSP is a retirement savings plan, so it qualifies. A TFSA is a general savings account that can be used for anything, so it does not, and US dividends paid into it lose 15% that cannot be recovered.

Do I need a W-8BEN for my RRSP?

Your broker needs your RRSP documented to apply the treaty exemption. A W-8BEN is what gets your TFSA and non-registered accounts the 15% treaty rate. Ask your broker what it needs for the RRSP, then check that your first US dividend shows no tax withheld.

Does the exemption apply to Canadian-listed ETFs that hold US stocks?

No. The exemption applies to US-listed securities your RRSP holds directly. When a Canadian-listed fund holds US shares, the fund is the shareholder and is not a retirement arrangement, so US tax can be withheld inside the fund, and your RRSP cannot recover it. This is a point about structure, not a view on any fund.

Does the US withholding exemption continue in a RRIF?

Yes. A RRIF is a retirement arrangement under the treaty, so US-listed securities held directly in a documented RRIF keep paying dividends with no US withholding. Questrade, National Bank, Desjardins and Scotia iTRADE state US-dollar RRIFs with no fee. Wealthsimple's RRIF has no US-dollar side, and moomoo, Webull and TD Easy Trade list no RRIF.

Do a spousal RRSP and a LIRA get the exemption?

Yes. A spousal RRSP is an RRSP, and LIRAs and LIFs are locked-in retirement accounts, so US-listed securities held directly in them are exempt from US withholding once documented. Questrade states US dollars in its registered accounts, National Bank in its spousal RRSP and LIRA, and Desjardins in its LIRA and LIF.

What happens to my US holdings in an RRSP at 71?

By the end of the year you turn 71 you must withdraw the RRSP, transfer it to a RRIF or buy an annuity. A direct transfer to a RRIF has no tax withheld, and US holdings can move in kind if the RRIF can hold them. Check that your broker offers a RRIF with a US-dollar side well before then.
