1. How to Use the TFSA Contribution Room Calculator

Start with three facts about you. Your birth year tells the calculator when you turned 18. The year you became a resident tells it when a newcomer's room began; leave it blank if you have always lived in Canada. The years-abroad box takes any full calendar years you lived outside Canada, such as "2016, 2017", because those years add no room.

Your province. This sets the age at which you can open a TFSA: 18 in Alberta, Manitoba, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec and Saskatchewan, and 19 in British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, the Northwest Territories, Nova Scotia, Nunavut and Yukon. It does not change your room: someone who must wait until 19 to open a TFSA still earns room from the year they turn 18.

Then your history. Enter total contributions and total withdrawals made before 2026, then contributions and withdrawals made so far in 2026. Keep the two periods separate. They are treated differently, because withdrawals made in 2026 do not add room until January 1, 2027.

2. The Rules Behind the Numbers

Your start year. The calculator starts counting in the latest of three years: 2009, when TFSAs began; the year you turn 18; and the year you became a resident. From that year to 2026 it adds each year's limit, except any year you list as a full year abroad.

The room formula. Room for the year = the current year's limit + unused room from earlier years + withdrawals made in the previous year - contributions made this year. Unused room carries forward with no expiry, so the calculator simply adds up every year.

No proration. The year you turn 18, arrive in Canada or leave Canada each count in full. A December arrival gets the same room for that year as a January arrival.

The limits it uses. These are CRA's annual TFSA dollar limits. The limit is indexed to inflation and rounded to the nearest $500.

What it leaves out. Investment growth and losses do not affect room, so the calculator never asks for your balance. 3. Worked Examples: Three People, Three Results

This example is hypothetical.

Leah: born 2000, always resident, some activity.

  1. She turned 18 in 2018, so she has earned every limit from 2018 to 2026: $57,000 ($5,500 + 4 x $6,000 + $6,500 + 3 x $7,000).
  2. Before 2026 she contributed $15,000 and withdrew $3,000 (in 2024). Room on January 1, 2026: $45,000 ($57,000 - $15,000 + $3,000).
  3. She has contributed $4,000 so far in 2026. Room now: $41,000 ($45,000 - $4,000).
  4. She has made no withdrawals in 2026, so on January 1, 2027 she starts with $41,000 plus the 2027 limit.

This example is hypothetical.

Marco: arrived in Canada in 2019 at age 30, never contributed.

  1. His start year is 2019, the year he became a resident. Years before he arrived do not count.
  2. Room earned 2019 to 2026: $51,500 (4 x $6,000 + $6,500 + 3 x $7,000).
  3. With no contributions, his room now is $51,500. If he had been treated as eligible since 2009, the figure would wrongly show $109,000, so the residency box matters.

This example is hypothetical.

Ruth: born 1990, nearly full, one deposit too many.

  1. She was 18 or older in 2009, so she has earned $109,000.
  2. She contributed $100,000 before 2026 and never withdrew. Room on January 1, 2026: $9,000 ($109,000 - $100,000).
  3. In April 2026 she deposits $12,000. Her room is now minus $3,000 ($9,000 - $12,000), so she has a $3,000 excess.
  4. She notices and withdraws $3,000 in July. The excess existed in April, May, June and July, so the tax is $120 ($3,000 x 1% x 4 months).

5. How Withdrawals and Re-Contributions Work in the Calculator

Earlier withdrawals add room now. Withdrawals made before 2026 have already been added back to your room on the January 1 after each one. That is why the calculator adds your total pre-2026 withdrawals straight into your room on January 1, 2026.

This year's withdrawals wait. A withdrawal made in 2026 does not change your room for 2026. The calculator shows it only in your January 1, 2027 figure. For example, if Leah from section 3 withdrew $10,000 in 2026, her room now would still be $41,000, and her January 1, 2027 room would be $51,000 ($41,000 + $10,000) plus the 2027 limit.

Putting money back. You can redeposit at any time up to your room now. Anything above that figure is an excess until the next January 1. Run the calculator before you put back a withdrawal made this year, because the redeposit uses room just like a new deposit.

Growth comes back too. Enter withdrawals at the amount actually taken out, including any growth. If you contributed $10,000 and later withdrew $14,000, enter $10,000 as a contribution and $14,000 as a withdrawal. Your room ends up $4,000 higher than if you had never contributed, which is correct.

Transfers are not withdrawals. If you moved a TFSA from one institution to another by direct transfer, leave it out of both boxes. If you withdrew and redeposited it yourself, enter both amounts, because that is how CRA counts them. Our TFSA withdrawal rules guide covers timing and transfers in detail, and our guide on how to transfer a TFSA explains the direct-transfer process.

6. If the Calculator Shows You Are Over

A negative result is an excess. If your room now is below zero, you have contributed more than your room allows. The calculator shows the amount and an estimate of the monthly tax.

The tax. An excess is taxed at 1% per month on the highest excess amount in each month it remains. There is no grace amount, and a month counts in full even if the excess existed for only part of it.

What to do. Please withdraw the excess as soon as you find it. Then file the TFSA Return and pay the tax by June 30 of the following year. The excess also ends when new room arrives on January 1 and covers it, but waiting costs 1% for every month until then.

Deposits while abroad. A contribution made while you are a non-resident is taxed at 1% per month on its own, until it is withdrawn or you become a resident again. If it also exceeds your room, the 1% excess tax applies on top.

Check the cost. Use this calculator to see the tax on any excess for the number of months it stayed in the account, and how much you save by removing it a month sooner.

7. How Much TFSA Room Do I Have? Our Figure vs CRA My Account

Two sources, one answer. CRA keeps its own record of your TFSA room, which you can see in CRA My Account. It is built from the contributions and withdrawals your TFSA issuers report to CRA. Our calculator is built from what you enter. When both use the same history, they agree.

Why they can differ. CRA's figure may not yet include recent transactions, especially deposits and withdrawals made this year. An institution may have reported a transfer as a withdrawal and a new contribution. Or a TFSA you forgot about may show up in CRA's records but not in yours.

How to compare. Start with the room on January 1 of the current year, because that is the figure least affected by timing. Compare it with the calculator's "Room on January 1, 2026" line. If they match, apply this year's deposits yourself. If they do not, list every contribution and withdrawal by year and institution and look for the gap.

When CRA shows less than we do. Treat the lower figure as your limit until you know why. Depositing up to the higher figure risks the 1% monthly tax if CRA's records turn out to be right.

Fixing a reporting error. If an institution reported a transaction wrongly, ask it to correct what it filed with CRA. Keep statements that show each transaction, because they are your evidence if CRA later says you were over.

Use both. CRA's figure is the official record. The calculator is a fast way to check it, plan this year's deposits, and see your January 1, 2027 starting point before CRA updates anything. How we check the limits it uses is set out in our editorial standards.

8. What Your TFSA Room Could Grow To

Room is the ceiling, growth is the point. Every dollar of room you fill can earn interest, dividends and gains that are never taxed in a TFSA. The longer the money stays in, the larger the share of the final balance that comes from growth.

A simple illustration. This example is hypothetical, and 5% is not a forecast. Depositing $7,000 at the start of each year and earning 5% a year, compounded yearly, gives $7,350 after one year ($7,000 x 1.05), $15,067.50 after two ($14,350 x 1.05) and $23,170.88 after three ($22,067.50 x 1.05). After 20 years the balance is $243,034.76, of which $140,000 is deposits ($7,000 x 20) and $103,034.76 is growth. Future TFSA limits are not known, so the $7,000 a year is an assumption.

Catching up with a lump sum. Someone who deposits the full $109,000 of room at once and earns a hypothetical 5% a year for 10 years ends with $177,549.51 ($109,000 x 1.05 to the power of 10). All $68,549.51 of growth is tax-free inside the TFSA.

Try your own numbers. This calculator adds monthly deposits at the end of each month and shows the balance year by year. Because it uses monthly deposits, its results differ slightly from the yearly example above.

Where the money sits matters. Savings accounts and GICs give a fixed or variable interest rate; investments can earn more or less. Compare current high-interest savings rates and GIC rates, or the brokerage options in our best TFSA accounts list and the Wealthsimple TFSA review. 8 Ways to Use the TFSA Calculator Well

1. Run it before every large deposit

A two-minute check before you move money helps prevent a common TFSA penalty. It matters most when you have several TFSAs or made a withdrawal earlier in the year.

2. Run it again each January

On January 1 your withdrawals from last year and the new limit arrive together. Once CRA publishes the 2027 limit, rerun the calculator to see your full 2027 room.

3. Treat the lower figure as your limit

If CRA's figure and ours differ, use the lower one until you have found the reason. The cost of waiting is small; the cost of an excess is 1% a month.

4. Keep transfers out of the totals

Direct transfers between your TFSAs do not use room. Including them makes your room look smaller than it is and can hide room you could use.

5. Run it for both partners

Each spouse or partner has separate room. Running the calculator for each shows the household total and which account has space for the next deposit.

6. Test a move abroad before you go

Enter the full years you expect to be away to see what room you would give up. Make any deposit you want while you are still a resident.

7. Check before automatic deposits begin

Monthly pre-authorized deposits keep running until you stop them. Use your room figure to set an amount and an end date that stay inside it.

8. Save the result with the date

Note your room and the date you checked. A short record of each check makes next year's calculation faster and gives you evidence if a figure is ever questioned.

TFSA Contribution Room Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate my TFSA contribution room?

Add every annual TFSA limit from the year you turned 18 or became a resident (but not before 2009) to the current year. Subtract all your contributions and add back all withdrawals made in earlier years. Then subtract what you have contributed this year. The result is your room now. Our calculator does each step for you.

How much TFSA room do I have if I have never contributed?

If you were 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009, you have $109,000 in 2026. If you turned 18 or arrived later, your room starts in that year: for example, $57,000 if you turned 18 in 2018, or $21,000 if you became a resident in 2024 as an adult.

How much TFSA room does a 25-year-old have?

A person who is 25 at the end of 2026 was born in 2001 and turned 18 in 2019. If they have been a resident since then and never contributed, they have $51,500 of room in 2026. That is the sum of the limits from 2019 to 2026.

Does the calculator include the 2027 TFSA limit?

Not yet. The 2027 TFSA limit has not been published. The calculator shows your January 1, 2027 room as your unused 2026 room plus your 2026 withdrawals, "plus the 2027 limit". We will add the official figure as soon as CRA publishes it.

Why is my CRA My Account figure different from the calculator?

CRA's figure comes from what your TFSA issuers report, so it may not include recent transactions, and a transfer may have been reported as a withdrawal and a new deposit. Compare the January 1 room in both, then list your transactions by year to find the gap. Until you know the cause, treat the lower figure as your limit.

Should I include TFSA transfers in my contributions?

No, not if they were direct transfers between your own TFSAs, because those do not affect your room. If you withdrew money and redeposited it yourself, enter it as both a withdrawal and a contribution, because that is how it counts. Transfers to a former partner under a court order or written agreement are also left out.

How do withdrawals affect my TFSA room?

A withdrawal is added back to your room on January 1 of the year after you make it. It does not increase your room in the same year. The calculator adds withdrawals made before 2026 to your current room and shows 2026 withdrawals in your January 1, 2027 figure.

How much TFSA room does a newcomer to Canada have?

A newcomer who arrives at 18 or older earns room from the year they become a resident, and that year counts in full. A person who became a resident in 2019 and never contributed has $51,500 in 2026, and a 2026 arrival has $7,000. Years before arrival never count.

What does it mean if the calculator shows negative room?

It means you have an excess: you contributed more than your room allows. The excess is taxed at 1% per month on the highest excess in each month. Withdraw it as soon as possible, then file the TFSA Return and pay the tax by June 30 of the following year.

Does investment growth count toward my TFSA room?

No. Growth and losses inside a TFSA do not use or add room. Only contributions use room, and only withdrawals add it back, on the next January 1. That is why the calculator never asks for your account balance. Growth still matters when you withdraw, because the full amount taken out returns as room.

You know your room. Next, choose the account that will hold it.
