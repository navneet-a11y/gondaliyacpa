What is the best FHSA investment for your timeline?

Buying within about three years. The deduction is the main benefit, and losing part of the down payment to a market drop would hurt more than missing some growth. FHSA savings at 2.85% (Saven) or a GIC that matures before your closing date keeps the full balance available.

Buying in five years or more, or unsure. Up to 15 years can pass between opening an FHSA and the end of its participation period, and an unused FHSA can move to an RRSP. A longer horizon leaves room for a self-directed or managed portfolio of ETFs, with the risk that its value can fall.

Splitting the difference. You can hold more than one FHSA, and the room is shared across them. You could keep a savings FHSA for the down payment you expect to use soon and invest a second FHSA for the longer term. 5. USD FHSA and US Stocks in an FHSA

Few FHSAs hold US dollars. Wealthsimple lets you add a USD account to an FHSA ($10 a month plus tax on Core; included from $100,000 of assets). Questrade says US dollars can be held in its registered accounts at no extra cost. National Bank Direct Brokerage, Qtrade and TD Easy Trade offer US dollars in other registered accounts but not in the FHSA. Disnat does not list a US-dollar FHSA, and Interactive Brokers, BMO, CIBC and Scotia iTRADE do not say.

US dividends lose 15%. The FHSA is not a retirement arrangement under the Canada-US tax treaty, so the US withholds 15% of dividends when your broker has a Form W-8BEN on file (30% without it), and the tax cannot be recovered inside the FHSA. This example is hypothetical: $400 of US dividends in a year loses $60 ($400 x 15%).

Currency risk meets a deadline. A down payment is paid in Canadian dollars. US holdings in an FHSA carry both market risk and exchange-rate risk up to the day you sell, and converting back costs 1.5% or more at most brokers that publish a rate. For money needed on a fixed date, that is two sources of risk instead of one.

If the FHSA becomes an RRSP. If you never buy and transfer the FHSA to an RRSP, directly held US-listed shares are then in an account the treaty exempts. Room starts only when you open. No FHSA room builds up before you open your first FHSA, and the carry-forward in the opening year is $0. This example is hypothetical: if you open an FHSA in 2026 and contribute nothing, you have $16,000 of room in 2027 ($8,000 + $8,000 carried forward). If you wait and open in 2027, you have only $8,000 that year.

The opening-year trap. Because carry-forward is $0 in the opening year, depositing $16,000 in that year creates an $8,000 excess ($16,000 - $8,000). The tax is $80 a month ($8,000 x 1%). If the deposit is made in June and left in place, from June to December that is $560 ($80 x 7). New room on January 1 ends the excess, or you can remove it earlier with a designated withdrawal or transfer on Form RC727.

The calendar-year deadline. Unlike an RRSP, FHSA contributions made in the first 60 days of a year cannot be deducted for the previous year. Please plan your 2026 contribution for December 31, 2026 or earlier.

8. Qualifying Withdrawals, the Home Buyers' Plan and Couples

What makes a withdrawal qualifying. You must be a first-time home buyer at the time of the withdrawal (no owned home you lived in during the current year, ignoring the 30 days before the withdrawal, or the previous four calendar years). You need a written agreement to buy or build a qualifying home with completion before October 1 of the year after the withdrawal. The home must not have been bought more than 30 days before the withdrawal, you must intend to live in it within one year, and you must be resident in Canada. You request it on Form RC725.

After the first qualifying withdrawal. The participation period ends on December 31 of the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. Anything left must be withdrawn or transferred to an RRSP or RRIF by then.

Adding the Home Buyers' Plan. You can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal of up to $60,000 from RRSPs for the same home, if you meet the conditions of each at the time. HBP money must be repaid over up to 15 years; for first withdrawals made from 2022 to 2028, repayment starts in the fifth year after the withdrawal. FHSA money is never repaid.

Couples. Each spouse or partner who qualifies can open their own FHSA, with their own $8,000 a year and $40,000 lifetime limit, and their own HBP limit. The first-time buyer test also looks at your current spouse or partner: if you live in a home your spouse owns, you do not qualify to open an FHSA. This example is hypothetical: a couple who each fill an FHSA ($40,000 each) and each use the full HBP ($60,000 each) could put $200,000 toward one home before growth ($80,000 + $120,000), provided both have that much in RRSPs.

9. If You Do Not Buy: Moving an FHSA to an RRSP

The RRSP route. If you do not buy a home, you can transfer your FHSA directly to an RRSP or RRIF. The transfer is tax-deferred and does not use any RRSP deduction room, as long as you have no excess FHSA amount. Only a direct transfer qualifies; a withdrawal followed by an RRSP contribution does not.

The taxable route. You can also take a taxable withdrawal, which is added to your income. Anything still in an FHSA after the participation period ends loses its FHSA status, and its fair market value is added to your income for that year.

A worked comparison. This example is hypothetical. You contributed $40,000 over five years, deducted all of it, and the FHSA has grown to $46,000. You decide not to buy. A direct transfer moves $46,000 into your RRSP, uses none of your RRSP room, and keeps the deductions you already claimed. Taking it as a withdrawal instead would add $46,000 to your income in one year.

Pick a provider that can take the transfer. All 11 brokers on this list also offer an RRSP, and both ranked robo-advisors with an FHSA (Wealthsimple Managed Investing and RBC InvestEase) offer RRSPs too, so the transfer can stay at the same provider. If your FHSA is at a provider without the RRSP you want, a direct transfer to an RRSP elsewhere also qualifies.

11. How to Move an FHSA to Another Provider

Ask for a direct transfer. Open the new FHSA, then ask the new provider to transfer the account from the old one. Please do not withdraw and redeposit: a withdrawal that is not a qualifying withdrawal for a home is taxable, and putting the money back uses new FHSA room.

Costs. Section 4 lists each broker's exit fee and which receiving brokers cover it. Two extra costs apply to FHSAs moved early or in part: Qtrade charges $100 if you close an account within its first year, and Disnat charges $25 per security on a partial transfer.

In kind or cash. Moving holdings in kind avoids selling and buying again. Wealthsimple's fractional shares cannot be transferred to another institution, so they are sold first. A non-redeemable GIC must normally mature before it can be moved as cash.

Timing around a purchase. If your closing date is close, a transfer in progress can delay a withdrawal. It is usually simpler to make the qualifying withdrawal from the provider you have. Our transfer guide explains the forms and what to check afterwards.

8 FHSA Strategies for First-Time Buyers

1. Open as soon as you qualify

Opening starts your $8,000 of yearly room, even if you deposit only a small amount. Opening in 2026 and contributing in 2027 gives you $16,000 of room in 2027 instead of $8,000.

2. Contribute by December 31

There is no 60-day period for an FHSA. Put your contribution in by the last day of the year, and leave a few business days for the deposit to settle.

3. Deduct in a higher-income year

If your income is low now, contribute but hold the deduction for a later year. At $75,000 in Ontario an $8,000 deduction saves $2,522.00; at $50,000 it saves $1,674.00.

4. Match investments to your closing date

Use savings or GICs that mature before you expect to buy, and keep longer-term investing for money you will not need for several years.

5. Stack the FHSA and the HBP

You can use both for the same home. One buyer could combine $40,000 of FHSA contributions with up to $60,000 from RRSPs under the Home Buyers' Plan, before growth.

6. Choose a provider with no FHSA fee

Interactive Brokers, Questrade, Wealthsimple, Disnat, RBC, Scotia iTRADE and CIBC charge no account fee on an FHSA. BMO charges $0 under $25,000, and TD $0 if the FHSA is your only TD account.

7. Move unused money to an RRSP

If you do not buy, a direct transfer to an RRSP or RRIF keeps the money tax-deferred and uses no RRSP room. Do it before the participation period ends.

8. Watch the 15-year clock

Your FHSA must close by December 31 of the year of its 15th anniversary, the year you turn 71, or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal, whichever comes first. Note the date when you open.

Illustrative Example: A Couple Saving With Two FHSAs

This example is hypothetical. Aisha and Ben live in Ontario, rent, and have never owned a home. Aisha's taxable income is $75,000 and Ben's is $50,000. Each opens an FHSA in January 2026 at a broker with no FHSA fee, and each contributes $8,000 a year from 2026 to 2030, holding savings and GICs timed to mature by 2031. Each also has $20,000 in an RRSP. Tax figures use 2026 rates throughout and assume their incomes stay the same.

  1. Room. Each gets $8,000 a year from 2026. After five years each reaches the $40,000 lifetime limit ($8,000 x 5).
  2. Combined FHSA contributions. $40,000 x 2 = $80,000.
  3. Aisha's tax savings. $2,522.00 a year at $75,000 in Ontario. Over five years: $2,522.00 x 5 = $12,610.00.
  4. Ben's tax savings. $1,674.00 a year at $50,000 in Ontario. Over five years: $1,674.00 x 5 = $8,370.00.
  5. Combined tax saved. $12,610.00 + $8,370.00 = $20,980.00.
  6. Home Buyers' Plan. Each withdraws their $20,000 RRSP under the HBP for the same home: $20,000 x 2 = $40,000. Because the withdrawals come in 2031, after the temporary deferral window for first withdrawals made up to 2028, the standard rule applies as it stands today: repayment starts in the second year after the withdrawal, at least $1,333.33 each a year ($20,000 / 15).
  7. Total toward the home. $80,000 from the FHSAs + $40,000 from the HBP = $120,000, before any interest or growth. The FHSA withdrawals are not taxed and never repaid.

The deduction is the same at any provider. Choosing an FHSA with no account fee keeps up to $100 a year each in their accounts, and choosing savings or GICs that mature by 2031 protects the down payment from a market drop near closing.
