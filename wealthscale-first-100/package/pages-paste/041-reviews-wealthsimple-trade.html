1. Wealthsimple Trade Review: Verdict and Score

The short version. For an investor who buys Canadian-listed ETFs and stocks and holds them, Wealthsimple's self-directed platform costs nothing to use: no commission, no account fee and no fee to leave. That is why it scores 4.44 out of 5 on cost and 4.0 / 5 overall, third of 13 brokers as of October 22, 2026. Wealthsimple describes the product as self-directed investing, and its trading web page is still titled Wealthsimple Trade.

3. Currency Conversion and USD Accounts

The fee on US trades. When you buy a US-listed security from a Canadian-dollar account, Wealthsimple converts at its corporate exchange rate and adds a 1.5% fee. The fee is the same on Core, Premium and Generation, and it applies again when you sell and the US-dollar proceeds come back as Canadian dollars. US dividends paid into a Canadian-dollar account are converted the same way, including when dividend reinvestment is on.

The USD account. A USD account lets you hold US dollars and trade US-listed securities with them at no conversion fee. You still pay to convert Canadian dollars into it, but the rate falls with the size of each conversion: 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000 up to $24,999.99, 0.5% from $25,000 up to $99,999.99, and 0% from $100,000. Your membership tier does not change these rates; only the size of the transaction does.

4. Wealthsimple Options Trading

Pricing. Since October 2025, Wealthsimple charges $0 commission and $0 per contract on equity and ETF options for every tier. In our options profile, four trades a month of two contracts each (96 contracts a year), the cost at Wealthsimple is $0.00, against $62.40 at Interactive Brokers Canada (48 orders x US$1.30, counted at par). That gives Wealthsimple a full 5.0 on options cost.

What you can do in each account. In registered accounts, such as a TFSA or RRSP, and in margin accounts, you can buy calls, buy puts, sell cash-secured puts and sell covered calls. Every other strategy, such as spreads or selling uncovered options, needs a margin account. Options trade on the web platform and in the mobile app.

Costs that remain. An instruction to exercise early or not to exercise costs US$45. Options on US-listed stocks and ETFs are priced in US dollars, so check how premiums are converted in your account before you trade. Wealthsimple's fee schedule also prices some index options, but its options page describes only US-listed stocks and ETFs, so we are confirming which index options are offered before we list them.

Options and tax. Options income in a TFSA is sheltered like any other TFSA investment income, with one exception: if you trade with the frequency and experience of a professional trader, the CRA may tax the account's income as business income. It gives no numeric test. Options income in an RRSP is taxed only when you withdraw. Our TFSA guide explains the business-income risk in more detail.

How it ranks. Wealthsimple is second of 13 in our best options trading platforms ranking at 4.3 / 5, just ahead of Questrade on the unrounded score. Interactive Brokers Canada leads at 4.6 / 5 on higher platform and cash-and-margin scores, even though its per-contract fees are higher.

5. Fractional Shares, Recurring Investments and DRIP

Fractional shares. You can buy part of a share in thousands of Canadian and US stocks and ETFs from as little as $1, at $0 commission, with orders filled in real time during market hours. Fractional orders do not run in pre-market, post-market or overnight sessions. Fractional shares also cannot be transferred to another institution, which matters if you ever leave: plan to sell them, or round them up to whole shares, before you move.

Recurring investments. Automatic purchases can run daily, weekly, every two weeks or monthly, and work for stocks, ETFs and crypto. Two limits apply. In an RESP, a recurring purchase is funded only from cash already in the account. And recurring investments are not available in joint non-registered accounts that have US dollars enabled.

Dividend reinvestment. You can turn dividend reinvestment on for a trading account, and it applies to the whole account rather than security by security. Wealthsimple lists no reinvestment fee, but when US dividends are reinvested in a Canadian-dollar account they are converted first, so the 1.5% fee applies. On US$1,000 of yearly dividends, that is $15.00 ($1,000 x 1.5%, counted at par).

Why this matters for small investors. Fractional shares, $0 commissions and recurring buys together mean a $50 weekly plan puts $2,600 a year ($50 x 52) to work with no trading cost at all. At a broker that charges $9.99 a trade, the same 52 purchases would cost $519.48 ($9.99 x 52), about 20% of the money invested. Fractional shares are one of the four features behind Wealthsimple's 5.0 on our beginner platform test, alongside the mobile app, DRIP and no stated minimum.

What to check. Wealthsimple describes fractional trading as covering thousands of stocks and ETFs, not every listed security. Please confirm a security shows a fractional option in the app before you set up a recurring plan for it. Our best investing apps in Canada ranking compares these features across platforms, and Wealthsimple ranks first of 10 there at 4.5 / 5.

8. Trading Hours and Limits

24-hour weekday trading. US-listed stocks and ETFs trade 24 hours a day, five days a week at Wealthsimple, from Sunday 8 pm to Friday 8 pm Eastern time. Canadian securities are not part of the 24-hour program.

Why limit orders matter after hours. Fewer buyers and sellers trade outside regular hours, so prices can move further between bids and offers. A limit order sets the worst price you will accept, which protects you from filling far away from the last regular-session price. Please set limits close to where you want to trade, and check that an order has filled before assuming you hold the shares.

Deposit limits. Instant deposits, which let you trade before the money arrives from your bank, go up to $50,000 on Core and up to $250,000 on Premium and Generation. Instant withdrawals to a debit card cost 2.5%; a standard withdrawal avoids that fee.

Other limits to know. Registered accounts allow only four options strategies (buying calls, buying puts, cash-secured puts and covered calls). Some margin accounts start restricted, with a $1,000 borrowing limit. Recurring buys are not available in joint non-registered accounts with US dollars enabled. A corporation can hold up to eight investing accounts and one margin account. Fractional shares cannot leave Wealthsimple in a transfer.

9. App, Web and Active Trader Status

Two ways to trade. Wealthsimple's self-directed investing runs in the Wealthsimple mobile app and in a web app. Both handle stocks, ETFs and options, and the app also holds chequing, savings, crypto and managed accounts, so you can move cash between them without leaving it. Signing in with a passkey brings a guarantee: passkey users are reimbursed for all direct losses from unauthorized activity.

Active Trader status. Clients who place 50 or more manual trades in a calendar month become Active Traders, with a one-month grace period if activity drops. Active Traders get phone trades at no charge (normally $45), phone pickup in under a minute, email replies within four hours, early access to beta features and lower crypto fees, from 0.5% down to 0% by volume. They can also use Trade Plus, a charting and execution tool tied to Active Trader status; Wealthsimple shows no price for it.

Support by tier. Core clients get standard support, Premium clients get priority phone support, and Generation clients get priority phone and priority email support, with 24/7 chat also listed for Generation.

What our platform score counts. Our platform test gives a point each for fractional shares, DRIP, options, an advanced trading platform and a mobile app. Wealthsimple earns four of the five; it does not receive the advanced-platform point. On the separate beginner test it earns a perfect 5.0, which reflects how simple the app is for a first-time investor.

Who may want more. Traders who want multi-monitor charting, complex order types or detailed market depth for every client may prefer a broker built around a desktop platform. Interactive Brokers Canada offers Trader Workstation, IBKR Desktop and IBKR Mobile, and Questrade names Questrade Pro as its active trading platform.

10. Transfers In and Out

Transferring out costs $0. Wealthsimple charges nothing for outgoing account transfers to another institution. By contrast, Questrade charges $150 for a full or partial transfer out. The one practical issue is fractional shares, which cannot be transferred, so sell them or round them up first.

Transfer fee coverage. When you transfer $25,000 or more into Wealthsimple, it covers the transfer-out fee your old institution charges, up to $150 per account, as long as the account stays funded for 90 days. The threshold was $15,000 before April 10, 2025. Wealthsimple's own pages differ on whether the reimbursement is automatic or needs proof, so please keep the statement that shows your old institution's fee.

The 1% transfer match. Wealthsimple pays 1% of qualifying transfers of $25,000 or more made within 30 days of registering, on up to $2,000,000, for a maximum of $20,000. It is paid in 24 equal monthly payments into chequing. It applies to transfers into self-directed, managed and crypto accounts, covering the TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, non-registered and corporate account types. The match has run since May 4, 2026, with no end date; a separate 0.2% boost on amounts above $2,000,000 expired on August 31, 2026.

11. Is Wealthsimple Trade Safe? CIRO and CIPF

The dealer. Self-directed accounts are held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., an investment dealer that is a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) and of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF). Both memberships are stated, which is why Wealthsimple scores 4.0 on our safety test, the most a broker can get without bank ownership.

What CIPF covers. If a member firm becomes insolvent, CIPF protects the cash and securities it holds for you up to $1,000,000 in each of three categories: general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA), retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF) and RESPs. It does not cover a drop in market value, and it does not cover crypto assets.

Ownership and scale. As of December 18, 2023, subsidiaries and affiliates of Power Corporation of Canada controlled a majority of the voting securities of Wealthsimple Financial Corp., the dealer's parent. Wealthsimple reported $155.6 billion in assets under administration at June 30, 2026. It also reports SOC 1 and SOC 2 compliance.

What protects you from fraud. CIPF steps in only if the firm itself fails. For unauthorized activity in your account, Wealthsimple's own guarantee applies to clients who sign in with a passkey: they are reimbursed for all direct losses. Turning on a passkey is a simple step that brings you under that guarantee. Our Is Wealthsimple safe? guide covers chequing, savings and crypto protection too.

10 Strategies to Keep Wealthsimple Trading Costs Low

1. Convert once, trade many times

Every round trip through a Canadian-dollar account costs 1.5% twice. Holding US dollars in a USD account means proceeds and dividends stay in US dollars, so you pay to convert only when you first move money in.

2. Size conversions to the bands

The USD account charges 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000 per transaction. A conversion of $24,999.99 costs $250.00; one of $25,000 costs $125.00.

3. Use Canadian listings where they fit

Canadian-listed ETFs trade in Canadian dollars with no conversion at all. Compare a fund's expense ratio with the 1.5% each way you would pay to hold a US listing.

4. Keep US dividends in US dollars

In a Canadian-dollar account, every US dividend is converted at 1.5% before you get it or reinvest it. On US$1,000 a year, that is $15.00 that a USD account avoids.

5. Let options expire or auto-exercise

An early exercise or do-not-exercise instruction costs US$45. If your plan does not need one, letting the normal expiry process run avoids that charge.

6. Trade online, not by phone

Phone trades cost $45 unless you are an Active Trader. Placing the same order in the app or web platform costs $0.

7. Borrow at a higher tier

Margin falls 0.5% at Premium and again at Generation. On a $20,000 margin balance for a year, Core costs $990.00 and Premium $890.00, a $100.00 saving.

8. Move $25,000 or more at once

Transfers of $25,000 or more get your old institution's fee covered, up to $150 per account. Moving an account in one transfer, rather than in smaller partial transfers, keeps the transfer above the threshold.

9. Avoid instant withdrawals

Instant withdrawals to a debit card cost 2.5%, or $25.00 on $1,000. A standard withdrawal to your bank avoids that fee, so plan cash needs a few days ahead.

10. Sell fractions before you leave

Fractional shares cannot be transferred out. Selling them or rounding them up to whole shares before a transfer avoids a stuck position and a second trip.

Illustrative Example: Three Ways to Buy US Stocks

This example is hypothetical. Arjun is a Core client with a non-registered account. This year he puts $20,000 into US-listed stocks, later sells $5,000 of them and receives $400 of US dividends. He holds the rest. We count US-dollar amounts at par for simplicity and leave out the stock price changes.

  1. Route A, buying from a Canadian-dollar account: conversion in ($20,000 x 1.5% = $300.00), conversion of the sale ($5,000 x 1.5% = $75.00) and of the dividends ($400 x 1.5% = $6.00), for ($300.00 + $75.00 + $6.00 = $381.00).
  2. Route B, a USD account with one $20,000 conversion: conversion ($20,000 x 1.0% = $200.00) plus the Core USD account fee ($10 x 12 = $120.00), for ($200.00 + $120.00 = $320.00). The sale and dividends stay in US dollars.
  3. Saving of Route B over Route A: ($381.00 - $320.00 = $61.00).
  4. Route C, a USD account with one $25,000 conversion (keeping $5,000 in US dollars for later): conversion ($25,000 x 0.5% = $125.00) plus the fee ($120.00), for ($125.00 + $120.00 = $245.00).
  5. Saving of Route C over Route A: ($381.00 - $245.00 = $136.00).

At Premium, the $120.00 fee drops to $0.00, so Route B would cost $200.00 and Route C $125.00. The plus-tax part of the USD account fee is left out here; add your province's sales tax for the exact figure.
