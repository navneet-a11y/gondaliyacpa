1. EQ Bank Review: Our Verdict

The short version. The EQ Bank Personal Account is the top-scoring account in our 2026 chequing ranking at 4.6 / 5. It charges nothing to hold, pays interest on every dollar and does not cap how often you move money. It scores the maximum 5.0 on fees, transactions and ATM access. Only two things hold it back: its interest sub-score and the lack of cheques.

Where it earns its score. The fee test carries 35% of the score, and EQ passes it outright with a $0 monthly fee and no balance condition. The contrast with big-bank accounts is sharp. TD Unlimited Chequing costs $17.95 a month, or $215.40 a year, unless you keep $4,000 in it every day. RBC Signature No Limit Banking costs $16.95 a month, or $203.40 a year, with no balance waiver at all. EQ asks for neither.

Where it loses points. EQ's headline 2.75% depends on a pay direct deposit. Our rubric counts a direct-deposit bonus at half its extra value, so EQ is scored at 1.875% (1.00% + 1.75% ÷ 2), which gives an interest sub-score of 3.81. It also scores 4.0 rather than 5.0 on protection and features because the account does not offer cheques.

The rate question. Without a pay deposit, 1.00% trails Wealthsimple Chequing's Core rate of 1.25% and Manulife Bank's Advantage Account at 1.50%. It is also well below the best everyday savings rates in our register, such as Oaken Financial at 2.80%. If you cannot move your pay, EQ's 30 Day Notice Savings account at 2.75% is the way to get its top rate.

A caution on GICs. EQ Bank sells GICs from a $100 minimum, but three of its own pages showed three different sets of GIC rates on October 22, 2026. We are confirming the correct rates with EQ and do not print them here. Treat any EQ GIC rate you see as unconfirmed until it appears in your own account at the time you buy.

2. EQ Bank Personal Account: Rates and How the 2.75% Works

Two rates in one account. The EQ Bank Personal Account pays a base rate of 1.00% on your whole balance. When you direct deposit your pay, EQ adds a 1.75% bonus, for 2.75% in total. Both rates took effect May 15, 2026, and were current when we checked on October 22, 2026. There is no promotional rate that expires after a few months: the 2.75% is the everyday rate for people who qualify.

What the gap is worth. On $10,000 kept for a year with no change in rate, the base rate earns $100.00 ($10,000 x 1.00%) and the full rate earns $275.00 ($10,000 x 2.75%). That is $175.00 more for moving your pay. On $25,000 the gap grows to $437.50 ($25,000 x 1.75%). The bonus is a large part of the account's value, so check that you can meet it before you switch.

What counts as a pay deposit. EQ ties the bonus to pay that is direct deposited into the account. The minimum amount, how often it must arrive and whether pension or government deposits count were not part of the figures we checked. Please confirm the exact qualifying rule with EQ before you rely on the 2.75%, and keep the confirmation with your records.

Rates move with the market. The Bank of Canada's policy rate has been 2.25% since October 30, 2025, and the big banks' prime rate has been 4.45% since the same date. The remaining 2026 rate announcements are on October 28 and December 9. Savings rates at online banks are variable and can change after a policy decision, so treat any rate as true on the date shown.

3. EQ Bank Fees: What Costs $0 and What Still Costs Money

The everyday account. The Personal Account has a $0 monthly fee and no minimum balance, so there is no balance to track to avoid a charge. EQ publishes unlimited transactions at no charge and unlimited Interac e-Transfers at no charge. That puts it among the eight $0-monthly-fee accounts in our chequing ranking, and it scores highest of them.

ATM withdrawals. EQ has no ATM network of its own. Instead, it reimburses ATM fees when you withdraw cash with the EQ Bank Card at any ATM in Canada. You may still see the operator's fee at the machine; EQ pays it back. The figures we checked cover ATMs in Canada only, so ask EQ how withdrawals outside Canada are treated before you travel.

What can still cost money. International transfers from the US Dollar Account go through Wise, and Wise charges its own fee. Currency conversion between Canadian and US dollars uses EQ's exchange rate on the transfer date, and any gap between that rate and the market rate is a cost even though no separate fee appears. EQ's charges for items such as returned payments or stopped payments were not part of the figures we checked, so ask for EQ's full fee schedule if you expect to use those services.

Keeping $4,000 idle every day to avoid a fee is itself a cost: that $4,000 earns 0.000% at TD Unlimited, while it would earn $110.00 a year at EQ's 2.75% ($4,000 x 2.75%).

4. EQ Bank Notice Savings: 10 Day and 30 Day Accounts

Two notice accounts. Alongside the Personal Account, EQ offers 30 Day Notice Savings at 2.75% and 10 Day Notice Savings at 2.35%, both effective May 15, 2026. There is no minimum balance and no monthly fee. EQ lists no promotional rate on either account.

How a notice account works. With a notice account you tell the bank in advance that you want to withdraw. The money becomes available once the notice period ends: 10 days or 30 days here. In return for that wait, the bank pays more than its base rate. This makes notice savings a middle ground between an everyday account and a GIC: your money is not locked for a full term, but it is not instant either.

What the rates earn. On $20,000 held for a year at unchanged rates, 30 Day Notice Savings earns $550.00 ($20,000 x 2.75%), 10 Day Notice Savings earns $470.00 ($20,000 x 2.35%) and the Personal Account at its base rate earns $200.00 ($20,000 x 1.00%). For someone who cannot direct deposit their pay, the 30-day account is the only way to earn EQ's top rate.

When notice savings fits. Notice savings suits money you will not need on short notice: the second layer of an emergency fund, a tax bill due in several months, or cash waiting for a home purchase. It does not suit money for rent and bills. If you might need cash within days, keep that portion in the Personal Account and the rest in notice savings.

How it compares. Without any notice period, Oaken Financial pays 2.80% (rates as of October 16, 2026), Saven Financial pays 2.85% and Wealthsimple Savings pays 2.5% with no withdrawal limits or notice. Saven is covered by Ontario's credit union insurer rather than CDIC, and Wealthsimple Savings is covered by CIPF rather than CDIC. 5. TFSA, RRSP and FHSA Accounts at EQ Bank

What EQ offers. EQ Bank offers TFSA, RRSP and FHSA versions of its accounts. Cash held in an EQ TFSA, RRSP or FHSA savings account earns 1.50% (effective May 15, 2026). You can also hold EQ GICs inside each of these accounts; EQ's registered GIC tables repeat its regular GIC rates, which are being confirmed (see section 6).

2026 limits. The 2026 TFSA contribution limit is $7,000, and someone who has been 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009 and never contributed has $109,000 of room. The 2026 RRSP dollar limit is $33,810, and your own limit is the lesser of 18% of last year's earned income and that dollar amount, adjusted for pension adjustments and unused room. The FHSA allows $8,000 a year to a $40,000 lifetime maximum.

What 1.50% earns. A full 2026 TFSA contribution of $7,000 in EQ's TFSA cash savings earns $105.00 in a year ($7,000 x 1.50%). The same $7,000 earns $199.50 at Saven Financial's 2.85% registered savings rate, or $262.50 in a 1-year GIC at Oaken Financial's 3.75% ($7,000 x 3.75%). For cash you plan to leave in a TFSA, EQ's registered savings rate is not its strongest product.

Moving registered money. Use a direct transfer between institutions. A direct transfer from one TFSA to another does not affect your room. If you withdraw and redeposit yourself, the deposit counts as a new contribution, and the withdrawn amount only comes back as room on January 1 of the next year. Excess TFSA contributions are taxed at 1% a month. Our TFSA withdrawal rules guide covers the timing.

Separate CDIC cover. CDIC insures TFSAs, RRSPs and FHSAs as separate categories, each up to $100,000 at one member institution. So registered deposits at EQ have their own cover on top of the $100,000 for deposits in your own name.

9. Is EQ Bank Safe? CDIC Coverage Explained

Who stands behind EQ. EQ Bank is a trade name of Equitable Bank, and Equitable Bank is a member of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation. If a member institution fails, CDIC repays eligible deposits up to its limits. You do not need to apply or pay for this cover; it comes with eligible deposits at a member.

How much is covered. CDIC insures eligible deposits up to $100,000 per insured category at each member institution, and the limit includes both principal and interest. The categories are: deposits in one name, joint deposits, deposits held in trust, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, RESPs, RDSPs and FHSAs. Each category is insured separately.

What that means in practice. A single person at EQ with $100,000 in their own name, $100,000 in a TFSA and $100,000 in an RRSP could have up to $300,000 insured at EQ, because each sits in a different category. A joint account with a spouse would be a fourth category. Balances above $100,000 in one category are not insured, so savers with more should spread money across categories or institutions.

What CDIC covers and does not. CDIC covers deposits in Canadian or foreign currency, GICs and term deposits. It does not cover mutual funds, stocks, bonds, ETFs or crypto assets. EQ's GICs are eligible for CDIC protection.

10. How EQ Bank Interest Is Taxed

Interest is fully taxable. Interest from the Personal Account, notice savings, the US Dollar Account and non-registered GICs is taxable every year. All of it is included in income, so it is taxed at your full marginal rate. Banks report interest on a T5 slip, and you must report all interest you earn even if no slip arrives.

A worked rate. In Ontario, someone with $80,000 of taxable income in 2026 pays 20.5% federal and 9.15% Ontario tax on the next dollar, or 29.65% on extra interest. At that rate, $275.00 of EQ interest costs $81.54 in tax ($275.00 x 29.65%) and leaves $193.46. The same interest inside a TFSA leaves the full $275.00.

GICs longer than a year. For a non-registered GIC that pays interest only at maturity, you still report the interest that builds up each year, not just in the year it is paid. So a 5-year GIC creates taxable income every year of its term.

US dollar interest. Interest earned in the US Dollar Account is taxable in Canada and must be reported in Canadian dollars on your return.

Effect on benefits. Interest raises your net income, and net income drives several benefits. For the 2026 income year, the Old Age Security recovery tax takes 15% of net income above $95,323. For the July 2026 to June 2027 benefit year, the Canada Child Benefit starts to shrink at an adjusted family net income of $38,237, with a second reduction step at $82,847. Interest earned inside a TFSA does not count toward net income. Please check how extra interest affects your own benefits before you move a large sum into a taxable account.

Registered accounts. In an RRSP or FHSA, interest grows without yearly tax; contributions are deductible and withdrawals are taxed, except qualifying FHSA withdrawals for a first home. See our TFSA vs RRSP comparison for which shelter fits which goal.

11. Limits and Gaps to Know Before You Switch

No pay deposit, low rate. The single biggest catch is the rate split. Self-employed people, and anyone whose pay cannot be sent to EQ, start at 1.00%. On $10,000 that is $100.00 a year instead of $275.00. If that is you, plan to use 30 Day Notice Savings for most of your balance, or compare other high-rate savings accounts.

No cheque writing. The account takes mobile cheque deposits but does not issue cheques. Most people now pay bills online, but rent, some daycares and some offices still want a cheque. Keep a second account for that if you need it.

No welcome bonus. EQ states no welcome offer on its Personal Account. Some rivals pay to switch: Simplii offers $350 to new clients who set up a direct deposit of $100 or more for four straight months (offer ends January 31, 2027), and Tangerine offers $250 for moving a payroll deposit (offer ends October 31, 2026). A bonus is a one-time payment, while a rate gap repeats every year, so weigh both.

GIC rates in doubt. Until EQ's GIC rates are confirmed, you cannot compare them with confidence. Buy only at a rate you can see and save in your account at the time of purchase.

Online only. EQ is an online bank, so account opening and everyday banking happen in the app or online rather than at a counter. If you value walking into a branch for a bank draft or a signature, a big-bank account may suit you better.

Registered savings rate. Cash in an EQ TFSA, RRSP or FHSA earns 1.50%, well below the 2.75% in notice savings. For registered cash, a GIC or a higher-rate registered savings account elsewhere can pay more.

Details we could not confirm. We could not confirm the minimum balance on the US Dollar Account, the coverage wording for US dollar deposits, or card features beyond ATM access. We leave those out rather than guess, and we will add them once they are confirmed. See our editorial standards for how we handle unconfirmed figures.

Illustrative Example: One Year of Interest at EQ Bank

This example is hypothetical. Priya lives in Ontario and has $80,000 of taxable income in 2026, so her marginal rate on interest is 29.65% (20.5% federal plus 9.15% Ontario). She keeps $6,000 in the Personal Account with her pay direct deposited, $15,000 in 30 Day Notice Savings and $7,000 in an EQ TFSA cash savings account. We assume rates and balances stay the same for 12 months and ignore compounding.

  1. Personal Account interest: ($6,000 x 2.75% = $165.00).
  2. 30 Day Notice Savings interest: ($15,000 x 2.75% = $412.50).
  3. TFSA cash savings interest, tax-free: ($7,000 x 1.50% = $105.00).

  5. Tax on that interest: ($577.50 x 29.65% = $171.23), leaving ($577.50 - $171.23 = $406.27).
  6. Total kept after tax: ($406.27 + $105.00 = $511.27).
  7. Without the pay deposit, the Personal Account would earn ($6,000 x 1.00% = $60.00), which is ($165.00 - $60.00 = $105.00) less before tax.
