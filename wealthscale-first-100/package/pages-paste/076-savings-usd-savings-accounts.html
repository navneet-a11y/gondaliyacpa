2. Why Hold US Dollars in Canada

You earn or receive US dollars. If you are paid in US dollars, receive US pension or rental income, or sell US investments, a USD account lets you keep that money in US dollars instead of converting it at once. You choose when to convert, rather than accepting whatever exchange rate applies on the day it arrives.

You spend US dollars. Snowbirds, frequent travellers and people with US bills or property can pay those costs from US dollars they already hold, which avoids converting twice: once into Canadian dollars when the money arrives and again into US dollars when you spend it.

You invest in US stocks. Holding US dollars can reduce conversion costs when you buy US-listed investments, although savings accounts and brokerage accounts are different products. Our guide on how to buy US stocks in Canada covers the investing side, and best brokers for US stocks ranks the platforms.

What a USD account is not for. Converting Canadian dollars only to earn interest rarely makes sense. The best no-fee Canadian dollar savings rate on October 22, 2026 was 2.85%, above the best USD savings rate of 2.50%, and every conversion has a cost. On $10,000, the gap alone is $35 a year before any conversion cost.

Currency moves cut both ways. If you hold US dollars and later convert them back, a change in the exchange rate can add to or subtract from your result, often by more than a year of interest. A USD account suits money you expect to spend or invest in US dollars, not a bet on the exchange rate.

6. Currency Conversion: The Cost That Matters Most

Every conversion has a cost. When you turn Canadian dollars into US dollars, you pay either a stated fee or a spread built into the exchange rate, and often you pay again when you convert back. On a USD savings account, that cost can be larger than a year's interest.

A published example. Wealthsimple publishes tiered fees for converting between Canadian and US dollars in its USD account: 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000 to $24,999.99, 0.5% from $25,000 to $99,999.99 and 0% from $100,000. The fee depends on the size of each conversion, not on your membership tier. The USD account itself costs Core clients $10 plus tax a month and is included for Premium and Generation clients. 7. Deposit Insurance for US Dollar Deposits

CDIC covers foreign-currency deposits. CDIC insures eligible deposits in Canadian or foreign currency, including GICs and term deposits, at member banks and trust companies. The limit is $100,000 per insured category at each member institution, including interest. The categories are deposits in one name, joint deposits, deposits held in trust, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, RESPs, RDSPs and FHSAs.

The limit is in Canadian dollars. CDIC's $100,000 limit is a Canadian dollar amount, so a US dollar balance counts at its Canadian dollar value. When the US dollar is worth more than the Canadian dollar, a balance below US$100,000 can be above the limit. At an exchange rate of 1.35, assumed here only for illustration, US$74,074.07 is worth C$99,999.99 (US$74,074.07 x 1.35). Keep a margin below the limit if your US dollar balance is large.

What the account pages said. None of the USD account pages we checked stated deposit insurance for the USD account itself: EQ Bank, RBC, Manulife Bank, National Bank, Scotiabank and TD. All six institutions are CDIC members. Please confirm in the account agreement that your USD account is an eligible deposit before you rely on CDIC cover.

Wealthsimple. Wealthsimple's pages did not state CIPF or CDIC cover for its USD savings accounts when we checked. Its Canadian dollar Savings account is protected by CIPF up to $1,000,000, not by CDIC, so do not assume the USD version is CDIC-insured. Our guide is Wealthsimple safe explains its protection by product.

US dollars at an investment dealer. Cash held at a CIPF member investment dealer is protected by CIPF if the dealer fails: $1,000,000 for general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA) and a separate $1,000,000 for retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF). CIPF does not cover market losses or crypto assets.

Credit unions and other savers. Saven Financial, a credit union division covered by DICO, and Alterna Bank, a CDIC member, list no US dollar account, so their insurance does not come into play here.

8. Tax on US Dollar Interest

Taxed in Canada, reported in Canadian dollars. Interest on a US dollar account held by a Canadian resident is taxable income in Canada, at your marginal rate, like interest on a Canadian dollar account. You report it in Canadian dollars, converting with the exchange rate on the day you received it, or an average rate for the year when interest is paid regularly. Use the same method each year.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. US$250.00 of interest at an assumed exchange rate of 1.35 is C$337.50 (US$250.00 x 1.35). At a 29.65% marginal rate, which applies at $80,000 of taxable income in Ontario in 2026, the tax is C$100.07 (C$337.50 x 29.65%), and you keep C$237.43. Our tax on savings interest table shows marginal rates in nine provinces.

Currency gains and losses. When you convert US dollars back into Canadian dollars, a change in the exchange rate since you acquired them can produce a foreign exchange gain or loss. For individuals, a small amount of net currency gain each year is exempt, and the rest is treated as a capital gain or loss. Keep a record of the exchange rate each time money goes into the account.

No US withholding on Canadian bank interest. US withholding tax, 15% under the Canada-US tax treaty for US dividends when your broker has your W-8BEN, applies to dividends from US companies. It does not apply to interest a Canadian institution pays on your USD deposit, which is Canadian income.

Registered accounts. None of the banks we checked published a US dollar savings rate for a TFSA or RRSP. If you hold US dollars inside a registered plan, interest is not taxed while it stays in the plan. For US investments in a TFSA, see US stocks in a TFSA.

10. How to Choose a USD Savings Account

Start with what the US dollars are for. Spending within months points to an account with easy withdrawals. Money held for a year or more can earn more in a term deposit. Money for US investments may belong in a brokerage account.

Compare the rate after fees at your balance. National Bank pays nothing below $5,000. Scotiabank charges US$1 a month below a US$200 daily balance. RBC charges $3.00 for each debit after the first each month. Work out what you would earn and pay for your own balance and withdrawal pattern.

Count the conversion cost both ways. If you need to convert Canadian dollars to fund the account, the conversion may cost more than the interest (section 6). An account is worth most to people who already have US dollars.

Check how money moves in and out. RBC's U.S. High Interest eSavings has no Interac e-Transfer and no ATM access. EQ sends international transfers through Wise. Make sure the account can send money where you need it, at a cost you accept.

Confirm insurance in the agreement. The USD account pages we checked did not state their coverage, even though the institutions are CDIC members. Remember that the limit is in Canadian dollars.

11. How to Open a USD Account and Fund It

What you will be asked for. Expect to give your legal name, date of birth, Canadian address and Social Insurance Number, because the institution reports your interest to the CRA, and to verify your identity with government photo ID.

Funding with US dollars you already hold. If you receive US dollars, have them paid straight into the USD account where the provider allows it, or move them from another USD account. Wealthsimple notes that direct deposit into its USD accounts is not yet available.

Funding by converting Canadian dollars. If you convert, compare the exchange rate or fee before you move money. EQ converts at its own rate on the transfer date. At providers with tiered fees, one larger conversion may cost less than several small ones.

Sending US dollars out. Check the route before you need it. EQ uses Wise for international transfers, with Wise's fee. RBC's U.S. High Interest eSavings allows one debit a month before charging $3.00 and has no Interac e-Transfer, so plan withdrawals around those limits.

Keep records for tax. Note the exchange rate on the day each deposit goes in and each conversion comes out. You will need those rates to report interest in Canadian dollars and to work out any currency gain or loss when you convert back.

12. Common Mistakes With USD Savings Accounts

Treating RBC's 4.00% as a savings rate. It is a 1-year term deposit special. Fix: compare it with other fixed-term options, and compare savings accounts with EQ's 2.50% and the big-bank rates in our table.

Leaving large US dollar balances at near-zero rates. US$10,000 at TD's 0.010% earns US$1.00 a year; at EQ's 2.50% it earns US$250.00. Fix: move idle US dollars to the highest rate you can use, and keep only what you spend in a low-rate account.

Ignoring the Canadian dollar value of the CDIC limit. Fix: value your USD balance in Canadian dollars and keep each category below $100,000 with a margin for interest and exchange-rate moves.

Assuming every USD account is CDIC-insured. Wealthsimple's pages did not state cover for USD savings, and brokerage cash is CIPF-protected, not CDIC. Fix: confirm the protection for the specific account before you deposit.

Reporting US dollar interest as if it were Canadian dollars. Fix: convert each interest amount at the rate on the day received, or a yearly average, and report the Canadian dollar total.

Paying withdrawal fees on a low-rate account. Fix: batch withdrawals, or use a chequing account for US dollars you spend often.

How It Works: Six Steps to a Better USD Rate

  1. Check what your US dollars earn now. Find the rate on your statement. At 0.05% or less, US$10,000 earns US$5.00 a year or less.
  2. Decide how much you will spend soon. Keep that amount where you can reach it easily, and treat the rest as savings.
  3. Compare rates, fees and debit limits. Use the table above for your balance and your number of withdrawals.
  4. Confirm insurance and transfer routes. Read the agreement for CDIC eligibility and check how the account sends and receives US dollars.
  5. Move US dollars without converting where you can. Transfer existing US dollars; convert Canadian dollars only when you need US dollars anyway.
  6. Record rates for tax. Keep the exchange rate for each deposit and each interest payment, and report interest in Canadian dollars.

10 Strategies for US Dollar Savings

1. Keep US dollars in US dollars

If you earn and spend US dollars, holding them in a USD account avoids paying a conversion cost twice. The interest is a bonus on top of the conversion you did not have to make.

2. Move idle US dollars to the top rate

On US$10,000, EQ's 2.50% pays US$249.00 a year more than TD's 0.010% (US$250.00 minus US$1.00). Keep a small amount at your main bank for spending and move the rest.

3. Lock what you will not need for a year

RBC's 4.00% 1-year USD term deposit special pays US$400.00 on US$10,000, US$150.00 more than EQ's variable 2.50%. It fits only money you are sure you will not need before the term ends.

4. Convert in fewer, larger amounts

Where fees are tiered, size matters. At Wealthsimple's published rates, converting $10,000 costs $100.00, while $9,999.99 costs $150.00 and two conversions of $5,000 cost $150.00 together.

5. Use Norbert's Gambit for large conversions

For large amounts, converting through a security listed in both currencies at a brokerage can cost less than a bank's exchange rate. It takes more steps; our Norbert's Gambit guide walks through them.

6. Match the account to your withdrawals

If you withdraw once a month or less, RBC's or Scotiabank's debit limits cost nothing. If you withdraw weekly, a USD chequing account such as TD's avoids per-debit fees.

7. Keep an exchange-rate log

Write down the rate on the day each deposit goes in and each conversion comes out. It makes reporting interest in Canadian dollars simple and lets you work out any currency gain or loss.

8. Value your balance in Canadian dollars

Check your USD balance against CDIC's Canadian dollar limit at least once a year, and after any big move in the exchange rate, so a growing balance does not drift past $100,000 in one category.

9. Compare with Canadian dollar rates first

The best no-fee Canadian dollar savings rate (2.85%) was higher than the best USD rate (2.50%) on October 22, 2026. Unless you need US dollars, Canadian dollar savings may pay more and avoid conversion entirely.

10. Recheck after rate decisions

The Bank of Canada sets the Canadian policy rate (2.25%), with decisions on October 28 and December 9, 2026. USD savings rates are set by each provider, so check yours a few times a year whatever the Bank of Canada does.

Illustrative Example: US$20,000 Held for Winters in Arizona

This example is hypothetical. Daniel lives in Ontario, has $80,000 of taxable income, and keeps US$20,000 he earned from US work to spend on winters in the US. It sits in a big-bank USD account paying up to 0.05%. For the tax steps, assume an exchange rate of 1.35; this is for illustration only and is not a current rate.

  1. Today. At up to 0.05%, his US$20,000 earns at most US$10.00 a year (US$20,000 x 0.05%). His balance stays above US$200, so the US$1 monthly fee is waived.
  2. At 2.50%. In the EQ Bank US Dollar Account, the same US$20,000 earns US$500.00 a year (US$20,000 x 2.50%).
  3. Tax on the new interest. US$500.00 is C$675.00 (US$500.00 x 1.35). At his 29.65% marginal rate, tax is C$200.14 (C$675.00 x 29.65%), so he keeps C$474.86.
  4. Tax on the old interest. US$10.00 is C$13.50; tax is C$4.00, so he kept C$9.50. After tax, the move is worth C$465.36 a year (C$474.86 minus C$9.50).
  5. Conversion avoided. Because he spends the money in US dollars, he converts nothing. Had he converted US$20,000 into Canadian dollars and back at a 1.5% fee each way, the first conversion alone would have cost US$300.00 (US$20,000 x 1.5%), more than half of a year's interest at 2.50%.
  6. Insurance check. US$20,000 is C$27,000.00 at 1.35 (US$20,000 x 1.35), well inside CDIC's $100,000 limit for his single-name category, provided the agreement confirms the USD account is an eligible deposit.
  7. A fixed-rate option. If he will not need US$10,000 of it until the winter after next, RBC's 4.00% 1-year special would pay US$400.00 on that part, against US$250.00 at 2.50%.

USD Savings Account FAQ

What is the best USD savings account in Canada?

On October 22, 2026, the highest US dollar savings rate we confirmed was 2.50% at the EQ Bank US Dollar Account, effective May 15, 2026, with no everyday banking fees. The next best rates were RBC at 0.250%, Manulife Bank at 0.20% and National Bank at 0.100% on US$10,000. On US$10,000, EQ pays US$250.00 a year and RBC US$25.00.

Do the big banks pay interest on US dollar savings?

Very little. RBC's U.S. High Interest eSavings showed 0.250%, National Bank's USD Progress Account 0.100% from US$10,000, Scotiabank's U.S. Dollar Daily Interest Account up to 0.05% and TD's U.S. Daily Interest Chequing Account 0.010%. BMO, CIBC and Tangerine showed no USD rate on their pages when we checked on October 22, 2026.

Is a US dollar account covered by CDIC?

CDIC covers eligible deposits in Canadian or foreign currency at member institutions, up to $100,000 per insured category at each member, including interest. The limit is in Canadian dollars, so a USD balance counts at its Canadian dollar value. The USD account pages we checked did not state their own coverage, so confirm eligibility in the account agreement.

Is RBC's 4.00% US dollar rate a savings account?

No. RBC's 4.00% is a limited-time special on a 1-year U.S. Dollar Term Deposit, so your money is committed for the year. RBC's US dollar savings account, U.S. High Interest eSavings, showed 0.250%. On US$10,000, the term deposit pays US$400.00 for the year, against US$250.00 in EQ Bank's 2.50% savings account.

Does Wealthsimple have a USD savings account?

Yes. Wealthsimple offers up to eight USD savings accounts with no monthly fee and no minimum, and direct deposit into USD is not yet available. Its USD page and its help article showed different rates on October 22, 2026, so we have not listed one. Its pages also did not state CIPF or CDIC cover for USD savings.

How is interest on a US dollar account taxed in Canada?

It is taxable income in Canada at your marginal rate, reported in Canadian dollars. Convert each amount at the exchange rate on the day you received it, or an average rate for the year if interest is paid regularly. At an assumed rate of 1.35, US$250 of interest is C$337.50, and at 29.65% the tax is C$100.07.

How much does it cost to convert Canadian dollars to US dollars?

It depends on the provider. Wealthsimple publishes fees of 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000 for conversions in its USD account. EQ Bank converts at its own exchange rate on the transfer date. A 1.5% fee each way equals 1.2 years of interest at 2.50%.

What fees do USD accounts charge?

EQ Bank states no everyday banking fees. RBC's U.S. High Interest eSavings includes one debit a month, then charges $3.00 each. Scotiabank charges US$1 a month unless you keep a US$200 daily balance, with two debits included and US$1.25 for each extra one. TD's U.S. Daily Interest Chequing Account has a $0 monthly fee.

Should I convert Canadian dollars to earn a USD savings rate?

Usually it does not pay on its own. The best no-fee Canadian dollar savings rate was 2.85% on October 22, 2026, higher than the best USD rate of 2.50%, and every conversion has a cost, often charged again when you convert back. A USD account suits US dollars you already have or will spend, not a conversion made only for interest.

What happens to my USD savings when the exchange rate moves?

Your US dollar balance does not change, but its Canadian dollar value does. When you convert back, you may have a currency gain or loss; for individuals, a small amount of net gain each year is exempt and the rest is treated as a capital gain or loss. A move in the exchange rate can outweigh a year of interest.

Which USD account is best for frequent withdrawals?

A US dollar chequing account suits frequent withdrawals better than a savings account. TD's U.S. Daily Interest Chequing Account has a $0 monthly fee but pays 0.010%. RBC's U.S. High Interest eSavings allows one debit a month before charging $3.00, and Scotiabank's U.S. Dollar Daily Interest Account allows two before charging US$1.25 each.

Does the Bank of Canada rate affect USD savings rates?

Not directly. The Bank of Canada sets the Canadian policy rate, 2.25% since October 30, 2025, with decisions left on October 28 and December 9, 2026. USD savings rates are set by each provider. TD's US prime rate was 7.00% while its U.S. Daily Interest Chequing Account paid 0.010%, so USD savings rates do not simply follow US lending rates either.
