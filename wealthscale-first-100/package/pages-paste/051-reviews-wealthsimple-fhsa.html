2. The Four Wealthsimple FHSA Versions

One account type, four ways to run it. Wealthsimple offers the Wealthsimple first home savings account in four versions. Each is a real FHSA with the same CRA rules, but they differ in who chooses the investments, what you pay and how the account is protected.

Self-directed. The self-directed FHSA gives you access to 14,000+ stocks and ETFs, with $0 commission on listed Canadian and US securities. You can buy fractional shares from $1, set recurring investments and turn on dividend reinvestment. You can also trade options: in a registered account like an FHSA, Wealthsimple allows buying calls and puts, selling cash-secured puts and selling covered calls.

Managed. The managed FHSA puts your money in a Wealthsimple portfolio. A Classic portfolio holds 8 to 10 ETFs, rebalances automatically when you deposit, withdraw or change your goal, and reinvests dividends into the parts of the portfolio that are underweight. About 0.3% to 0.5% of the account is held as a cash buffer, and deposits are invested within 5 business days.

Automated Investing. Automated Investing costs 0.25% a year, capped once the balance reaches $100,000 at $250 per account per year. Wealthsimple lists the FHSA among its eligible accounts. Because the FHSA's lifetime contribution limit is $40,000, most FHSAs will pay the percentage fee rather than reach the cap.

Savings. Wealthsimple's FHSA page offers a savings version. Its pages disagree on the rate, so we do not print one. It is covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per beneficiary in the FHSA category and is not protected by CIPF, unlike the investing versions.

What is not allowed. Crypto cannot be held in a Wealthsimple FHSA. An FHSA cannot be used as collateral for Wealthsimple's portfolio line of credit. Wealthsimple has not stated whether its Private Market Fund can be held in an FHSA.

How the costs are worked out. On $16,000, two years of full contributions, the Core management fee is $80.00 ($16,000 x 0.5%) and Classic fund costs at 0.15% add $24.00 ($16,000 x 0.15%). On $40,000, the full lifetime limit, the fee is $200.00 ($40,000 x 0.5%) and fund costs add $60.00 ($40,000 x 0.15%). On Premium, the fee falls to 0.4%: $64.00 on $16,000 and $160.00 on $40,000.

When managed makes sense. A managed FHSA suits a buyer who wants a diversified portfolio without choosing funds, does not want to rebalance and values the discipline of automatic deposits. The fee buys simplicity, and on an account that tops out at $40,000 of contributions it is a modest dollar amount.

When self-directed makes sense. A self-directed FHSA suits a buyer who is comfortable buying a small number of Canadian-listed ETFs and checking them once or twice a year. It also suits a buyer whose purchase is close and who wants to hold cash or short-term holdings without paying a management fee. Tax-loss harvesting does not apply. Wealthsimple offers tax-loss harvesting on managed accounts for Premium and Generation clients, but it has no use inside an FHSA, where gains are not taxed as they occur. The feature matters only in non-registered accounts.

6. US Dollars and US Stocks in a Wealthsimple FHSA

USD is allowed. Wealthsimple lets you add a USD account to an FHSA. That is unusual: National Bank Direct Brokerage, Qtrade and TD Easy Trade keep the FHSA in Canadian dollars only. With a USD side, you can buy and sell US-listed securities without paying the 1.5% conversion fee on each trade.

What it costs. The USD account costs $10 a month plus tax on Core, or $120 a year before tax, and is included on Premium and Generation. Converting money inside the USD account costs 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000. With an $8,000 yearly limit, most FHSA conversions fall in the 1.5% band, so on Core the USD account adds cost unless you trade US securities often.

US withholding tax. US dividends paid into an FHSA are withheld at 15% with a W-8BEN on file, or 30% without one. The FHSA is not a retirement arrangement under the Canada-US tax treaty, so the exemption that protects RRSPs does not apply, and the tax cannot be recovered as a foreign tax credit. On US$400 of US dividends, US$60.00 is withheld at 15%.

A simpler route. Canadian-listed ETFs that hold US or global stocks trade in Canadian dollars, so they need no conversion and no USD account in the FHSA. That is a general option, not a recommendation of any fund. Our Wealthsimple USD accounts review explains the USD account in detail, and our guide to buying US stocks in Canada covers the trade-offs.

Time horizon. An FHSA has a limited life: it ends on December 31 of the earliest of three years: the year of the 15th anniversary of your first FHSA, the year you turn 71, or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. Currency swings over a short horizon can move the Canadian-dollar value of US holdings up or down. Weigh that before holding a large share of a down payment in US dollars.

7. Transferring an FHSA to Wealthsimple

Transfer-out fees covered up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more. Wealthsimple reimburses the transfer-out fee your old institution charges, up to $150 per account, on transfers of $25,000 or more. The account must stay funded for 90 days. The threshold was $15,000 before April 10, 2025. Wealthsimple's pages describe the claim process in two different ways, one saying it is automatic and another asking for proof, so confirm the steps with Wealthsimple before you transfer.

The FHSA limit makes $25,000 harder to reach. With $8,000 of room a year, contributions alone reach at most $24,000 by the end of the third calendar year ($8,000 x 3 = $24,000), so a young FHSA can sit below $25,000 unless growth lifts it. Wealthsimple's pages state the threshold without saying whether transfers into several accounts at once can be added together to reach it. If your FHSA is below $25,000, confirm this before you count on the reimbursement. TD, RBC, Scotia iTRADE and National Bank Direct Brokerage each charge $150 to transfer out, so that is often the amount at stake.

Always use a direct transfer. Moving an FHSA between institutions must be done as a direct transfer, started by the receiving institution. Withdrawing the money yourself and depositing it at Wealthsimple is not a transfer: the withdrawal would be taxable unless it is a qualifying home withdrawal, and the new deposit would use FHSA room. Our guide to transferring an investment account walks through the process.

FHSA to RRSP. Wealthsimple confirms you can transfer an FHSA to an RRSP without using RRSP room. Under CRA rules, a direct transfer from an FHSA to an RRSP or RRIF is tax-deferred and does not reduce your unused RRSP deduction room, as long as you have no excess FHSA amount. This is the main exit if you never buy a home.

RRSP to FHSA. CRA allows RRSP-to-FHSA transfers within your FHSA limits, though they are not deductible. Wealthsimple's FHSA page does not address RRSP-to-FHSA or FHSA-to-FHSA transfers, so confirm with Wealthsimple before you start one. Our FHSA vs RRSP Home Buyers' Plan guide explains when each route makes sense.

9. FHSA Rules Recap for 2026

The rules are the same everywhere. The FHSA's limits, deadlines and tax treatment come from the CRA, not from Wealthsimple. Every provider must follow them. This recap gives the figures that apply in 2026; our full FHSA guide covers each rule in depth.

Room only starts when you open. Unlike the TFSA, FHSA room does not build up from age 18. It begins in the year you open your first FHSA, with no carry-forward in that first year. A buyer who expects to want an FHSA soon gains by opening one now, even with a small deposit, so that $8,000 of room starts to accrue. Use the FHSA room calculator below to see your room by year opened and contributions made.

Carry-forward has a cap. If you open in 2026 and contribute $5,000, you carry forward $3,000, so your 2027 room is $11,000 ($8,000 + $3,000). Unused room above $8,000 is not carried, so the most you can ever add in one year is $16,000. Check eligibility with our FHSA eligibility guide and plan contributions with the FHSA calculator.

Mind the December 31 deadline. FHSA contributions must be made by December 31 to count for that year. Money deposited in January or February cannot be deducted for the previous year, unlike the RRSP. If you plan a year-end contribution, fund the account early enough that the deposit settles in time.

Over-contributions cost 1% a month. The tax is 1% a month on the highest excess amount in each month until it is removed. A $2,000 excess left for three months costs $60.00 ($2,000 x 1% x 3). It can be fixed with a designated withdrawal or transfer, a taxable withdrawal or new room on January 1.

10. Is a Wealthsimple FHSA Safe? CIPF and CDIC Cover

Investing versions: CIPF. A self-directed, managed or Automated Investing FHSA is held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., a CIRO dealer member and CIPF member. CIPF places the FHSA in its general group, together with cash, margin and TFSA accounts, with a combined limit of $1,000,000. If the firm failed and client property were missing, CIPF would cover the missing cash and securities up to that limit.

Shared limit. Because the FHSA shares CIPF's general limit with your TFSA, non-registered and margin accounts, a client with $900,000 in other general accounts and $40,000 in an FHSA has $940,000 in the group ($900,000 + $40,000 = $940,000), still inside the $1,000,000 limit.

Savings version: CDIC, not CIPF. The FHSA savings version is a Registered Savings Account. Wealthsimple states these are covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per beneficiary per category and are not protected by CIPF. The FHSA is its own CDIC category, separate from a TFSA or RRSP savings account. With a $40,000 lifetime contribution limit, an FHSA savings balance stays under $100,000 unless interest lifts it to two and a half times the amount contributed ($40,000 x 2.5 = $100,000), and interest counts toward the CDIC limit.

What no plan covers. Neither CIPF nor CDIC covers a fall in market value. If your down payment is invested in a stock-heavy portfolio and markets fall the year you plan to buy, no protection plan makes up the loss. Matching your investments to your purchase date is a choice only you can make.

11. Opening an FHSA and Using the Wealthsimple App

No stated minimum. Wealthsimple states no minimum to open an FHSA, and the Core tier starts at $1 in assets. You open the account in the Wealthsimple mobile app or the web app, and you choose the version when you open it: self-directed, managed, Automated Investing or savings.

Eligibility is on you. Wealthsimple will ask you to confirm you meet the CRA conditions, but the rules are yours to meet. You must be a Canadian resident, at least 18 (19 where that is the age of majority), no older than 71 at December 31 of the year you open the account, and a first-time home buyer under the CRA's four-year test. The conditions apply on the day the account is opened.

Funding and timing. Instant deposits are available up to $50,000 on Core and $250,000 on Premium and Generation. Recurring investments can be set daily, weekly, every two weeks or monthly, in stocks and ETFs. Fractional shares let a self-directed FHSA buy from $1, in real time during market hours, and dividend reinvestment can be switched on for the whole account.

Trading features. The self-directed FHSA can buy 14,000+ stocks and ETFs. US-listed stocks and ETFs trade 24 hours a day, five days a week, from Sunday 8 pm ET to Friday 8 pm ET, with extended-hours orders as limit orders. Options are limited in registered accounts to buying calls and puts, selling cash-secured puts and selling covered calls.

Withdrawing for your home. When you buy, you make a qualifying withdrawal using CRA Form RC725, which confirms you meet the conditions at the time of withdrawal. You can also combine it with the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan, which allows up to $60,000 for withdrawals after April 16, 2024. See our Home Buyers' Plan guide and our FHSA vs TFSA comparison for using both.

12. Wealthsimple FHSA Pros and Cons

Pros

  • $0 commission on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs
  • $0 to open, keep, close or transfer out
  • Four versions: self-directed, managed, Automated Investing and savings
  • Managed Investing scores 4.2 / 5, rank 1 of four robo-advisors
  • USD account allowed in an FHSA, which several brokers do not offer
  • Transfer-out fees covered, up to $150 per account, on $25,000 or more
  • FHSA transfers count toward the 1% transfer match
  • No stated minimum, fractional shares from $1 and recurring investments

Cons

  • 1.5% conversion fee on US trades from a CAD account
  • USD account costs $10 a month on Core
  • Managed fee of 0.5% on Core is above Questwealth's 0.25%

  • The $25,000 transfer threshold is hard to reach with one FHSA
  • Unclear whether FHSA balances count toward tier status
  • No FHSA-specific promotion

Weighing them. For most first-time buyers the pros dominate: there is no cost to hold the account and no commission to invest in Canadian-listed ETFs. The cons matter most to buyers who want US-listed holdings on Core or who would pay a 0.5% fee for many years.

How to Open and Fund a Wealthsimple FHSA

  1. Confirm you qualify. Check residency, age and the first-time buyer test for you and your current spouse or partner before you open, since the conditions apply at opening.
  2. Choose the version. Pick self-directed, managed, Automated Investing or savings. The version decides your fees and whether CIPF or CDIC covers the account.
  3. Open the account. Open the FHSA in the Wealthsimple app or web app. Opening starts your $8,000 of room for the year, even before you deposit.
  4. Fund it or transfer in. Deposit new money, or ask Wealthsimple to transfer an existing FHSA directly from your old institution. Never withdraw and redeposit an FHSA yourself.
  5. Set recurring contributions. Schedule daily, weekly, every-two-weeks or monthly investments, and keep the yearly total within your room.
  6. Claim the deduction. Report contributions on your tax return. Deduct them that year or carry the deduction forward to a higher-income year.
  7. Withdraw for your home. When you buy, complete Form RC725 and make a qualifying withdrawal. If you do not buy, transfer the FHSA to an RRSP before the account's time limit.

10 Strategies to Get More From a Wealthsimple FHSA

1. Open early, even with a little

FHSA room starts only in the year you open your first FHSA. Opening this year, even with a small deposit, starts $8,000 of room and lets up to $8,000 of unused room carry into next year.

2. Hold the deduction for a higher-income year

You can carry FHSA deductions forward, even after the account closes. If you expect a raise, contribute now for tax-free growth and claim the deduction when your marginal rate is higher.

3. Contribute before December 31

FHSA contributions have no 60-day window. Money that arrives in January counts for the new year, not the old one, so schedule year-end deposits early.

4. Use Canadian-listed ETFs to avoid FX

US trades from the CAD side cost 1.5% each way. Canadian-listed ETFs trade in Canadian dollars with $0 commission and no conversion. This is a general option, not a product pick.

5. Match risk to your purchase date

Money you need within a year or two is exposed to market drops that no protection plan covers. Moving toward cash or the savings version as the purchase date nears reduces that exposure.

6. Combine transfers for the 1% Match

The match needs $25,000 of net qualifying transfers, and FHSA, TFSA, RRSP and non-registered transfers all count. Moving several accounts at once can clear the threshold, and $48,000 earns $480.00 over 24 months.

7. Pair the FHSA with the Home Buyers' Plan

You can use both for the same home. The HBP allows up to $60,000 from an RRSP, on top of your FHSA, which can make a much larger down payment.

8. Each partner opens their own

FHSA room is personal. Two first-time buyers each get $8,000 a year and $40,000 lifetime, for $80,000 of combined contribution room ($40,000 x 2).

9. Reach Premium to cut the managed fee

On Premium, the managed fee drops from 0.5% to 0.4%. Combining household assets can reach $100,000 sooner, though Wealthsimple does not say whether FHSA balances count toward your tier.

10. Plan the exit if you do not buy

If you never buy, a direct transfer to an RRSP keeps the money tax-deferred without using RRSP room. Leaving it past the account's time limit makes the full value taxable.

Illustrative Example: A Two-Year Wealthsimple FHSA in Alberta

This example is hypothetical. Jordan, 28, lives in Alberta, rents and has never owned a home. His 2026 taxable income is $80,000, so his combined marginal rate is 30.5% (20.5% federal plus 10% Alberta). He opens a managed Wealthsimple FHSA on the Core tier in 2026. Fees are as of October 22, 2026, and we ignore investment returns.

  1. 2026 room: $8,000. Jordan contributes $5,000, leaving ($8,000 − $5,000 = $3,000) unused.
  2. 2026 tax saving from deducting $5,000: ($5,000 x 30.5% = $1,525.00). His taxable income falls to ($80,000 − $5,000 = $75,000), which is still in the same federal and Alberta brackets, so the full 30.5% applies.
  3. 2027 room: the new $8,000 plus the $3,000 carried forward: ($8,000 + $3,000 = $11,000). Jordan contributes $11,000.
  4. Total contributed after two years: ($5,000 + $11,000 = $16,000). Lifetime room left: ($40,000 − $16,000 = $24,000).
  5. Yearly Wealthsimple fee on $16,000 at 0.5%: ($16,000 x 0.5% = $80.00).
  6. Classic portfolio fund costs at up to 0.15%: ($16,000 x 0.15% = $24.00), for a total of up to ($80.00 + $24.00 = $104.00) a year.
  7. Had Jordan chosen a self-directed FHSA with Canadian-listed ETFs, he would pay $0 in commissions to Wealthsimple, plus the fund costs of whatever ETFs he chose.
  8. Had he contributed the full $8,000 in 2026 instead, his deduction would save ($8,000 x 30.5% = $2,440.00).

When Jordan buys his first home and meets the qualifying conditions, he can withdraw the whole FHSA, including any growth, without tax. If he does not buy, he can transfer it directly to an RRSP without using RRSP room.

Wealthsimple FHSA FAQ

Does Wealthsimple offer an FHSA?

Yes. Wealthsimple offers the First Home Savings Account in four versions: self-directed (Trade) with $0 commissions, managed portfolios, Automated Investing and a savings version. There is no stated minimum and no account fee. Crypto cannot be held in a Wealthsimple FHSA, and the FHSA cannot be used as collateral for Wealthsimple's line of credit.

What are the Wealthsimple FHSA fees?

A self-directed FHSA charges $0 commission on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs, plus 1.5% to convert currency on US trades from a CAD account. A managed FHSA costs 0.5% a year on Core and 0.4% on Premium and Generation, plus fund costs. Automated Investing costs 0.25%. Opening, closing and transferring out cost $0.

Can I have a managed FHSA at Wealthsimple?

Yes. Wealthsimple Managed Investing offers the FHSA. It builds and rebalances a portfolio for 0.5% a year on Core and 0.4% on Premium, plus fund costs of 0.12% to 0.15% on Classic portfolios. Wealthsimple Managed Investing scores 4.2 / 5 in our robo-advisor ranking, rank 1 of four, as of October 22, 2026.

Is there a minimum to open a Wealthsimple FHSA?

No minimum is stated. Wealthsimple's Core tier starts at $1 in assets, and fractional shares let a self-directed FHSA invest from $1. Opening the account starts your $8,000 of FHSA room for that year, even if you deposit little or nothing at first.

Can I hold crypto in a Wealthsimple FHSA?

No. Wealthsimple offers crypto only in a non-registered crypto account. You cannot hold crypto in a Wealthsimple FHSA, TFSA or RRSP. Crypto coins at Wealthsimple are also not covered by CIPF or CDIC, unlike the investing versions of the FHSA.

Can I hold US dollars in a Wealthsimple FHSA?

Yes. Wealthsimple lets you add a USD account to an FHSA, for $10 a month plus tax on Core, included on Premium and Generation. US dividends in an FHSA are still withheld at 15% with a W-8BEN, and that tax cannot be recovered, because the FHSA does not get the treaty exemption that RRSPs do.

Does Wealthsimple cover FHSA transfer fees?

Yes, on transfers of $25,000 or more. Wealthsimple reimburses the transfer-out fee your old institution charges, up to $150 per account, if the account stays funded for 90 days. Its pages describe the claim process differently, so please confirm the steps before you transfer. Wealthsimple charges $0 to transfer out.

Does the 1% transfer match apply to an FHSA?

Yes. FHSA transfers into Trade or Managed accounts count toward Wealthsimple's 1% Match. You must transfer at least $25,000 within 30 days of registering, and the bonus is 1% on up to $2,000,000, a maximum of $20,000, paid in 24 monthly payments to Wealthsimple chequing, with a 20% withdrawal buffer for 730 days.

Is a Wealthsimple FHSA protected by CIPF?

The investing versions are. A self-directed, managed or Automated Investing FHSA sits in CIPF's general group with your TFSA, cash and margin accounts, sharing a $1,000,000 limit. The savings version is a Registered Savings Account covered by CDIC up to $100,000 and not by CIPF.

How much can I contribute to an FHSA in 2026?

$8,000 a year, plus up to $8,000 of unused room carried forward from the previous year, so at most $16,000 in one year. The lifetime limit is $40,000. Room starts only in the year you open your first FHSA, and contributions must be made by December 31, with no 60-day period.

Can I move my Wealthsimple FHSA to an RRSP?

Yes. Wealthsimple allows FHSA to RRSP transfers, and under CRA rules a direct transfer from an FHSA to an RRSP or RRIF is tax-deferred and does not use RRSP room, provided you have no excess FHSA amount. This is the usual exit if you do not buy a home.

Is Wealthsimple or Questrade better for an FHSA?

Both score 4.0 / 5, with Questrade ahead on its unrounded score. Both charge $0 commissions. Wealthsimple charges $0 to transfer out and allows a USD account in the FHSA, at $10 a month on Core. Questrade lets registered accounts hold US dollars without a separate fee but charges $150 to transfer out.

Can I use an FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan together?

Yes. CRA rules let you use both for the same qualifying home if you meet the conditions for each at the time of withdrawal. The Home Buyers' Plan allows up to $60,000 from your RRSPs for withdrawals after April 16, 2024, on top of your FHSA withdrawal.
