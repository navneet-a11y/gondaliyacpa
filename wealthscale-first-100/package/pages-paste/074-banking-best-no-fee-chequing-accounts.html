2. What "No Monthly Fee" Covers and What It Does Not

The promise is narrow. A no fee chequing account removes the monthly account charge. It does not promise that nothing else costs money. Every account in this list publishes at least one charge, a condition or a coming change that can turn $0 into a cost.

Three kinds of "no fee". First, no fee with no strings: EQ Bank, Wealthsimple, Simplii, Tangerine and PC Money charge nothing monthly whatever your balance. Second, no fee with a condition: Manulife needs $1,000 in the account to avoid per-transaction charges, and KOHO Essential needs direct deposit or $1,000 a month in deposits. Third, no fee for now: Neo's account fee is $0, but three new charges start on January 1, 2027 for clients without a membership.

What sits outside the monthly fee. Charges you can still meet include ATM withdrawals outside a bank's network, the machine owner's surcharge, cancelling an e-Transfer, non-sufficient-funds charges, overdraft interest, extra cheque books, outgoing wires and foreign currency conversion on card purchases. Section 3 lists each one by account.

The hidden cost of a 0% rate. A $0 account that pays nothing on your balance still costs you the interest you could have earned. On $3,000, the gap between no published rate and EQ Bank's 2.75% with direct deposit is $82.50 a year.

Big-bank accounts that are $0 for some people. Several big-bank accounts are $0 only for certain groups, which is why they are not ranked here. National Bank The Minimalist is $0 for ages 0 to 24. Scotiabank's Basic Bank Account is $0 for seniors and is waived for Indigenous Peoples, RDSP beneficiaries, Disability Tax Credit recipients and social assistance recipients. TD waives its Minimum Chequing Account for certain groups, including GIS recipients and newcomers in their first year. If you qualify, these can give you a branch and cheques at $0.

The same $4,000 in a no-fee account earns $110.00 a year at EQ Bank with direct deposit (2.75%), $80.00 at KOHO Essential (2%), $70.00 at Wealthsimple Core with direct deposit (1.75%) or $60.00 at Manulife (1.50%).

When a package can still make sense. If your balance sits well above the waiver line every day and you use a branch, cheques or a bank's credit products often, the package costs you nothing in fees. The cost is the interest that balance is not earning. Our full chequing ranking scores all six packages, and our bank and platform reviews cover individual providers in more depth.

A middle path. Keep a no-fee account for pay, bills and interest, and drop the big-bank account to its lowest-fee plan ($3.95 or $4.00 a month) for cheques and branch visits. That costs $47.40 or $48.00 a year rather than up to $215.40.

The CDIC rule in one line. CDIC insures eligible deposits, principal and interest together, up to $100,000 per insured category at each member institution. 11. How to Choose a No Fee Chequing Account

Match the account to how you use money. The steps below put the factors in the order that usually decides the choice.

  1. Decide where your pay will land. If you can direct deposit your pay, EQ Bank (2.75%), KOHO Essential ($0 plan fee) and Wealthsimple (an extra 0.50% at Core and Premium) all reward it.
  2. Check your lowest balance. If you often drop below $1,000, Manulife's per-transaction charges will apply; choose an account with no balance rule.
  3. Count your cash withdrawals. Frequent cash users should favour reimbursement (EQ Bank in Canada, Wealthsimple worldwide) or a network near home.
  4. Decide if you need cheques. Manulife, Tangerine and Wealthsimple publish cheque writing; EQ Bank does not.
  5. Pick your protection structure. EQ Bank, Manulife, Tangerine and PC Money are CDIC members; Simplii falls under CIBC; Wealthsimple, KOHO and Neo use trust arrangements.
  6. Look at the offer last. If two accounts fit equally well, a live offer can break the tie. Note the end date first.

A simple rule of thumb. Pay by direct deposit and little cash: EQ Bank. Travel or spend in foreign currency: Wealthsimple. Need cheques and keep $1,000: Manulife. Want a no-strings $0 account with branch-bank ATMs: Simplii or Tangerine.

12. How to Switch to a No-Fee Account

Downgrade or close. You do not have to close your big-bank account to stop paying for it. Ask to move it to the lowest-fee plan, or to a $0 plan if you qualify by age or status, and keep it for cheques or a mortgage. If you no longer need it, close it after everything has moved.

Open and test first. Open the no-fee account, link your old account, and move a small amount both ways. Send yourself an e-Transfer and pay one small bill.

Move your pay and automatic payments. Give your employer the new direct deposit details. Then move each pre-authorized payment, one at a time, and confirm each one has come out of the new account before moving the next.

Mind the old account's balance rule. If the old account is a package with a balance waiver, moving money out mid-month can trigger that month's full fee. Move the bulk of the balance just after a statement date.

Time any welcome offer. Simplii's $350 needs 4 straight months of direct deposit and Tangerine's $250 needs your payroll moved, so point your pay at the new account from the first pay date.

Registered accounts move differently. A TFSA or RRSP should move by direct transfer between institutions, not by withdrawal. See how to transfer a TFSA, RRSP or investment account.

13. Mistakes to Avoid With No-Fee Accounts

Dropping below a balance rule. Manulife's $1,000 line turns a $0 month into a $25.00 month for a typical pattern of use.

Forgetting KOHO's deposit rule. A month without direct deposit or $1,000 in deposits costs $7.

Ignoring Neo's January 1, 2027 change. Without a membership, ordinary e-Transfers and withdrawals start to cost money. The Essentials membership is free of charge and removes those fees.

Using any ATM out of habit. At Tangerine and PC Money, two out-of-network withdrawals a month cost $36.00 a year.

Keeping savings in a 0% account. Simplii, Tangerine and PC Money do not publish a chequing rate. A spare $10,000 earns $275.00 a year at EQ Bank with direct deposit.

Assuming every account takes cheques. EQ Bank does not offer cheque writing, and Neo's mobile cheque deposit is not yet available.

Illustrative Example: Leaving a $17.95 Account

This example is hypothetical. Maya has a TD Unlimited Chequing Account and a balance that usually sits around $2,500, below the $4,000 minimum daily balance. She pays $17.95 every month and takes out cash twice a month at machines that are not part of any network she would join.

  1. What she pays now. $17.95 x 12 = $215.40 a year, and she earns 0.000% interest.
  2. Option A: Tangerine. $0 monthly fee. If she keeps using out-of-network ATMs, two withdrawals a month cost 2 x $1.50 x 12 = $36.00. She saves $215.40 - $36.00 = $179.40 a year, or the full $215.40 if she uses Scotiabank or Global ATM Alliance machines.
  3. Option B: EQ Bank with her pay direct deposited. $0 monthly fee and ATM fees reimbursed in Canada. Interest is $2,500 x 2.75% = $68.75. Her yearly position improves by $215.40 + $68.75 = $284.15.
  4. Option C: Manulife Bank. $0 monthly fee as long as she never drops below $1,000. Interest is $2,500 x 1.50% = $37.50, so she is $215.40 + $37.50 = $252.90 better off, and she keeps the ability to write cheques.
  5. What she weighs. Option B gives the most money. Option C keeps cheques. Option A keeps cash access through a large network but no published interest rate. Options A and C leave out any surcharge a machine's owner may add, since owners set their own.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best no fee chequing account in Canada?

On our published score, the EQ Bank Personal Account is the best no fee chequing account in Canada at 4.6 / 5. It charges no monthly fee, needs no minimum balance, includes unlimited transactions and e-Transfers, reimburses ATM fees across Canada and pays 2.75% with pay direct deposit. Wealthsimple Chequing is second at 4.4 / 5.

Is a no-fee chequing account really free?

The monthly fee is $0, but other charges can apply. Examples include $1.50 for out-of-network ATM withdrawals at Tangerine and PC Money, $3.50 to cancel an e-Transfer at PC Money, $1.00 per transaction at Manulife below $1,000, and Neo's new fees from January 1, 2027 for clients without a membership.

Which no-fee chequing account pays the most interest?

EQ Bank pays the most, 2.75%, when your pay is direct deposited. Without direct deposit, KOHO Essential pays 2% after you opt in, though its $0 plan fee itself needs direct deposit or $1,000 a month in deposits. Wealthsimple pays 1.25% to 2.25% depending on tier and direct deposit.

Why is KOHO Essential on a list of no-fee accounts?

KOHO Essential has a $7 list price, but it costs $0 a month when you set up direct deposit or deposit $1,000 each month, with no minimum balance. Our fee factor scores it 4.5 rather than 5.0 to reflect that condition.

Do no-fee chequing accounts have CDIC protection?

Yes, in different ways. EQ Bank, Manulife, Tangerine and PC Money are CDIC members, and Simplii is covered through CIBC. Wealthsimple and KOHO are not members but place funds in trust at member institutions: up to $1,000,000 combined at Wealthsimple and up to $100,000 at KOHO (after opting in to interest). Neo, also not a member, says chequing funds are eligible for CDIC insurance up to $100,000 through a member it does not name.

Which no-fee account lets me write cheques?

Manulife Bank offers personal and official cheques with no charge to write them. Tangerine gives you a first book of 50 cheques at no charge, then $50 a book. Wealthsimple gives you a chequebook once your pay is direct deposited. EQ Bank offers mobile cheque deposit only.

What is changing at Neo in 2027?

From January 1, 2027, Neo Chequing clients without a membership pay $1 per e-Transfer sent, $1.50 per cash withdrawal and $4.99 for non-sufficient funds. All three are $0 with any Neo membership, and the Essentials membership has no fee. Paid Build and Grow memberships cost $9.99 and $14.99 a month.

Can I avoid ATM fees with a no-fee account?

Yes. EQ Bank reimburses ATM fees anywhere in Canada, and Wealthsimple reimburses eligible operator fees worldwide. Tangerine, Simplii, PC Money and Manulife (above $1,000) give you no-fee withdrawals inside their networks and charge elsewhere: $1.50 in Canada at Tangerine and PC Money.

Which no-fee accounts have a welcome offer right now?

With published terms on October 22, 2026: Simplii pays $350 after 4 straight months of $100+ direct deposits (ends January 31, 2027), Tangerine pays $250 for moving your payroll (ends October 31, 2026), Manulife pays 3.00% for 730 days on accounts opened by October 30, 2026, and Neo pays $100 after $1,000 of spending (end date not stated).

Is a no monthly fee bank account better than a big-bank package?

For many balances, yes. A package costs $15.95 to $17.95 a month, most waive it only if you hold $4,000 or $4,500, and none of the big banks published a positive chequing rate. A no-fee account charges nothing and can pay interest, but has no branches. Some people keep both, with the big-bank account on its cheapest plan.

Can I get a $0 account at a big bank?

Only if you qualify. National Bank The Minimalist is $0 for ages 0 to 24, Scotiabank's Basic Bank Account is $0 for seniors and several eligible groups, and TD waives its Minimum Chequing Account for certain groups, including newcomers in their first year. Otherwise these accounts cost $3.95 a month.

Is interest on a no-fee chequing account taxable?

Yes. Interest earned in a chequing account outside a registered plan is taxable income in the year you earn it, at your full marginal rate. Interest earned inside a TFSA is not taxed, so savings you will not spend soon may be better held there.
