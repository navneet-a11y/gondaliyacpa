3. Spousal RRSP Support by Provider

How a spousal RRSP works. You contribute to an RRSP in your spouse's or common-law partner's name, using your own deduction room, and you claim the deduction. Your spouse owns the plan and is normally taxed on withdrawals. The aim is to split retirement income so the couple pays less tax in total. Your spouse's own room is not affected.

The three-year attribution rule. If your spouse withdraws in a year when you contributed to any spousal RRSP for them, or in either of the two previous calendar years, the withdrawal is generally taxed in your hands. The contributor reports it on Form T2205.

Contributing after 71. You must stop contributing to your own RRSP on December 31 of the year you turn 71, but you can keep contributing to a spousal RRSP until the end of the year your spouse turns 71, as long as you still have room from earned income. This example is hypothetical: you are 73, your spouse is 68 and you have $12,000 of unused RRSP deduction room carried forward. You can still put $12,000 into a spousal RRSP and deduct it, at any time until the end of the year your spouse turns 71.

4. USD RRSP: US Dollars and the Treaty Exemption

Why US shares often belong in an RRSP. Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest paid to an arrangement operated exclusively to provide pension or retirement benefits. RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs qualify, so US-listed securities held directly in them are exempt from US withholding once your broker has the account documented. A TFSA or FHSA does not qualify, and loses 15% of US dividends.

A worked comparison. This example is hypothetical. $2,000 of dividends from directly held US-listed shares loses $300 in a TFSA ($2,000 x 15%) and $0 in an RRSP. If the RRSP holds only Canadian dollars, each dividend is converted at the broker's rate: at 1.5% that costs $30 a year ($2,000 x 1.5%). In an RRSP that holds US dollars at no extra cost, it costs nothing until you choose to convert.

7. RRSP Rules Recap for 2026: Limit, Room and Deadline

The deduction limit. Your RRSP deduction limit for a year is the lesser of 18% of your previous year's earned income and that year's dollar limit, minus any pension adjustment and past service pension adjustment, plus any pension adjustment reversal and your unused room carried forward. The 2026 dollar limit is $33,810 and the 2027 limit, already published, is $35,390.

Income needed for the maximum. To reach the full 2026 limit from new room alone, you needed $187,833.33 of earned income in 2025 ($33,810 / 18%). Carrying room and deductions forward. Unused deduction room carries forward with no time limit. You can also contribute now and deduct later: contributions you do not deduct can be claimed in a future year, which can be worth more if your income, and so your tax rate, will be higher then.

The deadline. The RRSP contribution year ends on the 60th day of the following year. The deadline for the 2025 tax year was March 2, 2026. Canada Revenue Agency has not yet posted the deadline for the 2026 tax year, so please confirm it before you rely on a date.

Over-contributions. You can be up to $2,000 over your deduction limit without penalty if you were 18 or older at any time in the previous year. Anything above that is taxed at 1% a month. You report it on Form T1-OVP within 90 days after the end of the calendar year, and you can ask for a waiver on Form RC2503 if the excess came from a reasonable error. Payroll contributions to a group RRSP at work use the same room, so add them up before you contribute on your own. A worked withdrawal. This example is hypothetical. An Alberta resident with $80,000 of income withdraws $20,000. The broker withholds $6,000 ($20,000 x 30%). The Home Buyers' Plan. First-time buyers can withdraw up to $60,000 from RRSPs under the Home Buyers' Plan without tax, provided they have a written agreement to buy or build, intend to live in the home within one year, and are resident in Canada. For first withdrawals made from January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2028, repayment starts in the fifth year after the withdrawal and runs for up to 15 years. The Lifelong Learning Plan. You can withdraw up to $10,000 a year and $20,000 in total to pay for full-time education for you or your spouse, and repay it over 10 years.

Room does not come back. Unlike a TFSA, a regular RRSP withdrawal never restores the room you used. Only HBP and LLP amounts are repaid into the plan. That is why an RRSP is a poor place for an emergency fund.

11. How to Transfer an RRSP Without Tax

Use a direct transfer. Open the new RRSP first and ask the new provider to transfer the plan from the old one. The money moves between institutions without passing through your hands, so nothing is withheld and nothing is added to your income. Please never withdraw and redeposit: the withdrawal is taxed, tax is withheld, and the room you used is gone for good.

What a withdrawal would cost instead. This example is hypothetical. Redepositing it needs $20,000 of fresh deduction room, which you may not have, and the original $20,000 of room is not restored.

8 RRSP Account Strategies That Cut Costs and Tax

1. Pick a provider that can take you past 71

If you plan to keep the account into retirement, choose one of the 10 brokers that offers a RRIF. You avoid a forced transfer, and possibly a transfer fee, in the year you turn 71.

2. Put US dividend payers in a USD RRSP

US-listed shares held directly in an RRSP are exempt from US withholding, unlike in a TFSA. An RRSP that holds US dollars at no extra cost also avoids converting each dividend.

3. Consolidate small RRSPs

Five brokers can charge $100 a year on small or quiet RRSPs. Merging two or three old plans at one provider with no such fee can save more than lower commissions would.

4. Contribute now, deduct later

If your income is low this year, you can contribute and carry the deduction forward. At $50,000 in Ontario a $10,000 deduction saves $2,055.00; at $150,000 it saves $4,340.96.

5. Use a spousal RRSP before 65

Pension splitting covers RRIF income from 65. A spousal RRSP lets a couple split income earlier, or split more than half, as long as withdrawals wait out the three-year attribution window.

6. Keep a small cash buffer

Registered debit balances cost up to 21.00% a year. If your broker charges account or USD fees, leave enough cash to cover them so the account never goes negative.

7. Withdraw in low-income years

Every withdrawal is added to your income. Taking money out in a year with little other income, such as a career break, means it is taxed at a lower rate than in a high-income year.

8. Make the new provider pay the exit fee

Use a direct transfer started by the receiving provider. Questrade rebates up to $150 per account, RBC up to $200 on $15,000 or more, and Wealthsimple up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more. Ask before you start a transfer.

Illustrative Example: Consolidating Two RRSPs

This example is hypothetical. Daniel is 45 and lives in Ontario. His 2026 taxable income is $75,000 and his RRSP deduction limit is $25,000. He has a $12,000 RRSP at a bank broker that charges $100 a year on retirement accounts under $25,000 with fewer than 12 trades, and $9.99 a trade. He makes four trades a year there. He also has a $30,000 RRSP at a $0-commission broker that holds US dollars at no extra cost and rebates transfer fees up to $150 per account.

  1. Current cost of the small RRSP. $100 fee + 4 trades x $9.99 ($39.96) = $139.96 a year.
  2. The transfer. His bank broker charges $150 to transfer out. The receiving broker rebates up to $150 per account. Net cost = $150 - $150 = $0. No tax is withheld and his room is unchanged.
  3. One account. His combined RRSP is $42,000 ($12,000 + $30,000), in one plan that also offers a RRIF for later.
  4. His 2026 contribution. He contributes $10,000. Tax saved = $3,115.00: federal $2,050.00 ($10,000 x 20.5%) + Ontario $915.00 ($10,000 x 9.15%) + Ontario Health Premium $150.00 ($750 - $600).
  5. Room left. $25,000 - $10,000 = $15,000, which carries forward.
  6. Ten years on. At today's prices, closing the small RRSP saves $1,399.60 in fees and commissions ($139.96 x 10), before any growth on the money saved.

The tax saving comes from the deduction, which any RRSP provides. The fee saving comes from choosing where to hold it.

Are US dividends taxed in an RRSP?

Not by the US, if you hold US-listed shares directly. The Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends paid to retirement arrangements such as RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs once your broker has the account documented. A Canadian-listed fund that owns US shares still has US tax withheld inside the fund. Withdrawals from the RRSP are taxed in Canada as income.

You can go up to $2,000 over without penalty if you were 18 or older in the previous year.

What happens to my RRSP when I turn 71?

December 31 of the year you turn 71 is the last day to contribute to your own RRSP. By then you must withdraw it, transfer it to a RRIF or buy an annuity.
