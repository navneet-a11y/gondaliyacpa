6. TFSA Rules Recap for 2026: Limit, Room and Withdrawals

The 2026 limit. The TFSA dollar limit for 2026 is $7,000, the same as in 2024 and 2025. The limit is indexed to inflation and rounded to the nearest $500. Canada Revenue Agency has not yet published the 2027 limit, so treat any figure you see for 2027 as an estimate.

Total room since 2009. If you were 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009 and never contributed, your total room in 2026 is $109,000, the sum of every annual limit below. 8. Safety: CIPF, CDIC and Provincial Deposit Insurance

Investments at a broker: CIPF. All 13 brokers on this list are members of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF), directly or through the dealer that holds client accounts. CIPF covers cash and securities that go missing if a member firm fails, up to $1,000,000 for all general accounts combined, a group that includes cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA accounts. RRSPs, RRIFs and LIFs have a separate $1,000,000 limit. CIPF does not cover a fall in market prices, unsuitable advice, the failure of a company you invested in, or crypto assets.

Who regulates the brokers. The Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) oversees all investment dealers in Canada, and every CIPF member firm is a CIRO member. Interactive Brokers, Wealthsimple, moomoo, Webull, Qtrade, RBC, BMO, Scotia and Desjardins state their CIRO membership on their own pages; for the others it follows from their CIPF membership.

10. How to Transfer a TFSA to a New Provider

Always use a direct transfer. Open the new TFSA first, then ask the new provider to transfer the account from the old one. A direct transfer between your own TFSAs has no effect on your contribution room. Please do not withdraw the money and deposit it yourself: the deposit counts as a new contribution, and the withdrawal is added back only on January 1 of the next year.

What a do-it-yourself switch can cost. This example is hypothetical. You have $7,000 of room left in 2026. In March you withdraw your whole $20,000 TFSA and in April you deposit it at a new broker. The deposit uses $20,000 of room, so you are $13,000 over ($20,000 - $7,000). The tax is $130 a month ($13,000 x 1%), or $1,170 from April to December ($130 x 9), plus the work of filing a TFSA Return.

In kind or in cash. You can usually move holdings as they are (in kind) or have them sold and move the cash. Moving in kind avoids selling and buying again, which matters at brokers that charge commissions. Fractional shares are an exception at Wealthsimple: they cannot be transferred to another institution, so they must be sold first.

Fees, partial transfers and rebates. Partial transfers cost $25 per security at Disnat and $50 at moomoo and Webull, so moving everything at once is often cheaper. Section 5 lists which receiving brokers cover the exit fee; among robo-advisors, CI Direct Investing covers up to $150 on transfers of $25,000 or more and RBC InvestEase up to $200 on $15,000 or more. Keep the old statement that shows the fee, because some rebates need proof.

GICs and timing. A non-redeemable GIC cannot be cashed before it matures, so plan a cash transfer for its maturity date. Our guide to transferring a TFSA, RRSP or investment account covers forms, timing and what to check after the move.

What You Need and What You Get

What you need to open a TFSA

  • Residency in Canada for tax purposes
  • Age 18 or older (19 in provinces and territories where that is the age of majority)
  • A valid Social Insurance Number
  • Your available TFSA room, ideally checked against your Canada Revenue Agency records
  • A Canadian bank account to fund the TFSA
  • Your choice of successor holder (a spouse or common-law partner) or beneficiary
  • A recent statement from your old provider if you plan to transfer
  • A Form W-8BEN on file if you will hold US-listed shares

What you get from a TFSA

  • Investment income and gains that are not taxed in Canada
  • Withdrawals that are not taxed and not added to your income
  • Withdrawn amounts added back as room on January 1 of the next year
  • Unused room that carries forward with no time limit
  • The freedom to hold several TFSAs under one combined room limit
  • A separate CDIC category of $100,000 for TFSA deposits at each member
  • CIPF coverage of up to $1,000,000 for general accounts at a member broker
  • A successor holder option that passes the account to your spouse or partner without using their room

8 TFSA Account Strategies That Cut Costs

1. Ask the new broker to pay the exit fee

Most brokers charge $150 to transfer out. Questrade rebates up to $150 per account, RBC up to $200 on $15,000 or more, and Wealthsimple up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more. Ask before you sign, and keep the statement that shows the fee.

2. Convert once, then hold US dollars

If you buy US shares to hold for years, a TFSA with a US-dollar side at no extra cost lets you convert once and reinvest US dividends without converting back. At 1.5% each way, avoiding one round trip on $10,000 saves about $300.

3. Keep US dividend payers in an RRSP

US dividends lose 15% in a TFSA, and the tax cannot be recovered. Directly held US-listed shares in an RRSP are exempt under the Canada-US tax treaty.

4. Beat the small-account fee

CIBC charges $100 a year at $10,000 or less, TD $25 a quarter under $15,000 of household assets without a waiver, and National Bank $100 under $20,000 if you are over 30. On a $7,000 TFSA, $100 is 1.43% of the balance ($100 / $7,000), more than any ranked robo-advisor charges. Choose a broker with no such fee until your balance passes the threshold.

5. Withdraw in December, not January

Room from a withdrawal returns on January 1 of the next year. Taking money out in December 2026 rather than January 2027 gets the room back a year sooner, on January 1, 2027.

6. Spread big deposits across CDIC members

CDIC insures TFSA deposits up to $100,000 per member institution. If your TFSA cash and GICs exceed that, split them across two or more members, or use separate institutions for each account type.

7. Track every TFSA you hold

All your TFSAs share one room limit. Add up contributions across every provider before you deposit, especially after a transfer or a withdrawal in the same year.

8. Name a successor holder

Naming your spouse or common-law partner as successor holder lets the TFSA pass to them without using their room. Canada Revenue Agency notes that Quebec does not recognize TFSA successor holder designations, so Quebec residents should ask how to pass the account on.

Illustrative Example: Moving a $30,000 TFSA

This example is hypothetical. Priya is 38, lives in Ontario, and has been 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009. Before 2026 she contributed $40,000 to her TFSA in total, and in 2025 she withdrew $10,000. Her TFSA at a bank-owned broker is now worth $30,000. She buys one Canadian ETF a month and converts $6,000 a year to buy US shares.

  1. Her room on January 1, 2026. Total limits since 2009 are $109,000. Her 2025 withdrawal came back on January 1, 2026. Room = $109,000 - $40,000 + $10,000 = $79,000.
  2. The transfer. She opens a TFSA at a $0-commission broker that rebates transfer fees up to $150 per account, and asks it to start a direct transfer. Her old broker charges $150; the rebate covers it. Net cost = $150 - $150 = $0. Her room stays at $79,000.
  3. Trading costs before and after. At $9.99 a trade, 12 purchases cost $119.88 a year (12 x $9.99). At $0 a trade they cost $0. Saving = $119.88 a year.
  4. Conversion costs before and after. Her old broker's spread was 1.26%, so $6,000 cost $75.60 ($6,000 x 1.26%). The new broker charges 1.5%, or $90.00 ($6,000 x 1.5%). Extra cost = $90.00 - $75.60 = $14.40 a year.
  5. Net yearly saving. $119.88 - $14.40 = $105.48 a year at today's prices.
  6. Her 2026 contribution. She adds the 2026 limit of $7,000. Room left = $79,000 - $7,000 = $72,000.
  7. Ten years on. If prices stay the same, the switch saves $1,054.80 over 10 years ($105.48 x 10), before any growth on the money saved.

The lesson: a lower commission can be offset in part by a higher conversion fee, so compare both. If Priya were saving for a first home instead, an FHSA could add a tax deduction; see FHSA vs TFSA for a down payment and our best FHSA accounts ranking.

Can I have a TFSA at more than one provider?

Yes. You can hold any number of TFSAs at different banks, brokers and robo-advisors. All of them share one combined contribution room, so add up what you put into every account before you deposit. Moving money between your own TFSAs by direct transfer does not use any room.

How much does it cost to transfer a TFSA to another broker?

Most brokers charge $150 to transfer a full TFSA out, as of October 22, 2026. Wealthsimple charges $0 and moomoo $75. Always use a direct transfer so your room is not affected.

Do I pay tax on US dividends in a TFSA?

Yes. The US withholds 15% of dividends paid into a TFSA when your broker has a Form W-8BEN on file, or 30% without it. The treaty exemption for retirement accounts does not apply to a TFSA, and the tax cannot be claimed back. For example, $1,000 of US dividends loses $150 in a TFSA. Directly held US-listed shares in an RRSP are exempt.

Is my TFSA protected if my broker fails?

Investments and cash at a CIPF member broker are covered up to $1,000,000 for all general accounts combined, including TFSAs, if the firm fails. CIPF does not cover market losses. Deposits and GICs at a CDIC member are insured up to $100,000 in a separate TFSA category. Credit union deposits are covered by provincial plans instead of CDIC.

Can I hold a GIC in my TFSA?

Yes. GICs are qualified TFSA investments and the interest is tax-free. As of October 22, 2026, the highest 5-year TFSA GIC rates we verified are 4.50% at Oaken Financial (CDIC) and Saven Financial (DICO). A non-redeemable GIC cannot be cashed before it matures, so you cannot withdraw that money from the TFSA until then.

What happens if I put too much in my TFSA?

The excess is taxed at 1% a month on the highest excess amount in each month it stays in the account. There is no $2,000 cushion as there is for an RRSP. Withdraw the excess as soon as you notice it, then file a TFSA Return and pay any tax by June 30 of the following year.
