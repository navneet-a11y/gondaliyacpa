2. What a LIRA Is and How Locked-In Money Works

Where the money comes from. Many LIRAs begin when someone leaves an employer and moves the value of a registered pension plan out of the plan. Pension law says that money must still provide retirement income, so it goes into a locked-in account rather than a regular RRSP. A LIRA can also receive money from another locked-in account.

What "locked in" means. You cannot take money out of a LIRA as cash the way you can from an RRSP, and you cannot add new contributions. The investments grow sheltered from tax, as in an RRSP, until the money becomes retirement income; our RRSP guide explains the tax rules the two accounts share. Whether any locked-in money can be paid out early depends on the pension law behind it. We could not verify those rules for every jurisdiction, so this page does not describe them. If you have a question about it, please ask your LIRA provider or the pension regulator for your plan's jurisdiction.

How it turns into income. When you want income, a LIRA is moved into a life income fund (LIF), or into another locked-in income fund where your jurisdiction uses one, or used to buy a life annuity. A LIF pays you each year within a minimum and a maximum. LIF payments are taxable income and qualify for pension income splitting once you are 65.

5. LIRA to LIF: Minimum and Maximum Withdrawals

Converting. When you want income, the money in your LIRA moves into a LIF (or the locked-in income fund your jurisdiction uses). From then on, you take a yearly payment that sits between a minimum and a maximum.

The minimum under federal rules. For a federally regulated LIF, the yearly minimum is the same Income Tax Act minimum that applies to a RRIF: the January 1 value times a factor for your age. If you do not choose an amount, the minimum is paid.

The maximum under federal rules. Until the year you turn 90, the most you can take from a federally regulated LIF is set by a formula: the balance divided by an annuity factor. From the year you turn 90, there is no maximum and the whole balance can be paid out. Provincially regulated LIFs have their own maximums, which vary by province.

8. Self-Directed LIRA Investing: Holdings, US Dollars and US Stocks

What a self-directed LIRA can hold. At a broker, a self-directed LIRA can hold stocks, ETFs, bonds, GICs and cash, much like an RRSP. You choose the mix. WealthScale does not recommend specific investments, but a LIRA often has a long time before it pays out, and a LIF that is already paying needs cash on hand for each payment. Our guide on how to invest in Canada explains the main asset types.

US dividends are exempt from US withholding. LIRAs and LIFs are retirement arrangements under Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty, so US dividends and interest paid into them are exempt from US withholding once the broker has the account documented. In a TFSA or non-registered account, 15% is withheld instead.

Holding US dollars. A LIRA that can hold US dollars avoids converting on every US trade. Questrade lets registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost; Desjardins offers US-dollar LIRAs and LIFs with no fee stated; National Bank offers a US-dollar LIRA with no US-dollar fee listed; Wealthsimple adds a US-dollar account to a LIRA for $10 plus tax a month on Core ($120 a year before tax) but not to a LIF; our Wealthsimple USD accounts review explains that account. The other brokers did not state a US-dollar option for locked-in accounts.

What a US-dollar account saves. It lets sales and dividends stay in US dollars, but the first move into US dollars is still a conversion: inside a Wealthsimple USD account, a conversion under $10,000 costs 1.5%. 9. Safety: CIPF and CDIC for Locked-In Accounts

CIPF. All nine brokers on this page are, or belong to, firms on the Canadian Investor Protection Fund's member list. If a member dealer fails, CIPF covers missing cash and securities, with separate $1,000,000 limits for general accounts, for retirement accounts and for RESPs. CIPF's published retirement category names RRSPs, RRIFs and LIFs; please ask your dealer which category your LIRA is reported in. CIPF does not cover market losses or crypto assets.

CDIC. CDIC insures eligible deposits and GICs, not stocks, bonds, ETFs or mutual funds. Its separate categories include RRSPs and RRIFs, each up to $100,000 per member institution. If your LIRA or LIF holds GICs or deposits, please ask the issuing institution which category they fall under.

CIRO. The Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization regulates every investment dealer in Canada, and CIPF member firms are CIRO members.

10. How to Choose the Best LIRA Account for You

Start with your paperwork. The best LIRA account is one that offers the exact locked-in type your pension law requires, at a fee that does not apply to your balance.

  1. Find the account name and jurisdiction. Check your pension paperwork for the account type (LIRA, LRSP, RLSP) and the pension law that applies.
  2. Size the account. Under $25,000, rule out brokers whose $100 fee would apply, or check whether a waiver fits you.
  3. Plan for the LIF. Choose a broker that also offers the income account you will need later, so you can convert without moving firms.
  4. Decide on US dollars. If you will hold US-listed investments, favour a broker that lets both the LIRA and the LIF hold US dollars.
  5. Choose self-directed or managed. A $0-trade broker costs $0 in broker fees; the ranked robo-advisors cost $222.00 to $498.00 a year on $60,000.
  6. Check the move. Note the transfer-out fee at your current firm and any rebate or offer at the new one.
  7. Open and transfer. Open the matching locked-in account, then have the pension plan or your old broker transfer the money directly.

One firm for everything. If you also hold an RRSP or RRIF, keeping the LIRA at the same broker puts all your retirement money in one view. Our best RRSP accounts ranking uses the same score.

10 Strategies for a Locked-In Account

1. Match the account to the paperwork

Open the locked-in type named in your pension documents, under the right jurisdiction, before you ask for the transfer. It avoids a rejected transfer and weeks of delay.

2. Pick a broker with no small-balance fee

Questrade, Wealthsimple, Qtrade, BMO and RBC charge no account fee on small LIRAs. Since you cannot top up a LIRA, that saving repeats every year.

3. Plan for the LIF from day one

All of the top six brokers here offer a LIF. Choosing one of them means your conversion later is a paperwork change, not a transfer.

4. Keep US holdings in US dollars

At Questrade or Desjardins, both the LIRA and the LIF can hold US dollars. That avoids repeated conversions over many years.

5. Use the treaty exemption

US dividends in a LIRA or LIF are exempt from US withholding once documented. Confirm your broker has the paperwork on file so 15% is not withheld in error.

6. Ask about consolidating small accounts

If you hold two small LIRAs under the same pension law, ask whether they can be combined at one broker. A larger single account may clear a fee threshold and is easier to manage.

7. Let a rebate pay for the move

Questrade rebates up to $150 per account, RBC up to $200 on $15,000 or more and Wealthsimple up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more.

8. Hold cash ahead of LIF payments

Keep the next payment in cash so you are never forced to sell on the day it is due.

9. Split LIF income at 65

Couples can allocate up to 50% of LIF payments to the lower-income spouse on Form T1032 once the transferring spouse is 65.

10. Keep your jurisdiction on file

Note which pension law applies to each locked-in account. Every future transfer, conversion and payout question starts there.

Illustrative Example: From Pension to LIRA to LIF

This example is hypothetical. Farah is 52 and leaves her employer in 2026. Her pension is federally regulated, and she chooses to move its value of $85,000 into a LIRA at a self-directed broker. She plans four ETF purchases a year and wants some US-listed holdings.

  1. Account fees. At $85,000, she is above every small-balance threshold on this page, so none of the nine brokers charges her a LIRA fee.
  2. Trading costs. Four trades cost $0.00 at Questrade, Wealthsimple, Desjardins, National Bank, Qtrade or BMO. At Scotia iTRADE they cost $39.96 (4 x $9.99).
  3. US dollars. At Questrade, her LIRA can hold US dollars at no extra cost. At Wealthsimple on Core, US-listed trades from the Canadian-dollar LIRA are converted at 1.5%, so converting $20,000 costs $300.00 ($20,000 x 1.5%); a US-dollar account costs $120 a year and lets later sales and dividends stay in US dollars.
  4. US withholding. Once her broker has the account documented, US dividends paid into her LIRA have no US tax withheld.
  5. Converting at 65. Suppose her LIF is worth $120,000 on January 1 of a year when she is 65. Under federal rules, the minimum is $4,800 ($120,000 x 4.00%). The maximum is set by the federal formula until the year she turns 90.
  6. At 71. If the LIF is worth $110,000 on January 1 of the year she is 71, the minimum is $5,808 ($110,000 x 5.28%).
  7. Splitting. From 65, she and her spouse can elect to split up to 50% of her LIF payments on Form T1032.
  8. The broker choice. A broker that offers a LIF and lets it hold US dollars, such as Questrade or Desjardins, lets her keep the same holdings in the same currency from age 52 through retirement.

Best LIRA Account FAQ

What is the best LIRA account in Canada?

On our published score, Questrade offers the best LIRA account at 4.0 / 5. It lists a LIRA, a locked-in RRSP and a LIF, charges $0 on stock and ETF trades, has no account fees and lets registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost. Wealthsimple is a close second at 4.0 / 5, and Desjardins is third at 3.9 / 5.

What is a LIRA?

A locked-in retirement account holds pension money that pension law keeps locked in, often moved from an employer's pension plan when you leave the job. You cannot add contributions or take the money out as cash the way you can with an RRSP. Later it becomes a life income fund, another locked-in income fund or a life annuity.

Can I hold a LIRA at an online broker?

Yes. Questrade, Wealthsimple, Desjardins Online Brokerage, National Bank Direct Brokerage, Qtrade, BMO InvestorLine and Scotia iTRADE list LIRAs, RBC names them in its offer terms, and CIBC lists locked-in accounts. Interactive Brokers, moomoo, Webull and TD Direct Investing did not list them on the pages we checked.

What is the difference between a LIRA and a LIF?

A LIRA holds locked-in money while it grows; nothing is paid out. A LIF is the income stage: it pays you each year an amount between a minimum and a maximum. Under federal rules, the LIF minimum is the same as a RRIF's, and the maximum is set by a formula until the year you turn 90.

How much must I withdraw from a LIF each year?

For a federally regulated LIF, the minimum is the RRIF minimum: the January 1 value times a factor for your age, such as 4.00% at 65, 5.28% at 71 and 6.82% at 80. On $100,000 at 71, that is $5,280. Provincially regulated LIFs follow provincial rules, so please confirm with your provider.

Is there a maximum I can take from a LIF?

Yes, until late in life. For a federally regulated LIF, the yearly maximum is the balance divided by an annuity factor until the year you turn 90; from then on there is no maximum. Provincially regulated LIFs have their own maximums, which vary by province.

Can I take money out of a LIRA early?

That depends on the pension law that governs your money, which may be federal or provincial. We could not verify the early-payout rules for every jurisdiction, so this page does not describe them. Please ask your LIRA provider or the pension regulator for your plan's jurisdiction before you make plans that rely on it.

Which brokers charge fees on small LIRAs?

Desjardins charges $100 a year on a LIRA under $15,000 and on a LIF under $25,000. Scotia iTRADE charges $100 under $25,000 with fewer than 12 trades a year, National Bank $100 per account root under $20,000 unless waived, and CIBC $100 at $10,000 or less. Questrade, Wealthsimple, Qtrade, BMO and RBC show no account fee.

Can a LIRA hold US dollars?

At some brokers. Questrade lets registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost, Desjardins offers US-dollar LIRAs and LIFs with no fee stated, and National Bank offers a US-dollar LIRA. Wealthsimple adds a US-dollar account to a LIRA for $10 a month on Core, but not to a LIF.

Are US dividends in a LIRA taxed by the US?

No, once your broker has the account documented. The Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest paid to retirement arrangements, and LIRAs and LIFs qualify, as do RRSPs and RRIFs. In a TFSA or non-registered account, 15% is withheld instead when a W-8BEN is on file.

How do I move my pension into a LIRA?

If your pension plan lets you move its value out when you leave, open a LIRA of the type your plan requires, then give the plan administrator the broker's transfer details. The money moves directly between institutions. Wealthsimple states that it accepts pension transfers in, and the other ranked brokers list LIRAs.

Is my LIRA protected if my broker fails?

All nine brokers here are, or belong to, CIPF members. CIPF covers missing cash and securities if a member fails, with separate $1,000,000 limits by account group; its retirement category names RRSPs, RRIFs and LIFs, so please ask your dealer which category your LIRA falls in. Market losses are not covered.
