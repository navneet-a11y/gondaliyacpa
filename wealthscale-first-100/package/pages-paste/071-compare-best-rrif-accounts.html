Withholding. No tax is withheld on the minimum. Anything above it is withheld at 10%, 20% or 30% depending on the size of the excess (5%, 10% or 15% federal in Quebec, plus 14% Quebec tax).

2. RRIF Minimum Withdrawals: Factors and Worked Amounts

The formula. Each year's minimum is the RRIF's fair market value on January 1 multiplied by a prescribed factor for your age at the start of the year (or your spouse's or partner's age, if you elected it when you set up the RRIF). Up to age 70, the factor is 1 divided by (90 minus your age). From 71, CRA's chart sets the factor. No minimum is due in the year you open the RRIF; the first one is due the following year.

3. Scheduling RRIF Withdrawals

One payment or many. The minimum is a yearly total. You can take it in a single payment or spread it across the year, and you can take more than the minimum at any time. Payment frequencies are set by each provider, so please ask your broker which schedules it offers before you set one up.

Cash has to be there on the day. At a self-directed broker, a scheduled payment can only be made from cash. If the RRIF is fully invested, you need to sell something before each payment date. Some retirees hold the next few payments in cash or short-term GICs inside the RRIF, and refill that reserve from dividends or a sale once or twice a year. The GIC vs high-interest savings comparison explains the trade-offs for that reserve.

What the cash earns. Interest on uninvested cash varies widely among RRIF brokers. The master comparison table above lists each rate: Interactive Brokers pays 1.675% on Canadian cash above CAD 13,000, Desjardins pays its prime rate minus 5.50% on balances under $50,000, five brokers pay 0% or effectively 0% on ordinary balances, and Wealthsimple, BMO and RBC do not publish a rate for trading accounts.

Withdrawal fees. BMO lists $0 for RRSP and RRIF deregistration. Interactive Brokers lists two free withdrawals a month, then CAD 2.00 by EFT or CAD 12.00 by wire. The other brokers did not publish RRIF withdrawal fees on the pages we checked, so please ask before you choose a monthly schedule.

Timing within the year. Taking the minimum late in the year leaves the money invested longer; taking it early removes the risk of having to sell after a fall in December. Neither is right for everyone. What matters is that the full minimum is paid out within the calendar year.

4. Withholding Tax on RRIF Withdrawals

No withholding on the minimum. CRA's payer guide tells RRIF payers not to withhold income tax from the minimum amount. Only the part of a year's withdrawals above the minimum is withheld, at the same rates as an RRSP lump sum, based on the size of that excess.

Worked amounts. Outside Quebec, an excess of $3,000 has $300 withheld ($3,000 x 10%), an excess of $7,800 has $1,560 withheld ($7,800 x 20%) and an excess of $20,000 has $6,000 withheld ($20,000 x 30%). A Quebec resident taking a $7,800 excess has $780 withheld federally ($7,800 x 10%) and $1,092 provincially ($7,800 x 14%), $1,872 in all.

Withholding is not your final tax. Every RRIF payment, minimum or not, is taxable income in the year you receive it, and your payer reports it on a T4RIF slip. The withholding is a prepayment. If your marginal rate is higher than what was withheld, you will owe the difference when you file; if it is lower, you get a refund. Some retirees ask their payer whether it can withhold more, so they do not face a bill in April. The RRSP tax calculator shows marginal rates by province.

7. Self-Directed RRIF Accounts and US Holdings

US dividends in a RRIF are exempt from US withholding. Article XXI(2) of the Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest paid to retirement arrangements. RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs qualify, so US-listed securities held directly in a self-directed RRIF are exempt from US withholding once your broker has the account documented. That is a real advantage over a TFSA or non-registered account, where 15% is withheld.

Holding US dollars avoids repeat conversions. If your RRIF can hold US dollars, US-listed dividends and sale proceeds can stay in US dollars until you choose to convert. Questrade allows it at no extra cost; Desjardins, National Bank and Scotia iTRADE offer US-dollar RRIFs with no US-dollar fee stated; Qtrade charges US$15 a quarter unless you have Investor Plus. Wealthsimple does not allow US dollars in a RRIF, so each US trade there is converted at 1.5%.

8. Pension Credits, Income Splitting and the OAS Recovery Tax

Pension income amount. RRIF payments are eligible pension income from age 65 (or earlier if received because of a spouse's or partner's death). They can support the federal pension income amount, which is the lesser of $2,000 and your eligible pension income.

Pension income splitting. Up to 50% of eligible pension income can be allocated to a spouse or common-law partner. RRIF and LIF payments qualify when the transferring spouse is 65 or older at year end. Both spouses must be Canadian residents on December 31, not living apart, and file a joint Form T1032.

The OAS recovery tax. If your 2026 net income is above $95,323, you repay 15% of the amount over that threshold from your Old Age Security, with the repayment taken from July 2027 to June 2028. Service Canada estimates OAS is fully recovered at $155,320 for ages 65 to 74 and $161,320 at 75 or older. A large RRIF withdrawal can push income over the threshold: net income of $100,000 is $4,677 over, so $701.55 is repaid ($4,677 x 15%).

The OAS recovery calculator below estimates how much OAS you would repay at a given net income, so you can test a RRIF withdrawal before you take it.

Putting the three together. A couple aged 65 or older can use splitting to move RRIF income from the higher-income spouse to the lower-income one, which may help keep both below the OAS threshold. Payment dates for OAS and CPP are on our CPP and OAS payment dates page.

9. Safety: CIPF and CDIC for RRIFs

CIPF. At a dealer that is a member of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund, your RRSPs, RRIFs and LIFs are covered together up to $1,000,000 if the firm fails and cash or securities go missing. That limit is separate from the $1,000,000 for general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA, FHSA) and the $1,000,000 for RESPs. CIPF does not cover market losses or crypto assets. All ten brokers on this page are, or belong to, firms on CIPF's member list.

CDIC. Deposits and GICs held in a RRIF at a CDIC member are insured in their own RRIF category, up to $100,000 per member institution. Stocks, bonds, ETFs and mutual funds are not covered by CDIC.

10. How to Choose Among RRIF Providers in Canada

Start with what the RRIF has to do. A RRIF pays you an income for the rest of your life, so the right broker is the one that makes withdrawals cheap and simple at your balance, not the one with the lowest headline commission.

  1. Size the RRIF. Under $25,000, rule out brokers whose $100 fee would apply, or check a waiver. Above it, the fees on this page mostly disappear.
  2. Decide on managed or self-directed. At $250,000, the three ranked robo-advisors cost from 0.32% to 0.75% a year including fund costs; a self-directed RRIF at a $0-trade broker costs $0 in broker fees.
  3. Check US-dollar support. If you hold US-listed investments, choose a broker that lets a RRIF hold US dollars. Wealthsimple does not.
  4. Check spousal needs. If you will convert a spousal RRSP, confirm the broker offers a spousal RRIF; National Bank lists one and Wealthsimple does not.
  5. Look at cash and payments. Compare interest on cash and any withdrawal fees, and ask which payment schedules are available.
  6. Confirm the minimum setup. Decide whether to elect a younger spouse's age before you sign; it cannot be added later.
  7. Plan the transfer. Note the transfer-out fee at your current firm and any rebate at the new one.

Keeping everything together. If you also hold a LIRA or LIF, a broker that offers both RRIFs and locked-in accounts keeps statements and withdrawals in one place. Our best LIRA accounts ranking lists them.

11. Converting an RRSP or Moving a RRIF

The age 71 deadline. December 31 of the year you turn 71 is the last day you can contribute to your own RRSP. By then, you choose to withdraw the money (with tax withheld), transfer it to a RRIF or buy an annuity. Amounts transferred directly to a RRIF have no tax withheld; they are taxed later as payments come out. You can also open a RRIF earlier: the prescribed factors start well before 71.

Converting at the same broker. Converting at the firm that already holds your RRSP is usually the simplest route; ask whether your investments can move across as they are. Your first minimum is due the year after the RRIF is set up, which gives one year of growth before payments must start. Our RRSP guide covers the options at 71 in more detail.

Moving a RRIF to another broker. The new broker requests the transfer with its form, so the money stays registered. Please ask both firms which one will pay any minimum still owed for the year of the move.

10 Strategies to Keep More of Your RRIF

1. Elect the younger age at setup

If your spouse or partner is younger, base the minimum on their age when you open the RRIF. At 72 against 65, the minimum on $250,000 falls from $13,500 to $10,000.

2. Clear the $25,000 line or avoid it

Desjardins charges $100 a year under $25,000, Scotia iTRADE $100 under $25,000 with fewer than 12 trades, and National Bank $100 under $20,000. Consolidating two small RRIFs at one broker can clear the line; otherwise choose a broker with no RRIF fee.

3. Keep US dollars as US dollars

Hold US-listed investments at a broker that lets the RRIF keep US dollars. Convert only when you need Canadian dollars for a payment, not on every trade.

4. Use the treaty exemption

US dividends paid into a RRIF are exempt from US withholding once the account is documented. Make sure your broker has your documentation on file so 15% is not withheld in error.

5. Hold a payment reserve

Keep the next few payments in cash or short GICs inside the RRIF so you never have to sell on a bad day. Where cash earns 0%, consider GICs for that reserve.

6. Split pension income at 65

Allocate up to 50% of RRIF income to a lower-income spouse with Form T1032. It can lower the couple's total tax and help both stay under the OAS threshold.

7. Test big withdrawals against $95,323

Before taking a large amount above the minimum, add it to your 2026 net income. Each dollar over $95,323 costs 15 cents of OAS.

8. Move surplus withdrawals to a TFSA

If you take more than you spend, a TFSA can shelter it again: the 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000. Our TFSA guide explains how room builds.

9. Plan for tax at filing time

If the 10%, 20% or 30% rates, or no withholding on the minimum, will leave you owing tax, set money aside or ask your payer whether it can withhold more, so you are not hit with one large bill.

10. Review the broker every few years

As the RRIF shrinks, a fee threshold you once cleared may apply again. Recheck fees, cash interest and US-dollar support against this list.

Illustrative Example: A $300,000 RRIF at Age 72

This example is hypothetical. Gordon lives in Ontario and turned 72 in 2025, so he is 72 at the start of 2026. He converted his RRSP to a RRIF in 2024, the year he turned 71, without electing the age of his wife, Linda, who is 66 at the start of 2026. His RRIF is worth $300,000 on January 1, 2026. His other income for 2026, from CPP, OAS and a small pension, is $70,000.

  1. This year's minimum. $300,000 x 5.40% = $16,200, or $1,350 a month if paid monthly ($16,200 / 12).
  2. What the election would have saved. Using Linda's age of 66, the factor would be 4.17%: $300,000 x 4.17% = $12,510, or $1,042.50 a month. The election can only be made at setup, so it is too late for this RRIF.
  3. Taking more. Gordon wants $24,000 this year. The excess over the minimum is $7,800 ($24,000 - $16,200). No tax is withheld on the $16,200 minimum; the $7,800 excess falls in the over $5,000 band, so $1,560 is withheld ($7,800 x 20%).
  4. The OAS check. His 2026 net income would be $94,000 ($70,000 + $24,000), below the $95,323 threshold, so no OAS is repaid. Had he taken $30,000, his income would be $100,000, which is $4,677 over, and $701.55 of OAS would be repaid ($4,677 x 15%).
  5. Splitting. Because Gordon is 65 or older, he and Linda can elect to split up to 50% of his RRIF income on Form T1032, which may lower their combined tax.
  6. The broker question. Gordon holds $40,000 of US-listed ETFs. At a broker where his RRIF holds US dollars, the dividends arrive in US dollars with no US withholding once documented. At Wealthsimple, where a RRIF cannot hold US dollars, buying $20,000 more of US-listed ETFs would cost $300.00 to convert ($20,000 x 1.5%).

Best RRIF Account FAQ

What is the best RRIF account in Canada?

On our published score, Interactive Brokers Canada offers the best RRIF account at 4.4 / 5, thanks to the lowest average cost in our two test profiles, conversion with no markup and interest on cash above CAD 13,000. Questrade and Wealthsimple follow at 4.0 / 5. Questrade lets a RRIF hold US dollars at no extra cost; Wealthsimple does not.

How much do I have to withdraw from a RRIF each year?

The minimum is the RRIF's value on January 1 times a factor for your age: 4.00% at 65, 5.00% at 70, 5.28% at 71, 5.40% at 72, 6.82% at 80 and 20.00% from 95. On $100,000 at 72, that is $5,400. No minimum is due in the year you open the RRIF.

Is tax withheld on RRIF withdrawals?

Not on the minimum amount. Withdrawals above the minimum are withheld at 10% on an excess of $5,000 or less, 20% over $5,000 up to $15,000 and 30% over $15,000 outside Quebec. In Quebec, federal rates are 5%, 10% and 15%, plus 14% Quebec tax. All RRIF payments are still taxable income.

Can I hold US dollars in a RRIF?

At some brokers. Questrade lets registered accounts hold US dollars at no extra cost, Desjardins, National Bank and Scotia iTRADE offer US-dollar RRIFs with no US-dollar fee stated, and Qtrade charges US$15 a quarter unless you have Investor Plus. Wealthsimple does not allow US dollars in a RRIF.

Does Wealthsimple offer a spousal RRIF?

No. Wealthsimple says a spousal RRIF is currently unavailable, and a US-dollar account cannot be added to its RRIFs. It does offer regular RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs with no account fees and $0 stock and ETF trades. National Bank Direct Brokerage lists a spousal RRIF, and CI Direct Investing lists a spousal RIF.

Which RRIF providers charge fees on small balances?

Desjardins charges $100 a year on RRIFs under $25,000, Scotia iTRADE $100 under $25,000 with fewer than 12 trades a year, National Bank $100 per account root under $20,000 unless a waiver applies, and CIBC $100 at $10,000 or less. Questrade, Wealthsimple, Qtrade, BMO and RBC show no RRIF account fee.

Can I use my spouse's age for the RRIF minimum?

Yes, if you elect it when you set up the RRIF. A younger spouse's age gives a lower factor: at 72 against 65, the minimum on $250,000 falls from $13,500 to $10,000. The election cannot be added after the RRIF is set up, so please decide before you sign.

Is a self-directed RRIF better than a managed one?

It depends on whether you want to choose and sell investments yourself. On $250,000, the three ranked robo-advisors cost $800 to $1,875 a year including fund costs, while a self-directed RRIF at a $0-trade broker with no account fee costs $0 in broker fees plus the costs of the funds you pick.

Are US dividends in a RRIF taxed by the US?

No, once your broker has your account documented. The Canada-US tax treaty exempts dividends and interest paid to retirement arrangements such as RRSPs, RRIFs, LIRAs and LIFs. In a TFSA, RESP or non-registered account, 15% is withheld instead when a W-8BEN is on file.

When do I have to convert my RRSP to a RRIF?

By December 31 of the year you turn 71, the last day you can contribute to your own RRSP. You can withdraw the money, transfer it to a RRIF or buy an annuity. A direct transfer to a RRIF has no tax withheld, and the first minimum is due the year after the RRIF is set up.

Can RRIF withdrawals reduce my OAS?

Yes. RRIF payments are income, and if your 2026 net income is above $95,323, you repay 15% of the excess from your OAS between July 2027 and June 2028. Net income of $100,000 means $701.55 repaid. Pension income splitting with a spouse can help keep each person below the threshold.

Is my RRIF protected if my broker fails?

At a CIPF member dealer, your RRSPs, RRIFs and LIFs are covered together up to $1,000,000 for missing cash and securities, separate from your other accounts. CIPF does not cover market losses. Deposits and GICs in a RRIF at a CDIC member are insured up to $100,000 per institution in the RRIF category.
