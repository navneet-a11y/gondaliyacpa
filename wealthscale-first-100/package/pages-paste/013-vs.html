TFSA vs RRSP. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000, and someone eligible since 2009 who has never contributed has $109,000 of room. The 2026 RRSP limit is 18% of your 2025 earned income, up to $33,810. An RRSP deduction is worth most when your tax rate now is higher than it will be when you withdraw; a TFSA withdrawal is never taxed and the amount is added back to your room on January 1 of the next year.

FHSA vs RRSP and FHSA vs TFSA. An FHSA gives $8,000 of room a year up to $40,000 for life, a deduction like an RRSP and a tax-free qualifying withdrawal like a TFSA. The Home Buyers' Plan lets you take up to $60,000 from an RRSP but must be repaid over 15 years; for first withdrawals from 2022 to 2028, repayment starts in the fifth year after the first withdrawal. You can use an FHSA and the HBP for the same home, and an unused FHSA can move to an RRSP without using RRSP room.

GIC vs HISA. In our rate tables on October 22, 2026, the best 5-year GIC paid 4.50% (Oaken Financial and Saven Financial), the best 1-year GIC 3.85% (Hubert Financial and Outlook Financial) and the best everyday savings account without a paid plan 2.85% (Saven Financial). CDIC covers eligible deposits and GICs at member institutions up to $100,000 per insured category; some providers in our tables, such as Hubert Financial and Outlook Financial, are covered by a provincial deposit insurer instead. 10. How to Compare Two Platforms in 6 Steps

Use the same yardstick on both. A fair comparison prices both platforms for your own pattern and checks the same features in the same order. Please work through these steps with your real numbers.

  1. Write down your pattern. Note your Canadian and US trades a month, how much you convert to US dollars a year, any options trades and your balance.
  2. Price both in the calculator. Enter that pattern in the yearly cost calculator above and note both totals, including account fees you would not avoid.
  3. Check the accounts. Confirm both offer every account you need: TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, RESP, spousal RRSP or LIRA.
  4. Compare US dollar handling. Look at the conversion fee and whether you can hold US dollars in registered accounts, and at what cost.
  5. Compare the scores that match you. Use the overall score for a general choice, the US stocks, beginners or options score for a specific need, and read each review's cons.
  6. Check safety and the switch. Confirm CIRO and CIPF membership, then check the transfer-out fee at your current broker and whether the new one covers it.

Broker Comparison: FAQ

Is Wealthsimple or Questrade better?

They are nearly tied at 4.0 / 5, with Questrade ahead by 0.025 on the unrounded score. Questrade wins for US stocks because it includes US dollars in registered accounts at no cost. Wealthsimple wins for beginners, has a lower margin rate and charges $0 to transfer out. Their trading costs are the same in all three of our profiles.

Is Interactive Brokers better than Wealthsimple?

On our overall score, yes: 4.4 / 5 against 4.0 / 5. Interactive Brokers Canada costs $2.00 to convert $10,000 against $150.00 and has a 3.675% CAD margin rate. Wealthsimple charges $0 commissions, lists every account type including an RESP and a LIRA, and has the higher beginners score, 4.5 / 5 against 4.4 / 5.

Is Questrade or Interactive Brokers better for US stocks?

Both score 4.0 / 5 on our US stocks score, and Questrade is ahead on the unrounded figures, 4.035 against 4.013. Questrade charges $0 US commissions and includes US dollars in registered accounts. Interactive Brokers Canada charges far less to convert currency, a US$2 minimum per conversion, but does not state USD pricing for registered accounts.

Is Wealthsimple or moomoo better for a beginner?

Wealthsimple scores 4.5 / 5 for beginners against moomoo Canada's 3.3 / 5. Wealthsimple offers fractional shares from $1, a DRIP, $0 commissions and every account type. moomoo charges per share, with minimums of CA$1.49 and US$1.99, and does not offer fractional shares, a DRIP, an FHSA or an RESP.

Should I choose Wealthsimple Chequing or EQ Bank?

EQ Bank scores 4.6 / 5 against 4.4 / 5. With your pay direct deposited, EQ pays 2.75% against Wealthsimple's 1.75% on Core. Without a pay deposit, Wealthsimple's 1.25% beats EQ's 1.00%, and Generation clients earn 2.25%. EQ is a CDIC member itself; Wealthsimple holds deposits in trust at CDIC members and offers a chequebook with direct deposit.

Should I use a TFSA or an RRSP first?

It depends on your tax rate. An RRSP deduction is worth most when your rate now is higher than your expected rate when you withdraw. A TFSA suits a lower rate now, or money you may need, because withdrawals are tax-free and the room comes back the next January 1. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000; the RRSP limit is 18% of 2025 earned income, up to $33,810.

Can I use an FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?

Yes. You can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and an RRSP Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal for the same home. The FHSA gives up to $40,000 of lifetime room that never has to be repaid, and the HBP allows up to $60,000 from your RRSP that must be repaid over 15 years.

How do you decide the winner of a comparison?

Each category goes to the provider with the better published figure or score, using the same rules as our rankings. When two scores show the same number to one decimal, the higher unrounded score wins. Payment never affects the result; WealthScale has no paid or affiliate links today.

Can I compare two brokers that do not have a versus page?
