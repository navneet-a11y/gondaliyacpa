1. How to Use the FHSA Calculator

Year you opened your first FHSA. This FHSA contribution calculator starts from one date. Pick the calendar year your first FHSA was opened, not the year you became eligible. Room does not build before you open an account, so this one input sets everything else. If you have not opened one yet, choose 2026 to see what opening this year gives you.

Year you were born. The calculator uses this to find the year you turn 71, which can end your FHSA before its 15th anniversary.

Contributions by year. For each year from your opening year to 2026, enter what you put in. Include transfers from an RRSP, because they count toward your $40,000 lifetime limit. For 2026, enter what you have contributed so far this year. Leave out investment growth: only money going in counts.

Reading the results. "FHSA room left in 2026" is what you can still contribute before December 31, 2026. "Room for 2026" is your total room for the year before contributions. "Lifetime room left" is $40,000 minus everything you have put in. "Room on January 1, 2027" assumes no more contributions this year. If what you entered is more than your room, the calculator flags the excess, which is taxed at 1% a month until it is removed.

Where the numbers come from. Your statements from each FHSA issuer show what you contributed. If you hold more than one FHSA, please add the contributions from every account together, because your room is shared across all of them.

What it does not do. The room calculator does not check eligibility or estimate tax. Use the FHSA eligibility checker further down this page for the first and the province table in section 5 for the second. For other accounts, our investing, savings and tax calculators include a TFSA contribution room calculator that works the same way.

2. The Rules Behind the Numbers

The formula. Your room for a year is $8,000 plus your unused room from the year before, with the unused part capped at $8,000, and never more than your lifetime room left. In the year you open your first FHSA, the carry-forward is $0, so your room is $8,000.

The lifetime cap overrides carry-forward. Once your contributions are within $16,000 of $40,000, your room can be smaller than $8,000 plus the carry-forward. Someone who has put in $32,000 has $8,000 of lifetime room left, so their room is $8,000 even with $8,000 carried forward.

Growth is free of the cap. Investment returns do not use room. A $40,000 account that grows to $46,000 has used $40,000 of lifetime room, and the full $46,000 can come out tax-free in a qualifying withdrawal for a home.

Calendar years only. Room is counted by calendar year. Opening on December 15 gives you that year's $8,000. Contributing on January 5 uses the new year's room and counts as a contribution for the new year, for both room and the deduction. How we source and date these figures is set out in our editorial standards.

Why 2023, 2024 and 2025 look the same. After one year with no contributions, your carry-forward hits the $8,000 cap. Each extra empty year adds nothing more to next year's room. So someone who opened in 2023 and never contributed has the same $16,000 in 2026 as someone who opened in 2025.

What opening early did gain. Compared with opening in 2026, an early opener with an empty account reaches $40,000 one year sooner, because the carry-forward adds $8,000 to 2026. Contributing $16,000 in 2026 and $8,000 in each of 2027, 2028 and 2029 reaches $40,000 in 2029 ($16,000 + $8,000 x 3 = $40,000). The lifetime limit is the same $40,000 for everyone, and the early opener's 15-year clock also ends sooner.

How the 2029 and 2030 dates work. For a 2026 opener, the maximum is $8,000 a year from 2026 to 2030 ($8,000 x 5 = $40,000), so the limit is reached in 2030. For a 2027 opener, it is 2027 to 2031.

Closing dates assume no earlier trigger. The last column shows the 15th anniversary year. Your account closes earlier if you turn 71 first, or by the end of the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. Our FHSA deadline rules explain the three triggers.

4. FHSA Carry-Forward Examples: Three Worked Cases

How to read these. Each case is hypothetical and applies the formula from section 2: room = $8,000 + the smaller of last year's unused room and $8,000, capped by lifetime room left.

Case 1: Bea, a partial year. Bea opened her first FHSA in 2024 and contributed $8,000 that year and $2,000 in 2025.

  • 2025 room: $8,000 ($8,000 + $0 unused from 2024). Unused: $6,000 ($8,000 minus $2,000).
  • 2026 room: $14,000 ($8,000 + $6,000). She contributes nothing, so $14,000 is unused.
  • 2027 room: $16,000 ($8,000 + $8,000, because only $8,000 of the $14,000 carries forward).
  • Lifetime room left: $30,000 ($40,000 minus $10,000).

Case 2: Carlos, a big catch-up year. Carlos opened in 2023 with $3,000, then contributed nothing in 2024 or 2025.

  • 2024 room: $13,000 ($8,000 + $5,000 unused from 2023).
  • 2025 room: $16,000 ($8,000 + $8,000, because only $8,000 of the $13,000 unused carries forward).
  • 2026 room: $16,000. He contributes the full $16,000, bringing his total to $19,000 ($3,000 + $16,000).
  • 2027 room: $8,000 ($8,000 + $0 unused). Lifetime room left: $21,000 ($40,000 minus $19,000).

Case 3: Dana, the lifetime cap. Dana opened in 2023 and contributed $8,000 in each of 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026, for $32,000 ($8,000 x 4). She contributes nothing in 2027.

  • 2027 room: $8,000. Lifetime room left: $8,000 ($40,000 minus $32,000).
  • 2028 room: $8,000, not $16,000. The formula gives $16,000 ($8,000 + $8,000 carried forward), but her lifetime room left is only $8,000, so the cap wins.

What the cases show. Carry-forward helps you catch up one missed year, never more. Once you are near $40,000, the lifetime cap decides. If you want to check your own numbers, the FHSA room calculator at the top of this page runs the same formula for every year since you opened.

Why Ontario dips at $100,000. At $75,000, the deduction also lowers the Ontario Health Premium by $150. At $100,000 the premium is the same $750 before and after the deduction, so the saving is smaller even though income is higher.

Carrying the deduction forward. Because you choose the year, a contribution made in a low-income year can be deducted in a higher-income year. This example is hypothetical: an Alberta resident who contributes $8,000 while earning $50,000 would save about $1,760 by deducting it now, or $2,440 by deducting it in a later year at $100,000 of income, using 2026 rates. That is $680 more ($2,440 minus $1,760) for the same contribution.

A deduction is not a refund. The figures show tax saved. Whether it arrives as a refund depends on how much tax was withheld from your pay. For other amounts, the RRSP refund calculator works for any deduction, and our FHSA deduction rules explain what can and cannot be deducted.

7. FHSA Plus the Home Buyers' Plan for Couples

Two accounts, two plans. Each partner who qualifies can open an FHSA with separate $8,000 annual and $40,000 lifetime limits, and each can withdraw up to $60,000 from their own RRSPs under the Home Buyers' Plan. FHSA and HBP withdrawals can be used for the same home if the conditions of each are met at the time of each withdrawal.

The repayment difference. FHSA withdrawals are never repaid. HBP withdrawals are repaid over 15 years, starting in the second year after the first withdrawal. For first withdrawals from 2022 to 2025, repayment starts in the fifth year, and the CRA says that relief has been extended to first withdrawals from January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2028. A missed repayment is added to income.

The spouse rule for couples. If one partner owns the home you both live in, neither of you passes the FHSA opening test until the look-back clears. A partner who opens an FHSA before moving into the other's home keeps it open. Our FHSA spouse rule page covers every combination.

8. FHSA Eligibility and the Closing Deadline

The four opening tests. To open your first FHSA you must be a resident of Canada, at least 18 (19 where that is the age of majority), no older than 71 at December 31 of the year you open, and a first-time home buyer. That last test means you did not live in a home owned by you or your current spouse or common-law partner in the current year before opening or the previous four calendar years.

The four-question checker below gives a quick read on whether you can open an FHSA today.

The closing deadline. Your FHSA must be closed by December 31 of the year in which the earliest of three things happens: its 15th anniversary, the year you turn 71, or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. Anything left after that loses FHSA status, and its fair market value is added to your income.

9. Common FHSA Calculator Mistakes

Entering the year you became eligible. Room starts when you open your first FHSA, not when you could have. Entering an earlier year overstates your room and can lead to an over-contribution taxed at 1% a month.

Assuming carry-forward stacks up. Unused room carries forward only up to $8,000. Two empty years give $16,000 of room, not $24,000.

Leaving out RRSP transfers. Money moved from an RRSP into an FHSA counts toward the $40,000 lifetime limit. Enter it with your contributions for that year.

Counting a January deposit for last year. The FHSA has no 60-day period. A deposit made on February 10 counts for the year it was made, for both room and the deduction.

Reading a projection as a promise. The balance table and the first-home planner use example returns. Actual returns can be higher, lower or negative.

Forgetting more than one account. If you have FHSAs at two institutions, add both sets of contributions together. Your room is shared across all of them.

8 Ways to Get More From Your FHSA Room

1. Open now if you qualify

Room does not build until you open. Opening in 2026 gives $8,000 of room this year and lets you carry up to $8,000 into 2027, so you can contribute $16,000 next year if this year is tight.

2. Never leave more than one year unused

Carry-forward stops at $8,000. A second empty year adds nothing to next year's room and pushes the date you reach $40,000 later, closer to your 15-year or age 71 deadline.

3. Contribute before December 31

There is no 60-day window. Set a reminder for early December so a holiday-season transfer still lands in time for the year's room and deduction.

4. Deduct in your highest-rate year

FHSA deductions carry forward indefinitely. Use the province table above to compare the saving at today's income with the saving at the income you expect later, using 2026 rates as a guide.

5. Count RRSP transfers against the cap

An RRSP-to-FHSA transfer uses lifetime room and is not deductible. Weigh it against simply contributing new cash, which does earn a deduction, before you move RRSP money in.

6. Pair the FHSA with the HBP

One buyer can combine $40,000 of FHSA contributions with up to $60,000 from RRSPs under the HBP. A couple can double both. Drawing on the FHSA first leaves less to repay.

7. Match investments to the purchase date

Use the projection table as a range, not a promise. Money needed within a year or two may suit savings or GICs; a longer timeline may suit a portfolio that can rise and fall.

8. Note your closing year today

The room calculator shows the year your FHSA must close. Put it in your calendar with a reminder a year earlier, so you can buy, transfer to an RRSP or RRIF, or withdraw in time.

FHSA Calculator Questions Answered

How does the FHSA calculator work out my room?

It starts with $8,000 in the year you opened your first FHSA, then for each later year adds $8,000 plus your unused room from the year before, capped at $8,000, and never more than your lifetime room left out of $40,000. It subtracts what you entered for each year to show your room left for 2026 and your room on January 1, 2027.

How much FHSA room do I have in 2026 if I opened in 2023 and never contributed?

$16,000. Your room for 2026 is the $8,000 annual amount plus $8,000 carried forward. Carry-forward is capped at $8,000, so the unused room from 2023, 2024 and 2025 does not add up to more. The same $16,000 applies if you opened in 2024 or 2025 and have not contributed.

Can FHSA carry-forward exceed $8,000?

No. Unused FHSA room carries forward only up to $8,000, so the most room you can have in one year is $16,000. Unused room above the cap does not roll into next year's limit, but it does not reduce your $40,000 lifetime limit either. You can still reach $40,000 by contributing in later years.

Do transfers from my RRSP count toward the $40,000 limit?

Yes. The $40,000 lifetime limit counts both cash contributions and amounts transferred from an RRSP into an FHSA. Unlike a cash contribution, a transfer from an RRSP is not deductible. Enter RRSP transfers with your contributions in the calculator so your lifetime room left is correct.

How much tax does an $8,000 FHSA deduction save?

It depends on your province and income. Using 2026 rates, an $8,000 deduction saves an estimated $1,674 at $50,000 of income in Ontario and $3,473 at $150,000. In Alberta the saving is $1,760 at $50,000 and $2,880 at $150,000. The province table on this page shows four income levels for every province and territory except Manitoba.

Can I carry forward my FHSA deduction?

Yes. You can deduct FHSA contributions in the year you make them or in any later year, even after your FHSA is closed. Carrying the deduction to a year with a higher income can raise its value. An Alberta resident would save $1,760 deducting $8,000 at $50,000 of income, or $2,440 at $100,000, using 2026 rates.

How much can a couple use from FHSAs and the Home Buyers' Plan?

Up to $200,000 before growth if both partners qualify: $40,000 of FHSA contributions and up to $60,000 of HBP withdrawals each. FHSA withdrawals for a qualifying home are never repaid. HBP withdrawals are repaid over 15 years, at a minimum of $4,000 a year each after a full $60,000 withdrawal.

When does my FHSA have to be closed?

By December 31 of the year in which the earliest of three events happens: the 15th anniversary of opening your first FHSA, the year you turn 71, or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. An FHSA opened in 2026 by someone born in 1957 must close by December 31, 2028, the year they turn 71.

Does the projected balance include tax?

No tax is taken off the projection, because growth inside an FHSA is not taxed and a qualifying withdrawal for a first home is tax-free. The returns used are examples, not forecasts. If the money is not used for a home, a taxable withdrawal is added to income, while a direct transfer to an RRSP or RRIF is taxed only when withdrawn later.

What happens if I contribute more than my FHSA room?

The excess is taxed at 1% a month on the highest excess amount in each month until it is removed. You can fix it with a designated withdrawal or transfer using Form RC727, a taxable withdrawal, or new room on January 1. A $2,000 excess costs $20 for each month it remains.
