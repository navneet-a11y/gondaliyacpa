Managed. A managed TFSA invests in a Wealthsimple portfolio. Redemptions happen in monthly liquidity windows and may be paused. It is a very different product from an ETF portfolio, with higher fees and less access to your money.

3. Crypto, Options and Margin in a Wealthsimple TFSA

No crypto in the TFSA. Wealthsimple offers crypto only in a non-registered crypto account. You cannot hold crypto in a Wealthsimple TFSA, RRSP or FHSA. Crypto bought in a non-registered account has no TFSA tax shelter, so gains are taxable, and the coins are not covered by CIPF or CDIC. Our Wealthsimple crypto review covers that account.

Options are allowed, with limits. Listed options are qualified TFSA investments under CRA rules. In a registered account such as a TFSA, Wealthsimple allows four strategies: buying calls, buying puts, selling cash-secured puts and selling covered calls. All other strategies need a margin account. Equity and ETF options cost $0 commission and US$0 per contract on every tier.

Margin buying power. Wealthsimple lets you link a TFSA to a margin account for buying power. The borrowing happens in the margin account, which is not a TFSA, so interest is charged there: 4.95% on Core, 4.45% on Premium and 3.95% on Generation for Canadian dollars. Borrowed money is not tax-sheltered and adds risk.

Line of credit collateral. A TFSA can be pledged as collateral for Wealthsimple's portfolio line of credit, which lends up to 35% of eligible collateral, needs at least $1,000 of collateral, has no credit check and no minimum payment. Rates are 4.95% on Core, 4.45% on Premium and 3.95% on Generation. A limited-time offer gives 0% for 3 months on a first loan of up to $3,000. If the TFSA falls in value, the amount you can borrow falls too.

A CRA warning for active traders. CRA states that a TFSA holder who trades with the frequency and experience of a professional trader may have the income taxed as business income. CRA gives no trade count that triggers this. $0 commissions make frequent trading cheap, but they do not change the tax rule, so heavy day trading inside a TFSA carries real tax risk.

Prohibited and non-qualified holdings. A non-qualified or prohibited investment in a TFSA is taxed at 50% of its fair market value, and income on a prohibited investment attracts a 100% advantage tax. A prohibited investment is one closely connected to you, such as a 10% or larger interest in a company. Most securities listed on a designated stock exchange, including shares, ETF units and options, are qualified investments.

6. Wealthsimple TFSA USD: Holding US Dollars and US Stocks

A USD TFSA is available. Wealthsimple lets you add a USD account to a TFSA. With US dollars in the account, you can buy and sell US-listed stocks and ETFs with no conversion fee on each trade. It costs $10 a month plus tax on Core, or $120 a year before tax, after a 30-day trial, and is included on Premium and Generation, though you still opt in.

Conversion inside the USD TFSA. Moving money between the Canadian and US sides costs 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000, based on the size of each conversion. With a $7,000 yearly TFSA limit, new contributions converted one year at a time fall in the 1.5% band, so the USD TFSA saves money mainly when you convert an existing balance in one block or trade US positions often.

7. Wealthsimple TFSA Transfer: Fees, Rules and Timing

Transfer-out fees covered up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more. Wealthsimple reimburses the transfer-out fee your old institution charges, up to $150 per account, on transfers of $25,000 or more, as long as the account stays funded for 90 days. The threshold was $15,000 before April 10, 2025. Wealthsimple's TFSA page adds that conditions apply. Its pages describe the claim process in two different ways, one automatic and one asking for proof, so please confirm the steps with Wealthsimple before you start.

What you would otherwise pay. TD Direct Investing, RBC Direct Investing, Scotia iTRADE, National Bank Direct Brokerage, Questrade and Qtrade each charge $150 to transfer an account out, and Webull charges $150 for a full transfer. On a transfer of $25,000 or more, Wealthsimple's reimbursement of up to $150 per account covers a $150 fee in full. Wealthsimple itself charges $0 to transfer out, so leaving later costs nothing at its end.

Always use a direct transfer. Under CRA rules, a direct transfer between TFSAs has no effect on your contribution room. If you withdraw the money yourself and redeposit it at Wealthsimple, the redeposit counts as a new contribution, and the withdrawal is added back to your room only on January 1 of the next year. That can create an over-contribution taxed at 1% a month. Ask Wealthsimple to request the transfer from your old institution instead.

In cash or in kind. A transfer can move your holdings as they are or as cash after a sale, depending on what both institutions support. Selling inside the TFSA before a transfer has no tax effect, but you are out of the market while the cash moves. Note that fractional shares cannot be transferred from Wealthsimple to another institution, so plan to sell them if you ever move a Wealthsimple TFSA out.

Separation and divorce. A direct transfer between spouses or partners on breakdown of the relationship does not affect either person's room, if they live separate and apart and the transfer follows a court order or written separation agreement. 9. TFSA Rules Recap for 2026

The rules come from the CRA. TFSA limits and rules are set by the CRA and apply at every provider. Anyone who is a Canadian resident for tax purposes, 18 or older and has a valid social insurance number can open one. Where the age of majority is 19, you open at 19 and keep the room from the year you turned 18. Room builds from the year you turn 18, even if you do not open an account.

How your room is worked out. Your room each year is the current year's limit, plus unused room from earlier years, plus anything you withdrew in the previous year, minus what you have contributed this year. Unused room carries forward without limit. The cumulative $109,000 applies only to someone who was 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009 and never contributed. The 2027 limit has not been published yet; the limit is indexed to inflation and rounded to the nearest $500.

Newcomers and non-residents. A newcomer's room starts on the day they become resident, and only years of residency count: someone who became resident in 2024 had $7,000 of room that year, not the cumulative total. In the year you arrive or leave, you get the full annual limit. You earn no new room in a year you are a non-resident, and contributions made while non-resident are taxed at 1% a month.

Check your own room. The TFSA room calculator below works out your room from your birth year, residency and past contributions and withdrawals. See our TFSA contribution limit page and TFSA calculator guide for more.

On death. A surviving spouse or common-law partner can be named successor holder and take over the TFSA with its tax-free status; their own room is not affected if the deceased's TFSA had no excess. Other designated beneficiaries receive the value at death tax-free, while earnings after death are taxable. Quebec does not recognize TFSA successor holder designations. 10. Withdrawals, Over-Contributions and Common Mistakes

Withdrawals come back next year. You can withdraw from a Wealthsimple TFSA at any time without tax. But the amount you take out is added back to your room only on January 1 of the following year. Withdrawals do not change the current year's room.

The re-contribution trap. The mistake CRA itself warns about is withdrawing and putting money back in the same year when you have no unused room. Suppose you have used all your room, withdraw $2,000 in March and put it back in June. The June deposit is a $2,000 over-contribution until January 1, because the March withdrawal only restores room next year.

What an over-contribution costs. The tax is 1% a month on the highest excess amount in each month, with no grace amount. A $2,000 excess left for three months costs $60.00 ($2,000 x 1% x 3). Withdraw the excess as soon as you notice it, then file the TFSA Return and pay by June 30 of the following year. Use our TFSA withdrawal rules guide before you take money out.

Several TFSAs, one room. Holding a Wealthsimple TFSA alongside a bank TFSA is allowed, but your room is shared. Contributions to both count against the same limit, and neither institution knows what you put in at the other. Keep a running total of contributions across all your TFSAs.

Moving money the wrong way. Withdrawing from an old TFSA and depositing at Wealthsimple uses room, while a direct transfer does not. If you want to move a TFSA, ask Wealthsimple to request a direct transfer, as section 7 explains.

Assuming the 2027 limit. Figures for the 2027 TFSA limit circulating online are estimates. Contribute based on room you have now, and wait for the published 2027 figure before planning a January deposit above your current room.

11. Is a Wealthsimple TFSA Safe? CIPF and CDIC Cover

Investing versions: CIPF. A self-directed, managed or Automated Investing TFSA is held by Wealthsimple Investments Inc., a CIRO dealer member and CIPF member. CIPF puts the TFSA in its general group with your cash, margin and FHSA accounts, under a combined $1,000,000 limit. If the firm failed and client property were missing, CIPF would cover the missing cash and securities up to that limit.

The limit is shared. A TFSA at its full $109,000 of cumulative room, plus a $40,000 FHSA and a $600,000 non-registered account, totals $749,000 in the general group ($109,000 + $40,000 + $600,000 = $749,000), inside the $1,000,000 limit. A TFSA alone is unlikely to reach the limit, since cumulative room is $109,000; large non-registered balances are what push a client past it.

Savings version: CDIC, not CIPF. A TFSA opened as a savings account is a Registered Savings Account, covered by CDIC up to $100,000 per beneficiary in the TFSA category and not by CIPF. A savings TFSA with more than $100,000 has the excess above CDIC's limit.

No plan covers market losses. Neither CIPF nor CDIC pays if your TFSA investments fall in value. A TFSA invested in stocks can drop, and losses inside a TFSA cannot be claimed against other income, because income and losses inside a TFSA are not part of your taxable income.

How to Open or Transfer a Wealthsimple TFSA

  1. Find your room. Please check your latest notice of assessment or CRA account for your TFSA room before you contribute, and subtract anything you have put into other TFSAs this year.
  2. Choose the version. Pick self-directed, managed, Automated Investing, savings or the Private Market Fund. The version sets your fees and whether CIPF or CDIC covers the account.
  3. Open the account. Open the TFSA in the Wealthsimple app or web app. No minimum is stated.
  4. Transfer, do not withdraw. To move an existing TFSA, ask Wealthsimple to request a direct transfer from your old institution, so the move does not use room.
  5. Register for the 1% Match if it applies. If you are moving $25,000 or more, register for the Match and start the transfers within 30 days.
  6. Add USD if you hold US stocks. On Premium or Generation, opt in to the included USD TFSA. On Core, check the break-even before you pay $10 a month.
  7. Set recurring contributions. Schedule deposits daily, weekly, every two weeks or monthly, and keep the yearly total within your room.

10 Strategies for a Lower-Cost Wealthsimple TFSA

1. Favour Canadian-listed ETFs for low cost

Canadian-listed ETFs trade in Canadian dollars at $0 commission with no conversion fee. Over many years, avoiding a 1.5% fee each way on US trades keeps more of your tax-free growth. This is a general option, not a product pick.

2. Convert in one block

In a USD TFSA, one $25,000 conversion costs $125.00 at 0.5%, while two $12,500 conversions cost $250.00 at 1.0%. If you plan to move a balance into US dollars, size each conversion to the next fee threshold.

3. Keep US dividend payers in the RRSP

US dividends in a TFSA lose 15% to withholding, while an RRSP is exempt under the treaty once documented. If you hold both accounts, place US-listed dividend payers in the RRSP where you can.

4. Move accounts together

The 1% Match counts TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, non-registered and corporate transfers. Moving several accounts at once can clear the $25,000 minimum and the transfer-fee reimbursement threshold together.

5. Always transfer directly

A direct transfer does not use room; withdrawing and redepositing does. A $2,000 accidental over-contribution held three months costs $60.00 in tax.

6. Use household assets for Premium

Combined household assets of $100,000 cut the managed fee from 0.5% to 0.4% and include the USD TFSA. On a $109,000 managed TFSA, that saves $109.00 a year in management fees.

7. Compare managed and Automated Investing

On $50,000, the Core managed fee is $250.00 a year, while Automated Investing is $125.00. Check which service matches what you want before choosing the higher fee.

8. Contribute early in the year

New room arrives every January 1. Contributing earlier gives tax-free growth more time to work, while keeping within your room.

9. Keep a contribution log

Wealthsimple cannot see what you put into a TFSA elsewhere. Track every contribution and withdrawal across all your TFSAs so you never exceed your shared room.

10. Watch for trading-business risk

$0 commissions make frequent trading cheap, but CRA can tax a TFSA holder who trades like a professional on business income. Long-term investing keeps the TFSA's tax-free status clear.

Illustrative Example: Moving a TFSA to Wealthsimple

This example is hypothetical. Aisha, 36, has a $36,000 TFSA at TD Direct Investing, where she pays $9.99 per trade and makes 12 trades a year. She has $7,000 of unused 2026 room. She moves her TFSA to Wealthsimple on the Core tier and registers for the 1% Match. Fees and terms are as of October 22, 2026, and we ignore investment returns.

  1. TD charges $150 to transfer out. Because the transfer is $25,000 or more and she keeps the account funded for 90 days, Wealthsimple reimburses up to $150 per account, which covers the fee: ($150 − $150 = $0) net cost.
  2. 1% Match on the $36,000 transfer: ($36,000 x 1% = $360.00), paid as 24 monthly payments of ($360.00 ÷ 24 = $15.00) into Wealthsimple chequing.
  3. She contributes her $7,000 of 2026 room, for a TFSA of ($36,000 + $7,000 = $43,000).
  4. Her old trading cost at TD: (12 x $9.99 = $119.88) a year. At Wealthsimple, 12 trades of listed Canadian stocks and ETFs cost $0.00.
  5. If she chose a managed TFSA instead: Wealthsimple fee ($43,000 x 0.5% = $215.00) plus Classic fund costs of up to ($43,000 x 0.15% = $64.50), for up to ($215.00 + $64.50 = $279.50) a year.
  6. If she chose Automated Investing: ($43,000 x 0.25% = $107.50) a year.

Because she moved the account by direct transfer, the $36,000 did not use any TFSA room, and her $7,000 contribution used only her 2026 room. If she later needs to withdraw within 730 days, she should check the Match's 20% withdrawal buffer first.

Wealthsimple TFSA FAQ

Is the Wealthsimple TFSA good?

For most investors, yes. Wealthsimple scores 4.0 / 5 in our ranking, third of 13 brokers that offer a TFSA, as of October 22, 2026. It charges $0 commission on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs, $0 account fees and $0 to transfer out. Its main cost is the 1.5% currency conversion fee on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account.

What are the Wealthsimple TFSA fees?

A self-directed TFSA has $0 commission on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs and 1.5% to convert currency on US trades from a CAD account. A managed TFSA costs 0.5% a year on Core and 0.4% on Premium and Generation, plus fund costs. Automated Investing costs 0.25%, capped at $250 a year. Opening, closing and transferring out cost $0.

Can I hold crypto in a Wealthsimple TFSA?

No. Wealthsimple offers crypto only in a non-registered crypto account, so crypto cannot be held in a Wealthsimple TFSA, RRSP or FHSA. Gains on crypto in a non-registered account are taxable, and the coins are not covered by CIPF or CDIC.

Can I hold US dollars in a Wealthsimple TFSA?

Yes. A USD TFSA costs $10 a month plus tax on Core after a 30-day trial and is included on Premium and Generation. It lets you trade US-listed securities with no conversion fee on each trade. US dividends in a TFSA are still withheld at 15% with a W-8BEN, and that tax cannot be recovered.

How do I transfer my TFSA to Wealthsimple?

Open a Wealthsimple TFSA and ask Wealthsimple to request a direct transfer from your old institution. A direct transfer does not affect your TFSA room. Do not withdraw and redeposit the money yourself: the redeposit would count as a new contribution and could create an over-contribution taxed at 1% a month.

Does Wealthsimple pay my TFSA transfer fee?

Yes, on transfers of $25,000 or more. Wealthsimple reimburses the transfer-out fee your old institution charges, up to $150 per account, if the account stays funded for 90 days. Its pages describe the claim process differently, so confirm the steps first. Wealthsimple charges $0 to transfer out.

Does the 1% Match apply to TFSA transfers?

Yes. Transfers into a Trade or Managed TFSA count toward Wealthsimple's 1% Match. You need at least $25,000 of net qualifying transfers, started within 30 days of registering. The bonus is 1% on up to $2,000,000, paid over 24 months into Wealthsimple chequing, with a 20% withdrawal buffer for 730 days.

What is the TFSA limit for 2026?

The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000. Someone who was 18 or older and resident in Canada every year since 2009 and never contributed has $109,000 of cumulative room. Withdrawals are added back on January 1 of the following year. The 2027 limit has not been published yet.

Is a Wealthsimple TFSA protected?

The investing versions sit in CIPF's general group with your cash, margin and FHSA accounts, sharing a $1,000,000 limit if the firm fails and property is missing. A TFSA savings account is covered by CDIC up to $100,000 and not by CIPF. Neither plan covers market losses.

Can I trade options in a Wealthsimple TFSA?

Yes, with limits. In a registered account such as a TFSA, Wealthsimple allows buying calls, buying puts, selling cash-secured puts and selling covered calls. Other strategies need a margin account. Equity and ETF options cost $0 commission and US$0 per contract.

Does withdrawing from my Wealthsimple TFSA reduce my room?

Not permanently. A withdrawal is added back to your room on January 1 of the following year. Within the same year it does not restore room, so putting the money back before January can cause an over-contribution if you have no other unused room.

Is there a minimum for a Wealthsimple TFSA?

No minimum is stated, and Wealthsimple's Core tier starts at $1 in assets. Fractional shares let a self-directed TFSA invest from $1. The Private Market Fund version is different: it has a $10,000 minimum and requires $50,000 of investable assets.

Is Wealthsimple or Questrade better for a TFSA?

Both score 4.0 / 5, with Questrade ahead on its unrounded score. Both charge $0 commissions. Wealthsimple charges $0 to transfer out and offers managed and Automated Investing TFSAs, but charges Core clients $10 a month for USD. Questrade lets registered accounts hold US dollars with no separate fee but charges $150 to transfer out.
