TFSA vs RRSP: Which Should You Use First?

The TFSA vs RRSP choice comes down to one test: compare your tax rate today with the rate you expect when you take the money out. The RRSP usually wins when today's rate is clearly higher. The TFSA usually wins when the rates are similar, when you may need the money before retirement, or when RRSP withdrawals would shrink benefits such as Old Age Security. For 2026, the TFSA limit is $7,000 and the RRSP limit is 18% of 2025 earned income up to $33,810. This page compares the two accounts on 20 points and works out the tax at four incomes in four provinces.

At a glance

The short answer. An RRSP gives you a deduction now and taxes the money later. A TFSA gives no deduction but never taxes the money again. If your marginal tax rate is the same at both points, the two accounts leave you with exactly the same after-tax amount.

When the RRSP tends to win. When your marginal rate today is clearly higher than your expected rate at withdrawal, for example a mid-career earner who expects a modest retirement income, and when an employer matches group RRSP contributions.

When the TFSA tends to win. When your income is low or your rate later may be as high as today, when you may need the money before retirement, and when RRSP or RRIF withdrawals would trigger the OAS recovery tax above $95,323 of 2026 net income or reduce the Guaranteed Income Supplement.

2026 room. TFSA: $7,000 for 2026, and $109,000 in total for someone who has been 18 or older and resident since 2009. RRSP: 18% of 2025 earned income up to $33,810, plus unused room. CRA has published the 2027 RRSP dollar limit of $35,390; the 2027 TFSA limit has not been published yet.

Buying a first home. The FHSA combines a deduction with a tax-free withdrawal, the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan lends you up to $60,000 of your own RRSP, and the TFSA can be used for anything. All three can work together for one purchase.

1. TFSA vs RRSP: The Short Answer

It is a tax-rate question. Both accounts shelter investment growth from tax. The difference is when you pay tax on the money you put in. With a TFSA you pay it now, at today's rate, before you contribute. With an RRSP you pay it later, at the rate that applies when you withdraw. Whichever rate is lower is the better deal.

The RRSP is the better fit when your marginal rate today is clearly above the rate you expect in retirement, you are unlikely to need the money before retirement, an employer matches your contributions, or you want to hold US-listed dividend payers without the 15% US withholding.

The TFSA is the better fit when your income is low or modest, you expect a similar or higher income later, you may need the money for a car, a job loss, a business or a home, or your retirement income will be high enough that RRSP withdrawals would trigger the OAS recovery tax or reduce income-tested benefits.

Many people should use both. The accounts are not rivals. A common pattern is to contribute to the RRSP, then put the tax refund into the TFSA. That captures the deduction and builds a flexible tax-free pool at the same time. The sections below show the arithmetic behind each rule.

What this page does not do. It explains rules and works through hypothetical numbers. It does not recommend any investment, and the right split for you depends on your income, pension, benefits and plans. Our guides to the TFSA and the RRSP cover each account in depth.

2. How Each Account Works

The TFSA. A tax-free savings account is open to residents of Canada who are 18 or older (19 in provinces where that is the age of majority) and have a social insurance number. You contribute money you have already paid tax on. Nothing earned inside is ever taxed, and withdrawals are tax-free for any purpose. Every dollar you withdraw is added back to your room on January 1 of the next year. The 2026 limit is $7,000; someone who has been 18 or older and resident every year since 2009 has $109,000 of total room if they have never contributed.

The RRSP. A registered retirement savings plan builds room from earned income: 18% of last year's earned income, up to $33,810 for 2026, less any pension adjustment, plus unused room. Contributions are deducted from income, growth is tax-deferred, and every withdrawal is taxed as income. Withdrawals do not create new room. By the end of the year you turn 71, the RRSP must become a RRIF or annuity, or be cashed out.

Same qualified investments. Both accounts can hold the same range of investments: cash, GICs, listed stocks and ETFs, bonds and funds. CRA says the qualified investments for a TFSA are generally the same as for an RRSP. Investment choice therefore does not decide between them, with one exception covered below: US dividends.

Same protection. At a CDIC member, deposits and GICs in a TFSA and in an RRSP are each insured up to $100,000 as separate categories. At a CIPF member dealer, a TFSA falls under general accounts and an RRSP under retirement accounts, each covered up to $1,000,000 combined if the firm fails.

Where to open them. Most banks, brokers and robo-advisors offer both, often in one app. Compare costs in our rankings of the best TFSA accounts and the best RRSP accounts, which follow our published broker rating methodology. Our page on how WealthScale makes money explains our business model.

3. Tax Now vs Tax Later: The Marginal Rate Test

The core arithmetic. Take $1,000 of pre-tax salary. In a TFSA, you first pay tax at your current marginal rate, say 30%, leaving $700 to invest. In an RRSP, the deduction cancels the tax on that $1,000, so all $1,000 goes in. Suppose both investments double. The TFSA holds $1,400, tax-free. The RRSP holds $2,000, which is taxed on withdrawal. At a 30% rate on withdrawal, you keep $1,400 ($2,000 x 70%), exactly the same as the TFSA.

The rule that follows. If your rate at withdrawal is lower than your rate now, the RRSP leaves you with more. If it is higher, the TFSA does. If the rates are equal, the accounts tie. The growth rate and the number of years do not change which account wins; they only change the size of the gap.

The refund has to be invested. The tie only holds if the RRSP saver invests the whole pre-tax amount. In practice that means contributing the gross amount, or contributing the after-tax amount and then adding the refund. Someone who contributes $700 to an RRSP and spends the $210 refund ends up with less than the TFSA saver ($700 x 30% = $210).

Use marginal, not average, rates. The deduction removes your top dollars of income, so it saves your marginal rate. A withdrawal adds dollars on top of your other retirement income, so it is taxed at your marginal rate then, plus any benefit clawbacks. Comparing average rates, or comparing today's marginal rate with tomorrow's average rate, gives the wrong answer.

Estimating the later rate. Add up the income you expect in retirement: CPP, OAS, any workplace pension, rental income and the RRIF minimums your RRSP will force out. The marginal rate at that income, plus any clawback, is your later rate. People with a large defined benefit pension often find their later rate is as high as today's, which favours the TFSA.

5. Flexibility and Withdrawals

TFSA withdrawals are clean. You can take money out of a TFSA at any time, for any reason, with no tax and nothing withheld. The amount comes back as new room on January 1 of the next year. That makes the TFSA the natural home for an emergency fund, a car or renovation fund, and money for a goal that may change.

RRSP withdrawals cost twice. Outside Quebec, the issuer withholds 10% on up to $5,000, 20% over $5,000 up to $15,000, and 30% over $15,000; in Quebec the federal part is 5%, 10% and 15%, plus Quebec tax. The full amount is then added to your income, and the room is gone for good. A $10,000 RRSP withdrawal at 35 costs the tax and also $10,000 of tax-deferred space that never returns.

The re-contribution trap. TFSA room from a withdrawal returns only on January 1. Putting the money back in the same year, without other unused room, is an over-contribution taxed at 1% a month with no buffer. Please wait until January, or check your room first. Our TFSA withdrawal rules page covers the timing.

Planned versus unplanned withdrawals. An RRSP works when withdrawals are planned for low-income years: early retirement before pensions start, a sabbatical, parental leave, or a year back at school. It works badly as an emergency fund, because emergencies tend to arrive in years when you are still earning.

Forced withdrawals later. A TFSA never forces money out. An RRSP must become a RRIF by the end of the year you turn 71, and a RRIF has yearly minimums: 5.28% of the January 1 value at age 71, rising each year. Those minimums can push retirement income, and the tax rate on it, higher than expected. Our RRIF minimum withdrawal table lists every factor.

6. Effect on Benefits and Clawbacks

Why benefits change the answer. Many benefits are reduced as net income rises. RRSP and RRIF withdrawals count as income; TFSA withdrawals do not. For a retiree, that can add a benefit clawback on top of income tax, which raises the real rate on RRSP money well above the bracket rate.

OAS recovery tax. For 2026 income, Old Age Security is reduced by 15% of net income above $95,323, and it is fully recovered at $155,320 for ages 65 to 74 and $161,320 at 75 and over. This example is hypothetical: an Ontario retiree aged 70 with $95,323 of net income takes an extra $10,000 from a RRIF. Income tax rises by $3,148.00, and the OAS recovery tax adds $1,500.00 ($10,000 x 15%). That is $4,648.00 in total, or 46.48% of the withdrawal. The same $10,000 from a TFSA costs $0.

The Guaranteed Income Supplement. GIS goes to low-income OAS recipients and stops entirely for a single person with income of $23,112 or more (October to December 2026 figures). For anyone likely to qualify, RRSP savings can be partly lost to GIS reductions on top of tax, while TFSA withdrawals leave GIS untouched. This is the strongest single case for the TFSA.

The Canada Child Benefit works the other way. For working parents, an RRSP deduction lowers adjusted family net income and raises the next year's CCB. Under the formula for July 2026 to June 2027, which is based on 2025 income, a family with two children and income above $82,847 loses 5.7% of each extra dollar, so a $7,000 deduction in that band was worth $399 a year in CCB ($7,000 x 5.7%) on top of the tax saved. The figures for the benefit year based on 2026 income have not been published yet.

Plan both ends. An RRSP can win at contribution time thanks to the CCB and lose at withdrawal time because of OAS or GIS. The CPP and OAS payment dates page and our GST/HST credit page cover current amounts and dates.

7. Limits and Room

TFSA room is flat. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000 for everyone who is 18 or older and resident in Canada, whatever their income. Room starts in the year you turn 18, or the day you become resident if you arrive later. Unused room carries forward with no expiry, and withdrawals are added back the next January. The 2027 limit has not been published yet.

RRSP room follows income. The RRSP deduction limit for 2026 is 18% of 2025 earned income, capped at $33,810, minus any pension adjustment, plus unused room. Someone earning $60,000 gets $10,800 of new room ($60,000 x 18%). It takes $187,833.33 of 2025 earned income to reach the full $33,810. CRA has already published the 2027 dollar limit: $35,390.

Which gives more room. New RRSP room exceeds the $7,000 of new TFSA room only once prior-year earned income passes $38,888.89 ($7,000 / 18%), assuming no pension adjustment. Below that, the TFSA offers more new room each year. High earners can shelter far more in an RRSP, up to $33,810 for 2026 against $7,000 in a TFSA.

Over-contributions. Both accounts charge 1% a month on an excess. The RRSP allows a $2,000 buffer for people who were 18 or older last year; the TFSA has no buffer at all. TFSA excesses are reported on the TFSA return, due June 30 of the following year; RRSP excesses on Form T1-OVP within 90 days after year-end.

Where to check. Your notice of assessment shows RRSP room. TFSA room is tracked by CRA from information issuers send, which can lag recent transactions, so keep your own record. The TFSA contribution room calculator and our page on the RRSP contribution limit help you work out both.

8. Employer Matching and Group RRSPs

A match changes the maths. If your employer adds money when you contribute to a group RRSP, the match is an immediate return before any tax effect. A 50% match on $4,000 adds $2,000 at once ($4,000 x 50%). No TFSA feature competes with that, so contributions that earn the full match generally come before anything else.

How the match is taxed. Employer contributions to a group RRSP are added to your employment income on your T4 and then deducted as RRSP contributions, so they use your RRSP room. If the employer pays into a deferred profit sharing plan instead, the contribution creates a pension adjustment that reduces next year's RRSP room.

Tax relief on each pay. Payroll contributions to a group RRSP usually reduce the tax withheld from each paycheque, so the saving arrives as you go. That makes it easier to contribute the gross amount, which is what the RRSP needs to beat the TFSA.

Check the rules and the fees. Some group plans restrict withdrawals while you work there, a DPSP can require a period of service before employer money vests, and group plans can carry higher fund fees than a self-directed account. When you leave the employer, you can usually move the group RRSP to your own RRSP by direct transfer, without tax. Our guide on transferring an RRSP or investment account explains how.

Without a match. If there is no match, a group RRSP is just an RRSP with payroll convenience, and the usual rate test applies. A lower earner with no match may still be better served by a TFSA, even if the group plan is easy.

9. Buying a Home: FHSA, HBP and TFSA

The FHSA usually comes first. A First Home Savings Account gives a deduction like an RRSP and a tax-free withdrawal like a TFSA when you buy a qualifying first home. Room is $8,000 a year from the year you open it, with up to $8,000 carried forward and a $40,000 lifetime limit. Contributions must be made by December 31; there is no 60-day window. If you never buy, the balance can move to an RRSP without using RRSP room.

The Home Buyers' Plan. The HBP lets each first-time buyer withdraw up to $60,000 from RRSPs tax-free for a qualifying home and repay it over 15 years. For first withdrawals from 2022 to 2028, repayments start in the fifth year after the withdrawal, so a 2026 withdrawal is first repaid in 2031. Missed repayments are added to income. RRSP money contributed in the 89 days before an HBP withdrawal may not be deductible.

Using all three. CRA allows an FHSA qualifying withdrawal and an HBP withdrawal for the same home. A TFSA can top up the rest, with no repayment and no deadline. A couple who both qualify can each use their own FHSA, HBP and TFSA room.

TFSA vs RRSP for a down payment. If you will buy in a few years, the RRSP's value depends on the HBP, and the repayments then compete with your future RRSP contributions for 15 years. A TFSA gives tax-free growth with no repayment schedule. The RRSP route makes most sense when your income is high now and you want the deduction; the TFSA route when your income is modest or the timing is uncertain.

10. Special Cases

US dividends. RRSPs and RRIFs are exempt from US withholding under Article XXI of the Canada-US tax treaty when US-listed securities are held directly. TFSAs are not: 15% of US dividends is withheld (30% without a Form W-8BEN), and a TFSA cannot claim it back. This example is hypothetical: $1,000 of US dividends costs $150 in a TFSA and $0 in an RRSP. Our page on US stocks in a TFSA explains the detail.

Newcomers. TFSA room starts the day you become resident, with the full annual limit in the year you arrive. RRSP room needs a year of Canadian earned income and a filed return, so most newcomers can use a TFSA a year before an RRSP.

Leaving Canada. A TFSA builds no new room while you are non-resident, and contributions made then are taxed at 1% a month. Withdrawals while non-resident are not taxed in Canada, though your new country may tax the income. RRSP withdrawals while non-resident have 25% withheld unless a treaty reduces it.

At death. A TFSA can pass to a spouse named as successor holder, who simply takes it over tax-free. An RRSP's full value is generally taxed on the final return unless it rolls to a spouse or partner or a qualifying dependent child or grandchild. For a single person, a large RRSP can mean a large final tax bill; a TFSA does not.

Incorporated business owners. RRSP room comes only from salary, not dividends. An owner who pays dividends only has TFSA room but no new RRSP room, which often settles the question.

Members of generous pension plans. A large defined benefit pension both cuts RRSP room through the pension adjustment and keeps retirement income high, which raises the later tax rate. Many plan members find the TFSA the better place for extra savings.

11. Using Both Together

The refund-to-TFSA pattern. Contribute to the RRSP, then put the refund into the TFSA. This example is hypothetical: an Ontario resident earning $80,000 contributes $7,000 to an RRSP. The deduction saves $2,075.50, which goes into the TFSA. The household now has $9,075.50 working in tax-sheltered accounts ($7,000 + $2,075.50) for an out-of-pocket cost of $7,000.

Split by purpose. Many households use the TFSA for anything they may need within ten years and the RRSP for money that is truly for retirement. That keeps the RRSP out of emergencies and keeps the TFSA from being emptied by impulse.

Hold the deduction, not the money. If your income is low now but will rise, you can contribute to the RRSP and claim the deduction later. Another approach is to fill the TFSA while your income is low and move money to the RRSP in a higher-income year, when the deduction is worth more.

Plan the order of withdrawals in retirement. Drawing RRSP or RRIF money in low-income years, before CPP and OAS start, can keep later income under the OAS threshold. The TFSA then covers large one-off costs without adding to income. Holding both gives you control over your taxable income each year.

Asset location. Because RRSPs are exempt from US withholding on directly held US-listed securities, and TFSAs are not, households that hold both often keep US-listed dividend payers in the RRSP and other holdings in the TFSA. That is a general rule about tax treatment, not a recommendation of any investment.

12. Order of Priority: TFSA or RRSP First?

A general order. These rules are general information, built on the tax logic above. Your situation, including debts, pensions and benefits, can change the order.

  1. Take any employer match first. Contribute enough to a group RRSP to get the full match.
  2. Keep an emergency fund in a TFSA. Flexible, tax-free money comes before locked-away retirement money.
  3. First-time buyer? Fund the FHSA. A deduction plus a tax-free withdrawal beats either account alone for a home.
  4. Income in the first federal bracket? The 2026 first federal bracket ends at $58,523. In that bracket, the TFSA usually comes next, unless you expect very low retirement income.
  5. Income in the higher brackets? The RRSP usually comes next, and the refund goes into the TFSA.
  6. Expect GIS or a high retirement income? Lean toward the TFSA, because RRSP withdrawals would face benefit reductions or high rates.
  7. Room left in both? Fill whichever you have not used, and revisit the split when your income changes.

Revisit every year. Your marginal rate, benefits and plans change. A quick check each spring, before the RRSP deadline, keeps the split right.

13. Common TFSA vs RRSP Mistakes

Spending the RRSP refund. The RRSP only matches or beats the TFSA if the whole pre-tax amount is invested. Spending the refund turns a fair comparison into a losing one.

Using an RRSP as an emergency fund. Withdrawals during working years are taxed at your working-year rate and the room is lost. A TFSA is built for that job.

Ignoring clawbacks. A retiree with OAS and income above $95,323 of 2026 net income faces an extra 15% on RRSP withdrawals. Leaving it out of the comparison overstates the RRSP's value.

Comparing today's marginal rate with tomorrow's average rate. Withdrawals stack on top of CPP, OAS and pension income, so they are taxed at the marginal rate. Using an average rate makes the RRSP look better than it is.

Re-contributing to a TFSA too soon. Room from a withdrawal returns on January 1, not the same day. Putting it back early is an over-contribution with no buffer.

Holding US-listed dividend payers in the TFSA by default. The 15% US tax withheld in a TFSA is lost for good, while an RRSP holding the same US-listed securities directly is exempt.

10 TFSA and RRSP Strategies That Save You Tax

1. Take the full employer match

Matched group RRSP contributions give an immediate return no TFSA can equal. Contribute at least enough to get every matched dollar, then decide where the rest goes.

2. Send every RRSP refund to the TFSA

The refund is what lets the RRSP compete. Moving it into a TFSA keeps the whole pre-tax amount invested and builds a flexible pool at the same time.

3. Fill the TFSA while your income is low

Students and early-career workers often save little tax with an RRSP deduction. Filling the TFSA first, while still filing returns to build RRSP room, keeps the deduction for later years when it is worth more.

4. Deduct in the high-income year

RRSP contributions can be deducted in any later year. If a raise, bonus or capital gain is coming, holding the deduction until then raises its value.

5. Use the FHSA before either for a first home

For a first home, the FHSA gives a deduction and a tax-free withdrawal. Pair it with the Home Buyers' Plan and TFSA savings for a larger down payment.

6. Draw RRSP money early in retirement

Withdrawing RRSP or RRIF money in the years before CPP and OAS start, while income is low, can shrink later RRIF minimums and keep income under the OAS threshold.

7. Locate US-listed dividend payers in the RRSP

RRSPs are exempt from US withholding on directly held US-listed securities; TFSAs lose 15% for good. Placing such holdings in the RRSP is a rule about tax treatment, not an investment pick.

8. Even out incomes with a spousal RRSP

Contributing to a spousal RRSP for the lower-income partner can keep both retirement incomes lower, reducing tax and the chance of an OAS clawback.

9. Keep the emergency fund in the TFSA

A TFSA withdrawal has no tax, no withholding and no lost room. An RRSP withdrawal in a working year has all three costs.

10. Name a successor holder and beneficiaries

A spouse named as TFSA successor holder takes over the account tax-free, and a spouse named on an RRSP can receive it as a tax-deferred rollover. Keep both designations current.

Illustrative Example: One Saver, Three Retirements

This example is hypothetical. Sam lives in Ontario, earns $80,000 and has $7,000 of pre-tax salary to save. Tax figures use 2026 brackets for both today and retirement, with basic personal amounts, the Ontario surtax and Health Premium; age and pension credits are left out. Sam's investments double before retirement in both accounts.

  1. RRSP route. Sam contributes the full $7,000. The deduction saves $2,075.50, which exactly cancels the tax on that $7,000 of salary. The RRSP doubles to $14,000 ($7,000 x 2).
  2. TFSA route. Sam pays $2,075.50 of tax on the $7,000 first and contributes $4,924.50 ($7,000 - $2,075.50). The TFSA doubles to $9,849.00 ($4,924.50 x 2), and nothing is taxed on withdrawal.
  3. Retirement A: $40,000 of other income. Withdrawing $14,000 raises Sam's tax by $2,821.47 (tax on $54,000 minus tax on $40,000), leaving $11,178.53 ($14,000 - $2,821.47). The RRSP comes out $1,329.53 ahead ($11,178.53 - $9,849.00).
  4. Retirement B: $60,000 of other income. The withdrawal raises tax by $4,301.00, leaving $9,699.00. The TFSA comes out $150.00 ahead ($9,849.00 - $9,699.00), because the rate on the withdrawal (30.72%) is slightly above the 29.65% saved going in.
  5. Retirement C: $90,000 of other income, receiving OAS. The withdrawal raises tax by $4,317.50 and pushes net income to $104,000, which is $8,677 over the $95,323 OAS threshold ($104,000 - $95,323), adding $1,301.55 of OAS recovery tax ($8,677 x 15%). Sam keeps $8,380.95 ($14,000 - $4,317.50 - $1,301.55). The TFSA comes out $1,468.05 ahead ($9,849.00 - $8,380.95).

TFSA vs RRSP FAQ

Is a TFSA or an RRSP better?

Neither is better for everyone. If your marginal tax rate today is higher than the rate you expect when you withdraw, the RRSP usually leaves you with more. If the rates are similar or the later rate is higher, the TFSA does. If both rates are equal, the two accounts give exactly the same after-tax result, as long as the RRSP refund is invested.

Should I contribute to my TFSA or RRSP first?

As a general rule, take any employer RRSP match first, keep an emergency fund in a TFSA, and use an FHSA if you are a first-time buyer. After that, lower earners usually favour the TFSA and higher earners the RRSP, with the refund going into the TFSA. Expected benefits such as GIS or OAS can change the order.

At what income does an RRSP make sense over a TFSA?

There is no single cut-off, because it depends on your income later too. Using 2026 brackets, a $7,000 RRSP deduction saves 21.84% of the amount at $55,000 of income in Ontario but 29.65% at $80,000 and 40.23% at $120,000. The higher the saving now compared with your expected rate in retirement, the stronger the case for the RRSP.

Do TFSA withdrawals affect OAS or GIS?

No. TFSA withdrawals are not income, so they do not count toward the OAS recovery tax, the Guaranteed Income Supplement or other income-tested benefits. RRSP and RRIF withdrawals do count. For 2026 income, OAS is reduced by 15% of net income above $95,323, which can make RRSP withdrawals much more costly for retirees with higher incomes.

How much tax does an RRSP contribution save compared with a TFSA?

A TFSA contribution saves no tax going in. An RRSP deduction of $7,000 in 2026 saves $2,075.50 at $80,000 of income in Ontario, $1,974.00 in British Columbia, $2,135.00 in Alberta and $2,528.23 in Quebec. That saving is not a gift: the RRSP money is taxed when you withdraw it, so the later rate decides the true benefit.

Can I use both a TFSA and an RRSP?

Yes, and many people should. A common approach is to contribute to the RRSP, invest the tax refund in the TFSA, and keep short-term or emergency money in the TFSA. Having both also lets you control your taxable income in retirement by choosing which account to draw from each year.

Which is better for buying a home, a TFSA or an RRSP?

For a first home, the FHSA usually beats both, with a deduction and a tax-free withdrawal. The RRSP Home Buyers' Plan lets you withdraw up to $60,000 tax-free, but you must repay it over 15 years. A TFSA can be used for any home with no repayment. You can use the FHSA and the HBP for the same home and top up from a TFSA.

What happens to TFSA and RRSP room after a withdrawal?

A TFSA withdrawal is added back to your room on January 1 of the next year, so you can re-contribute it then. An RRSP withdrawal does not restore room; it is lost for good. The exceptions are Home Buyers' Plan and Lifelong Learning Plan withdrawals, which you repay to the RRSP instead.

Are US dividends taxed differently in a TFSA and an RRSP?

Yes. RRSPs and RRIFs are exempt from US withholding under Article XXI of the Canada-US tax treaty when US-listed securities are held directly. In a TFSA, 15% of US dividends is withheld (30% without a W-8BEN form) and cannot be recovered. The RRSP exemption does not reach US tax withheld inside a Canadian-listed fund.

What are the 2026 TFSA and RRSP limits?

The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000, and total room since 2009 is $109,000 for someone who has been 18 or older and resident every year. The 2026 RRSP limit is 18% of 2025 earned income up to $33,810, minus any pension adjustment, plus unused room. CRA has published the 2027 RRSP limit of $35,390; the 2027 TFSA limit is not yet published.

Should I use a TFSA or RRSP if my employer matches?

Take the match first. Employer matching on a group RRSP is an immediate return before any tax effect, which outweighs the TFSA's advantages for most people. Employer contributions use your RRSP room and are taxed as employment income, then deducted. Once you have the full match, apply the usual tax-rate test to decide where extra savings go.
