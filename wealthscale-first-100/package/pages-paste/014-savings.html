3. HISA vs GIC vs TFSA Savings

Two products and a wrapper. A high-interest savings account and a GIC are products: they decide your rate and when you can reach the money. A TFSA is a wrapper: it decides the tax. You can hold a savings account or a GIC inside a TFSA, an RRSP or an FHSA, so the real choice is which product, then which wrapper.

What the TFSA is worth. This example is hypothetical. A $7,000 TFSA contribution, the 2026 limit, in a 1-year GIC at 3.85% earns $269.50 ($7,000 x 3.85%), all tax-free. Outside a TFSA, at a 29.65% marginal rate (Ontario, $80,000 of taxable income in 2026), the tax would be $79.91 ($269.50 x 29.65%), leaving $189.59. In a 2.85% TFSA savings account the same $7,000 earns $199.50, also tax-free.

The trade-off in one line. A GIC pays more and fixes the rate; a savings account keeps the money within reach. On $10,000 for a year, a 3.85% GIC earns $385.00 and a 2.80% savings account with interest paid monthly earns $283.62. Our GIC vs HISA comparison shows the full arithmetic, the after-tax gap and how to ladder GICs.

Other wrappers. An RRSP gives a deduction now and taxes withdrawals later, which suits retirement money. An FHSA gives a deduction and a tax-free qualifying withdrawal for a first home, with $8,000 of room a year and $40,000 for life. Our TFSA guide and FHSA vs TFSA comparison explain which to fill first.

4. Where to Keep an Emergency Fund

Access comes first. An emergency fund is for the day you lose income or face a large, sudden bill. Its first job is to be there that day. That rules out non-redeemable GICs, which you cannot cash early, and investments whose value can fall just when you need them. A high-interest savings account at an insured institution does the job.

How much to hold. The right size depends on your job security, your household and your fixed costs. This example is hypothetical: a household with $3,000 of essential monthly costs that chooses a four-month buffer needs $12,000 ($3,000 x 4). At 2.80% that buffer earns $336.00 a year; at a 0.55% big-bank rate it earns $66.00.

A layered setup. Keep about a month of costs in chequing or a savings account you can reach instantly. Hold the rest in a high-interest savings account. If you are confident part of it will not be needed within 30 days, a notice account such as EQ Bank's 30 Day Notice Savings at 2.75% can hold that part. Keep the layers at CDIC members or provincially insured credit unions, and under the insurance limit.

The TFSA option. A savings account inside a TFSA can hold an emergency fund too. You can withdraw any time, the interest is tax-free, and the amount you take out comes back as room on January 1 of the next year. Re-contributing earlier in the same year can cause an over-contribution, taxed at 1% a month. See our TFSA withdrawal rules.

Use the savings goal calculator to see how much to set aside each month to reach your target. For example, $12,000 over 12 months is $1,000 a month ($12,000 ÷ 12).

6. CDIC and Provincial Deposit Insurance

The CDIC limit. The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation insures eligible deposits at member institutions up to $100,000 per insured category, per member. The $100,000 includes both principal and interest. Coverage is automatic; you do not apply for it.

The categories. These are insured separately: deposits in one name, joint deposits, deposits held in trust, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, RESPs, RDSPs and FHSAs. So one person can have $100,000 in their own name and another $100,000 in a TFSA at the same member, both fully insured.

What is covered. Savings and chequing deposits, GICs and term deposits, in Canadian or foreign currency. Not covered: mutual funds, stocks, bonds, ETFs and crypto assets.

Credit unions. Provincial credit unions are insured by their province instead. Saven Financial states its deposits are insured up to $250,000 by DICO in Ontario. Hubert Financial, Outlook Financial and Achieva Financial are covered by the Deposit Guarantee Corporation of Manitoba, which Outlook says guarantees 100% of deposits.

Fintech accounts. Some popular accounts are not held at a CDIC member directly. Wealthsimple Chequing cash sits in trust at partner banks that are members, for up to $1,000,000 combined; Wealthsimple Savings is covered by CIPF, not CDIC. KOHO and Neo are not CDIC members: KOHO balances are eligible for CDIC cover up to $100,000 through a trust when you opt in to interest, and Neo Chequing balances are eligible up to $100,000 through a member, while Neo has not stated the cover for Neo Savings. Our guide to whether Wealthsimple is safe explains the trust model.

Large balances. If you hold more than $100,000 in one category, please spread it across categories or across institutions, and leave room for interest on long GICs.

8. Promotions, Paid Plans and Notice Accounts: Read the Fine Print

Promotional rates. Banks use short promotions to win new clients. Simplii paid 4.60% for 5 months on up to $200,000 to new clients opening by October 31, 2026, then its everyday rate of 0.30% on balances up to $50,000. RBC paid 4.60% for 90 days to first-time clients opening by 3:00 PM EST on October 27, 2026, then 0.550%. Manulife paid 3.00% for 730 days on net new deposits up to $500,000 in new accounts opened by October 30, 2026. National Bank's Cash Advantage Solution paid 3% on new deposits from October 19, 2026 to February 28, 2027.

What a promotion is really worth. This example is hypothetical. On $10,000, Simplii's 4.60% for 153 days pays $192.82 ($10,000 x 4.60% x 153/365), and the remaining 212 days at 0.30% pay $17.42. The year's total, $210.24, is a 2.10% return, below an everyday 2.80% account. A promotion pays off only if you move the money when it ends.

Paid plans. KOHO's Everything plan paid 3.5% for $14.75 a month billed yearly ($177 a year). Against Saven's 2.85%, the extra 0.65% covers the fee only above $27,230.77 ($177 ÷ 0.65%). Neo's Grow membership paid 2.75% for $14.99 a month, or $179.88 a year. Compare the net amount, not the headline rate.

Notice accounts. EQ Bank's 30 Day Notice Savings paid 2.75% and its 10 Day Notice Savings 2.35% (effective May 15, 2026). You give notice before withdrawing, so they suit money you will not need within the notice period. Our EQ Bank review explains how they work.

Balance tiers. Some accounts pay little on small balances. Scotiabank's High Interest Savings Account paid 0.00% on a relationship balance under $10,000, and TD ePremium Savings paid 0.000% under $10,000. Check the rate on your balance, not the top tier.

10. How to Choose the Best Place to Save Money in Canada

  1. Sort your money by date. Split it into money for this month, money you might need at any time, and money with a known future date.
  2. Pick a no-fee everyday account. Run bills through a chequing account with no monthly fee, ideally one that pays interest. Our chequing ranking scores 20 options.
  3. Put the emergency fund in high-interest savings. Choose a top everyday rate at a CDIC member or an insured credit union. Ignore short promotions unless you will move the money when they end.
  4. Match dated money to GIC terms. For money needed in one to five years, choose GICs that mature just before the date, or build a ladder.
  5. Use the TFSA first for interest. If you have TFSA room, hold interest-earning savings inside it so the interest is tax-free.
  6. Check the insurance. Stay within $100,000 per insured category at each institution, including interest.
  7. Diary a review. Recheck your rates after Bank of Canada announcements, including October 28 and December 9, 2026, and before each GIC matures.

Best Place to Save Money in Canada: FAQ

Where is the best place to save money in Canada right now?

It depends on when you need the money. For money you may need any day, the top everyday savings rates on October 22, 2026, were 2.85% at Saven Financial and 2.80% at Oaken Financial. For money with a known date, GICs paid more: 3.85% for 1 year and 4.50% for 5 years at the top of our register.

What is the highest savings account rate in Canada?

Among accounts without a paid plan, Saven Financial's 2.85% was the highest everyday rate in our register on October 22, 2026, followed by Oaken Financial at 2.80%. KOHO paid 3.5% on its Everything plan, which costs $14.75 a month billed yearly. Short promotions reached 4.60% at Simplii and RBC for new clients.

What are the best GIC rates in Canada?

On October 22, 2026, the best rates in our register were 3.85% for 1 year at Hubert Financial and Outlook Financial, 4.25% for 2 years at Saven Financial, and 4.50% for 5 years at Oaken Financial and Saven Financial. Oaken's rates are covered by CDIC; Saven is covered by DICO, and Hubert and Outlook by the Manitoba deposit guarantor.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

In a high-interest savings account you can reach any day, at a CDIC member or an insured credit union. Avoid non-redeemable GICs, which you cannot cash early. Many people keep the first month of costs in chequing and the rest in savings. A TFSA savings account works too, because withdrawals are allowed any time and interest is tax-free.

Is my money safe in an online bank?

At a CDIC member, eligible deposits are insured up to $100,000 per insured category, including interest, the same as at a big bank. EQ Bank (Equitable Bank) and Oaken (Home Bank or Home Trust) are covered this way. Credit unions and their divisions use provincial insurers, and some fintech accounts reach CDIC cover through trust deposits at partner banks.

Is a TFSA a savings account?

No. A TFSA is a registered account that shelters income from tax; you choose what it holds. It can hold a savings account, GICs or investments. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000, and withdrawals come back as contribution room on January 1 of the next year. Interest earned inside a TFSA is tax-free.

Is a GIC better than a high-interest savings account?

A GIC usually pays more and fixes your rate, but a non-redeemable GIC locks your money until maturity. On $10,000 for a year, a 3.85% GIC earns $385.00 and a 2.80% savings account with interest paid monthly earns $283.62. Use savings for money you may need and GICs for money with a date.

Where can I save US dollars in Canada?

The highest US dollar savings rate in our register was 2.50% at EQ Bank's US Dollar Account (effective May 15, 2026), which earns US$250.00 a year on US$10,000. Big-bank US dollar accounts paid much less, such as 0.010% at TD. Conversions between currencies carry a cost, so a US account suits US dollars you already hold.

Is interest on savings taxable in Canada?

Yes, outside a registered account. Savings and GIC interest is fully taxable at your marginal rate every year, including interest that builds up on a multi-year GIC before it is paid. Inside a TFSA, interest is tax-free. Inside an RRSP or FHSA, it is taxed only when withdrawn, except qualifying FHSA withdrawals for a first home.

How often do savings rates change?

Savings rates are variable and can change at any time, often after Bank of Canada decisions. The policy rate has been 2.25% since October 30, 2025, with the remaining 2026 announcements on October 28 and December 9. GIC rates are fixed once you buy, but new GIC rates change too. We date every rate on this page.
