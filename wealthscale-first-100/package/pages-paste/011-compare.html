10. How to Compare Online Brokers in Canada and Choose One

Use your own numbers. The best broker for a buy-and-hold ETF investor is rarely the best for someone trading US stocks every week. Please work through these steps with your own balance and habits before you open an account.

  1. Price your trading pattern. Count your Canadian trades, US trades and conversions a year, and run them through the cost calculator above. Add any account fee you would not avoid.
  2. Confirm the accounts you need. Check that the broker offers every account you want to hold, including an FHSA, RESP, spousal RRSP or LIRA if you need one.
  3. Plan for US dollars. If you buy US-listed shares, compare the conversion fee and whether you can hold US dollars in your TFSA and RRSP, and at what cost.
  4. Check the tools. Decide whether you need fractional shares, a DRIP, options, an advanced platform or a mobile app, and filter for them with the matching tool above.
  5. Check safety. Confirm the broker is a dealer member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) and a member of CIPF, which protects client property up to $1,000,000 per account category if the firm fails.
  6. Plan the move. Ask for a direct transfer from your current institution so registered accounts stay tax-free, and check whether the new broker covers the transfer-out fee.

What CIRO and CIPF do. CIRO regulates investment dealers in Canada and sets the rules they follow with client accounts. CIPF steps in only if a member firm becomes insolvent and client property is missing: it covers up to $1,000,000 for general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA and FHSA combined), $1,000,000 for retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF and LIF) and $1,000,000 for RESPs. Neither protects you from a fall in the value of your investments.

How we keep the comparison fair. Every score on this page comes from the same published rules, and no provider can pay to change a score or a position. Today WealthScale has no paid or affiliate links; our page on how WealthScale makes money explains how any future partner links will be labelled. Compare Online Brokers: FAQ

What is the cheapest online broker in Canada?

It depends on how you trade. In our active profile, with two Canadian and one US trade a month and $6,000 converted a year, Interactive Brokers Canada is cheapest at $44.00 a year. For one Canadian trade a month and no conversions, Wealthsimple, Questrade, Qtrade Direct Investing, National Bank Direct Brokerage, BMO InvestorLine and Desjardins Online Brokerage all cost $0.00 a year at published prices.

Which online brokers charge $0 commission in Canada?

Seven brokers charge $0 on online Canadian and US stock and ETF trades: Wealthsimple, Questrade, Webull Canada, Qtrade Direct Investing, National Bank Direct Brokerage, BMO InvestorLine and Desjardins Online Brokerage. Other costs remain, such as currency conversion on US trades, pass-through exchange fees on some US orders and, at Webull Canada, a $0.70 monthly administration fee per account.

Which broker has the lowest currency conversion fee?

Interactive Brokers Canada charges 0.20 basis points with a minimum of US$2 per conversion, so converting $10,000 costs about $2.00. TD Direct Investing is next among brokers that publish a rate, at 1.26% under $25,000, or $126 on $10,000. Wealthsimple, Questrade and Webull Canada charge 1.5%, or $150 on $10,000.

Can I hold US dollars in my TFSA or RRSP?

At many brokers, yes. Questrade includes US dollars in registered accounts at no cost. Wealthsimple charges $10 a month on Core and includes it on Premium and Generation. Qtrade Direct Investing charges US$15 a quarter per account, free with Investor Plus. National Bank Direct Brokerage and Desjardins Online Brokerage list USD registered accounts with no fee stated.

Which brokers offer both an FHSA and an RESP?

Nine brokers list both: Wealthsimple, Questrade, Qtrade Direct Investing, National Bank Direct Brokerage, CIBC Investor's Edge, RBC Direct Investing, BMO InvestorLine, Scotia iTRADE and Desjardins Online Brokerage. Interactive Brokers Canada and TD Direct Investing list an FHSA but not an RESP on the pages we checked, and moomoo Canada and Webull Canada list neither.

Is my money safe at an online broker?

Each broker we score is, or belongs to, a firm on CIPF's member list. If a member firm fails and client property is missing, CIPF covers up to $1,000,000 for general accounts, $1,000,000 for retirement accounts and $1,000,000 for RESPs. CIPF does not cover investment losses, and crypto is not CIPF-protected at the platforms that state their coverage.

Is a robo-advisor cheaper than an online broker?

Usually not. On $50,000, the ranked robo-advisors cost from $185 a year at Questwealth Portfolios to $415 at CI Direct Investing, counting the management fee and fund costs. A self-directed investor buying ETFs at a $0-commission broker pays only the funds' own costs. The robo-advisor's extra cost pays for portfolio building, rebalancing and, at some, access to advisors.
