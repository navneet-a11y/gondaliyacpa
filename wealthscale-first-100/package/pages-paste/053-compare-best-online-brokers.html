10. Safety and Regulation: CIRO and CIPF

8 Strategies to Cut Your Brokerage Costs

1. Price your own pattern

Rankings use standard profiles. Enter your trades, conversions and options in the cost calculator above. The cheapest broker for a monthly ETF buyer is often not the cheapest for an active US trader, and the gap can run to hundreds of dollars a year.

2. Batch large conversions

Five bank brokers publish lower spreads from $25,000 (US$25,000 at BMO and National Bank). At CIBC, one $30,000 conversion at the 0.9% tier costs $270, against $480 for six $5,000 conversions at 1.6%. At Interactive Brokers, the US$2 minimum makes any conversion size cheap.

3. Match the account to the withholding rule

US dividends in an RRSP or RRIF avoid the 15% US withholding tax under the treaty. In a TFSA, FHSA or RESP that tax is lost. Keep a Form W-8BEN on file at every broker to avoid the 30% rate.

4. Claim the transfer reimbursement

Wealthsimple covers the old broker's fee up to $150 per account on $25,000 or more, Questrade up to $150 per account, RBC up to $200 on $15,000 or more, and Qtrade up to $150 under its offer. Ask before you sign the transfer form.

5. Stay above fee thresholds

Consolidating accounts at one broker can lift your balance past $10,000, $15,000 or $20,000, where CIBC, TD and National Bank waive their fees. At Wealthsimple, $100,000 in combined household assets makes the USD account $0.

6. Use the bank's $0 lists

If you stay with a bank broker, check its commission-free ETF list: more than 180 at CIBC, more than 200 at Scotia iTRADE and more than 50 at RBC. A monthly purchase of a listed ETF costs $0 instead of up to $119.88 a year.

7. Read every trade confirmation

Pass-through ECN, exchange and SEC fees appear on trade confirmations. If you see them often, change your order routing or order type, or ask the broker why they apply.

Illustrative Example: Moving a TFSA From a $9.99 Broker

This example is hypothetical. It uses published prices as of October 22, 2026, and ignores investment returns and taxes.

  1. Starting point. Amira holds a $30,000 TFSA at a broker charging $9.99 a trade with a 1.26% conversion spread under $25,000 (TD Direct Investing's standard price). She buys two ETFs a month and converts $6,000 a year to US dollars.
  2. Current yearly cost. Commissions: 24 x $9.99 = $239.76. Conversion: $6,000 x 1.26% = $75.60. Total: $239.76 + $75.60 = $315.36 a year.
  3. Cost at a $0 broker with a 1.5% conversion fee. Commissions: 24 x $0 = $0. Conversion: $6,000 x 1.5% = $90.00. Total: $90.00 a year. Her conversion cost rises by $14.40 ($90.00 minus $75.60), but she saves $239.76 in commissions.
  4. Yearly saving. $315.36 minus $90.00 = $225.36 a year. Over 10 years that is $225.36 x 10 = $2,253.60, before any growth on the money saved.
  5. Transfer cost. Her old broker charges $150 to transfer out. Wealthsimple covers that fee, up to $150 per account, on transfers of $25,000 or more, and Questrade rebates up to $150 per account, so at either the fee is reimbursed. At a broker that covers nothing, her first-year saving falls to $225.36 minus $150 = $75.36.
  6. Room check. Because the TFSA moves by direct transfer, her contribution room is unchanged. Had she withdrawn the $30,000 and redeposited it in the same year, the redeposit would use $30,000 of room.

The same pattern at Interactive Brokers, on orders of 100 shares or fewer, would cost 24 x $1.00 = $24.00 in commissions plus 4 x $2.00 = $8.00 in conversions if she converts in four lots: $32.00 a year in total.
