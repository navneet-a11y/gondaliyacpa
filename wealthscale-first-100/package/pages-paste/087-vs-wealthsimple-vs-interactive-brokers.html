1. Wealthsimple vs Interactive Brokers: Quick Verdict

Two opposite pricing models. Wealthsimple removes the commission and earns more on currency conversion: 1.5% on every US trade from a Canadian-dollar account. IBKR charges a small commission on almost every stock trade and almost nothing to convert currency. Which one is cheaper depends on how often you trade and how much you move between Canadian and US dollars, which is why we work through four investor profiles in section 9.

IBKR wins the overall score. It has the best cost score of the 13 brokers (4.65), because its per-share prices and US$2 conversion minimum add up to $44 a year on our active profile, against $90 at Wealthsimple, all of it Wealthsimple's 1.5% conversion fee. It also scores full marks on cash and margin (5.0) and platform features (5). Wealthsimple's lead on account types (5.0 against 4.0) is not enough to close the gap.

Wealthsimple wins for small, regular investors. If you buy Canadian ETFs every month and rarely touch US dollars, $0 commissions beat IBKR's $1 minimum, and Wealthsimple's app, fractional shares from $1 and automatic recurring buys suit small deposits. It also ranks first of 13 in our beginners ranking.

2. Commissions: $0 vs Per-Share Pricing

Wealthsimple: $0 on listed stocks and ETFs. Every online trade of a listed Canadian or US stock or ETF is commission-free, on every tier. Pass-through exchange, ECN, TAF or ORF fees may apply to some US-listed orders and show on the trade confirmation. The cost that remains is currency conversion on US trades, covered in section 3.

IBKR: fixed pricing. Canadian stocks and ETFs cost CAD 0.01 a share, with a minimum of CAD 1 and a maximum of 0.5% of the trade value. US stocks and ETFs cost USD 0.005 a share, with a minimum of USD 1 and a maximum of 1%. For most trades under 100 Canadian shares or 200 US shares, you pay the minimum.

IBKR: tiered pricing. The alternative tiered plan charges CAD 0.008 a share on Canadian stocks at volumes of 300,000 shares a month or fewer (minimum CAD 1, maximum 0.5%) and USD 0.0035 a share on US stocks (minimum USD 0.35, maximum 1%), plus exchange and regulatory fees. Because those added fees vary by order, our worked numbers use fixed pricing.

3. Currency Conversion and USD Accounts

This is where IBKR pulls ahead. IBKR quotes currency with no markup and charges a commission of 0.20 basis points (0.002%) of the amount, with a minimum of US$2 per conversion. Up to $100,000, you pay the US$2 minimum; on $1,000,000 the fee is US$20. That puts IBKR first of the nine brokers in our lowest currency conversion fees list, where Wealthsimple ranks third.

Wealthsimple has two routes. Buy a US stock from a Canadian-dollar account and Wealthsimple converts at 1.5% on top of its corporate exchange rate, on every tier, and converts the proceeds back when you sell. Or add a USD account, which costs $10 plus tax a month on Core after a 30-day trial and is included for Premium and Generation; inside it, each conversion costs 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000, and US trades carry no conversion fee.

5. Managed Portfolios vs Doing It Yourself

Only one side has a robo-advisor in our data. Wealthsimple runs Managed Investing, which ranks first of the four robo-advisors in our best robo-advisors in Canada ranking at 4.2 / 5. Our register does not cover an Interactive Brokers managed service, so this section compares Wealthsimple's managed options with building the same kind of ETF portfolio yourself at either broker.

Wealthsimple Managed Investing. The fee is 0.5% a year on Core, 0.4% on Premium (from $100,000 in total Wealthsimple assets) and 0.4% on Generation, falling to 0.2% at $10,000,000. Classic portfolios hold 8 to 10 ETFs with fund costs (MER) between 0.12% and 0.15%, are rebalanced automatically, and reinvest dividends into whichever ETF is underweight. About 0.3% to 0.5% of the account is held in cash, and deposits are invested within 5 business days. Tax-loss harvesting comes with Premium and Generation.

Wealthsimple Automated Investing. A lower-cost option at 0.25% a year, capped at $250 an account once the balance reaches $100,000. It is offered in the TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, LIRA, LIF, RRIF and individual and joint non-registered accounts. In non-registered accounts its direct indexing includes tax-loss harvesting.

8. Safety: CIRO and CIPF

Equal safety scores. Both brokers state their CIRO membership and are on CIPF's current member list, so both score 4.0 on our safety factor. Wealthsimple Investments Inc. is an investment dealer and CIRO dealer member. Interactive Brokers Canada Inc. states that it is a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization and a member of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund. Neither gets the extra point our rubric gives to firms owned by a bank or a financial cooperative group.

How CIPF protection applies to each account. CIPF covers cash and securities held by a member firm that becomes insolvent, up to $1,000,000 per client in each category. It does not cover market losses, unsuitable advice, an issuer's insolvency or crypto assets.

Wealthsimple vs IBKR FAQ

Is Wealthsimple or Interactive Brokers better?

Interactive Brokers has the higher score: 4.4 / 5 and first of 13 in our ranking, against 4.0 / 5 and third for Wealthsimple, as of October 22, 2026. IBKR is better for frequent currency conversion, US trading, margin and advanced platforms. Wealthsimple is better for beginners, small monthly buys, RESP, LIRA and LIF accounts, and managed portfolios, and it charges $0 per trade.

Is Interactive Brokers cheaper than Wealthsimple?

It depends on what you trade. For small Canadian ETF buys, Wealthsimple is cheaper: $0 against IBKR's CAD 1 minimum, or $12 a year on 12 buys. For anything involving US dollars, IBKR is usually cheaper: converting $10,000 costs US$2 at IBKR against $150 on a US trade from a Wealthsimple Canadian-dollar account. On our scoring profiles IBKR averages $28 a year and Wealthsimple $45.

How much does each charge to convert Canadian dollars to US dollars?

IBKR adds no markup and charges 0.20 basis points, with a minimum of US$2 per conversion, so any amount up to $100,000 costs US$2. Wealthsimple charges 1.5% on US trades from a Canadian-dollar account. Inside its USD account, a conversion costs 1.5% under $10,000, 1.0% from $10,000, 0.5% from $25,000 and 0% from $100,000.

Does Interactive Brokers charge commission on Canadian stocks?

Yes. Under fixed pricing, IBKR charges CAD 0.01 a share on Canadian stocks and ETFs, with a minimum of CAD 1 and a maximum of 0.5% of the trade value. A $500 buy of 20 units costs CAD 1.00. Wealthsimple charges $0 on listed Canadian and US stocks and ETFs, though a 1.5% conversion fee applies to US trades from a Canadian-dollar account.

Which is better for US stocks, Wealthsimple or IBKR?

IBKR. It scores 4.0 / 5 for US stocks and ranks second of 13, while Wealthsimple scores 3.7 / 5 and ranks fifth. IBKR's US$2 conversion minimum and no-markup rate outweigh its USD 1 minimum commission. Wealthsimple offers US dollars in a TFSA, FHSA, RRSP or RESP through a USD account with a $10 a month Core fee, while IBKR does not state it on the pages we checked. Both trail Questrade, which charges no fee for it.

Which has lower margin rates?

Interactive Brokers. It charges 3.675% on CAD margin up to CAD 130,000 and 5.38% on USD up to USD 100,000. Wealthsimple charges 4.95% CAD on Core, 4.45% on Premium and 3.95% on Generation. On a $20,000 loan for a year, that is $735 at IBKR against $990 at Wealthsimple Core, as of October 22, 2026.

Does Interactive Brokers pay interest on cash?

Yes. IBKR pays 1.675% on CAD balances above CAD 13,000 and 3.38% on USD balances above USD 10,000, with the full rates for accounts whose net asset value is above USD 100,000. Wealthsimple does not publish a rate for cash in trading accounts, but its separate Savings account pays 2.5% to all clients.

Which is better for beginners?

Wealthsimple, narrowly. It ranks first of 13 in our beginners ranking at 4.5 / 5, with IBKR second at 4.4 / 5. Both offer fractional shares, a mobile app, DRIP and no account minimum. Wealthsimple edges ahead on the score because it offers more account types, including the RESP, LIRA and LIF, and it also charges $0 commissions and offers automatic recurring buys.

Which is better for options trading?

IBKR has the higher options score, 4.6 / 5 and first of 13, against 4.3 / 5 and second for Wealthsimple, because the score also weighs platforms, margin and safety. On price alone Wealthsimple wins: $0 a contract on equity and ETF options against US$0.65 at IBKR, or $0 against $62.40 a year for 48 two-contract trades.

Does Interactive Brokers offer an RESP, LIRA or LIF?

IBKR's pages that we checked describe TFSA, RRSP, spousal RRSP, FHSA, RRIF, cash, margin, joint, trust and corporate accounts, but not the RESP, LIRA or LIF. Wealthsimple offers all three. If you need one of them, Wealthsimple or Questrade are the confirmed options among the brokers we rank highest.

Is Interactive Brokers as safe as Wealthsimple?

Both score 4.0 on our safety factor. Interactive Brokers Canada Inc. and Wealthsimple Investments Inc. each state CIRO membership and are on CIPF's member list, which protects up to $1,000,000 per category if a member firm fails. CIPF does not cover market losses or crypto assets.

What does it cost to move between IBKR and Wealthsimple?

Leaving Wealthsimple costs $0, but its fractional shares cannot be transferred and are sold first. IBKR does not publish a fee for Canadian transfers out. Wealthsimple covers the other firm's transfer-out fee up to $150 per account on transfers of $25,000 or more held for 90 days. Always request a direct transfer from the receiving broker.
