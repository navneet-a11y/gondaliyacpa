13. Common Investing App Mistakes

Ignoring conversion costs. A $0 commission on US stocks still means paying 1.5% or 1.6% to convert at many apps. Over a year of regular US buying, that can be the largest cost you pay.

Choosing an app without the right account. Webull and moomoo do not offer an FHSA, and TD Direct Investing lists only five account types. Opening the wrong app and moving later can cost $150 or more in transfer fees.

Paying small-trade fees. TD's $1.99 partial-share fee is almost 4% of a $50 purchase, and moomoo's CA$1.49 minimum is 1.49% of a $100 trade. For small, regular amounts, use an app with $0 trades and fractional shares at no extra fee.

Letting account fees run. Webull's $0.70 a month applies to each account, and small balances at TD, Disnat and CIBC can trigger quarterly or yearly fees. Check the waiver rules once a year.

Trading too often. An app makes trading easy, but frequent trading can raise costs and, inside a TFSA, the CRA can treat income from trading with the frequency and skill of a professional as business income. A plan you stick to beats a busy screen.

Skipping two-step verification. Your phone is the key to your account. Use a passkey or authenticator app and a unique password from day one.

10 Strategies to Pay Less on an Investing App

1. Match the app to your trades

RRSPs and RRIFs are exempt from US withholding on dividends under the Canada-US treaty, while a TFSA loses 15%. Put US dividend holdings where the treaty helps, if you hold them at all.
