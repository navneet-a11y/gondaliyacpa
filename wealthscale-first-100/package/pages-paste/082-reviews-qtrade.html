3. Currency Conversion and USD Accounts at Qtrade

No published conversion rate. Qtrade's fee schedule says currency conversions may apply and that Qtrade "may earn revenue on these transactions". It does not publish a spread or a percentage. That makes Qtrade one of four brokers on our list without a single published conversion rate, with moomoo Canada (a 0% fee but a spread of undisclosed size), Scotia iTRADE and Desjardins Online Brokerage (up to 1.90%, depending on the amount). Our scoring counts an unpublished rate at 1.5%, the median of the rates other brokers publish, so Qtrade is not rewarded for the gap.

Why it matters. On US trades the conversion is usually the largest cost once commissions are $0. Brokers that publish a rate charge from 1.26% (TD Direct Investing, under $25,000) to about 1.70% (National Bank Direct Brokerage, under US$25,000), and Interactive Brokers Canada charges 0.20 basis points with a US$2 minimum. Without a published figure, you cannot price a Qtrade conversion in advance. Compare the exchange rate on your Qtrade confirmation with the market rate at the same moment to see the spread you paid.

USD registered accounts. Qtrade offers a USD side for RRSP, RRIF and TFSA accounts. It costs US$15 a quarter per account (US$60 a year), and is included at no charge for Investor Plus clients. The FHSA is not offered in US dollars. With three USD registered accounts, a standard client pays US$180 a year. Holding US dollars lets you buy US stocks with US dollars from sales and dividends, instead of converting every time money moves.

Is the USD fee worth it. The answer depends on how much you would otherwise convert. As a yardstick, 60 dollars a year equals a 1.5% spread on 4,000 dollars of conversions (60 / 1.5% = 4,000), ignoring the exchange rate between the two currencies. If you convert less than that each year in an account, paying the quarterly fee may cost more than it saves. If you hold US-listed stocks that pay dividends, a USD side lets those dividends be paid and kept in US dollars.

US withholding tax. The USD fee does nothing about US tax on dividends. Under the Canada-US tax treaty, US dividends paid into a TFSA, FHSA, RESP or non-registered account are withheld at 15% when the broker has your Form W-8BEN, or 30% without it. Dividends paid to an RRSP, RRIF, LIRA or LIF are exempt under Article XXI(2) of the treaty once the account is documented. Our guide to US stocks in a TFSA explains why the TFSA cannot recover that 15%.

Ways to cut the cost. Investors who move large sums sometimes use Norbert's Gambit, buying an interlisted security in one currency and selling it in the other. With $0 commissions, the two trades cost nothing at Qtrade, but you need Qtrade to move the shares between the CAD and USD sides, and that process was not part of the figures we checked. 5. Qtrade Platforms, Tools and Research

What Qtrade offers. Qtrade runs a web trading platform and a mobile app for iOS and Android. Its investor pages also name two tools: Portfolio Analytics, for reviewing what you hold, and Portfolio Creator, an ETF portfolio tool shown on the investor home page. DRIP enrolment is self-serve, so you can set up dividend reinvestment yourself without calling.

What is not named. Qtrade's platform page does not mention fractional shares, so we do not credit them. It also does not name a separate advanced or active-trader platform of the kind TD (TD Active Trader), RBC (Trading Dashboard) or Interactive Brokers (TWS and IBKR Desktop) offer. Those two gaps are why Qtrade scores 3 of 5 on platform features. If you trade many times a day and want a dedicated active-trading platform, a broker that names one may suit you better.

Dividend reinvestment. A DRIP uses each cash dividend to buy more shares of the same stock or ETF. Self-serve enrolment is useful when you hold many dividend payers and want each one reinvested without placing orders. Check whether reinvestment buys whole shares only; the figures we checked do not say how Qtrade handles the leftover cash.

Research and education. The Qtrade pages we checked name Portfolio Analytics and Portfolio Creator but do not list third-party research providers, screeners or analyst reports. We do not score research quality, because there is no published measure we can compare across brokers. If research matters to you, ask Qtrade which reports and screeners are included at no extra cost before you transfer.

Who the platform suits. A buy-and-hold investor who adds money monthly needs an order screen, a holdings view and a way to reinvest dividends. Qtrade names a web platform, a mobile app, Portfolio Analytics and self-serve DRIP enrolment for that pattern. Our beginner score weights fractional shares, a mobile app, DRIP and no minimum; Qtrade's beginner platform sub-score is 3.5 of 5, losing only the fractional share points. 8. Cash Interest at Qtrade

What Qtrade pays. Qtrade pays 0.00% on Canadian dollar cash balances from $0 to $2,000,000, and 0.10% on the part above $2,000,000. US dollar cash earns 0.00%, a rate in effect since April 1, 2020. For almost every investor, uninvested cash at Qtrade earns nothing.

What that costs you. $10,000 left in cash for a year earns $0 at Qtrade. Even a client with $2,500,000 in cash would earn 0.10% only on the $500,000 above the threshold, or $500 a year ($500,000 x 0.10%). That is why Qtrade scores 0 of 2.5 for cash interest in our rubric, the same as Questrade, National Bank Direct Brokerage and CIBC Investor's Edge.

Who pays more. Interactive Brokers Canada pays 1.675% on Canadian dollar cash above CAD 13,000 and 3.38% on US dollar cash above USD 10,000, with the full rates for accounts whose net asset value is above USD 100,000. Wealthsimple publishes chequing rates of 1.25% to 2.25% by tier, but not a rate on cash inside trading accounts. TD Direct Investing and RBC Direct Investing do not publish a cash rate on the pages we checked.

What to do with idle cash. If you hold cash at Qtrade between purchases, the 0.00% rate is a small cost. If you hold it for months, it is a real one. Investors in that position often keep their cash reserve in a high-interest savings account and move it to the brokerage account only when they are ready to buy. Registered accounts. Cash inside a TFSA or RRSP at Qtrade also earns 0.00%, so idle money in a registered account is losing time on its tax shelter. If you contribute early in the year to use your $7,000 TFSA room, plan the purchase too, so the money is invested rather than waiting in cash.

9. Is Qtrade Safe? CIRO and CIPF Protection

Who holds your account. Qtrade Direct Investing is a division of Aviso Financial Inc. Aviso Financial Inc. shows membership in the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) and is on the Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) member list. CIRO oversees all investment dealers in Canada, and every CIPF member firm is a CIRO member.

What CIPF covers. If a member firm becomes insolvent and client property is missing, CIPF covers the shortfall up to set limits. It does not cover a fall in the value of your investments, unsuitable advice or the failure of a company whose shares you own. Crypto assets are excluded. The limits apply per group of accounts at each member firm.

What the limits mean in practice. A client with $600,000 across a TFSA and a cash account, and $800,000 across an RRSP and a RRIF, has each group inside its own $1,000,000 limit. A TFSA, FHSA and margin account together above $1,000,000 would leave the excess uncovered in the general group. CIPF is not deposit insurance: CDIC covers bank deposits up to $100,000 per category, while CIPF covers missing property at an investment dealer.

Our safety score. Qtrade scores 4.0 of 5 for safety. Our rubric gives 4 when a broker states both CIRO and CIPF membership, and adds 1 for ownership by a bank or a financial cooperative group; that extra point is not part of Qtrade's score. Bank-owned brokers such as RBC Direct Investing score 5.0 on this factor.

Your part. Use a strong, unique password and two-step verification where offered, keep your contact details current, and read each trade confirmation. No protection fund covers money you send to a fraudster yourself, so treat unexpected calls or messages about your account with caution.

Transferring in. Move registered accounts by direct transfer between institutions. 10 Strategies to Keep Qtrade Costs Low

1. Switch to electronic statements

Paper statements cost $6 a quarter, or $24 a year, for standard clients. Electronic statements cost nothing. Over 10 years that is $240 saved on one account, more if you hold several accounts with paper statements.

2. Hold US dividend payers in an RRSP

US dividends paid into an RRSP or RRIF are exempt from US withholding under the treaty once the account is documented. In a TFSA, 15% is withheld and cannot be recovered. Placing US dividend payers in the RRSP keeps more of each payment.

3. Add a USD side only where it earns its fee

Each USD registered account costs US$60 a year for standard clients. Add one where you hold US stocks and would otherwise convert dividends and sale proceeds back and forth, and skip it in accounts that hold only Canadian investments.

4. Convert less often, in larger amounts

Every conversion carries Qtrade's unpublished spread. Batching purchases so you convert once a quarter instead of every week reduces how many times you pay it. Compare the rate on each confirmation with the market rate at the time.

5. Consider Norbert's Gambit for large sums

With $0 commissions, the two trades in Norbert's Gambit cost nothing at Qtrade. Ask Qtrade first how it moves an interlisted security between the CAD and USD sides and how long that takes, since the price can move in the meantime.

6. Keep idle cash in a savings account

Cash at Qtrade earns 0.00% up to $2,000,000. Money you will not invest for months can earn interest in a high-interest savings account and move to Qtrade when you are ready to buy.

7. Watch assignment fees on covered calls

Each assignment costs a flat $30, which can exceed the commissions on the trade itself. If you sell covered calls, factor $30 into the premium you accept, or roll positions before expiry when that fits your plan.

8. Time your transfer to the offer

The Fall Offer 2026 reimburses up to $150 of transfer fees on $15,000 or more, and adds $100 if $25,000 is funded within 30 days. Moving one large account at once can meet both thresholds.

9. Do not close an account in its first year

Closing an account within 12 months of opening costs $100, and leaving before February 5, 2028 can also cost the offer reward. Open only the accounts you plan to keep.

10. Use direct transfers for registered accounts

Withdrawing from a TFSA and redepositing counts as a new contribution. A direct transfer keeps your room intact and avoids withholding tax on RRSP money, which is withheld at 10%, 20% or 30% outside Quebec on a lump-sum withdrawal.

The main Qtrade fees are US$15 a quarter per USD registered account ($0 with Investor Plus), $6 a quarter for paper statements, $100 to close an account in its first year, $30 per options assignment, $100 to set up a formal trust plus $50 a year, and $150 to transfer an account out. What does Qtrade charge to convert Canadian dollars to US dollars?

Qtrade does not publish a conversion rate. Its fee schedule says only that currency conversions may apply and that Qtrade may earn revenue on them.
