Authorities, Licensing and the Veterinary Line

Operating a boarding facility is not a provincially licensed profession in Ontario — there is no college and no protected title. What is real is municipal regulation: business licensing, kennel and boarding licences, limits on the number of animals kept, home-business permission and zoning all differ by municipality, and this matters more on a boarding page than anywhere else in pet care because boarding is the activity municipalities most often license. We name it as real and variable and send you to your municipality for the requirement itself. The Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019, enforced by Animal Welfare Services, applies to anyone with custody of an animal. And practising veterinary medicine is restricted to licensed veterinarians under the Veterinarians Act, regulated by the College of Veterinarians of Ontario. Where that boundary falls is your question for your own counsel and your veterinarian — we give no guidance on animal health, medication or welfare at all. This is an accounting page.

Pet Boarding Accounting & Tax FAQs

Should I incorporate my pet boarding business?
Incorporating gives you limited liability, which matters in a business that takes custody of other people's animals on its own premises, plus roughly a 12.2% Ontario combined rate on the first $500,000 of active income under ITA section 125 against a personal rate up to 53.53% when unincorporated. The decision turns on whether you earn more than you withdraw, because that surplus is what a corporation lets you defer. There is a second reason specific to boarding: a facility, its runs and its client base are assets a corporation can hold, finance and eventually sell in a way a sole proprietor cannot, and a lender funding more runs would far rather read a corporate statement. The rate only applies where the corporation is not a personal services business under ITA subsection 125(7), which is a question of fact on your own arrangements. When it makes sense, we handle the section 85 rollover on Form T2057.
Do I charge HST on pet boarding?
Yes. Boarding is an ordinary taxable supply at 13% in Ontario, and so are daycare days, add-on services sold alongside a stay and retail product sold at pickup. There is no health care exemption for animals, which surprises operators who assume that caring for a living creature must sit somewhere different in the Excise Tax Act. It does not. Registration becomes mandatory once your taxable supplies pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters, and that is the single event most owner-operators are unprepared for, because a facility often opens well below the threshold and crosses it during its first strong holiday season without noticing. Place of supply follows the recipient's address. We track the threshold for you rather than discovering it in arrears, and we file the GST34 returns with the retail and service lines kept distinct.
When do I recognize a holiday deposit I collected in October?
In the year the boarding is actually provided, not the year the money arrived. An amount received for a service not yet rendered is included in income under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a) and then reserved out under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m) to the extent the boarding has not yet been provided at your year-end. This is the single most expensive timing error on a boarding file, because the holiday deposit book and a December 31 year-end sit directly next to each other: a facility can be holding cash for a Christmas week and a March break it has not delivered, and taking all of it into income produces a tax bill on work still to come, in the year with the least cash left after heat, overnight wages and insurance. We reconstruct the deposit book from your own records, reserve what is undelivered, and carry the balance as a deferred deposit liability every month rather than once a year.
What happens when a client cancels and the deposit is not refunded?
It is reviewed against your actual paperwork rather than assumed. Two provisions of the Excise Tax Act are in play. ETA section 181.2 deals with gift certificates, and ETA section 182 deals with an amount paid or forfeited because of a breach, modification or termination of an agreement. Which, if either, applies to a given deposit or cancellation charge depends on how your booking terms are written, what the client actually agreed to, and what the payment was for. We will not tell you that your deposit is a gift certificate, and we will not tell you that your cancellation charge is a forfeited deposit under section 182, because the characterisation drives the sales tax treatment and it has to be reached on your own facts. What we will do is read your terms, raise both provisions, and document the position so it holds up if a reviewer asks.
How should I measure whether my facility is performing?
By revenue per available run-night, computed from your own booking records. You sell kennel-nights against a fixed number of runs, so total revenue tells you almost nothing on its own: the same sales figure can come from a full facility at a low rate or a half-empty one at a high rate, and those two situations call for opposite decisions. Dividing revenue by run-nights available, month by month, separates the pricing question from the demand question. It also matters because your costs barely move with occupancy. The building, the mortgage or rent, the heat, the overnight cover and the insurance are there whether the runs are full or empty, so operating leverage works powerfully in both directions. A facility that cannot say how full it was last March cannot price, cannot staff, and cannot tell a lender why a loan is serviceable. We report that figure monthly against your actual run count, and we do not quote you an industry average, because your own numbers are the only ones that mean anything.
What CCA class is the kennel building, the runs and the fencing?
An owned kennel building goes to Class 1. The runs themselves, fencing, kennel panels, gates, play structures, grooming and laundry equipment, heating and ventilation equipment and office fittings generally go to Class 8 at 20%. If you lease the facility, the build-out is a leasehold improvement in Class 13, amortised over the term of the lease, which on a converted building is often the largest single asset on the schedule. Computers go to Class 50 at 55%, booking and office software to Class 12 at 100%, and goodwill with any acquired client base to Class 14.1 at 5%. A transport vehicle sits in Class 10, and a vehicle meeting the ITA subsection 248(1) definition of an automobile that cost more than the prescribed limit falls into Class 10.1 instead, where each vehicle sits in its own class. We do not assert the limit or characterise your vehicle; we review it. The error we inherit most often is an entire conversion project pooled into one figure, which cannot be supported and understates the deduction on the fastest-depreciating assets.
Should the land and building sit in a separate holding company?
It is a real structuring question with real consequences, and it is modelled rather than answered in advance. Holding the real property outside the operating company changes creditor exposure, changes how a future sale can be structured, changes what a buyer is actually buying, and changes the planning around the Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption. It also carries costs and complications of its own, including on the transfer itself. We will not tell you that a holding company is appropriate for you, and we assert no land transfer tax, HST-on-real-property or rebate rate or amount, because those depend on the transaction and on advice from your lawyer. What we do is build the model on your facts, show you both structures side by side with the numbers attached, and make sure the decision is taken deliberately years before a sale rather than discovered during one.
Are my overnight kennel attendants employees or contractors?
That is a question of fact, and we will not pre-answer it for you. The relationship is tested against the factors in CRA guide RC4110: control over how and when the work is done, who supplies the tools and equipment, whether the worker can subcontract or hire helpers, and the chance of profit against the risk of loss, considered together with how integrated the worker is into the business. An attendant who works a scheduled overnight shift in your building, with your runs, your feed and your procedures, looks different on those factors from someone who covers relief weekends for several facilities on their own schedule. We run the analysis, document it, file T4A slips on the people who are genuinely contractors, and set up payroll with WSIB coverage where the answer is employment. Employment Standards Act, 2000 entitlements are real and they are employment counsel's to quantify, not ours.
What do I need to know about municipal kennel licensing?
That it is real, that it varies, and that it matters more for boarding than for anything else in pet care, because boarding is the activity municipalities most often license. Operating a boarding facility is not a provincially licensed profession in Ontario — there is no college and no protected title — but municipalities regulate the activity through business licensing, kennel and boarding licences, limits on the number of animals kept, home-business permission and zoning, and those rules differ from one municipality to the next. We will not tell you what your municipality requires, what it charges, how many animals you may keep or whether your address is zoned for it, because getting that wrong is expensive and the answer is local. We name it as a live requirement, we make sure licence and permit costs are captured properly in the ledger, and we tell you to confirm the requirement itself with your municipality before you build, expand or move.
Does the Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019 affect my accounting?
It affects your cost base, which is where it reaches us. The Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019 is the governing Ontario statute and Animal Welfare Services is the body that enforces it, and it applies to anyone who has custody of an animal — which plainly includes a boarding facility. Compliance has a real and recurring cost, and that cost belongs in the ledger and in the capital schedule rather than being absorbed invisibly. What we will not do is state what the Act requires, what any standard of care is, what inspection powers exist or what any penalty might be. We name the statute and the regulator, we make sure the money you spend on compliance is captured and claimed, and we leave the requirements themselves to the Act, to Animal Welfare Services and to your own counsel.
Can you advise me on vaccinations, medication or an animal's health?
No, and that is not a limitation we are shy about. Practising veterinary medicine is restricted to licensed veterinarians under the Veterinarians Act, with the College of Veterinarians of Ontario as the regulator, and diagnosing, treating, administering medication or advising on an animal's health sits on the far side of that line. We are a CPA firm. We give no guidance on animal health, vaccination, medication, behaviour, breed, nutrition, handling or welfare, in either direction, and we do not tell you where the boundary falls for any particular thing your staff does during a stay. That question belongs to your own legal counsel and your own veterinarian, and it is worth settling deliberately rather than by habit. What we handle is the money: your deposits, your occupancy, your payroll, your capital pools, your HST and your T2.
What can a pet boarding business write off?
Rent or mortgage interest and property tax on the facility, utilities and heating, feed and bedding, cleaning and sanitation supplies, laundry, waste removal, licence and permit fees, insurance premiums, wages for kennel and overnight staff with the employer's CPP, EI and WSIB, veterinary services you purchase, booking and accounting software subscriptions, advertising, professional fees, and bank and processing charges. On capital, the building goes to Class 1, runs, fencing, kennel panels, play structures and ventilation to Class 8 at 20%, a leased build-out to Class 13, computers to Class 50 at 55%, software to Class 12, goodwill and an acquired client base to Class 14.1, and a transport vehicle to Class 10 or Class 10.1, all on Schedule 8. ITA section 67.1 limits the food and beverage portion of an expense to 50%, which reaches staff and client entertainment rather than animal feed. A bad debt on an unpaid boarding invoice is deductible under ITA paragraph 20(1)(p). Records are kept six years under ITA section 230.
How do I value my boarding facility if I sell it?
A buyer prices three things: the capacity, the occupancy record behind it, and whatever the premises is worth on its own. Capacity is the run count, but a run count with no occupancy history attached is just a number, which is why a facility that has reported revenue per available run-night for years sells on better evidence than one that has only ever reported total sales. Deferred deposits are deducted rather than added, because stays already paid for are an obligation the buyer inherits. The structure decides what you keep: a share sale can access the $1.25M Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under ITA 110.6 where the shares qualify, which needs purification of retained cash and a runway of at least two years, while an asset sale triggers recapture where proceeds beat undepreciated capital cost and lands goodwill and the client base in Class 14.1. Where you own the land and building, how that property is held shapes the whole transaction, which is exactly why it should be modelled years ahead rather than during the deal.
