Alberta Income Tax Calculator and 2026 Tax Brackets

The Alberta tax brackets 2026 are 8% on taxable income up to $61,200, 10% up to $154,259, 12% up to $185,111, 13% up to $246,813, 14% up to $370,220 and 15% above $370,220. 1. Alberta Tax Brackets 2026: Six Rates From 8% to 15%

The 2026 structure. Alberta taxes income in six brackets. The 8% bracket on the first $61,200 was introduced on January 1, 2025, and for 2026 every threshold and credit amount rose by 2%. For each bracket you can see the Alberta tax it generates when filled and the cumulative amount before the basic personal amount credit.

Reading the cumulative column. Someone with exactly $154,259 of taxable income owes $14,201.90 of Alberta tax on the brackets, then subtracts the $1,821.52 basic personal amount credit, for $12,380.38 of Alberta tax. The table's figures make it easy to check a return by hand: find the bracket your income falls in, take the cumulative tax at the bracket below, and add your income above that threshold times the rate.

A wide first bracket. Alberta's 8% band runs to $61,200, wider than Ontario's $53,891 or British Columbia's $50,363 first band. Combined with the large basic personal amount, it means an Alberta resident with $61,200 of taxable income pays only $3,074.48 of Alberta tax ($4,896.00 - $1,821.52), an Alberta average rate of 5.02%.

The middle 10% band is very long. The 10% rate covers $93,059 of income, from $61,200 to $154,259. Most Alberta salaries fall entirely inside the first two brackets, so for most workers the Alberta part of each extra dollar is either 8 or 10 cents.

Who uses these brackets. You pay Alberta tax on the whole year's income if you lived in Alberta on December 31, 2026. A worker who moved from Edmonton to Saskatoon in December uses the Saskatchewan tax brackets for all of 2026, while someone who arrived in Alberta from British Columbia that month uses Alberta's.

2. Federal Tax Brackets 2026 for Alberta Residents

Federal tax is most of the bill. In Alberta, federal tax is about twice the provincial amount or more at every income in our tables: at $100,000, federal tax is $14,392.73 and Alberta tax is $6,954.48. Alberta residents file one return with the Canada Revenue Agency for both. The federal tax brackets are identical in every province.

Where the dividend rates come from. Combined dividend rates depend on Alberta's dividend tax credit for eligible and other than eligible dividends. Capital gains rates. Because only half of a gain is taxable, the capital gains column is half the ordinary rate: 15.25% for most Alberta middle earners and 24.00% at the top.

5. Alberta Basic Personal Amount 2026 and Alberta Credits

The Alberta basic personal amount 2026 is $22,769. It is the largest basic personal amount of any province or territory, ahead of Saskatchewan's $20,381 and well above Ontario's $12,989. Multiplied by Alberta's 8% lowest rate, it removes $1,821.52 of Alberta tax. It is non-refundable, so it reduces Alberta tax to zero at most.

What that means in dollars. A single Alberta resident pays no Alberta tax on the first $22,769 of taxable income. Federal tax starts earlier, at $16,452, because the federal basic personal amount is smaller. Someone with $20,000 of taxable income in Alberta pays $496.72 of federal tax and nothing to Alberta.

The supplemental credit. In the 2026 payroll formulas, Alberta adds a supplemental credit of 25% of the amount by which an employee's Alberta basic and payroll credits exceed $4,896. A single employee claiming only the basic personal amount does not reach it: $1,821.52 plus at most $371.40 for CPP and EI is $2,192.92. It matters mainly to employees who claim large personal credits on top of the basic personal amount.

The Canada workers benefit in Alberta. Alberta is one of three places, with Quebec and Nunavut, where Canada workers benefit amounts may differ from the rest of Canada under a reconfiguration agreement. If you are a low-income worker in Alberta, the national maximums on our Canada workers benefit page may not apply as written.

Federal credits Alberta residents claim. Each of these federal amounts becomes a credit at 14% for 2026: up to $1,501 for the Canada employment amount, up to $9,208 for the age amount (which shrinks above $46,432 of net income and disappears at $107,819), up to $2,000 for pension income and $10,341 for disability. The federal medical expense credit applies to costs above $2,890 or 3% of net income, whichever is less. Alberta has its own provincial versions of several of these, whose 2026 amounts we have not yet verified.

6. Surtaxes, Premiums and Abatements in Alberta

None apply. Alberta has no provincial surtax, no health premium charged through the income tax return and no low-income tax reduction. In the 2026 payroll formulas, a surtax and an extra premium appear only for Ontario, and tax reductions only for Ontario and British Columbia. The federal abatement is only for Quebec residents.

Why that matters. With no extras, Alberta tax is exactly the bracket tax minus credits. There are no short bands where the marginal rate spikes, as there are in Ontario around the Health Premium steps. A $1,000 raise anywhere between $61,200 and $117,045 is taxed at the same 30.50%.

What Alberta does instead. Alberta's relief for low earners comes through the size of the basic personal amount. A single Ontario resident pays no Ontario tax up to $18,930 because of the Ontario tax reduction, and a B.C. resident none up to $25,537 because of the B.C. tax reduction; an Alberta resident pays none up to $22,769 with no reduction at all, and that protection does not fade away as income rises. At $60,000, for example, the Alberta basic personal amount still saves $1,821.52, whereas the B.C. tax reduction is gone by $44,952.

Comparing with Ontario at the top. Ontario's surtax lifts its top combined rate to 53.53%; Alberta's top is 48.00%. At $250,000 of taxable income an Alberta resident pays $10,207.94 less than an Ontario resident ($89,472.25 - $79,264.31). See the Ontario tax brackets for how the surtax works.

9. Capital Gains Tax in Alberta

Half the gain is taxable. Only 50% of a capital gain is taxable in 2026, because the government announced in March 2025 that it was cancelling the proposed move to a two-thirds inclusion rate. The taxable half is taxed at your combined federal and Alberta rate, which makes the effective rate on a gain 15.25% for most middle earners and 24.00% at the top.

Add your other 2026 income and the gain, plus any losses carried forward, to see the federal and Alberta tax the gain triggers.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. A Calgary engineer on a $120,000 salary makes a $40,000 profit selling shares held outside any registered plan. The taxable $20,000 takes income from $120,000 to $140,000, all inside the 36% band. Tax rises from $27,609.73 to $34,809.73, an increase of $7,200.00 ($20,000 x 36%), or 18% of the gain.

11. Ways to Lower Your Alberta Tax

General, rule-based ideas. These strategies follow from Alberta's 2026 rules. They are not personal advice.

1. Time RRSP deductions for the 36% band

Between $117,045 and $154,259, a deduction saves 36% in Alberta, against 30.50% just below. If your income is about to rise into that band, carrying a deduction forward raises its value.

2. Use an FHSA for a first home

You can deduct contributions of up to $8,000 a year, to a $40,000 lifetime total, and take the money out tax-free for a qualifying first home. Alberta's age of majority is 18, so you can open one at 18. See the FHSA guide.

3. Shelter interest in a TFSA

Interest at $70,000 of income is taxed at 30.50% in Alberta. The $7,000 TFSA limit for 2026 lets that income grow tax-free.

4. Split income with a spouse

If one spouse earns much more, a spousal RRSP or pension splitting can move income from the 36% band to the other spouse's 22% band. See pension income splitting.

5. Use the spousal RRSP rules correctly

Money taken out in the calendar year of a spousal contribution, or in the two years after it, is taxed back to the contributing spouse. Our spousal RRSP page explains the timing.

6. Spread large capital gains

A gain that crosses $117,045 or $154,259 is taxed at a higher rate on the part above. Selling over two tax years, where practical, keeps more of it in lower bands.

7. Keep the business exemption in view

Owners of qualifying small businesses, farms and fishing property can shelter up to $1,275,000 of gains for 2026. Planning ahead keeps shares or property eligible.

8. Claim every payroll credit

Base CPP and EI give credits at 14% federally and 8% in Alberta. Self-employed people also deduct part of their CPP, which lowers taxable income.

9. Watch the OAS threshold after 65

Net income above $95,323 in 2026 triggers the 15% OAS recovery tax. RRIF and RRSP timing affects it; see our OAS clawback guide.

10. Check the Canada workers benefit

Low-income Alberta workers can get the Canada workers benefit, although its Alberta amounts may differ from the national ones. Filing a return is required to receive it.

12. Alberta Income Tax Calculator: Three Worked Examples

This example is hypothetical. The three Albertans below are all single with no dependants, and none claims more than the 2026 basic personal amounts.

  1. Sofia, Red Deer, $55,000 of taxable income. Federal: $55,000 x 14% = $7,700.00, less the $2,303.28 basic personal amount credit = $5,396.72. Alberta: $55,000 x 8% = $4,400.00, less $1,821.52 = $2,578.48. Total: $5,396.72 + $2,578.48 = $7,975.20, an average rate of 14.50%.
  2. Liam, Edmonton, $130,000 of taxable income. Federal: $20,190.23 on the first $117,045 + ($130,000 - $117,045) x 26% = $3,368.30, total $23,558.53, less $2,303.28 = $21,255.25. Alberta: $4,896.00 on the first $61,200 + ($130,000 - $61,200) x 10% = $6,880.00, total $11,776.00, less $1,821.52 = $9,954.48. Total: $21,255.25 + $9,954.48 = $31,209.73, an average rate of 24.01%.
  3. Noor, Calgary, $300,000 of taxable income. Her income is above $258,482, so her federal basic personal amount is the minimum $14,829, worth $2,076.06. Federal: $59,275.11 on the first $258,482 + ($300,000 - $258,482) x 33% = $13,700.94, total $72,976.05, less $2,076.06 = $70,899.99. Alberta: $25,925.40 on the first $246,813 + ($300,000 - $246,813) x 14% = $7,446.18, total $33,371.58, less $1,821.52 = $31,550.06. Total: $70,899.99 + $31,550.06 = $102,450.05, an average rate of 34.15%.

Each total matches the Alberta income tax calculator above. Noor's Alberta tax is less than half her federal tax, a pattern that holds at every income because Alberta's rates are low compared with the federal ones.

13. Common Mistakes With Alberta Tax

Applying the dividend credit to the cash dividend. Alberta's dividend tax credit is 8.12% for eligible dividends and 2.18% for other than eligible dividends, but of the grossed-up amount, not the cash received: $13,800 for $10,000 of eligible dividends and $11,500 for $10,000 of other than eligible dividends.

Thinking a raise moves all income to a higher rate. Crossing $61,200 puts only the dollars above $61,200 into the 10% bracket. A raise from $60,000 to $65,000 adds Alberta tax of $96.00 on the first $1,200 ($1,200 x 8%) and $380.00 on the next $3,800 ($3,800 x 10%).

Skipping the return on low income. With a $22,769 Alberta basic personal amount, many students and part-time workers owe nothing. They still need to file to receive the Canada workers benefit, the federal benefits that depend on a return and RRSP room. Our guide to filing taxes online and our ranking of tax software can help.

Forgetting instalments on self-employment income. If your net tax owing is more than $3,000 for 2026 and in either 2025 or 2024, CRA expects quarterly instalments on March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15. Contractors and other self-employed people usually have no tax withheld at source.

Using the wrong province after a move. Your province on December 31 sets the tax for the whole year. Someone who moves from Alberta to British Columbia in December 2026 pays B.C. tax on all of 2026.

Alberta Tax Brackets 2026: FAQ

What are the Alberta tax brackets for 2026?

Alberta has six 2026 rates: 8% on the first $61,200 of taxable income, then 10% to $154,259, 12% to $185,111, 13% to $246,813, 14% to $370,220 and 15% on anything above that. Each rate applies only to income within its bracket. Thresholds rose 2% for 2026, and federal tax is added on top.

What is the Alberta basic personal amount for 2026?

The Alberta basic personal amount for 2026 is $22,769, the largest of any province. At Alberta's 8% lowest rate, that amount is worth $1,821.52 off Alberta tax. A single person pays no Alberta tax on the first $22,769 of taxable income. The federal amount is separate: $16,452, worth $2,303.28.

What is the top tax rate in Alberta in 2026?

The top combined federal and Alberta rate for 2026 is 48.00% on salary and other ordinary income above $370,220: 33% federal plus Alberta's 15%. Capital gains at that level are taxed at 24.00%, because only half is taxable. Eligible dividends at that level are taxed at 34.31% and other than eligible dividends at 42.31%.

How much tax do I pay on $100,000 in Alberta?

On $100,000 of 2026 taxable income, a single Albertan with only the basic personal amounts owes $21,347.21 in total, made up of $14,392.73 federal and $6,954.48 provincial. The average rate is 21.35%, and each extra dollar is taxed at 30.50%, the combined rate from $61,200 to $117,045.

Does Alberta have a surtax or health premium?

No. Alberta has no provincial surtax, no health premium on the income tax return and no low-income tax reduction for 2026. In the 2026 payroll formulas, a surtax and a premium apply only in Ontario. Alberta tax is simply its bracket tax minus credits such as the $22,769 basic personal amount.

When did Alberta add the 8% tax bracket?

Alberta introduced its 8% bracket on January 1, 2025. For 2026 it covers taxable income up to $61,200, after a 2% increase in thresholds and credit amounts. The rest of Alberta's 2026 structure is 10%, 12%, 13%, 14% and 15%, with the 15% rate starting above $370,220.

What is the take-home pay on $80,000 in Alberta?

Out of an $80,000 Alberta salary in 2026, $60,697.50 is left once CPP ($4,230.45), CPP2 ($216.00), EI ($1,123.07), federal tax ($9,242.60) and Alberta tax ($4,490.38) are taken off. Paid monthly, that is $5,058.13 a pay; paid every two weeks, $2,334.52. The figures assume one employer and no other credits.

How much does a $5,000 RRSP contribution save in Alberta?

In Alberta, deducting $5,000 for 2026 cuts tax by $1,100.00 at $50,000 of taxable income and by $1,800.00 at $150,000. At $75,000 and at $100,000 the cut is the same $1,525.00, since the whole deduction falls in the 30.50% band between $61,200 and $117,045. Your refund also depends on the tax already withheld.

How are capital gains taxed in Alberta?

In Alberta, half of a 2026 capital gain is taxable at your combined federal and provincial rate. Our Calgary example, a $40,000 gain on a $120,000 salary, costs $7,200.00 in extra tax, 18% of the gain. Above $370,220 of income the effective rate on gains is 24.00%. A net capital loss can offset gains of the three previous years or any later year.

Is income tax lower in Alberta or British Columbia?

It depends on income. For 2026, B.C. charges less at most incomes from about $23,000 to about $149,600, and Alberta charges less above that. At $100,000, total tax is $21,347.21 in Alberta and $20,295.01 in B.C. At $250,000 it is $79,264.31 in Alberta and $82,643.18 in B.C.

Is Alberta tax lower than Saskatchewan tax?

Yes, at every income in our 2026 comparison. At $100,000 of taxable income, total tax is $21,347.21 in Alberta and $23,662.08 in Saskatchewan. Saskatchewan's top combined rate of 47.50% is slightly lower than Alberta's 48.00%, but Alberta remains cheaper in our calculations until income passes about $1,561,079.

What is Alberta's dividend tax credit for 2026?

Alberta's dividend tax credit is 8.12% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 2.18% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet AB428 (line 61520). CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; Alberta's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax. The federal gross-ups and credits are verified and explained in our dividend tax credit guide.

It leaves out credits other than the basic personal amounts, so treat it as an estimate. Our editorial standards explain how we check and correct figures.
