1. Northwest Territories Tax Brackets 2026 and Rates

Four territorial brackets. The Northwest Territories taxes 2026 income at four rates. Like every province, it charges each rate only on the income inside that band, so crossing a threshold changes the tax on the dollars above it and nothing else.

Thresholds of its own. Unlike Yukon, which borrows the federal thresholds, the Northwest Territories sets its own: $53,003, $106,009 and $172,346. Each one falls below the nearest federal threshold ($58,523, $117,045 and $181,440), so your territorial rate goes up a little before your federal rate does. That is why the combined table in section 3 has short bands such as $53,003 to $58,523 and $172,346 to $181,440.

Gentle rates at the bottom. The 5.9% first rate is among the lowest in Canada; only Nunavut's 4%, British Columbia's 5.6% and Ontario's 5.05% are lower. Combined with the $18,198 basic personal amount, a worker on $40,000 pays $1,286.32 of territorial tax, against $2,616.06 in Prince Edward Island at the same income.

Who pays NWT tax. If you lived in the Northwest Territories on December 31, 2026, the territory taxes your whole 2026 taxable income. Someone who moved to Alberta in December pays Alberta tax for the year instead. Taxable income is your total income less deductions such as RRSP and FHSA contributions, union dues and the enhanced part of CPP.

Using the running totals. Take $106,009 of taxable income: the first two bands add up to $7,685.69 of territorial tax before credits, shown in the table as $7,686. Taking off the $1,073.68 basic credit leaves $6,612.01. For any other income, the income tax calculator for Canada gives the exact figure.

5. Northwest Territories Basic Personal Amount 2026 and Credits

$18,198 for 2026. The Northwest Territories basic personal amount for 2026 is $18,198, the figure in CRA's payroll formulas effective July 1, 2026. It is a non-refundable credit at the 5.9% lowest rate, so it is worth $1,073.68 and can reduce territorial tax to zero but not below. Unlike the federal amount, it does not shrink at higher incomes.

How it compares. The NWT amount is larger than the federal $16,452 and Yukon's $16,452, but smaller than Quebec's $18,952, Nunavut's $19,659, Saskatchewan's $20,381 and Alberta's $22,769. A larger basic amount at a lower rate can be worth less than it looks: Alberta's $22,769 at 8% is worth $1,821.52, while the NWT's $18,198 at 5.9% is worth $1,073.68.

6. Surtaxes, Premiums and Abatements in the Northwest Territories

No surtax. CRA's 2026 payroll formulas include a provincial surtax only for Ontario. The Northwest Territories has none, so its 14.05% top rate is the full territorial rate on income over $172,346.

No health premium. Ontario is the only province with a health premium collected through income tax, up to $900 a year. NWT residents pay no equivalent; the Ontario Health Premium page explains Ontario's version for anyone weighing a move.

No abatement. The 16.5% federal abatement is for Quebec residents. NWT residents pay the full federal tax in section 4.

No low-income tax reduction. Ontario and British Columbia build low-income tax reductions into payroll; the Northwest Territories does not. In payroll and in our calculator, its larger basic amount keeps low-income tax small instead: on $20,000 of income, NWT tax is $106.32.

Why the top combined rate stays low. With no surtax on top, the top rate is simply 33% federal plus 14.05%, or 47.05%. That compares with 53.53% in Ontario, where the surtax lifts a 13.16% top bracket rate to an effective 20.53%.

What this page leaves out. The figures here cover income tax brackets and the credits in section 5. Other deductions and benefits that can apply to people living in the territories are not included in the calculator or the tables, because their 2026 figures are not yet confirmed in the sources we use.

The $60,000 row. Limit and deadline. Deduction room for 2026 starts at 18% of 2025 earned income, with $33,810 as the ceiling; a pension adjustment reduces it and room you never used is added on. Contributions made by March 1, 2027 can be deducted for 2026, since that is the 60th day of 2027. See the RRSP contribution limit for 2026.

Refund vs saving. A refund appears only if tax was withheld from your pay as though you had made no contribution. A payroll RRSP lowers withholding straight away, so the saving shows up in each pay instead.

9. Capital Gains and Dividends in the Northwest Territories

The 50% inclusion. Half of a 2026 capital gain on investments outside registered accounts is taxable, at your federal and territorial rates. Use the calculator below, preset to the NWT, or the full capital gains tax calculator page for more on adjusted cost base.

This example is hypothetical. A territorial resident whose other taxable income is $50,000 realizes a $16,000 capital gain on shares.

  1. Taxable part. $16,000 x 50% = $8,000, taking taxable income from $50,000 to $58,000.
  2. Federal tax. $58,000 is still under the $58,523 federal threshold, so $8,000 x 14% = $1,120.00.
  3. Territorial tax. The gain crosses $53,003: $3,003 x 5.9% = $177.18, plus $4,997 x 8.6% = $429.74, for $606.92.
  4. Total. $1,120.00 + $606.92 = $1,726.92, or 10.79% of the gain. The seller keeps $14,273.08.

Larger gains. Each extra dollar of gain costs 14.55% between $58,523 and $106,009 of taxable income, 19.10% from $117,045, 20.025% from $172,346 and 23.525% above $258,482. A large gain can be spread over two tax years by selling in two parts.

Losses and the exemption. Unused capital losses never expire: carry them forward without limit, or back to any of the last three years, and apply them against capital gains only. If identical property is bought back from 30 days before to 30 days after the sale, by you or by an affiliated person such as your spouse, and is still held 30 days after the sale, the loss is superficial and is denied. For 2026, the lifetime exemption shelters up to $1,275,000 of gains on qualified small business shares and qualified farm or fishing property. See capital losses, the superficial loss rule and the lifetime capital gains exemption.

Dividends. The Northwest Territories' dividend tax credit is 11.5% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 6% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet NT428 (line 61520). The federal gross-up and credit are 38% and 15.0198% for eligible dividends, 15% and 9.0301% for other than eligible dividends. Combined, eligible dividends are taxed from -9.14% between $18,198 and $53,003 to 28.33% over $258,482, and other than eligible dividends from 5.60% to 36.82%. The income tax calculator and the dividend tax calculator both include the NWT. Our dividend tax credit guide explains how the gross-up works.

11. Ways to Lower Your Northwest Territories Tax

General, rule-based strategies. They are not personal advice. Please run your own numbers in the calculators above before you act on any of them.

1. Know your two short bands

Income from $53,003 to $58,523 is taxed at 22.60% and from $172,346 to $181,440 at 40.05%. Knowing where they sit helps you time a deduction, a bonus or a sale.

2. Deduct RRSP contributions in high years

If your income rises and falls, contribute when cash allows and deduct in the year your marginal rate is highest. An RRSP deduction is worth 19.90% at $40,000 but 38.20% at $150,000.

3. Open a TFSA at 19

The age of majority in the Northwest Territories is 19, so that is when you can open a TFSA; room from the year you turned 18 carries forward. The 2026 limit is $7,000. Read the TFSA guide.

4. Use an FHSA for a first home

Each year you can put up to $8,000 into an FHSA and deduct it, to a lifetime maximum of $40,000, and withdrawals that qualify for a first home are tax-free. See the FHSA guide.

5. Keep income below $172,346 when you can

Above that line every dollar pays 14.05% territorial tax instead of 12.2%. On $10,000 that is $185 more territorial tax.

6. Build retirement income in two names

A spousal RRSP lets the higher earner deduct now while the lower earner withdraws later, subject to the three-year attribution rule.

7. Split pension income at retirement

Couples can move up to 50% of eligible pension income to the lower-income spouse. Our page on pension income splitting covers the conditions.

8. Protect your OAS

The OAS recovery tax takes 15% of net income above $95,323 for 2026. RRSP deductions and pension splitting can keep a retiree under it; see the OAS clawback guide.

9. Match losses with gains

Book a loss in a year when you also have a gain and the two are netted, shrinking the half of the gain that gets taxed. Respect the 30-day superficial loss window if you plan to buy back.

10. Get the saving in each pay

With regular RRSP contributions you can apply on Form T1213 for lower withholding, so you receive the tax saving through the year instead of as a refund.

12. Common Northwest Territories Tax Mistakes

Assuming NWT thresholds match the federal ones. Yukon's do; the NWT's do not. Its brackets break at $53,003, $106,009 and $172,346, a few thousand dollars below the federal thresholds, so your territorial rate changes first.

Rounding capital gains rates. Some rates on gains run to three decimals, such as 23.525% at the top. Rounding to 23.53% is a small error, but on a large gain it adds up. Please use the exact rate or the calculator.

Mixing up the two basic amounts. The federal amount is $16,452 and shrinks above $181,440; the NWT amount is $18,198 and never shrinks. Using one for both understates or overstates tax.

Confusing marginal and average rates. At $150,000 the marginal rate is 38.20% but the average rate is 25.62%. A raise is taxed at the marginal rate on the extra pay only, so it always leaves you better off.

Getting the province wrong. Where you lived on December 31 sets the province for the whole year. Anyone who moved into or out of the territory during 2026 should confirm the residence setting in their software before filing; see our guide to filing taxes online and our ranking of the best tax software in Canada.

Illustrative Example: Three NWT Residents and Their 2026 Tax

This example is hypothetical. Three people living in the Northwest Territories use the calculators on this page for 2026. Each has a single source of income and only the credits the calculators include.

  1. Gabe, salary $130,000. CPP is the maximum $4,230.45, CPP2 the maximum $416.00 and EI the maximum $1,123.07. Income tax is $29,229.65: $20,102.14 federal and $9,127.51 territorial. Take-home pay is $95,000.83 a year, or $3,653.88 every two weeks. With $10,000 of payroll RRSP contributions, income tax falls by $3,820.00, all at 38.20%: $2,600.00 federal ($10,000 x 26%) and $1,220.00 territorial ($10,000 x 12.2%). Take-home pay becomes $88,820.83 ($95,000.83 - $10,000 + $3,820.00).
  2. Hana, salary $48,000. CPP is $2,647.75 (($48,000 - $3,500) x 5.95%), EI $782.40 ($48,000 x 1.63%) and income tax $5,282.30 ($3,726.36 federal plus $1,555.94 territorial), leaving $39,287.55. A $3,000 payroll RRSP cuts income tax by $597.00: $420.00 federal ($3,000 x 14%) and $177.00 territorial ($3,000 x 5.9%).
  3. Ira, taxable income $175,000. Total tax is $48,033.26: $32,955.25 federal and $15,078.01 territorial. The $2,654 above $172,346 pays $372.89 at 14.05%, $49.10 more than at 12.2%. An $8,000 RRSP deduction saves $3,105.10: $2,080.00 federal ($8,000 x 26%) and $1,025.10 territorial ($2,654 x 14.05% = $372.89, plus $5,346 x 12.2% = $652.21).

What the three show: Gabe's whole deduction sits in one band, so it saves a flat 38.20%; Hana saves 19.90% on each RRSP dollar; and Ira's deduction pulls him back under the $172,346 line, so its first $2,654 saves the most.

Northwest Territories Tax Brackets 2026 FAQ

What are the Northwest Territories tax brackets for 2026?

Territorial tax for 2026 starts at 5.9% on income up to $53,003, rises to 8.6% up to $106,009 and 12.2% up to $172,346, and reaches 14.05% above that. Federal tax of 14% to 33% is added. The combined top rate is 47.05% on income over $258,482, the second-lowest in Canada after Nunavut.

What is the Northwest Territories basic personal amount for 2026?

It is $18,198 for 2026, worth a non-refundable credit of $1,073.68 at 5.9%. It does not shrink at higher incomes. The federal basic personal amount is $16,452, so income between $16,452 and $18,198 pays federal tax only, and no territorial tax.

What is the top marginal tax rate in the Northwest Territories?

The top combined rate is 47.05% on ordinary income over $258,482: 33% federal plus 14.05% territorial. From $181,440 to $258,482 it is 43.34%. Because only half a gain is taxable, a capital gain at the top pays 23.525%. Eligible dividends at the top are taxed at 28.33% and other than eligible dividends at 36.82%.

How much tax do I pay on $100,000 in the Northwest Territories?

With $100,000 of taxable income you owe $20,487.96 for 2026, of which $14,392.73 is federal and $6,095.24 territorial: 20.49% on average and 29.10% on the next dollar. A $100,000 salary leaves $75,204.48 of take-home pay after CPP, CPP2, EI and tax.

Does the NWT have a surtax or health premium?

No. CRA's 2026 payroll formulas include a provincial surtax and a health premium only for Ontario, and the 16.5% abatement applies only in Quebec. The NWT's top 14.05% rate is the full territorial rate, which keeps its top combined rate at 47.05%.

Why are there short tax bands in the NWT?

The NWT's thresholds fall a little below the federal ones, so for a few thousand dollars your territorial rate has risen while your federal rate has not. From $53,003 to $58,523 the combined rate is 22.60%, and from $172,346 to $181,440 it is 40.05%.

What is take-home pay on a $60,000 salary in the NWT?

After 2026 CPP of $3,361.75, EI of $978.00, $5,338.30 of federal tax and $2,383.94 of territorial tax, a $60,000 salary leaves $47,938.02. Paid every two weeks, that is $1,843.77 a cheque, or $3,994.83 if paid monthly, and deductions take 20.10% of gross pay. The take-home pay calculator on this page shows the same figures for any salary.

How much does an RRSP contribution save in the NWT?

Deducting $5,000 is worth $995.00 if your taxable income is $45,000, $1,226.01 at $60,000, $1,455.00 at $100,000 and $1,910.00 at $150,000. The saving per dollar is your combined marginal rate, from 19.90% to 47.05%. The RRSP calculator on this page is preset to the territory.

How are capital gains taxed in the Northwest Territories?

Half of a 2026 gain is added to taxable income and taxed at federal and territorial rates, from 7% just above $16,452 of taxable income to 23.525% at the top. A $16,000 gain on top of $50,000 of income adds $1,726.92 of tax, or 10.79% of the gain.

Can I calculate dividend tax for the NWT?

Yes. The Northwest Territories' dividend tax credit is 11.5% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 6% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet NT428 (line 61520). The calculator combines them with the federal 38% and 15% gross-ups and the federal credits. CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; the territory's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax.

Is NWT tax lower than Alberta tax?

At middle incomes, yes. At $75,000 the NWT total is $13,212.96 against Alberta's $13,722.21, and at $100,000 it is $859.25 lower. Below $35,612 of taxable income Alberta's larger basic amount makes it cheaper. At $150,000 the two are within $24.43, and at $250,000 Alberta is $1,065.46 cheaper because Alberta's rates stay at 14% or less up to $370,220, while the NWT's 14.05% rate starts at $172,346.
