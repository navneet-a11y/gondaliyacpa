1. How to Use the Income Tax Calculator Canada 2026

Province or territory. Pick the province or territory where you lived on December 31, 2026. That one province taxes your whole year's income, even if you moved in the middle of the year. If you moved from Alberta to Ontario in August, your 2026 return uses Ontario rates for all 12 months.

Employment and other ordinary income. Enter salary, wages, bonuses, taxable benefits, self-employment profit, interest, rental profit, pension income and RRSP or RRIF withdrawals. These are all taxed at the same full rates. If you are not sure of the total, your T4 slips and last year's return are good starting points. Enter the amount before any RRSP or FHSA deduction; those go in their own box.

Capital gains realized. Enter the full gain from selling investments in a non-registered account in 2026, not the taxable half. The calculator applies the inclusion rate for you. Gains inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA are not taxed this way, so leave them out. For a single sale, the capital gains tax calculator works out the gain from your sale price and adjusted cost base.

Dividends. Enter the actual dividends you received from Canadian companies, split into eligible and other than eligible amounts as shown on your slips. The calculator adds the gross-up and subtracts the dividend tax credits. Dividend tax credit rates are confirmed for all 13 provinces and territories. For Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and the three territories, the calculator uses the rates on CRA's 2025 provincial worksheets (line 61520); CRA publishes the 2026 worksheets in January 2027, and none of those six 2026 budgets changed personal income tax.

RRSP and FHSA deductions. Enter the total you will deduct for 2026. The calculator subtracts it from your income before working out tax, so you can see the effect of a contribution straight away.

Reading the results. You get taxable income, federal tax, provincial tax, total tax, your average rate on all income and your after-tax income, plus a table of marginal rates on the next $100 of each type of income. For more tools, see all our investing, savings and tax calculators.

3. Worked Examples: Three 2026 Tax Bills Line by Line

This example is hypothetical. Three people use the calculator for 2026, each with only ordinary income and no deductions.

  1. Amira, Nova Scotia, $55,000. Federal: all $55,000 sits in the 14% bracket, so tax before credits is $7,700.00 ($55,000 x 14%). The basic personal amount credit is $2,303.28 ($16,452 x 14%), leaving federal tax of $5,396.72. Nova Scotia: $2,724.46 on the first $30,995 ($30,995 x 8.79%) plus $3,588.75 on the next $24,005 ($24,005 x 14.95%) is $6,313.21, minus the provincial credit of $1,048.82 ($11,932 x 8.79%), which gives $5,264.39. Total: $5,396.72 + $5,264.39 = $10,661.11, an average rate of 19.38%. Her marginal rate is 28.95% (14% + 14.95%).
  2. Lucas, Ontario, $124,000. Federal: $8,193.22 ($58,523 x 14%) + $11,997.01 ($58,522 x 20.5%) + $1,808.30 ($6,955 x 26%) = $21,998.53, minus $2,303.28, gives $19,695.25. Ontario: $2,721.50 ($53,891 x 5.05%) + $4,931.30 ($53,894 x 9.15%) + $1,809.59 ($16,215 x 11.16%) = $9,462.39, minus the $655.94 credit ($12,989 x 5.05%), gives basic Ontario tax of $8,806.45. Surtax: $597.69 (20% x ($8,806.45 - $5,818)) + $489.76 (36% x ($8,806.45 - $7,446)). Health Premium: $750.00. Ontario total: $8,806.45 + $597.69 + $489.76 + $750.00 = $10,643.90. Total tax: $19,695.25 + $10,643.90 = $30,339.15, an average rate of 24.47%, with a marginal rate of 43.41%.
  3. Chloé, Quebec, $80,000. Federal before the abatement: $8,193.22 + $4,402.79 ($21,477 x 20.5%) = $12,596.01, minus $2,303.28, gives $10,292.73. The 16.5% abatement removes $1,698.30, leaving $8,594.43. Quebec: $7,608.30 ($54,345 x 14%) + $4,874.45 ($25,655 x 19%) = $12,482.75, minus the Quebec credit of $2,653.28 ($18,952 x 14%), gives $9,829.47. Total: $8,594.43 + $9,829.47 = $18,423.90, an average rate of 23.03%, with a marginal rate of 36.12%.

Each total matches the calculator's result for the same inputs (the calculator shows it rounded to the dollar), because the tables, the examples and the calculator share one set of 2026 figures.

What the examples show. Lucas pays a much higher marginal rate than Amira, but his average rate is only about five points higher, because his first dollars are taxed at the same low rates as hers. Chloé pays more provincial tax than federal tax, which happens only in Quebec. Ontario's surtax adds $1,087.45 to Lucas's bill ($597.69 + $489.76), which is why his Ontario marginal rate is 11.16% x 1.56 = 17.41% rather than 11.16%.

5. Marginal Tax Rate Calculator: Marginal vs Average Tax Rate in Canada

Average tax rate. Your average tax rate is total tax divided by income. An Ontario resident with $100,000 of taxable income pays $21,520.55, an average rate of 21.52%. This is the rate to use when you want to know what share of your income goes to tax overall, or to compare your tax with someone else's.

Marginal tax rate. Your marginal rate is the tax on your next dollar. The same Ontario resident pays 31.48 cents on the next dollar: 20.5% federal plus Ontario's 9.15% rate raised by the 20% surtax (9.15% x 1.2 = 10.98%). The calculator works it out by adding $100 to your income and measuring the extra tax, which captures surtaxes, premiums and credit phase-outs automatically.

Why both matter. The marginal rate tells you what a raise, a bonus, an RRSP deduction or an RRSP withdrawal is worth. The average rate tells you how heavy your overall bill is. A raise never leaves you worse off: only the new dollars are taxed at the higher rate, never the dollars you already earned.

Hidden spikes. Some marginal rates are much higher over short ranges. In Ontario, the Health Premium rises by 25 cents per dollar from $48,000 to $48,600 and again from $72,000 to $72,600, so the marginal rate at $48,300 is 44.05% and at $72,300 is 54.65%. Federally, between $181,440 and $258,482, the shrinking basic personal amount adds about 0.29 points, so the federal rate on those dollars is 29.29% rather than 29%.

Average tax rate in Canada at a glance. At $80,000, average rates range from 16.79% in Nunavut to 24.51% in Nova Scotia; at $150,000, from 23.51% to 31.91%. The tables in section 4 give every province, and our guide to marginal tax rates in Canada lists the combined rate at every bracket edge.

6. Credits the Calculator Includes and Leaves Out

Included. The federal basic personal amount with its 2026 phase-down, every province's basic personal amount (with Manitoba's phase-out and Yukon's federal mirror), the Ontario and B.C. low-income tax reductions, the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credits in all 13 provinces and territories, the Ontario surtax and Health Premium, and the Quebec abatement.

CPP, EI and the Canada employment amount. Employees get a non-refundable credit for base CPP and EI contributions and deduct the enhanced part of CPP from income. They also claim the Canada employment amount, $1,501 for 2026, which is worth $210.14 federally ($1,501 x 14%). The income tax calculator leaves these out; the take-home pay calculator in section 9 includes them. For an Ontario employee earning $80,000, they cut 2026 income tax from $15,497.25 to $14,127.85, a difference of $1,369.40.

Low-income provincial tax reductions. The calculator includes the two that CRA's 2026 payroll formulas list. British Columbia's tax reduction starts at $690 and shrinks by 3.56% of income above $25,570, reaching zero at $44,952. At $40,000 it is worth $176.29 ($690 - 3.56% x $14,430), so B.C. tax falls from $1,499.90 to $1,323.61. Ontario's tax reduction is two times a $300 basic amount, or $600, minus basic Ontario tax and surtax, never below zero. It cancels all Ontario tax up to $18,929.59 of income and is fully used up at $24,870.19. The calculator assumes no dependants for the Ontario reduction.

Personal and family credits. The calculator leaves out every credit other than the basic personal amounts. The 2026 federal figures include an age amount of $9,208 for people 65 or older, reduced above $46,432 of net income and gone at $107,819; a pension income amount of up to $2,000; a disability amount of $10,341; and a medical expense credit on costs above the lesser of 3% of net income and $2,890. The spouse or common-law partner amount, the amount for an eligible dependant, tuition and donations also lower tax for those who qualify, so the calculator overstates tax for them. At the 14% federal rate, the full age amount alone is worth $1,289.12 ($9,208 x 14%).

Refundable credits and benefits. The Canada Workers Benefit, the GST/HST credit and the Canada Child Benefit are paid separately from income tax and are not part of the result. Our Canada Workers Benefit and Canada Child Benefit pages cover them.

Why the estimate is still useful. Most credits are flat amounts that do not change with income, so they barely affect your marginal rate. Decisions that turn on the marginal rate, such as how much an RRSP deduction saves, come out nearly exact even when the total tax is a little high.

7. How RRSP and FHSA Deductions Change Your Tax

How a deduction works. RRSP and FHSA contributions are deducted from income before tax is worked out, so they save tax at your marginal rate. Enter the amount in the calculator's deduction box and compare the result with and without it.

RRSP room. Your 2026 RRSP deduction limit is the lesser of 18% of your 2025 earned income and $33,810, minus any pension adjustment, plus unused room carried forward. Contributions for 2026 can be made until March 1, 2027, the 60th day of 2027. Our RRSP contribution limit page explains how to find yours.

FHSA room. A first home savings account gives $8,000 of room a year, up to $40,000 over your lifetime. The contribution year ends December 31, with no 60-day window, but you can deduct a contribution in a later year, even after the account is closed. The FHSA guide covers who can open one.

8. Capital Gains and Dividends in the Canada Tax Calculator 2026

Capital gains. The calculator includes one half of your capital gains in taxable income. One half is the inclusion rate for 2026, as it was from 2001 through 2025; the proposed increase to two-thirds was cancelled. A $10,000 gain therefore adds $5,000 to taxable income, and the marginal rate on capital gains is about half the rate on ordinary income.

Eligible dividends. Eligible dividends, mostly from public Canadian companies, are grossed up by 38%. The federal dividend tax credit is 15.0198% of the grossed-up amount. The provincial credits the calculator uses are 10% in Ontario, 12% in British Columbia, 11.70% in Quebec, 14% in New Brunswick, 8.85% in Nova Scotia, 10.50% in Prince Edward Island, 6.3% in Newfoundland and Labrador, 8.12% in Alberta, 11% in Saskatchewan, 8% in Manitoba, 12.02% in Yukon, 11.5% in the Northwest Territories and 5.51% in Nunavut.

The provincial credits are 2.9863% in Ontario for 2026 (falling to 1.9863% in 2027), 1.96% in British Columbia, 3.42% in Quebec, 2.75% in New Brunswick, 1.5% in Nova Scotia, 1.3043% in Prince Edward Island, 3.2% in Newfoundland and Labrador, 2.18% in Alberta, 2.519% in Saskatchewan, 0.7835% in Manitoba, 0.67% in Yukon, 6% in the Northwest Territories and 2.61% in Nunavut.

8 Ways to Use Your Income Tax Calculator Canada Result

1. Price a raise or bonus

Multiply the raise by your marginal rate to see the tax on it. In Ontario at $80,000, a $5,000 bonus costs $1,482.50 in tax at 29.65%, leaving $3,517.50, before CPP and EI.

2. Size an RRSP or FHSA deduction

Try several amounts in the deduction box. Stop where the saving per dollar drops, because the deduction has reached a lower bracket.

3. Plan RRSP withdrawals

A withdrawal is taxed at your marginal rate in the year you take it. Low-income years, such as a career break, cost the least. Our RRSP withdrawal tax guide shows the withholding.

4. Avoid a balance owing

If you have income with no tax withheld, compare the calculator's total with the tax on your slips and set money aside for the difference before April.

5. Watch the Ontario premium bands

Ontario residents just above $48,000 or $72,000 face marginal rates over 44%. A small deduction that keeps income below the band start saves more than the bracket rates suggest.

6. Compare provinces before a move

Run the same income in two provinces. At $100,000 the gap between Ontario and Alberta is $173.34, but between Ontario and Nova Scotia it is $5,539.25.

7. Split income where the rules allow

Couples can split eligible pension income and use a spousal RRSP. Our pension income splitting guide explains the rules.

8. Mind benefit thresholds

Net income also drives the OAS recovery tax and family benefits. A deduction that lowers net income can save more than the calculator's tax figure; see the OAS clawback 2026 page.

Income Tax Calculator Canada: FAQ

How does the income tax calculator for Canada work?

It applies the 2026 federal brackets to your taxable income, subtracts the federal basic personal amount credit, then does the same with your province's brackets and basic amount. It adds the Ontario surtax and Health Premium, applies the Quebec abatement, and handles capital gains and dividends. CPP, EI and most other credits are left out, so the result is a close estimate.

How much tax do I pay on $60,000 in Canada?

On $60,000 of taxable income in 2026, total tax is $9,417.25 in Ontario, $9,015.01 in British Columbia, $9,171.21 in Alberta and $11,200.40 in Quebec. Federal tax outside Quebec is $6,192.73. These figures count only the basic personal amounts. An employee's CPP and EI credits and the Canada employment amount would lower them a little.

How much tax do I pay on $100,000 in Canada?

On $100,000 of taxable income in 2026, total tax is $21,520.55 in Ontario, $20,295.01 in British Columbia, $21,347.21 in Alberta and $25,647.40 in Quebec. Nunavut is lowest at $18,932.34 and Nova Scotia highest at $27,059.80. The tables on this page show the federal and provincial split for all 13 provinces and territories.

What is the difference between marginal and average tax rate?

Your average rate is total tax divided by income. Your marginal rate is the tax on your next dollar. In Ontario at $100,000, the average rate is 21.52% and the marginal rate is 31.48%. Use the marginal rate to judge a raise, a bonus or an RRSP deduction, and the average rate to see your overall tax burden.

Which province has the lowest income tax?

Among the provinces, at $80,000 of 2026 taxable income British Columbia has the lowest total tax at $14,655.01, followed by Alberta at $15,247.21 and Ontario at $15,497.25. Counting the territories, Nunavut is lowest at $13,432.34. At $250,000, Alberta is the lowest province at $79,264.31. The order changes with income, so please compare at your own income.

Does the calculator include CPP and EI?

No. The income tax calculator shows income tax only. CPP, CPP2 and EI contributions are separate payroll deductions, and the credits they create are not included, so an employee's final tax is usually slightly lower. The take-home pay calculator on this page includes CPP, CPP2, EI and, for Quebec, QPP and QPIP.

Does the calculator include low-income tax reductions?

Yes, for Ontario and British Columbia. The B.C. tax reduction starts at $690, shrinks by 3.56% of income above $25,570 and reaches zero at $44,952. Ontario's reduction is $600 minus basic Ontario tax and surtax, never below zero. At $40,000, the B.C. reduction is worth $176.29. The calculator assumes no dependants for the Ontario reduction.

What are Manitoba's 2026 tax brackets?

Manitoba taxes 2026 income at 10.8% up to $47,000, 12.75% from $47,000 to $100,000 and 17.4% above $100,000. The Income Tax Act (Manitoba) fixes these thresholds for 2024 onward with no indexing. A CRA rates page shows $47,564 and $101,200, which do not match the Act. The calculator uses the statutory figures.

How do RRSP and FHSA contributions reduce my tax?

Both are deducted from income, so they save tax at your marginal rate. An $8,000 FHSA deduction at $80,000 saves $2,522.00 in Ontario, $2,256.00 in British Columbia and $2,889.40 in Quebec in 2026. Enter your contribution in the calculator's deduction box to see your own saving.

Does the calculator work for Quebec?

Yes. For Quebec it applies the 2026 Quebec brackets from 14% to 25.75%, the $18,952 Quebec basic personal amount and the 16.5% abatement of federal tax. At $80,000, a Quebec resident pays $8,594.43 federal and $9,829.47 Quebec tax, a total of $18,423.90. Quebec residents file a separate Quebec return.
