At a glance

2026 Yukon brackets. 6.4% up to $58,523; 9% to $117,045; 10.9% to $181,440; 12.8% to $500,000; and 15% on taxable income over $500,000. The first three thresholds are the same as the federal ones.

Basic personal amount. The Yukon basic personal amount for 2026 is $16,452, worth a credit of $1,052.93 at 6.4%. Like the federal amount, it falls to $14,829 as net income rises from $181,440 to $258,482.

Top combined rate. 48% on ordinary income over $500,000 (33% federal plus 15% Yukon) and 24% on capital gains. Between $258,482 and $500,000 the combined rate is 45.8%.

Tax at a typical income. A Yukoner with $100,000 of taxable income owes $14,392.73 of federal tax and $6,425.47 of Yukon tax for 2026, or $20,818.20 in all. The average rate is 20.82%, and the next dollar is taxed at 29.50%.

A Yukon-only credit. Yukon is the only province or territory with its own Canada employment amount, $1,501 for 2026, worth $96.06 at 6.4%. The take-home pay figures include it.

Dividends. Yukon's dividend tax credit is 12.02% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 0.67% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet YT428 (line 61520). 1. Yukon Tax Brackets 2026 and Rates

Five brackets that track the federal ones. Yukon taxes income in five bands. Its first three thresholds, $58,523, $117,045 and $181,440, are exactly the federal thresholds, so a Yukoner's federal and territorial brackets change at the same incomes up to $181,440. Only the $500,000 threshold for the 15% rate is Yukon's own. Each rate applies only to the income inside its band.

Why the matching thresholds help. Because the federal and Yukon brackets break at the same points, your combined marginal rate changes only four times between $16,452 and $258,482, while the Northwest Territories, with thresholds of its own, has eight changes over the same range. That makes Yukon tax easier to plan: if you know your federal bracket, you know your Yukon bracket too.

The $500,000 bracket. Only income above $500,000 pays the 15% Yukon rate. Someone with $600,000 of taxable income pays $15,000 of Yukon tax on the top $100,000 ($100,000 x 15%), $2,200 more than the $12,800 it would cost at 12.8%. Below $500,000 the bracket has no effect at all.

What income the brackets use. The rates apply to taxable income, which is what remains after deductions like RRSP and FHSA contributions, union dues, child care costs and enhanced CPP contributions come off your total income. Residence on December 31, 2026 decides the territory: a person living in Yukon that day pays Yukon tax on the full year's taxable income, including income earned before an autumn move north.

Using the running totals. At $117,045 of taxable income, Yukon tax before credits is $9,012.45, the first two bands added together (the table rounds it to $9,012). Subtract the $1,052.93 basic personal amount credit and the Yukon tax is $7,959.52. The Canada-wide income tax calculator does the same sum for any province.

Federal and Yukon basic amounts move together. The federal basic personal amount is $16,452 up to $181,440 of net income and shrinks in a straight line to $14,829 at $258,482. Yukon's amount follows the same formula, so in that income range both credits shrink at once. That double phase-down is why Yukon's combined rate between $181,440 and $258,482 is 42.23% rather than the 41.8% you get by adding 29% and 12.8%.

Indexation. Federal brackets and amounts rose by 2.0% for 2026. Yukon's first three thresholds and its basic personal amount equal the 2026 federal figures, so a Yukoner can read both sets of brackets from one list.

The two credits at a glance. At incomes up to $181,440, the federal basic amount is worth $2,303.28 ($16,452 x 14%) and the Yukon amount $1,052.93 ($16,452 x 6.4%), a combined $3,356.21. Together they mean no income tax at all on the first $16,452 of taxable income. Compare every province's figure on our basic personal amount 2026 page.

5. Yukon Basic Personal Amount 2026 and Credits

$16,452, with a phase-down. The Yukon basic personal amount for 2026 is $16,452. CRA's payroll formulas set it equal to the federal amount, so it also drops to a minimum of $14,829 for net incomes of $258,482 or more. At $230,000 of income, both the federal and Yukon amounts are $15,429.01. Most provinces use a flat basic amount. Manitoba reduces its amount at high incomes on its own schedule, and Yukon is the one that copies the federal reduction.

The Yukon Canada employment amount. Employees can claim the federal Canada employment amount of $1,501 for 2026. Yukon is the only province or territory with its own version, also $1,501. In CRA's payroll formulas the Yukon credit is 6.4% of $1,501, or of your income if that is lower, so up to $96.06. Combined with the federal credit of $210.14, an employee saves up to $306.20 from the two employment amounts.

6. Surtaxes, Premiums and Abatements in Yukon

No Yukon surtax. Yukon has no surtax on territorial tax in CRA's 2026 payroll formulas, which include a provincial surtax only for Ontario. No other province or territory has one.

No health premium. The only health premium collected through income tax is Ontario's, which reaches $900 a year at higher incomes. Yukoners pay none. Our Ontario Health Premium page explains the Ontario version for anyone moving south.

No abatement. Only Quebec residents get the 16.5% reduction of federal tax, so Yukoners pay the full federal amounts shown on this page.

No low-income reduction in payroll. Ontario and British Columbia have provincial tax reductions for lower incomes built into payroll; Yukon does not. In payroll and in our calculator, its relief for lower incomes comes from the $16,452 basic personal amount, which is larger than British Columbia's $13,216 and Ontario's $12,989.

The 15% rate is a bracket. Yukon's top 15% rate over $500,000 is sometimes described loosely as a surtax. It is an ordinary bracket rate charged on income above the threshold, not a charge on tax already calculated, so the top combined rate is simply 33% plus 15%, or 48%.

What this page leaves out. Everything here is built from the Yukon brackets, the basic and employment amounts and the federal credits in section 5. Yukoners may be able to claim other deductions or receive other payments, but we have not yet confirmed their 2026 amounts, so none of them is in the calculator or the tables.

Refund or lower withholding. The tax saved becomes a refund only if your employer withheld tax as though you had no RRSP. A payroll RRSP gives you the saving in each pay instead.

9. Capital Gains and Dividends in Yukon

Half the gain is taxed. For 2026, half of a capital gain on investments held outside registered accounts is added to taxable income and taxed at federal and Yukon rates. The calculator below, preset to Yukon, works out the tax on a sale. For a fuller walk-through of adjusted cost base, use the capital gains tax calculator.

This example is hypothetical. A Yukoner with $120,000 of other taxable income sells mutual fund units for a $30,000 capital gain.

  1. Taxable part. $30,000 x 50% = $15,000, so taxable income rises from $120,000 to $135,000.
  2. Federal tax. The whole $15,000 sits in the 26% federal band: $15,000 x 26% = $3,900.00.
  3. Yukon tax. It also sits in the 10.9% Yukon band: $15,000 x 10.9% = $1,635.00.
  4. Total. $3,900.00 + $1,635.00 = $5,535.00, which is 18.45% of the gain. The seller keeps $24,465.00.

Bigger gains reach higher bands. Had the same person realized a gain large enough to push taxable income past $181,440, each extra dollar of gain would be taxed at 21.11%, and past $258,482 at 22.9%. Spreading a large sale over two calendar years can keep more of the gain in a lower band.

Losses and the exemption. Net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely, against capital gains only, and the superficial loss rule denies a loss if you or an affiliated person buy the same property within 30 days before or after the sale and still hold it 30 days after. For qualified small business shares and qualified farm or fishing property, up to $1,275,000 of gains can be sheltered by the 2026 lifetime exemption. Our guides to capital losses, the superficial loss rule and the lifetime capital gains exemption cover the details.

Dividends in Yukon. Yukon's dividend tax credit is 12.02% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 0.67% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet YT428 (line 61520). The federal half is a 38% gross-up and 15.0198% credit for eligible dividends, and a 15% gross-up and 9.0301% credit for other than eligible dividends. Together they give combined rates from -9.16% on eligible dividends in the first band to 28.93% over $500,000, and from 12.30% to 44.04% on other than eligible dividends. The income tax calculator and the dividend tax calculator both include Yukon dividends.

11. Ways to Lower Your Yukon Tax

Rule-based ideas, not personal advice. These strategies use the 2026 Yukon and federal rules. Please test any of them in the calculators on this page before you act.

1. Open a TFSA at 19

The age of majority in Yukon is 19, so you open a TFSA at 19, but room from the year you turned 18 carries over. The 2026 limit is $7,000. See the TFSA guide.

2. Use the 29.5% band for RRSP deductions

Every RRSP dollar saves 20.40% below $58,523 and 29.50% above it. Deducting in years above the line, and carrying deductions forward from years below it, gets more from the same contribution.

3. Mind the phase-down band

Between $181,440 and $258,482 both basic amounts shrink, so the rate is 42.23%. A deduction or a deferred bonus that keeps income below $181,440 saves at least 36.90% and avoids the phase-down.

4. Open an FHSA if you are buying a first home

You can deduct up to $8,000 of FHSA contributions a year, to a lifetime total of $40,000, and a qualifying withdrawal for a first home is not taxed. The FHSA guide explains eligibility.

5. Contribute to a spousal RRSP

A higher-earning spouse can claim the deduction now while building savings in the lower earner's name, subject to the three-year attribution rule. See the spousal RRSP guide.

6. Split eligible pension income

Retired couples can move up to 50% of eligible pension income to the spouse with less income. Our page on pension income splitting shows the conditions.

7. Keep net income under the OAS threshold

The 2026 OAS recovery tax starts at $95,323 of net income and takes 15% of the excess. RRSP deductions and pension splitting can keep a senior below it; see the OAS clawback page.

8. Sell losers in the same year as winners

A capital loss realized in the year of a gain cuts the taxable half of the gain at once. Wait out the 30-day superficial loss window if you plan to buy back.

9. Keep interest inside registered accounts

Interest is taxed at your full Yukon and federal rate, up to 48%. Holding interest-paying investments in an RRSP or TFSA keeps it off your return.

10. Ask for lower withholding

With large regular RRSP contributions you can apply on Form T1213 for reduced tax withholding, so the saving arrives each pay rather than as a refund next spring.

12. Common Yukon Tax Mistakes

Treating the Yukon basic amount as flat. A calculator that applies $16,452 at every income gets high incomes wrong. Above $181,440 of net income the Yukon amount shrinks with the federal one, to $14,829 at $258,482. Using the full amount understates Yukon tax by up to $103.87 ($1,623 x 6.4%) at the top.

Missing the Yukon employment amount. Payroll systems apply the Yukon Canada employment amount automatically, but some estimates leave it out and overstate Yukon tax by up to $96.06.

Thinking the 15% rate applies to all your income. Only the dollars above $500,000 pay 15%. A person with $510,000 pays 15% on $10,000 and 12.8% on the income below.

Using last year's federal rate. The 2025 federal lowest rate was a blended 14.5%. For 2026 it is 14%, which also lowers the value of federal credits. Please make sure any spreadsheet uses 2026 figures.

Choosing the wrong province in tax software. Yukon residence on December 31 decides the whole year. If you moved to or from Yukon in 2026, check the province setting before you file. Our guide to filing taxes online and the ranking of the best tax software can help.

With $7,000 of payroll RRSP contributions, income tax falls by $2,065.00: $1,435.00 federal ($7,000 x 20.5%) and $630.00 Yukon ($7,000 x 9%), and yearly take-home pay becomes $59,488.02 ($64,423.02 - $7,000 + $2,065.00).
  2. Eli, taxable income $230,000. Both basic personal amounts have shrunk to $15,429.01 ($16,452 - $1,623 x ($230,000 - $181,440) / $77,042). Total tax is $70,115.00, split $48,855.27 federal and $21,259.73 Yukon; Eli's average rate is 30.48% and the next dollar is taxed at 42.23%. A $15,000 RRSP deduction saves $6,334.46: federal $4,394.24 ($15,000 x 29% = $4,350.00, plus $44.24 from a larger basic amount) and Yukon $1,940.22 ($15,000 x 12.8% = $1,920.00, plus $20.22).
  3. Faye, taxable income $600,000. Total tax is $240,758.12: $169,899.99 federal and $70,858.13 Yukon. Her average rate is 40.13% and her marginal rate 48%. The 15% Yukon rate on her top $100,000 costs $15,000, or $2,200 more than the $12,800 it would cost at 12.8%.

Yukon Tax Brackets 2026 FAQ

What are the Yukon tax brackets for 2026?

Yukon taxes 2026 income at 6.4% up to $58,523, 9% to $117,045, 10.9% to $181,440, 12.8% to $500,000 and 15% above $500,000. The first three thresholds match the federal ones. Federal tax, from 14% to 33%, is added, for a top combined rate of 48%.

What is the Yukon basic personal amount for 2026?

It is $16,452, the same as the federal amount, and gives a Yukon credit of $1,052.93 at 6.4%. Like the federal amount, it shrinks in a straight line to $14,829 as net income rises from $181,440 to $258,482. Together the two credits mean no income tax on the first $16,452.

Why does the Yukon basic personal amount go down at high incomes?

Yukon's basic personal amount follows the federal formula, which reduces the amount by $1,623 over the income range from $181,440 to $258,482. As both credits shrink, the combined marginal rate in that range is 42.23% instead of 41.8%. Above $258,482 both amounts stay at $14,829.

What is the top marginal tax rate in Yukon?

The top combined rate is 48% on ordinary income over $500,000: 33% federal plus 15% Yukon. From $258,482 to $500,000 it is 45.8%. Capital gains are taxed at half those rates, so the top rate on a gain is 24%. For dividends in that top band, the combined rate is 28.93% on eligible dividends and 44.04% on other than eligible dividends.

How much tax do I pay on $75,000 in Yukon?

Federal tax on $75,000 of 2026 taxable income is $9,267.73 and Yukon adds $4,175.47, so the bill is $13,443.20, an average of 17.92%. Your next dollar would be taxed at 29.50%. An employee with a $75,000 salary pays less, because CPP, EI and employment credits reduce the tax.

Does Yukon have its own Canada employment amount?

Yes. Yukon is the only province or territory with one: $1,501 for 2026, the same as the federal amount. In payroll it gives a Yukon credit of up to $96.06 (6.4% of $1,501). Together with the federal credit of $210.14, employees save up to $306.20.

Does Yukon have a surtax or health premium?

No. CRA's 2026 payroll formulas include a provincial surtax and a health premium only for Ontario. Yukon also has no abatement, which applies only in Quebec. Yukon's 15% rate over $500,000 is a normal bracket, not a surtax, so the top combined rate is simply 48%.

What is take-home pay on a $60,000 salary in Yukon?

For 2026, a $60,000 salary leaves $47,884.98 a year after CPP of $3,361.75, EI of $978.00, federal tax of $5,338.30 and Yukon tax of $2,436.98. That is $1,841.73 every two weeks or $3,990.41 a month, with 20.19% of pay going to deductions.

How much does an RRSP contribution save in Yukon?

In 2026 a $5,000 deduction cuts federal and Yukon tax by $1,020.00 for someone with $50,000 of taxable income, by $1,475.00 at $75,000, by $1,845.00 at $125,000 and by $2,111.49 at $200,000. The saving per dollar equals your combined marginal rate, from 20.40% to 48%. The RRSP calculator on this page is preset to Yukon.

How are capital gains taxed in Yukon?

Only half of a gain realized in 2026 counts as taxable income, and that half is taxed at your federal and Yukon rates. The rate on a gain is 10.20% in the first band and 24% at the top. A $30,000 gain on top of $120,000 of income costs $5,535.00, or 18.45% of the gain.

Can I enter dividends for Yukon in the calculator?

Yes. Yukon's dividend tax credit is 12.02% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 0.67% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet YT428 (line 61520). The calculator combines them with the federal 38% and 15% gross-ups and the federal credits of 15.0198% and 9.0301%. CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; Yukon's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax.

Is Yukon tax lower than British Columbia tax?

It depends on income. British Columbia charges less at $100,000 and below in our table: $13,245.01 against Yukon's $13,443.20 at $75,000. At $150,000 and $250,000 Yukon is cheaper, by $425.81 and $4,082.23, because British Columbia's rates climb to 20.5% while Yukon's top rate is 15%.
