1. Nunavut Tax Brackets 2026 and Rates

Four low rates. Nunavut has four territorial rates for 2026, and no province or territory starts lower than its 4%. As everywhere, each rate applies only to the income inside its band.

Doubling thresholds. Nunavut's second threshold, $111,602, is exactly twice the first, $55,801. The third, $181,439, sits one dollar below the federal $181,440 threshold. For that single dollar, between $181,439 and $181,440, the combined rate is 37.5% (26% federal plus 11.5%); every dollar above $181,440 then moves into the federal 29% band.

What the rates add up to. Even at $181,439 of taxable income, Nunavut tax before credits is $12,423.44, the sum of the first three bands. After the $786.36 basic personal amount credit it is $11,637.08, or 6.41% of income. A Nunavut resident earning $50,000 pays $1,213.64 of territorial tax ($50,000 x 4% = $2,000.00, less $786.36).

Who pays Nunavut tax. If you lived in Nunavut on December 31, 2026, Nunavut tax applies to your whole year's taxable income. A worker who moved to Nunavut in the autumn pays Nunavut tax for all of 2026, and one who moved south before December 31 does not. Taxable income, the figure the brackets use, is total income after deductions such as RRSP and FHSA contributions, union dues and enhanced CPP contributions.

Comparing with any income. The table and section 4 cover common incomes. For any other amount, or to compare Nunavut with a province you are thinking of moving to, use the income tax calculator for every province.

2. Federal 2026 Tax Brackets for Nunavut Residents

The larger share of your tax. In Nunavut, federal tax makes up most of the income tax bill: at $75,000, federal tax is $9,267.73 and Nunavut tax $2,789.61. Federal rates run from 14% to 33%. Our page on federal tax brackets for 2026 explains them in detail.

The 14% lowest rate. The federal lowest rate is 14% for the whole of 2026. The 2025 rate was a blended 14.5%, since the drop from 15% only began on July 1, 2025. Federal credits are also worked out at 14% for 2026.

The federal basic personal amount. It is $16,452, worth $2,303.28, for net income up to $181,440, and falls in a straight line to $14,829 at $258,482. Nunavut's own amount, $19,659, does not fall.

A federal-only band. Because Nunavut's basic amount is $3,207 larger than the federal one, income between $16,452 and $19,659 pays federal tax only. On $19,659, federal tax is $448.98 ($3,207 x 14%) and Nunavut tax is nil. Every province's amount is compared on our basic personal amount 2026 page.

Federal indexation. The federal brackets and amounts rose by the 2.0% indexation factor for 2026.

The lowest rates in Canada. From $19,659 to $55,801 the combined rate is 18%, and from $58,523 to $111,602 it is 27.5%, the lowest middle-income rate of any province or territory. At $75,000 an extra $1,000 of pay costs $275 in income tax, against $371 in Prince Edward Island.

5. Nunavut Basic Personal Amount 2026 and Credits

$19,659 for 2026. CRA's payroll formulas effective July 1, 2026 put the Nunavut basic personal amount at $19,659. It is a non-refundable credit at the 4% lowest rate, worth $786.36, and it applies in full at every income. Only Alberta ($22,769) and Saskatchewan ($20,381) have larger basic amounts.

Why a big amount is worth less here. A basic amount is multiplied by the lowest rate, and Nunavut's 4% is low. Nunavut's $19,659 is worth $786.36, while the Northwest Territories' smaller $18,198 at 5.9% is worth $1,073.68. Nunavut residents still pay less overall, because the low rate also applies to every dollar of income above the basic amount.

Other Nunavut credits. Territorial versions of the age, pension, disability and similar amounts are claimed on Nunavut's own return. No tax on low incomes. With only the basic amounts, federal tax starts above $16,452 and Nunavut tax above $19,659. Take $25,000: federal tax is $1,196.72 ($8,548 x 14%), Nunavut tax is $213.64 ($5,341 x 4%) and the total is $1,410.36.

6. Surtaxes, Premiums and Abatements in Nunavut

No surtax. Ontario is the only province with a surtax in CRA's 2026 payroll formulas. Nunavut's 11.5% top rate is the whole territorial rate on income over $181,439.

No health premium. Ontario alone collects a health premium through income tax, up to $900 a year. Nunavut residents pay none; the Ontario Health Premium page explains how Ontario's works.

No abatement. The 16.5% federal abatement applies only to Quebec residents, so Nunavut residents pay the full federal tax shown in section 4.

No low-income reduction in payroll. Ontario and British Columbia build low-income tax reductions into payroll; Nunavut does not. In payroll and in our calculator, its low rates and large basic amount keep low-income tax small: on $30,000 of taxable income, Nunavut tax is $413.64.

The Canada Workers Benefit. Nunavut is one of three places, with Alberta and Quebec, where Canada Workers Benefit amounts can differ from the standard federal figures under reconfiguration agreements. The 2026 Nunavut figures are not given in the sources we use, so they are not on this page; the Canada Workers Benefit guide covers the standard rules.

Limits of these figures. Our Nunavut numbers rest on the four brackets, the $19,659 basic amount and the federal credits listed in section 5. Any other deduction, credit or payment a Nunavut resident may claim is outside the calculator and the tables until its 2026 amount is confirmed.

Smaller savings at low rates. An RRSP deduction saves your combined marginal rate on each dollar. Because Nunavut's rates are the lowest in Canada, the saving is smaller here than elsewhere at the same income. The calculator below is preset to Nunavut.

The $60,000 row. A $5,000 deduction at $60,000 saves $1,121.98, only 22.44%, because it crosses two thresholds: $1,477 saves 27.5%, $2,722 saves 21% (between $55,801 and $58,523) and the last $801 saves 18%. At $50,000 the whole $5,000 saves 18%, or $900.00. At $125,000 it saves $1,750.00 (35%) and at $200,000, $2,039.75.

RRSP or TFSA? An RRSP deduction at 18% is worth less than at most incomes elsewhere. Withdrawals are taxed later at the rates of the province or territory where you live in the year you withdraw, so a Nunavut resident who expects to retire south may pay a higher rate on withdrawals than the rate the deduction saved. A TFSA gives no deduction but tax-free withdrawals. Our TFSA vs RRSP comparison shows how to weigh the two.

Limit and deadline. How much you can deduct for 2026 is capped at 18% of what you earned in 2025 or $33,810, whichever is less, after any pension adjustment and with unused room from past years added back. Contributions up to the 60th day of 2027 count for 2026, which puts the cut-off at March 1, 2027. See the RRSP contribution limit for 2026 and the RRSP tax refund calculator.

9. Capital Gains and Dividends in Nunavut

Half of a gain is taxed. For 2026, half of a capital gain on investments held outside registered accounts is added to taxable income. The calculator below starts on Nunavut, and the full capital gains tax calculator page explains adjusted cost base and selling costs.

This example is hypothetical. A Nunavut resident with $90,000 of other taxable income sells an investment for a $40,000 capital gain.

  1. Taxable part. $40,000 x 50% = $20,000, taking taxable income from $90,000 to $110,000.
  2. Federal tax. All $20,000 falls in the 20.5% federal band: $20,000 x 20.5% = $4,100.00.
  3. Nunavut tax. $110,000 is still below $111,602, so all $20,000 is taxed at 7%: $1,400.00.
  4. Total. $4,100.00 + $1,400.00 = $5,500.00, or 13.75% of the gain. The seller keeps $34,500.00.

Rates at higher incomes. A gain that pushes taxable income past $111,602 is taxed at 14.75%, past $117,045 at 17.5%, past $181,440 at 20.40% and past $258,482 at 22.25%. Above $117,045 of taxable income, Nunavut's rate on gains is the lowest in Canada at every income; between $111,602 and $117,045 it ties with Yukon at 14.75%.

Losses and the exemption. A net capital loss offsets only capital gains, but you can apply it to any of the three previous years or keep it with no expiry date. If you or an affiliated person buy the same property in the 30 days before or after a sale and still own it 30 days later, the loss is a superficial loss and is denied for now. Owners of qualified small business shares or qualified farm or fishing property can shelter gains up to the 2026 lifetime exemption limit of $1,275,000. Our pages on capital losses, the superficial loss rule and the lifetime capital gains exemption go further.

Dividends. Nunavut's dividend tax credit is 5.51% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 2.61% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet NU428 (line 61520). With the federal 38% and 15% gross-ups and the 15.0198% and 9.0301% federal credits, eligible dividends are taxed from -3.49% between $19,659 and $55,801 to 33.08% over $258,482, and other than eligible dividends from 5.72% to 37.79%. The income tax calculator and the dividend tax calculator both include Nunavut, and the dividend tax credit guide explains the gross-up and credit.

11. Ways to Lower Your Nunavut Tax

General strategies only. They follow the 2026 rules but are not personal advice. Please test them with the calculators on this page before you act.

1. Compare the RRSP with the TFSA

At 18% the RRSP deduction is modest, while TFSA withdrawals are never taxed. If you may retire in a higher-tax province, the TFSA can come out ahead. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000.

2. Deduct RRSPs above $58,523

The saving jumps from 21% to 27.5% at $58,523 and to 35% above $117,045. Contribute when you can, and claim the deduction in a year when your income sits in a higher band.

3. Open a TFSA at 19

Nunavut's age of majority is 19, the earliest age to open a TFSA there, and the room that built up from the year you turned 18 is still yours. The TFSA guide explains how room builds.

4. Use an FHSA for a first home

Deductible contributions of up to $8,000 a year and $40,000 in total, with tax-free qualifying withdrawals. See the FHSA guide.

5. Watch the $181,440 line

Above it the combined rate rises from 35% to 40.79%. A deduction or a deferred bonus that keeps income under the line saves 35 cents a dollar and avoids the federal phase-down.

6. Contribute to a spousal RRSP

A spousal RRSP gives the higher earner the deduction and moves future income to the lower earner, subject to the three-year attribution rule.

7. Split pension income in retirement

Couples can allocate up to 50% of eligible pension income to the lower-income spouse. Our page on pension income splitting explains who qualifies.

8. Keep an eye on OAS

The OAS recovery tax takes 15% of 2026 net income above $95,323. RRSP deductions and pension splitting can help a senior stay below it; see the OAS clawback page.

9. Pair losses with gains

Realizing a loss in the same year as a gain reduces the taxable half of the gain. Stay outside the 30-day superficial loss window if you plan to buy back.

10. Plan withdrawals around where you will live

RRSP and RRIF withdrawals are taxed by the province or territory where you live at the end of the year you take them. Withdrawals made while you still live in Nunavut use Nunavut's low rates.

12. Common Nunavut Tax Mistakes

Expecting a southern-size RRSP refund. A $5,000 deduction at $50,000 saves $900.00 in Nunavut, less than in most provinces. Please base your plans on Nunavut rates, not on a refund figure from a friend in another province.

Mixing up $181,439 and $181,440. Nunavut's 11.5% rate starts one dollar below the federal 29% band. It changes anyone's tax by 2.5 cents at most, but a spreadsheet that uses the federal threshold for both will misplace that single dollar.

Using the federal basic amount for Nunavut. Nunavut's $19,659 is $3,207 larger than the federal $16,452 and does not shrink. Using the federal figure overstates Nunavut tax by $128.28 ($3,207 x 4%).

Confusing marginal and average rates. At $100,000 the marginal rate is 27.50% and the average rate 18.93%. A raise is taxed only at the marginal rate on the extra pay.

Choosing the wrong province. Where you lived on December 31 decides the year. Rotational and fly-in workers who live elsewhere pay tax where they live, not where they work. Our guide to filing taxes online and the ranking of the best tax software can help you set it up correctly.

Illustrative Example: Three Nunavut Residents Check Their 2026 Tax

This example is hypothetical. Three people living in Nunavut use the calculators on this page for 2026. None has more than one income source, and none claims credits beyond those built into the calculators.

  1. Jo, salary $66,000. CPP is $3,718.75 (($66,000 - $3,500) x 5.95%), EI $1,075.80 ($66,000 x 1.63%) and income tax $8,449.80 ($6,500.72 federal plus $1,949.08 Nunavut). Take-home pay is $52,755.65 ($66,000 - $13,244.35), or $2,029.06 every two weeks. With $5,000 of payroll RRSP contributions, income tax falls by $1,375.00: $1,025.00 federal ($5,000 x 20.5%) and $350.00 Nunavut ($5,000 x 7%).
  2. Kai, taxable income $180,000. Kai owes $45,762.82, of which $34,255.25 is federal and $11,507.57 territorial; that is 25.42% on average and 35% at the margin. A $20,000 RRSP deduction saves exactly $7,000.00, because all of it stays between $117,045 and $181,439: $5,200.00 federal ($20,000 x 26%) and $1,800.00 Nunavut ($20,000 x 9%).
  3. Lee, salary $32,000. CPP is $1,695.75, EI $521.60 and income tax $2,061.10 ($1,656.15 federal plus $404.95 Nunavut), leaving take-home pay of $27,721.55. A $2,000 payroll RRSP saves $360.00, or 18%: $280.00 federal ($2,000 x 14%) and $80.00 Nunavut ($2,000 x 4%).

What the three show: Jo and Kai each save a flat rate because their deductions stay in one band, and Lee's 18% saving reflects the lowest combined first-band rate in Canada, which makes the TFSA worth a close look.

Nunavut Tax Brackets 2026 FAQ

What are the Nunavut tax brackets for 2026?

For 2026 the territorial rates are 4% on the first $55,801, then 7% up to $111,602, 9% up to $181,439 and 11.5% beyond that. Federal rates of 14% to 33% apply on top, and the combined top rate of 44.5%, on income over $258,482, is the lowest in Canada.

What is the Nunavut basic personal amount for 2026?

It is $19,659 for 2026, giving a non-refundable credit of $786.36 at 4%. It does not shrink at higher incomes. Because the federal amount is $16,452, income between $16,452 and $19,659 pays federal tax only. Only Alberta and Saskatchewan have larger basic amounts.

Does Nunavut have the lowest income tax in Canada?

On our calculator's basis, yes, at most incomes. Nunavut has the lowest total 2026 tax of the 13 provinces and territories at $50,000, $75,000, $100,000, $150,000 and $250,000, the lowest first rate at 4% and the lowest top combined rate at 44.5%. At $30,000, British Columbia is $6.03 lower. Personal credits can change the order for some people.

What is the top marginal tax rate in Nunavut?

The top combined rate is 44.5% on ordinary income over $258,482: 33% federal plus 11.5% Nunavut. From $181,440 to $258,482 it is 40.79%. The top rate on capital gains is 22.25%. At the top, eligible dividends are taxed at 33.08% and other than eligible dividends at 37.79%.

How much tax do I pay on $75,000 in Nunavut?

Taxable income of $75,000 brings $9,267.73 of 2026 federal tax and $2,789.61 of Nunavut tax, $12,057.34 together. Your average rate is 16.08%, and extra income is taxed at 27.50%. An employee on a $75,000 salary pays slightly less, thanks to CPP, EI and employment credits.

Does Nunavut have a surtax or health premium?

No. In CRA's 2026 payroll formulas, Ontario is the only place with a surtax or a health premium, and only Quebec residents get the 16.5% abatement. Nothing is added to Nunavut's 11.5% top rate, so the combined top rate stays at 44.5%.

What is take-home pay on a $60,000 salary in Nunavut?

A $60,000 salary in 2026 nets $48,772.89 once CPP ($3,361.75), EI ($978.00), federal tax ($5,338.30) and Nunavut tax ($1,549.07) come off. That is $1,875.88 per biweekly pay or $4,064.41 per monthly pay, the highest take-home pay for that salary in Canada.

How much does an RRSP contribution save in Nunavut?

Your tax falls by $900.00 when you deduct $5,000 from $50,000 of taxable income, by $1,121.98 at $60,000, by $1,750.00 at $125,000 and by $2,039.75 at $200,000. The saving per dollar is your combined marginal rate, from 18% to 44.5%, which is lower than in other provinces at most incomes.

How are capital gains taxed in Nunavut?

Half of a gain realized in 2026 is taxable, so the effective rate on a gain runs from 7% just above $16,452 of taxable income to 22.25% at the top. Someone with $90,000 of other income pays $5,500.00 of tax on a $40,000 gain, which is 13.75%.

Can I enter dividends for Nunavut?

You can. Nunavut's dividend tax credit is 5.51% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 2.61% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet NU428 (line 61520). The calculator combines them with the federal 38% and 15% gross-ups and the federal credits. CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; Nunavut's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax.

Is the Canada Workers Benefit different in Nunavut?

It can be. Nunavut is one of three jurisdictions, with Alberta and Quebec, where Canada Workers Benefit amounts can differ from the standard federal figures under reconfiguration agreements. The 2026 Nunavut amounts are not in the sources we use, so this page does not show them.
