Canadian Income Tax Guide 2026: Brackets, Rates and Calculators

Canadian income tax is charged twice on the same income: once by the federal government, at 2026 rates from 14% to 33%, and once by your province or territory at its own rates. Each level taxes income in brackets and gives a basic personal amount, $16,452 federally for 2026, so the first part of your income is tax-free. On $60,000 of taxable income, total income tax ranges from $7,932.34 in Nunavut to $12,204.61 in Nova Scotia. At a glance

Two layers of tax. Canadian income tax is federal tax plus provincial or territorial tax. Outside Quebec, CRA collects both through one return; Quebec residents file a separate Quebec return.

2026 federal brackets. 14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income, 20.5% up to $117,045, 26% up to $181,440, 29% up to $258,482 and 33% above that.

Basic personal amounts. $16,452 federally, reduced to $14,829 at high incomes, and from $11,932 in Nova Scotia to $22,769 in Alberta provincially.

Top combined rates. From 44.50% in Nunavut to 54.80% in Newfoundland and Labrador. Capital gains are taxed at half the ordinary rate.

Payroll deductions. In 2026, employees pay CPP of 5.95% on earnings from $3,500 to $74,600, CPP2 of 4% up to $85,000 and EI of 1.63% up to $68,900, on top of income tax.

1. How Canadian Income Tax Works

Federal plus provincial. Every resident of Canada pays federal income tax and income tax to the province or territory where they lived on December 31 of the year. The two are calculated separately, each with its own brackets and credits, and then added together. Outside Quebec, you report both on one return; Quebec residents file a federal return and a Quebec return.

Brackets. Each rate applies only to the income inside its bracket. Moving into a higher bracket never raises the tax on income you already had. That is why a raise always leaves you with more money after tax, even when it crosses a threshold.

Taxable income, deductions and credits. Tax starts from taxable income: total income minus deductions such as RRSP and FHSA contributions and the enhanced part of CPP. Non-refundable credits, such as the basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount and CPP and EI credits, then reduce the tax at the lowest rate, 14% federally. A deduction saves tax at your top rate; a credit saves it at the lowest rate.

Extras in some provinces. Ontario adds a surtax and the Ontario Health Premium and gives a low-income tax reduction. British Columbia has a low-income tax reduction of up to $690. Quebec residents get a 16.5% abatement of federal tax. Our calculators include all of them.

A worked figure. This example is hypothetical. On $60,000 of taxable income in Ontario, federal tax is $6,192.73 and Ontario tax, including a $600 Health Premium, is $3,224.52, for a total of $9,417.25. That is an average rate of 15.70%, while the next dollar is taxed at 29.65%. Our income tax calculator Canada 2026 gives the same result for any income.

4. Marginal and Average Tax Rates: How Much Tax Will I Pay in Canada?

5. Payroll Deductions and Take-Home Pay

What comes off a paycheque. Employers deduct income tax, CPP contributions and EI premiums from each pay. In Quebec, QPP replaces CPP and employees also pay QPIP premiums. For 2026, CPP is 5.95% on earnings from $3,500 to $74,600, to a maximum of $4,230.45, and CPP2 is 4% on earnings from $74,600 to $85,000, to a maximum of $416. EI is 1.63% on earnings up to $68,900, a maximum of $1,123.07.

6. Investment Income: Capital Gains, Dividends and Foreign Income

Capital gains. Half of a capital gain is added to taxable income for 2026, so a gain is taxed at half your ordinary marginal rate. A net capital loss can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely, but only against capital gains. The superficial loss rule denies a loss if you or an affiliated person, such as your spouse, buy the same property within 30 days before or after the sale and still hold it 30 days after.

Lifetime capital gains exemption. For 2026, the exemption is $1,275,000 on qualified small business corporation shares and qualified farm or fishing property, with a deduction limit of $637,500 because half of a gain is taxable.

Dividends. Eligible Canadian dividends are grossed up by 38% and earn a federal dividend tax credit of 15.0198% of the grossed-up amount; other than eligible dividends are grossed up by 15% with a 9.0301% credit. Provincial credits are added on top. Our dividend rates are complete for every province and territory.

US and foreign income. US dividends usually have 15% US tax withheld when a W-8BEN is on file, or 30% without one. In a non-registered account, the foreign tax credit is the lesser of the foreign tax paid and the Canadian tax on that income. Interest and foreign dividends are taxed at your full ordinary rate.

7. RRSPs, RRIFs and Retirement Income

RRSP deductions. An RRSP contribution is deducted from income, so it saves tax at your marginal rate. A $5,000 deduction at $75,000 of taxable income saves $1,632.50 in Ontario, including $150 of Health Premium, $1,410.00 in British Columbia and $1,525.00 in Alberta in 2026. This calculator works out the saving at any income in any province.

Withdrawals. RRSP withdrawals are taxed as income. Outside Quebec, the bank withholds 10% on amounts up to $5,000, 20% over $5,000 up to $15,000 and 30% over $15,000, but your final tax depends on your marginal rate. A RRIF has no tax withheld on the yearly minimum, and the lump-sum rates apply to anything above it.

Pension income. From 65, RRIF and other eligible pension income qualifies for the $2,000 pension income amount and can be split with a spouse, up to 50%. OAS is reduced by 15% of net income above $95,323 for the 2026 income year, and seniors also claim the $9,208 age amount, reduced above $46,432 of net income.

8. Filing Your Return and 2026 Deadlines

Deadlines. Most 2026 returns are due Friday, April 30, 2027, and any balance owing is payable the same day. Self-employed individuals have until June 15, 2027 to file their 2026 return, but any balance owing is due April 30, 2027. RRSP contributions count toward 2026 if they are made by Monday, March 1, 2027, the 60th day of 2027. When a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, a return is on time if it is received or postmarked by the next business day.

Instalments. Most individuals must pay 2026 instalments if their net tax owing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) for 2026 and in either 2025 or 2024. The 2026 instalment dates are March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15. Retirees whose pension or RRIF payments have little tax withheld can reach that level, so please check before the first date.

How to Work Out Your 2026 Canadian Income Tax

  1. Add up your income. Employment, self-employment, pension, interest, rent, half of capital gains and grossed-up dividends.
  2. Subtract deductions. RRSP and FHSA contributions, enhanced CPP, union dues, child care and other deductions give your taxable income.
  3. Apply the federal brackets. 14% up to $58,523, then 20.5%, 26%, 29% and 33%.
  4. Subtract federal credits. The basic personal amount, CPP, EI, the Canada employment amount and others, each at 14%.
  5. Repeat for your province. Use its brackets and basic personal amount, then add any surtax or premium and subtract any tax reduction.
  6. Add the two and compare with tax paid. The difference between your total and the tax withheld is your refund or balance owing.

Canadian Income Tax FAQ

How does Canadian income tax work?

Canadian income tax has two layers. The federal government taxes your taxable income at 2026 rates from 14% to 33%, and your province or territory taxes the same income at its own rates. Each layer applies its rates in brackets and gives a basic personal amount credit. Outside Quebec, both are paid through one federal return.

What are the federal tax brackets for 2026?

For 2026, federal tax is 14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income, 20.5% over $58,523 up to $117,045, 26% over $117,045 up to $181,440, 29% over $181,440 up to $258,482 and 33% above $258,482. The federal basic personal amount is $16,452, reduced to $14,829 at high incomes.

How much tax will I pay in Canada on $60,000?

It depends on your province. On $60,000 of taxable income in 2026, total income tax is $9,417.25 in Ontario, $9,015.01 in British Columbia, $9,171.21 in Alberta and $11,200.40 in Quebec, counting only the basic personal amount. An employee pays somewhat less income tax because CPP, EI and employment credits apply.

Which province has the lowest income tax?

At most incomes, Nunavut has the lowest income tax: 4% on its first bracket and 11.5% at the top, with a $19,659 basic personal amount. On $60,000 of taxable income in 2026, total tax there is $7,932.34. Among the provinces, British Columbia and Alberta are lowest at $60,000, at $9,015.01 and $9,171.21.

What is the difference between marginal and average tax rates?

The marginal rate is the tax on your next dollar. The average rate is total tax divided by income. In Ontario at $100,000, the marginal rate is 31.48% while total tax of $21,520.55 is an average of 21.52%. Use the marginal rate to value a raise or an RRSP deduction, and the average rate for budgeting.

What is the basic personal amount for 2026?

The federal basic personal amount for 2026 is $16,452 when net income is $181,440 or less, falling to $14,829 at $258,482 or more. Every province and territory has its own amount, from $11,932 in Nova Scotia to $22,769 in Alberta. Each amount is a credit, worth the lowest rate of that government.

Does an RRSP contribution reduce my income tax?

Yes. An RRSP deduction lowers taxable income, so it saves tax at your marginal rate. A $5,000 deduction at $75,000 of taxable income saves $1,632.50 in Ontario, $1,410.00 in British Columbia and $1,525.00 in Alberta in 2026. The RRSP refund calculator works out the saving for any income and province.

How are capital gains and dividends taxed in Canada?

Half of a capital gain is added to taxable income in 2026, so gains are taxed at half your ordinary rate. Eligible Canadian dividends are grossed up by 38% and earn a federal credit of 15.0198% of the grossed-up amount, plus a provincial credit, which keeps their rate low. Foreign dividends are taxed as ordinary income.

When is the deadline to file my 2026 tax return?

Most people must file their 2026 return by Friday, April 30, 2027. Self-employed individuals have until June 15, 2027 to file their 2026 return, but any balance owing is due April 30, 2027. The RRSP contribution deadline for the 2026 tax year is March 1, 2027.

Are CPP and EI part of income tax?

No. CPP and EI are payroll contributions, deducted from pay alongside income tax but separate from it. For 2026, employees pay CPP of 5.95% on earnings from $3,500 to $74,600, CPP2 of 4% up to $85,000 and EI of 1.63% up to $68,900. Base CPP and EI earn tax credits, and enhanced CPP is deductible.
