Manitoba Income Tax Calculator and 2026 Tax Brackets

Manitoba tax brackets 2026: the province taxes the first $47,000 of taxable income at 10.8%, income over $47,000 up to $100,000 at 12.75%, and everything above $100,000 at 17.4%. These thresholds are fixed in The Income Tax Act (Manitoba) for 2024 and every later year and are not indexed, so they are the same as in 2025. The Manitoba basic personal amount stays at $15,780. With federal tax added, a resident with $75,000 of taxable income pays $16,209.49 in total: $9,267.73 federal and $6,941.76 to Manitoba. 1. Manitoba Tax Brackets 2026 and Rates

Three rates, written into the statute. The Income Tax Act (Manitoba), section 4.1(2), sets basic Manitoba tax "for the 2024 taxation year and each subsequent taxation year" at 10.8% of taxable income, plus 1.95% of income over $47,000, plus 4.65% of income over $100,000. Added together, that gives the three rates most people quote: 10.8%, 12.75% (10.8% + 1.95%) and 17.4% (12.75% + 4.65%).

Reading the table. Each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its band. At $80,000 of taxable income, Manitoba tax before credits is $5,076.00 on the first $47,000 plus $4,207.50 on the next $33,000 ($33,000 x 12.75%), or $9,283.50. The basic personal amount credit of $1,704.24 then comes off, leaving $7,579.26 of Manitoba tax.

Frozen, not indexed. Section 4.1(3) of the Act indexed the bracket amounts only for the 2017 to 2023 taxation years. For 2024 onward, section 4.1(2) names fixed dollar amounts with no indexing wording, and Manitoba Finance states that indexation of the bracket thresholds is paused effective for the 2025 tax year. So while the federal brackets rose 2.0% for 2026, Manitoba's stayed put. A raise that only keeps pace with prices can therefore push a little more of your income into the 12.75% or 17.4% band each year, which is often called bracket creep.

Why some tables show $47,564 and $101,200. The Canada Revenue Agency's 2026 "current year" rates page, last modified June 25, 2026, lists Manitoba thresholds of $47,564 and $101,200. Those figures do not match The Income Tax Act (Manitoba), which holds the thresholds at $47,000 and $100,000 with no indexation for 2026. CRA's own 2026 payroll formulas and Manitoba payroll tables use $47,000 and $100,000. WealthScale uses the Act's figures in every table and calculator on this page, and will update the page if Manitoba or the CRA changes either figure. Please keep the difference in mind if another calculator gives you a slightly different Manitoba amount.

What the gap means for you. The two sets of thresholds change 2026 Manitoba tax by at most $66.80, the full gap at taxable incomes of $101,200 or more. Below $47,000 they give the same result. Manitoba has also passed amendments to its Income Tax Act that come into force on January 1, 2027; their effect on 2027 brackets has not been published in a form we could confirm, so nothing on this page assumes a 2027 change.

Which income the brackets apply to. The rates apply to taxable income: wages, self-employment and rental profit, interest, pensions and the taxable half of capital gains, minus deductions such as RRSP and FHSA contributions, the enhanced CPP deduction, union dues and child care expenses. Your province is the one you lived in on December 31, 2026, and it taxes your whole year's income.

The other half of your bill. Manitobans pay federal tax on the same taxable income. At $75,000, the federal share is $9,267.73 of the $16,209.49 total, or 57.17%. Federal brackets are identical in every province, and only Quebec residents receive a federal abatement.

Two systems moving apart. Federal thresholds were indexed by 2.0% for 2026; Manitoba's were not. The gap shows up at the bottom of the scale: the federal 14% band runs to $58,523, while Manitoba's 10.8% band ends at $47,000. Between those two points a Manitoban already pays the middle Manitoba rate but still the lowest federal rate, which creates the 26.75% band you will see in section 3.

The 14% lowest rate. For 2026 the lowest federal rate is 14% for the full year. In 2025 it was cut from 15% to 14% on July 1, so the 2025 rate was a blended 14.5%. At the same income, a Manitoban's federal tax for 2026 is therefore lower than for 2025 even before indexation.

Federal basic personal amount. The 2026 federal amount is $16,452 when net income is $181,440 or less, worth $2,303.28 ($16,452 x 14%). It falls in a straight line to $14,829 at $258,482. That phase-out adds about 0.29 percentage points to the federal rate in the 29% band. Manitoba tax starts first. Manitoba's basic personal amount ($15,780) is lower than the federal one ($16,452). A Manitoban with $16,000 of taxable income and no other credits owes $23.76 of Manitoba tax ($220 x 10.8%) but no federal tax. Seven of the ten provinces share this ordering; only Alberta, Saskatchewan and Quebec set a 2026 basic personal amount above the federal one.

A rate that falls at $400,000. Between $200,000 and $400,000 of net income, Manitoba takes back its basic personal amount in a straight line. Each extra dollar costs 0.85 cents of lost credit on top of the 17.4% rate ($15,780 x 10.8% / $200,000 = 0.852%). Once the amount is fully gone at $400,000, that extra charge stops and the rate drops from 51.25% to 50.4%. The federal amount has its own, separate phase-out from $181,440 to $258,482, so between $200,000 and $258,482 a Manitoban is losing part of both credits at once.

The middle bands. Most Manitoba employees fall between $58,523 and $100,000, where every extra dollar costs 33.25 cents. Just above $100,000 the rate jumps to 37.9%, and above $117,045 it reaches 43.4%. For comparison, a Saskatchewan resident at $110,000 pays 33% on the next dollar and a Manitoban pays 37.9%, because Manitoba's top rate begins at a much lower income.

Capital gains. Only half of a 2026 capital gain is added to taxable income, so the capital gains rate is half the ordinary rate in each band, from 12.40% to a peak of 25.63%.

Manitoba's dividend tax credit. Manitoba's credit equals 8% of the taxable (grossed-up) amount of an eligible dividend, but only 0.7835% of the taxable amount of an other than eligible dividend. That small credit is why other than eligible dividends cost 46.67% at the top, close to the 50.4% rate on ordinary income. Section 9 explains the source. Every province's ordinary rates are side by side on our marginal tax rates by province page.

5. Manitoba Basic Personal Amount 2026 and Credits

$15,780, unchanged. The Manitoba basic personal amount for 2026 is $15,780. Section 4.6(3) of the Act sets that amount for years after 2023, and its indexing rule covered only 2017 to 2022. CRA's Manitoba payroll guide puts it plainly: the maximum amount for 2026 remains $15,780. The credit equals the amount times the lowest Manitoba rate: $15,780 x 10.8% = $1,704.24.

The phase-out above $200,000. Starting with the 2025 tax year, Manitoba reduces the basic personal amount for higher earners. It shrinks in a straight line as net income rises from $200,000 to $400,000 and is zero at $400,000. At $250,000, a quarter of the way in, the amount is $11,835 ($15,780 - $15,780 x $50,000 / $200,000) and the credit is $1,278.18. At $300,000 the credit is $852.12, half its full value. Every $1,000 of income in the range removes $8.52 of credit, which is why the marginal rate table shows an extra 0.85 points between $200,000 and $400,000.

Lower than its neighbours. Manitoba's $15,780 sits below Saskatchewan's $20,381 and Alberta's $22,769, but above Ontario's $12,989. Manitoba's amount is frozen, while Saskatchewan and Alberta both raised theirs by indexation for 2026 (Saskatchewan also adds an extra $500 a year from 2025 to 2028), so the gap with those two provinces widens in every year they index.

6. Surtaxes, Premiums and Abatements in Manitoba

No surtax. Manitoba levies no provincial surtax for 2026. In CRA's 2026 payroll formulas, the provincial surtax factor applies only to Ontario, where it adds 20% of basic Ontario tax over $5,818 and 36% of basic Ontario tax over $7,446. Manitoba tax is bracket tax minus credits, with nothing added.

No health premium. Manitoba does not collect a health premium through the income tax system. The additional-tax factor in the 2026 payroll formulas exists only for the Ontario Health Premium, which reaches $900 a year; see our Ontario Health Premium guide if you are comparing a move east.

No abatement and no low-income reduction. The 16.5% federal abatement belongs to Quebec residents alone. CRA's payroll formulas also include a provincial low-income tax reduction only for Ontario and British Columbia, so none is applied to Manitoba in our tables.

The phase-out acts like a surtax band. Manitoba has no surtax, but its basic personal amount clawback behaves like one for net incomes from $200,000 to $400,000: it adds 0.85 percentage points to the rate, then disappears. Planning around that band is covered in section 11.

Other provincial costs are separate. Sales tax, property tax and land transfer tax are not part of your income tax return. Manitoba's land transfer tax, for example, is 0% on the first $30,000 of a property's value and then rises in steps to 2% on the amount over $200,000, so a $350,000 home carries $4,650 of tax ($300 + $600 + $750 + $3,000). Our land transfer tax calculator works it out for any price.

9. Capital Gains and Dividends in Manitoba

Enter your other income, the sale price and your cost to see the 2026 tax on a capital gain in Manitoba.

Half the gain is taxable. The capital gains inclusion rate for 2026 is one half. The proposed increase to two thirds was cancelled, as announced on March 21, 2025. Half of your net gain is added to taxable income and taxed at your combined rate, so a Manitoban pays an effective 12.40% on a gain in the 24.8% band, 16.63% in the 33.25% band and 21.70% in the 43.4% band.

Worked example. This example is hypothetical. Joel, a Winnipeg resident with $95,000 of other taxable income, sells shares in a non-registered account for a $40,000 gain. The taxable half is $20,000 ($40,000 x 50%), which raises his income to $115,000 and crosses Manitoba's $100,000 line. Tax on the gain: $5,000 x 33.25% = $1,662.50, plus $15,000 x 37.9% = $5,685.00, for $7,347.50 in total, or 18.37% of the gain. He keeps $32,652.50. Had he spread the sale over two tax years, more of the gain would have been taxed in the 33.25% band.

Losses and exemptions. Net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely, but only against taxable capital gains; our page on capital losses shows how. A loss is denied if you or an affiliated person buy the same property within 30 days before or after the sale and still hold it 30 days after the sale, under the superficial loss rule. Gains on qualified small business corporation shares and qualified farm or fishing property may be sheltered by the 2026 lifetime capital gains exemption of $1,275,000.

Dividends. Canadian dividends are grossed up (38% for eligible dividends, 15% for other than eligible dividends) and then earn federal and provincial dividend tax credits. Manitoba's dividend tax credit is 8% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 0.7835% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet MB428 (line 61520). CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; Manitoba's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax. The dividend tax credit guide explains how the credit works, and the dividend tax calculator runs your figures.

Inside registered accounts. Gains and dividends earned inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA are not taxed when they are earned, so none of the rates in this section apply to them. The capital gains tax calculator handles a sale with losses carried forward in any province.

11. Ways to Lower Your Manitoba Tax

These are general, rule-based approaches. Which ones fit depends on your income, family and plans.

1. Deduct down to $100,000

Manitoba income just over $100,000 is taxed at 37.9% combined. An RRSP or FHSA deduction that brings taxable income back to $100,000 saves 37.9 cents a dollar; below that line it saves 33.25 cents. Your notice of assessment shows how much RRSP room you have.

2. Hold deductions for a 43.4% year

You can contribute to an RRSP now and deduct later. A deduction used at $50,000 saves 26.75%; the same deduction in a year above $117,045 saves 43.4%. Carrying it forward can make sense before an expected promotion or large sale.

3. Open an FHSA before buying

A first home savings account gives $8,000 of deductible room a year, up to $40,000. The deduction saves at the same Manitoba rates as an RRSP, and a qualifying withdrawal for a first home is tax-free. See the FHSA guide.

4. Shelter interest in a TFSA

Interest is taxed at your full marginal rate, 33.25% for most middle-income Manitobans. The 2026 TFSA limit is $7,000, and nothing earned inside a TFSA appears on your return. Our TFSA guide covers room and withdrawals.

5. Even out income with a spousal RRSP

If one partner earns $130,000 and the other $40,000, the higher earner can deduct a spousal RRSP contribution at 43.4% while the money is later withdrawn in the lower earner's hands, subject to the three-year attribution rule.

6. Split eligible pension income

Couples can split up to half of eligible pension income. Moving income from a spouse in the 33.25% band to one in the 24.8% band saves 8.45 cents on each dollar moved. Read our pension income splitting guide for who qualifies.

7. Manage the $200,000 to $400,000 band

Between those net incomes Manitoba claws back its basic personal amount, adding 0.85 points to your rate. Timing a bonus, a large RRSP deduction or a capital gain to stay below $200,000 avoids that loss of credit in that year.

8. Spread large gains across years

Because Manitoba's thresholds are low and fixed, a big taxable gain can jump two bands in one year. Selling in two tax years, or pairing a sale with losses, can keep more of the gain in the 33.25% band.

9. Watch benefit and clawback lines

Deductions lower net income, which also drives the Canada Child Benefit and the OAS recovery tax of 15% on net income above $95,323 for 2026. Our OAS clawback page shows how a deduction in that range is worth more than the tax rates alone.

Manitoba Tax Brackets 2026 FAQ

What are the Manitoba tax brackets for 2026?

Manitoba taxes 2026 income at 10.8% up to $47,000, 12.75% over $47,000 up to $100,000, and 17.4% over $100,000. The Income Tax Act (Manitoba) fixes these thresholds for 2024 and every later year, so they match 2025. Federal tax is added on top, from 14% on the first $58,523 to 33% over $258,482.

Why does a CRA table show $47,564 and $101,200 for Manitoba?

The Canada Revenue Agency's 2026 rates page lists those thresholds, but they do not match The Income Tax Act (Manitoba), which holds them at $47,000 and $100,000 with no indexation for 2026. CRA's own Manitoba payroll tables use $47,000 and $100,000. WealthScale uses the Act's figures and will update this page if Manitoba or the CRA changes either. The difference is at most $66.80 of 2026 Manitoba tax.

What is the Manitoba basic personal amount for 2026?

It is $15,780, unchanged since 2024, and worth $1,704.24 of Manitoba tax. Starting with the 2025 tax year it is reduced for net income between $200,000 and $400,000 and is zero at $400,000. At $300,000 of net income, for example, the credit is worth half, $852.12.

Does Manitoba index its tax brackets to inflation?

Not for 2026. The Act indexed the brackets only for 2017 to 2023, and Manitoba Finance says indexation of the thresholds is paused effective for the 2025 tax year. The basic personal amount is also frozen. Federal brackets rose 2.0% for 2026, so the Manitoba and federal thresholds are drifting further apart each year.

How much tax do I pay on $75,000 in Manitoba?

On $75,000 of taxable income, 2026 tax is $16,209.49: $9,267.73 federal and $6,941.76 Manitoba. That is an average rate of 21.61%, and the next dollar is taxed at 33.25%. An employee on a $75,000 salary pays a little less income tax, because CPP and EI credits, the enhanced CPP deduction and the Canada employment amount apply.

What is the top marginal tax rate in Manitoba?

The combined rate on income over $400,000 is 50.4%: 33% federal plus 17.4% Manitoba. The highest rate of all, 51.25%, applies from $258,482 to $400,000, where Manitoba is clawing back its basic personal amount. On capital gains the top rates are 25.20% over $400,000 and 25.63% in that clawback band.

What is the take-home pay on $60,000 in Manitoba?

An employee earning $60,000 in 2026 takes home $45,772.41, or $1,760.48 every two weeks. Deductions are CPP of $3,361.75, EI of $978.00, federal tax of $5,338.30 and Manitoba tax of $4,549.55. This assumes one employer, an age of 18 to 69 and no other income or credits.

How much does a $5,000 RRSP contribution save in Manitoba?

A $5,000 deduction saves $1,240.00 at $45,000 of taxable income, $1,433.51 at $60,000, $1,895.00 at $105,000 and $2,170.00 at $160,000. The saving equals your marginal rate on the income removed, so it is largest just above the $100,000 and $117,045 lines. The RRSP refund calculator on this page tests any amount.

How are capital gains taxed in Manitoba in 2026?

Half of a net capital gain is added to taxable income and taxed at your combined rate. That is an effective 12.40% on the gain in the 24.8% band, 16.63% between $58,523 and $100,000, 18.95% from $100,000 to $117,045 and 21.70% from $117,045 to $181,440. Gains inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA are not taxed this way.

What is Manitoba's dividend tax credit for 2026?

Manitoba's dividend tax credit is 8% of the grossed-up amount for eligible dividends and 0.7835% for other than eligible dividends, the rates on CRA's Worksheet MB428 (line 61520). CRA publishes the 2026 worksheet in January 2027; Manitoba's 2026 budget made no change to personal income tax. Eligible dividends are grossed up by 38% and other than eligible dividends by 15%, and both also earn the federal credit.

Does Manitoba have a surtax or health premium?

No. Manitoba has no provincial surtax and no health premium for 2026; in CRA's 2026 payroll formulas both apply only in Ontario. Manitoba residents also get no federal abatement, which is for Quebec residents only. The one add-on is the clawback of the basic personal amount for net income between $200,000 and $400,000.

Is Manitoba income tax higher than in Saskatchewan or Ontario?

Yes, at every income in our comparison. On $60,000 of taxable income, 2026 tax is $11,221.99 in Manitoba, $10,462.08 in Saskatchewan and $9,417.25 in Ontario. At $250,000 the figures are $91,369.57, $84,617.51 and $89,472.25. Manitoba's early 12.75% and 17.4% thresholds and its frozen basic personal amount explain most of the gap.

Which province taxes me if I moved to Manitoba during 2026?

The province you lived in on December 31, 2026 taxes your income for the whole year. If you moved to Winnipeg from Regina in September, Manitoba's brackets apply to all of your 2026 income, including what you earned in Saskatchewan. Tax withheld by your earlier employer still counts toward your final bill when you file your return.
