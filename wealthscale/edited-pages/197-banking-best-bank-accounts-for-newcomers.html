2. Newcomer Bank Account Canada: Fee Waivers and Three-Year Cost

What a waiver is worth. A newcomer bank account in Canada is usually a regular package with its fee waived for a set time. The longer the waiver and the lower the fee after it, the less you pay. In dollars, the waivers are worth $430.80 at BMO (24 x $17.95), $215.40 at TD (12 x $17.95), $203.40 at Scotiabank (12 x $16.95), $191.40 in National Bank's first year at The Connected's price (12 x $15.95) and $155.40 at RBC (12 x $12.95).

Meeting a balance rule instead. After the waiver, TD, BMO, CIBC and Scotiabank charge $0 if you keep $4,000 in the account (every day, or as a minimum daily balance), and National Bank's The Connected is $0 at $4,500. If you can keep that much in chequing from the start, the three-year cost at those banks can be $0 too. Most new arrivals cannot, which is why we rank on the strict cost.

National Bank's conditions. Years 2 and 3 are free with a $4,500 minimum daily balance, or with a National Bank credit card, e-statements and a payroll deposit (or 2 bill payments a month). If your pay goes into the account and you take the card, you may meet the second condition without keeping a large balance, and then the three-year cost is $0.

Downgrading is an option. When a waiver ends, you can usually move to a cheaper plan at the same bank. TD's Minimum Chequing Account costs $3.95 a month ($47.40 a year) and TD waives it for newcomers in their first year. RBC Day to Day Banking, BMO Practical and CIBC Everyday cost $4 a month, and Scotiabank's Basic Bank Account $3.95. Section 10 compares them.

The waiver can change. RBC says it may withdraw or amend its newcomer waiver at any time, and CIBC may change or cancel its offer without notice. Please ask for the waiver's end date in writing when you open.

Age of majority. RBC, TD and BMO open their newcomer packages only to people who are adults under provincial law. That age is 19 in British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia and the three territories, and 18 in the other six provinces. A newcomer who is 18 in British Columbia may need to wait a year, or choose a bank without that rule.

Already a client. RBC's package is only for people with no RBC personal banking account, and Scotiabank excludes anyone who held a Scotiabank chequing account in the last 2 years. Mid-window arrivals. If you arrived 4 years ago, a 24-month waiver may run past your fifth anniversary; the banks did not say whether the waiver stops at 5 years. Ask when you open, and write down the date the fee will start.

11. Deposit Protection for Newcomers

The basic rule. At a CDIC member, eligible deposits in one person's name are insured to $100,000, interest included. RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC and National Bank are members, and Simplii deposits count as CIBC deposits.

More than one category. That $100,000 applies again, separately, to joint deposits, to deposits held in trust and to the deposits in each registered plan: RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RESP, RDSP and FHSA. Chequing and savings balances, GICs and term deposits can count; money you invest in shares, funds or crypto gets no CDIC cover.

Enter your balances in the calculator below to total them by CDIC category at one member and see what is insured.

Arriving with savings. If you bring a large sum, the limit is per category per member, not per account. $150,000 in your own name at one bank has $100,000 covered; the same money split between two unrelated member banks, or between your own name and a TFSA, can be fully covered. Simplii and CIBC share one limit because Simplii is a CIBC trade name.

Desjardins, credit unions and fintechs. Our check did not confirm which plan insures Desjardins deposits; caisses and credit unions under provincial rules rely on their province's plan, not CDIC. KOHO is not a CDIC member either, and only money you opt in to earn interest sits in trust at members. CIPF vs CDIC explains how investment accounts are protected.

13. How to Choose the Best Bank for Newcomers in Canada

Start with what you need in your first 90 days. The best bank for newcomers in Canada depends on your status, whether you need a credit card quickly, and whether you can keep $4,000 in chequing once a waiver ends. Work through these steps.

  1. Check your status and dates. Note your landing or arrival date. Most banks use 5 years, Desjardins 3 years, and RBC's no-history card 12 months for permanent residents and international students.
  2. Decide if you need a card now. If yes, RBC, TD and Scotiabank publish limits of up to $15,000 without credit history, and Simplii up to $10,000.
  3. Price the waiver end. Use the three-year cost in section 1 if you will not keep $4,000 in chequing. BMO and CIBC cost least among big banks; TD costs most.
  4. Match your documents. Study permit holders can use TD, Scotiabank or National Bank; workers with a 12-month permit fit CIBC; Desjardins needs age 25 or older.
  5. Look at the offer last. If two packages fit equally, an open offer can decide it. Simplii's $350 runs to January 31, 2027; CIBC's has no end date stated.
  6. Plan your second account. A big-bank package for the card and branch can sit alongside a no-fee online account for savings.

One bank can be enough. If Simplii's online setup and card limit fit your needs, its $0 fee means there is no waiver to manage. If you want a branch and a larger card limit, a big-bank package plus a reminder to downgrade or switch works well.

14. Opening Your First Canadian Account, Step by Step

  1. Gather your documents. Your PR card or COPR, or your work or study permit, plus your passport or other photo ID. RBC wants originals, not photocopies.
  2. Get a Canadian phone number. National Bank and Desjardins require one, and every bank will use it to verify you.
  3. Choose online or in person. Simplii, BMO and National Bank (from 18) let you open online; TD needs a branch appointment.
  4. Open the chequing package and ask for the waiver end date. Ask for it in writing.
  5. Apply for the card without credit history. Bring proof of income if you have it; TD and RBC base limits on income, and Scotiabank asks foreign workers for an employer letter.
  6. Set up direct deposit. Give your employer the account details. Several offers, such as Simplii's and CIBC's, need recurring direct deposits.
  7. Add savings and a TFSA when you have a SIN. Keep spending money in chequing and the rest where it earns interest.

15. Common Newcomer Banking Mistakes

Opening the wrong account first. RBC's package excludes existing RBC personal banking clients, and Scotiabank excludes anyone with a Scotiabank chequing account in the last 2 years. A basic account opened in your first week can block the better package.

Forgetting the waiver end date. TD's fee starts in month 13 at $17.95. Two unnoticed years cost $430.80.

Counting the headline value. "Up to" totals add waivers, cash and card rewards together, and each part has its own conditions. Count only the cash you will actually receive.

Missing a direct-deposit condition. Simplii's $350 needs 4 straight months, and CIBC's $500 needs a $500 monthly deposit within 60 days. One missed month can cost the bonus.

Waiting too long for the card. RBC's no-history card is for permanent residents and international students within 12 months of arriving.

Keeping savings in chequing. Big-bank packages pay 0.000% or no interest. Once you have a SIN, a TFSA or savings account earns more.

Assuming every account is CDIC-insured. KOHO's coverage depends on opting in to interest, and credit unions use provincial plans. Check who protects your money.

10 Strategies for Your First Years of Banking in Canada

1. Open the newcomer package before anything else

Because RBC and Scotiabank exclude existing clients, open the package you want first. A quick basic account somewhere else is fine; at the same bank it can disqualify you.

2. Pick the waiver you will actually use

BMO's and CIBC's 24 months are worth more than a 12-month waiver only if you stay. If you plan to switch after a year anyway, the waiver length matters less than the card.

3. Get the no-history card early

A card from your first months starts your Canadian credit file. RBC's program has a 12-month window for permanent residents and international students.

4. Pay the card in full every month

Paying on time and in full builds your credit file without costing interest. Set an automatic payment from chequing.

5. Calendar the fee start date

Write the month the waiver ends and set a reminder a month before. That is when you decide to keep, downgrade or switch.

6. Downgrade instead of paying $17.95

A $4.00 plan at the same bank costs $48 a year against $215.40 for a $17.95 package, and you stay with a bank you already know.

7. Let a no-fee account hold your savings

Keep spending money in the package and savings where it earns interest. EQ Bank pays 2.75% with your pay direct deposited, against 0.000% at TD.

8. Meet direct-deposit conditions with your pay

Offers from Simplii and CIBC need recurring direct deposits. Point your first Canadian paycheque at the account with the offer and keep it there until the bonus is paid.

9. Start your TFSA once you have a SIN

TFSA room starts in the year you become resident, and unused room carries forward. Once your SIN arrives, savings you will not need soon can earn tax-free interest there.

10. Spread large savings across banks

CDIC covers up to $100,000 per category at each member. If you arrive with more, two unrelated banks, or a TFSA alongside your own-name account, keep each part covered.

Illustrative Example: A Newcomer Couple's First Three Years

This example is hypothetical. Arjun, 29, and Leela, 28, land in Ontario as permanent residents in 2026. Both start jobs paid by direct deposit. They usually keep about $2,500 in chequing, below every $4,000 waiver, and each wants a credit card without Canadian credit history. Arjun compares TD and BMO; Leela looks at Simplii.

  1. TD for three years. 12 months free, then $17.95 a month for 24 months: 24 x $17.95 = $430.80.
  2. BMO for three years. 24 months free, then $17.95 a month for 12 months: 12 x $17.95 = $215.40. BMO saves Arjun $215.40 against TD ($430.80 - $215.40).
  3. BMO, then a switch. If Arjun moves his pay to a no-fee account at month 24, year three costs $0, saving another $215.40 against staying.
  4. Leela at Simplii. $0 a month for all 36 months, and a Cash Back Visa with up to a $10,000 limit and no annual fee. If she opens before January 31, 2027 and her pay of $100 or more is direct deposited for 4 straight months, she can also qualify for the $350 offer (as checked October 23, 2026).
  5. Deposit protection. Their $2,500 balances sit well inside CDIC's $100,000 limit per category at each member.

On these numbers, BMO for Arjun and Simplii for Leela keep their combined three-year bank fees at $215.40 ($215.40 + $0), against $861.60 if both stayed at TD (2 x $430.80).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best bank for newcomers in Canada in 2026?

On our fee-then-features order, Simplii's New to Canada Banking Program comes first because its No Fee Chequing Account never charges a monthly fee, and its Cash Back Visa offers up to $10,000 with no Canadian credit history. Which bank waives fees the longest for newcomers?

National Bank offers up to 3 years with no fixed monthly fees, but only year 1 is unconditional; years 2 and 3 need a $4,500 balance or a credit card, e-statements and payroll deposit. BMO and CIBC waive fees for 24 months with no conditions, and Desjardins waives its Unlimited plan for 2 years for newcomers 25 and older.

What documents do I need to open a bank account as a newcomer?

Usually proof of status and photo ID. That means a PR card, a Confirmation of Permanent Residence (IMM 5292 or IMM 5688), or a work or study permit, plus a passport or other government photo ID. RBC wants originals, National Bank and Desjardins need a Canadian phone number, and Simplii takes a passport and a selfie online.

Can I get a credit card without Canadian credit history?

Yes. RBC, TD and Scotiabank publish limits of up to $15,000 with no Canadian credit history, and Simplii's no-annual-fee Cash Back Visa goes up to $10,000. BMO and CIBC also offer cards without a credit history but did not publish limits. Every card is subject to credit approval, and limits depend on income.

Can international students open a newcomer bank account?

At some banks. Scotiabank's StartRight includes international students, TD accepts a study permit with proof of enrolment, and National Bank accepts a valid study permit. BMO and CIBC direct international students to separate student options. A student account at RBC, CIBC or Scotiabank is $0 until age 25, which often lasts longer than a newcomer waiver.

Can I open a newcomer bank account online?

Yes, at several banks. Simplii lets newcomers who arrived in the last 5 years apply online with a passport and a selfie, and processes applications within 2 business days. BMO opens online with a SIN and a Canadian address, and National Bank from age 18. TD requires an in-person branch appointment.

What happens when my newcomer fee waiver ends?

The package's regular fee starts unless you meet its balance rule: $17.95 at TD and BMO, $16.95 at Scotiabank, $12.95 at RBC and The Connected's $15.95 at National Bank. You can keep $4,000 or $4,500 in the account, downgrade to a plan costing about $4 a month, or switch to a no-fee account.

Which newcomer bank offers are still open?

As checked October 23, 2026, four run past November 4, 2026: a $350 offer at Simplii, ending January 31, 2027; up to $600 through CIBC's Newcomer Banking Bundle, with no end date stated; a cash bonus of up to $900 at BMO; and RBC's free small safety deposit box, to July 31, 2028. What does Scotiabank's StartRight program include?

StartRight is Scotiabank's newcomer program on the Preferred Package. Its credit card needs no Canadian credit history and has a limit of up to $15,000, subject to credit approval.

How long am I considered a newcomer by Canadian banks?

Usually 5 years from your arrival or landing date at RBC, TD, BMO, CIBC, Scotiabank, National Bank and Simplii. Desjardins uses 3 years. Some features have shorter windows: RBC's credit card without credit history is for permanent residents and international students within 12 months of arriving, and temporary resident workers within 48 months.

Is my money safe in a Canadian bank account?

Yes, at a CDIC member. Each member insures your eligible deposits up to $100,000 in every insured category, interest included. RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC and National Bank are members, and Simplii is covered through CIBC. Credit unions and caisses use provincial plans, and KOHO's coverage depends on opting in to interest.

Can I open a TFSA as a newcomer?

Yes, once you are an adult resident of Canada for tax purposes with a valid SIN. Your TFSA room starts in the year you become resident, and years before that give no room. Our TFSA guide for non-residents and newcomers explains how much room you get in your first year.

Should a newcomer choose a big bank or an online bank?

It depends on what you need first. A big-bank package gives branches, a larger card limit without credit history and a waiver of 12 to 24 months. Simplii never charges a monthly fee and opens online. You can also use both: a big-bank package for the card and an online account for savings.
