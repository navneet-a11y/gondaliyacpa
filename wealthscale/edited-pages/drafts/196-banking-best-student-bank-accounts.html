1. RBC Advantage Banking for students: best for using other banks' ATMs in Canada

$0 a month

Why it ranks here. RBC ties with every other account on fee ($0) and on unlimited transactions, and it is the only student account here that waives its own access fee at every non-RBC ATM in Canada. That places it first on the second feature test. It also stays at $0 for non-students under 25, so you do not need to prove enrolment until you turn 25.

Costs. $0 a month for full-time students and for non-students under 25. Once student status expires, RBC Advantage Banking costs $12.95 a month ($155.40 a year). Interac e-Transfers have no fee for the first 999 in a month, then cost $1 each. Transfers expire after 30 days, and RBC charges $5 to reclaim one that was not accepted and was not cancelled within 15 days. RBC lists Advantage Banking among its accounts that pay no interest.

Features. Unlimited debit transactions in Canada, plus unlimited RBC Virtual Visa Debit and Interac Flash purchases. Full-time students can open from age 13 (14 in Quebec). Under 18, you apply in the RBC Mobile app; from 18, online with a passport or driver's licence. Post-secondary students must carry at least 60% of a usual course load. RBC may ask for proof of enrolment, and your student status ends on the graduation date you give RBC, so please tell RBC if that date changes.

Offers, as checked October 23, 2026. RBC's ION+ Visa points offer ends November 4, 2026, so it is not listed. The card's $48 annual fee is rebated each year for eligible student account owners.

1. How We Ordered These Student Accounts

Fee first. The plan for this page sorts student accounts by fee, then by features. Every account here costs $0 a month for an eligible student (Desjardins calls its plan a free transaction plan), so the fee test is a seven-way tie. We do not break ties with a score we made up for this page. Our published chequing scores cover regular accounts, not student versions, so they do not apply here; our general rules for rating providers are on our methodology page.

Then features, one at a time. Three feature tests follow, always in this order, and the first test that splits two accounts settles their places:

  1. Unlimited transactions with no-fee Interac e-Transfers, as published. RBC's cap of 999 no-fee e-Transfers a month counts as a pass.
  2. The bank's own fee at other banks' ATMs in Canada. From best to worst: no bank fee at any other ATM; one withdrawal a month free or rebated; a fee on every other-bank withdrawal; a fee, or ATM terms, the bank did not publish.
  3. How long the $0 lasts. First, the age it lasts to without proof of enrolment: under 25 beats under 23, which beats "full-time students only". Then, whether the bank publishes that full-time students keep the $0 price past that age.

How each tie broke. All seven tie on fee. Desjardins drops to last on test 1 because its e-Transfer terms are not published. RBC wins test 2 outright. CIBC and BMO tie on test 2 at one withdrawal a month, and CIBC wins test 3 because Smart Start needs no student status. TD's $2.00 fee is published, so it ranks ahead of Scotiabank and National Bank, which did not publish their fees for this group. Those two tie at under 25 on the first part of test 3, and Scotiabank goes ahead on the second part because it publishes $0 pricing for full-time students 25 and older.

What the order leaves out. Offers appear on the cards but never move an account, because they are short-lived while the fee and features apply for years. Branch access, apps and credit cards are not part of the order either. If one of those matters to you, weigh it yourself. How WealthScale earns money is set out in our advertiser disclosure, and our rules for sourcing and dating figures are in our editorial standards.

The 60% course load rule. RBC counts a post-secondary student as full-time only with at least 60% of a usual course load. If you drop below that, you may stop qualifying even though you are still enrolled. Under 25 it does not matter at RBC, because non-students under 25 also pay $0.

Keep your bank informed. RBC asks students to report changes to their student status, and Scotiabank gives at least 60 days' notice before converting the account. Please keep your graduation date current with your bank, so the regular fee does not start a surprise month early or late.

9. Free Bank Account for Students: What Still Costs Money

$0 a month is not $0 for everything. Every account here is a free bank account for students in the sense that the monthly fee is $0, but each still has charges you can trigger. These are the ones the banks published.

  • Other banks' ATMs. TD charges $2.00 each; BMO charges $2 after the first each month; Scotiabank charges for shared ABM services at 16 and older without publishing the amount. The machine's owner can add its own fee at any bank, including RBC.

11. Deposit Protection for Student Accounts

Big-bank student accounts. Royal Bank of Canada, Toronto-Dominion Bank, Bank of Montreal, Bank of Nova Scotia, CIBC and National Bank of Canada all belong to the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation. Your student chequing balance counts toward a $100,000 limit, interest included, for eligible deposits in your own name at that bank.

Each category has its own limit. A joint account, a deposit held in trust, and the deposits inside each registered plan (TFSA, RRSP, RRIF, RESP, RDSP and FHSA) are insured apart from your own-name deposits, up to $100,000 each at the same member. Chequing, savings, GICs and term deposits can qualify; stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds and crypto never do.

Trade names share a limit. Simplii Financial is a trade name of CIBC, so a CIBC student account and a Simplii account in your own name share one $100,000 limit in that category. Tangerine Bank is a member in its own right, separate from Scotiabank.

Desjardins. Our check did not confirm which plan insures Desjardins deposits. A caisse or credit union regulated by its province is protected by that province's plan, not by CDIC, so ask Desjardins which plan applies to you.

Fintech accounts. Neither Wealthsimple nor KOHO is a CDIC member. Wealthsimple spreads chequing cash, held in trust, across up to 10 member institutions for combined coverage of up to $1,000,000; KOHO's trust coverage reaches only money you opt in to earn interest. Our CIPF vs CDIC guide compares deposit insurance with investment protection.

13. How to Choose the Best Student Bank Account in Canada

Use the same three tests we did, with your own habits. The best student bank account in Canada for you depends on where you take cash, how long you will be a student, and whether you want a bank's credit card. Work through these steps in order.

  1. Confirm you qualify. Check your age and course load: RBC needs 60% of a usual load for full-time status, TD's no-fee period ends at 23 without proof, and BMO is for full-time students only.
  2. Count your other-bank ATM visits. If you take cash at other banks' machines more than once a month, RBC's waiver saves the most; TD costs $2.00 each time.
  3. Work out your end date. Note when the $0 stops: your graduation date at RBC, December 1 of your graduation year at Scotiabank, 25 at CIBC and National Bank, 23 at TD.
  4. Price the regular package. Compare $12.95 at RBC with $16.95 or $17.95 at the others, in case you stay after school.
  5. Check offers last. If two accounts fit equally, an open offer can decide it. As checked October 23, 2026, CIBC's $125 offer has no end date stated.
  6. Add savings separately. Open a savings account or TFSA for money you will not spend soon.

Keep it simple. One student account plus one savings account covers most students. Adding a second chequing account only makes sense if it fills a clear gap, such as ATM access near campus.

14. How to Open and Switch a Student Account

Opening under 18. RBC lets students under 18 apply in the RBC Mobile app. TD lets 14-year-olds and older apply online, while under-14s open at a branch with a parent or guardian. Scotiabank lets 16-year-olds and older open online or in a branch; under 16 needs a branch visit with a parent or guardian.

Opening at 18 and older. RBC opens online with a passport or driver's licence. CIBC needs one government-issued photo ID for Canadian residents, and proof of student status for international students. Scotiabank asks students 25 and older for proof of full-time enrolment, such as a timetable or enrolment letter.

Moving from a youth or old student plan. If you hold Scotiabank's discontinued Student Banking Advantage Plan or Getting There Savings, Scotiabank says both were discontinued effective May 26, 2025 and changed to the Preferred Package for Students and Youth. At other banks, ask whether your youth account moves to the student version on its own.

Switching banks. Open the new account first. Give your employer or awards office the new account details for direct deposit, move any pre-authorized payments such as your phone bill, and keep the old account open until the last payment has cleared. Then close it, so its fee does not start when your student pricing ends. Moving a TFSA or RRSP uses a transfer form instead; see how to transfer an investment account.

Keep the paperwork. Save a copy of the offer terms and your proof of enrolment. If a reward does not arrive, or the bank starts charging a fee before your graduation date, those two documents settle it quickly.

15. Common Student Banking Mistakes

Forgetting the graduation date. At Scotiabank the $16.95 fee starts after December 1 of your graduation year. A year of fees you never noticed costs $203.40.

Falling below a full-time load. At RBC, dropping under 60% of a usual course load can end your student status. If you are under 25 it does not matter, but at 25 or older it does.

Paying ATM fees by habit. Four other-bank withdrawals a month cost $96 a year at TD and $72 at BMO. A different machine, or fewer larger withdrawals, avoids most of it.

Opening for an offer past its deadline. Student offers often close in the first weeks of a term. Check the end date on the bank's offer terms on the day you apply.

Missing an offer's follow-up steps. Opening the account is only step one. CIBC's $125 needs 5 eligible Visa Debit purchases within 60 days, and its bundle needs $100 or more in both chequing and eAdvantage Savings.

Carrying a credit card balance. A card with no annual fee can still cost 21.99% a year on what you owe.

Leaving savings in chequing. None of the big-bank student packages published a positive interest rate. Savings belong in a savings account or TFSA.

10 Strategies to Keep Student Banking at $0

1. Match the ATM rule to your campus

Find the machines you pass every day. If they belong to other banks, RBC's waiver of its own fee at non-RBC ATMs in Canada removes the bank charge; if they are TD machines, TD's account costs nothing to use them.

2. Take out cash less often

BMO and CIBC each cover one other-bank withdrawal a month. One larger withdrawal instead of four small ones keeps you inside that limit and saves $72 a year at BMO.

3. Choose an age-based account if your studies may pause

RBC, CIBC and Scotiabank are $0 under 25 even if you are not a student. A gap year or switch to part-time study then costs nothing.

4. Put your graduation date in your calendar

Set a reminder two months before your student pricing ends. That leaves time to meet a balance waiver, change plans or open a no-fee account.

5. Downgrade instead of paying full price

National Bank's The Minimalist costs $3.95 a month from 25 ($47.40 a year), against $191.40 for The Connected at full price.

6. Pair the student account with a no-fee online account

Keep the student package for the card and branch, and put savings in an online account that pays interest and never starts a monthly fee.

7. Cancel unaccepted e-Transfers yourself

At RBC, cancel a transfer nobody accepted within 15 days. If you wait, getting the money back costs $5.

8. Open for offers only before the deadline

Write the end date and every condition down on the day you apply. A day late means no reward, and a missed step later can cancel it.

9. Pay any card in full

A student card can build a credit history, but interest at 21.99% outweighs any reward. Set up an automatic full payment from your chequing account.

10. Start a TFSA when you are eligible

Once you are an adult resident with a SIN, move savings you will not spend soon into a TFSA. Interest earned inside it is not taxed, and our TFSA guide shows how much room you have.

Illustrative Example: Four Years of Student Banking

This example is hypothetical. Maya is 19 and starting a four-year degree in Ontario. She takes cash out at other banks' ATMs about 3 times a month, because the machines on her campus belong to other banks. She will graduate at 23 and expects to stay with the same bank for a year after school. She compares RBC, BMO and TD.

  1. Monthly fee while studying. All three charge $0 a month to a full-time student (48 months x $0 = $0).
  2. ATM fees at RBC. RBC waives its own fee at non-RBC ATMs in Canada, so her bank fee is $0 over 48 months (3 x $0 x 48). Any fee the machine's owner adds would apply at every bank.
  3. ATM fees at BMO while studying. The first withdrawal each month has no fee, then $2 each: (3 - 1) x $2 = $4 a month, or $192 over 48 months ($4 x 48).
  4. ATM fees at TD while studying. $2.00 each: 3 x $2 = $6 a month, or $288 over 48 months ($6 x 48).
  5. The year after graduation. At 23 she is still under 25, so RBC's account, and its ATM waiver, stay at $0 for non-students under 25. BMO's waiver is for full-time students, so the Performance Plan would cost $215.40 for the year (12 x $17.95) unless she keeps $4,000 every day, plus $48 in ATM fees under the same Performance Plan terms ($4 x 12): $263.40. TD did not publish its price after age 23 for a non-student.
  6. Five-year total. RBC: $0. BMO: $192 + $263.40 = $455.40. TD: $288 in ATM fees while studying, plus year-five costs that TD did not publish.
  7. Saving for a laptop. Maya also wants $2,400 for a laptop in 12 months, so she sets aside $200 a month ($2,400 / 12) in a separate savings account rather than leaving it in chequing.

On these habits, RBC's ATM waiver and its under-25 rule save Maya $455.40 against BMO over five years. A student who uses only her own bank's machines would see little difference between the three.
