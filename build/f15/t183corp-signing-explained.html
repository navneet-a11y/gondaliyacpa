What You Are Being Asked to Sign

Form T183CORP is the authorization that lets a CPA transmit your corporation’s T2 to the Canada Revenue Agency electronically. It is not the return. It is the piece that says the return is yours, that you have seen it, and that someone entitled to speak for the corporation says it may go.

Because it carries that return’s own figures, it cannot exist before the return does, and it cannot be signed in advance. That single fact decides the whole order of what follows. For the rules behind the form rather than the experience of signing it, the guide to T183CORP signing and transmission sets them out in full.

Where Signing Sits in the Sequence

Signing is the eighth of the ten steps on how nil T2 filing works. Everything before it exists to make the form accurate, and everything after it depends on the form being signed.

  1. You pay, and we prepare the return from the years you chose.
  2. A CPA reviews it, and Form T183CORP is generated from the reviewed figures.
  3. A single-use signing link reaches you by email.
  4. You open it and sign on an access-controlled screen.
  5. The signature is recorded with its date and time.
  6. Only then is the return transmitted.

Nothing is transmitted before the signature exists. If you never open the link, the return sits unfiled and nothing is sent to the Canada Revenue Agency in your name.

The Link, and the Screen It Opens

The link is single-use. It is addressed to you, it opens one signing screen for one set of returns, and it stops working once it has been used. There is no portal to register for, no password to invent and no account left behind afterwards.

The screen itself is access-controlled, which is the reason the form is never sent to you as a plain email attachment to print, sign and send back. An attachment can be forwarded, copied and signed by anyone holding it. A controlled screen reached by a single-use link can be tied to one signer and one moment.

What Your Signature Records

The signature is stored with the date it was made and the time to the second, in hours, minutes and seconds. That timestamp is not decoration. It is what shows the form was signed before the return was transmitted rather than afterwards, and it is the detail an electronic signature has to carry for the form to stand up later.

You do not have to do anything to produce it, and you cannot set it yourself. It is taken when you sign. The T183CORP signature requirement checker works through whether a given year needs a signature at all, and the answer on e-signing Form T183CORP covers the short version of this question.

Who Has to Be the One Signing

The signer has to be an authorized signing officer of the corporation: a director or an officer entitled to bind it. Not a bookkeeper, not a shareholder who holds no office, and not us. We prepare the return and we transmit it, and the authorization to do so has to come from the corporation itself.

Where several unfiled years are being filed together, each year is its own return and carries its own Form T183CORP, so there is a signature for each. They are presented together on the same screen rather than one link per year. The multi-year signing requirement checker shows how many a given catch-up involves.

Where the Signed Form Is Kept

The signed form is kept for six years. That retention is a requirement on the filing, not a preference of ours, and the T183CORP retention expiry calculator works out the date a particular year’s form may be disposed of.

It is not sent to the Canada Revenue Agency with the return. The Agency can ask for it, which is the reason it is kept at all, but it is not part of what is transmitted. The guide to mandatory electronic filing covers what transmission does involve.

Your return is prepared and transmitted in Canada. Our credentials are never shared and no part of your file is sent offshore. We are a registered EFILE electronic filer.

What Signing Does Not Hand Over

Form T183CORP authorizes one transmission of one return. It is not a representative authorization: it does not add us to your CRA account, it does not let us view your corporation’s tax information, and it does not continue into next year. A separate authorization would be needed for any of that, and this service does not ask for one.

So the form you sign is narrower than most people expect. It covers the years in front of you and nothing else.

If the Link Will Not Open

A single-use link that has already been used will not open a second time, and this is the commonest reason one appears broken. Reply to the email it came in and a fresh link is issued to the same address. Nothing is re-prepared and nothing is charged again, because the return itself is unchanged.

If the wrong person received the link, do not sign. The signature has to be the signing officer’s own. Tell us and the link is reissued to the right address.
