When an Ontario Corporation Must File Its Nil T2

The deadline is six months after the fiscal year-end, and it is the same for an Ontario corporation as for any other resident corporation, because Ontario corporate income tax is reported on the federal T2 rather than on a separate provincial return with its own date. Where a due date falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday recognised by the Canada Revenue Agency, a return received the next business day is on time. For Ontario corporations the business-day question matters in practice, because the statutory holidays that move a due date are the Ontario ones: Family Day in February and the August civic holiday both fall in months that are common fiscal year-ends.

Missing the Deadline

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.
