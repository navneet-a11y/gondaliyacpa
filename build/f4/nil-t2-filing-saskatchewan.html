One Return Settles Both Levels of Tax

Where a Saskatchewan corporation had no activity in a tax year, the return it still owes is the federal T2, and that single return is the whole of its corporate income tax obligation. It is due within six months of the fiscal year-end, in that year and in every year after it, until the corporation is formally dissolved. Having earned nothing changes what goes on the return and changes nothing about whether it is owed. We prepare it, a CPA reviews it, and we transmit it electronically for $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year.

Saskatchewan does not run a corporate income tax of its own. Provincial corporate income tax is collected through the federal T2, so the one return we prepare settles the Saskatchewan side and the federal side together. Only Quebec and Alberta are otherwise, and a Saskatchewan corporation has no equivalent of the Quebec CO-17 or the Alberta AT1 waiting behind the T2.

A count that turns down is dissolutions completing, and a dissolution cannot complete while a tax year is still unfiled.

The T2 due date comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province. Corporation counts are the Canada Revenue Agency’s own, rounded to the nearest ten, taken from the jurisdiction code on the T2 return, and they include tax-exempt and inactive corporations. They are refreshed when the Canada Revenue Agency reissues the table.

What Makes a Return Nil

A nil return reports no income and no expenses for the tax year. The corporation still exists, still has a Business Number, and still has a fiscal year-end, so the return is still due. Filing it keeps the corporation’s tax record current and keeps its standing with the Canada Revenue Agency intact.

A Quiet Year Is Not an Exemption

The commonest reason a Saskatchewan corporation falls behind is not a decision to stop filing. It is that nothing happened, so nothing prompted anyone, and a year went by without a return the Agency was still expecting. Nothing arrives to say a year has been missed until the Agency notices, and by then it is usually more than one.

An unfiled nil year does not close itself with time. It stays open, and it is still there when somebody wants to sell the corporation, borrow against it, or wind it up.

Who This Is For

This service is for a corporation with no activity in the tax year. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil.

Other cases that are not nil include a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, an amalgamation, or a corporation already dissolved or struck.
