Which Form a Saskatchewan Nil Year Goes On

The T2 Short is a two-page version of the T2 that a corporation may use where it meets every one of the Canada Revenue Agency’s conditions for it. Many nil Saskatchewan corporations do. Working out whether yours is one of them is our job rather than yours, and the answer does not change the price: the T2 Short is included in the $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year fee, not charged at a discount and not charged as an extra.

A corporation that does not qualify is filed on the full T2 at the same fee. Nothing about the quote depends on which form the year ends up on.

The Canada Revenue Agency assessed returns from 73,110 Saskatchewan corporations for the 2023 tax year, up from 70,550 in 2018. Among the provinces that grew at all over those six years, only Alberta grew more slowly. Saskatchewan also peaked before the series ended: it reached 73,770 in 2022 and gave back 660 of that in 2023. A count that turns down is dissolutions completing, and a dissolution cannot complete while a tax year is still unfiled.

The T2 due date comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province. Corporation counts are the Canada Revenue Agency’s own, rounded to the nearest ten, taken from the jurisdiction code on the T2 return, and they include tax-exempt and inactive corporations. They are refreshed when the Canada Revenue Agency reissues the table.

Eligibility Is Tested Per Year, Not Per Corporation

A corporation can qualify for the T2 Short in one tax year and not in the next. The conditions are tested against each year on its own, so a catch-up covering several years can transmit some of them on the short return and some on the full one.

That is a detail of preparation rather than a decision anybody has to make. We test each year as we reach it.

What the Short Return Does Not Shorten

Using the T2 Short does not reduce the schedules a year genuinely needs. GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 are required where they are required, Schedule 24 is required on a first return, and Schedule 50 is required for every shareholder at 10 per cent or more. All of them are included in the fee.

Nor does it shorten the list of returns owed, though in Saskatchewan that list is already short: provincial corporate income tax is collected through the federal T2, so whichever form the year goes on, that form is the only corporate income tax return the year needs.

Who This Is For
