What No Activity Means on a Return

No activity means the corporation had no income and no expenses in the tax year. Not low income, and not income that nets to nothing: none. A Quebec corporation in that position still files a T2 with the Canada Revenue Agency within six months of its fiscal year-end, and we file it for $300.00 plus applicable sales tax.

The Canada Revenue Agency assessed returns from 553,570 Quebec corporations for the 2023 tax year, up from 488,750 in 2018. That is the second largest corporate population in the country and the only large one still at its six-year high, having risen in every year the table covers. Quebec corporations also carry something no other province except Alberta does: a second corporate income tax return, filed with a second tax authority, on its own record.

The T2 due date comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province. Corporation counts are the Canada Revenue Agency’s own, rounded to the nearest ten, taken from the jurisdiction code on the T2 return, and they include tax-exempt and inactive corporations. They are refreshed when the Canada Revenue Agency reissues the table.

The Things People Think Are Nothing

Bank interest is income. Rent received is income. A dividend paid out or taken in is activity, and so is a sale of anything the corporation owned. A corporation that did one transaction in twelve months did not have a nil year.

Paying an annual registry fee or a bank charge is not income, but it is an expense, and a year with expenses and no income is a loss year rather than a nil one. The nil T2 eligibility checker walks through the distinction.

Why It Matters Which One It Is

A loss year is worth filing properly, because the loss can be carried forward against income in a later year, and that is a different return from a nil one.

If the year turns out not to be nil we tell you before you pay, and route you to our sister office rather than file the wrong return. The federal position is what we settle; the Quebec CO-17 is assessed separately by Revenu Quebec.

Who This Is For

This service is for a corporation with no activity in the tax year. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil.

Other cases that are not nil include a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, an amalgamation, or a corporation already dissolved or struck.
