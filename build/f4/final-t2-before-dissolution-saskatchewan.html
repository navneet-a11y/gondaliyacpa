Closing a Saskatchewan Corporation Needs Every Year Filed

A corporation being wound up files a final T2 for the period ending on dissolution, due within six months of that date. The final return is rarely the obstacle. What holds a dissolution up is the years behind it: a clearance certificate will not issue while any tax year is outstanding, and an outstanding nil year counts as much as any other. Each year is $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year.

This is the end of the process people reach and find blocked. The decision to close has been made, the corporation has done nothing for years, and the thing standing in the way is a stack of returns nobody filed because there was nothing to report.

The Canada Revenue Agency assessed returns from 73,110 Saskatchewan corporations for the 2023 tax year, up from 70,550 in 2018. Among the provinces that grew at all over those six years, only Alberta grew more slowly. Saskatchewan also peaked before the series ended: it reached 73,770 in 2022 and gave back 660 of that in 2023. A count that turns down is dissolutions completing, and a dissolution cannot complete while a tax year is still unfiled.

The T2 due date comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province. Corporation counts are the Canada Revenue Agency’s own, rounded to the nearest ten, taken from the jurisdiction code on the T2 return, and they include tax-exempt and inactive corporations. They are refreshed when the Canada Revenue Agency reissues the table.

What the Saskatchewan Numbers Show About This

Saskatchewan is one of the provinces whose corporate population has started to come down rather than up, and a falling count is dissolutions completing. Those are corporations that got to the end of this process. The ones that have not are the ones with a year still open.

We file every outstanding year and the final return together, so the sequence that has to be clean is clean before the clearance certificate is applied for.

The Order These Things Happen In

Outstanding years are filed first, oldest to newest. The final return for the short period ending on dissolution is filed last. Only then is the clearance certificate worth applying for, because the Agency will check the whole series.

There is no second series to close out alongside it. Saskatchewan corporate income tax is collected through the federal T2, so a corporation being wound up here has one set of returns to bring current, where a Quebec or Alberta corporation has two.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Who This Is For

This service is for a corporation with no activity in the tax year. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil.

Other cases that are not nil include a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, an amalgamation, or a corporation already dissolved or struck.
