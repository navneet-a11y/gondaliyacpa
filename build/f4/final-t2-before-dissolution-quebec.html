The Last Return a Quebec Corporation Files

A corporation being wound up files one last T2 covering the period ending on the date of dissolution, and that return is due within six months of the dissolution date. If the corporation had no activity in that final period, it is a nil return and we file it for $300.00 plus applicable sales tax. Every earlier year has to be filed too: a dissolution does not clear the years behind it.

The Canada Revenue Agency assessed returns from 553,570 Quebec corporations for the 2023 tax year, up from 488,750 in 2018. That is the second largest corporate population in the country and the only large one still at its six-year high, having risen in every year the table covers. Quebec corporations also carry something no other province except Alberta does: a second corporate income tax return, filed with a second tax authority, on its own record.

The T2 due date comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province. Corporation counts are the Canada Revenue Agency’s own, rounded to the nearest ten, taken from the jurisdiction code on the T2 return, and they include tax-exempt and inactive corporations. They are refreshed when the Canada Revenue Agency reissues the table.

Unfiled Years Block the Clearance Certificate

A clearance certificate is what confirms the corporation’s tax accounts are settled before its assets are distributed. It cannot be issued while a year is outstanding, so one unfiled nil year is enough to hold a dissolution open indefinitely.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Dissolving a Quebec Corporation Means Clearing Two Tax Authorities

The federal T2 record and the Quebec CO-17 record are settled separately. Filing the final federal T2 settles the federal side; the provincial side is assessed by Revenu Quebec on its own record and is not covered by the $300.00 fee.

We file the final federal T2 and any earlier federal years still outstanding, and we tell you how many we found, so the remaining provincial work is a known quantity rather than a surprise.

Who This Is For

This service is for a corporation with no activity in the tax year. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil.

Other cases that are not nil include a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, an amalgamation, or a corporation already dissolved or struck. Filings This Does Not Cover

  • The Quebec CO-17, the separate Quebec corporate income tax return filed with Revenu Quebec. It is not covered by the $300.00 fee.
  • Your Quebec annual filing with the provincial enterprise register. It is a registry filing rather than a tax return, it carries no tax, and it is not covered by the $300.00 fee.
  • A federal Corporations Canada annual return, at $12.00, if your corporation is federally incorporated.
  • A nil GST/HST return or a nil payroll return.
  • RC145 closure of a GST/HST or payroll account.
