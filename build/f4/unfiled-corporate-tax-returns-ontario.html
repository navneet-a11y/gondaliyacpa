Unfiled T2 Returns and What They Do to an Ontario Corporation

An unfiled year never closes itself. For an Ontario corporation the exposure runs on two tracks at once, because the Canada Revenue Agency and the Ontario Business Registry keep separate records and neither one forgives the other. On the tax side the Agency can issue a demand to file and then assess the corporation arbitrarily on figures of its own. On the registry side a corporation that stops filing its Ontario annual returns under the Corporations Information Act can be dissolved by order of the Director. Ontario corporate income tax is reported on the federal T2, so catching up the T2 years is what clears the tax side, and it is the step that has to happen before a clearance certificate is possible.

What an Open Year Costs

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Filings This Does Not Cover

  • Your Ontario annual return. Since October 2021 it is filed through the Ontario Business Registry, and it is not covered by the $300.00 fee.
  • A federal Corporations Canada annual return, at $12.00, if your corporation is federally incorporated.
  • A nil GST/HST return or a nil payroll return.
  • RC145 closure of a GST/HST or payroll account.
