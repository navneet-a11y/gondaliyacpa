Why Anything Sits Outside It at All

A nil T2 is one return, to one body, for one tax year. Most of what gets mistaken for part of it is not a corporate income tax return and never travels with one, so no fee for a T2 could include it. The exclusions below are grouped by the reason the T2 does not reach them rather than listed flat, because the reason is what tells you whether you owe the filing.

Registry Filings Are Not Tax Returns

Every corporation has a registry as well as a tax account, and the registry wants to know the corporation still exists and who its directors are. That is an annual return, and the word is the whole problem: it is not an income tax return and it reports no money.

If you are federally incorporated, Corporations Canada wants an annual return and charges $12.00 for it. If you are provincially incorporated, your province’s registry wants its own. In Ontario that has gone through the Ontario Business Registry since October 2021, which is why it no longer arrives attached to the T2 the way Schedule 546 once did. Neither is in the fee.

The Ontario annual return compared with the T2 sets the two side by side, and the registry filing checker says which of them you owe.

Two Provinces Collect Corporate Tax on Their Own Return

In most of Canada one T2 settles federal and provincial corporate income tax together, because the province levies it through the federal return. Two do not.

Quebec collects its corporate income tax on the CO-17, filed with Revenu Quebec, and Alberta collects its on the AT1. Each is a separate return in its own right rather than a schedule of the T2, so a Quebec or Alberta corporation with a dormant year has two returns to file and the fee covers one of them. The provincial return requirement checker answers it for any province.

Your Other CRA Accounts Are Filed Separately

A corporation can hold a GST/HST account and a payroll account alongside its corporate income tax account. They are separate accounts with separate filing obligations, and a dormant year does not close any of them.

A GST/HST return is still due for a period with no sales, and a payroll account still expects its filings even with no employees paid. Closing either one is its own form, RC145. None of that is in the fee, and the nil T2 compared with a nil GST/HST return explains why the two are not the same filing.

Bookkeeping, and Years With Something in Them

A nil year needs nil GIFI schedules, and preparing those from a dormant year is part of the fee. A year with transactions in it needs books before any return can be prepared, and that is bookkeeping rather than filing.

The same line decides the price. A tax year with no activity is filed here at the published fee. A year with investment income, rental income, or a dividend paid or received is not nil, moves to gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year, and is told to you before anything further is charged. If it is found after you have paid, you are refunded in full.

Incorporating, Dissolving and Clearance

Setting a corporation up and winding one down are both outside this service. Incorporation is its own filing to the registry. Dissolution needs a final T2 for the part year, the registry’s own dissolution filing, and in most cases a clearance certificate from the Canada Revenue Agency before assets are distributed.

The final return itself is a nil T2 where the last year had no activity, and that one is in the fee. The rest of the wind-up is not. The final T2 and dissolution guide sets out the sequence.
