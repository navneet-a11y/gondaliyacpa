How Many Years Can Be Filed

Every year since incorporation that has not been filed is still outstanding and can still be filed. An unfiled tax year does not expire, and there is no cut-off after which the Canada Revenue Agency stops wanting the return.

That cuts both ways. A year left unfiled stays open forever, which is the reason to file it; it also means nothing is lost by filing it late.

Every Year Is a Separate Return

Five missed fiscal year-ends mean five separate returns. Each year keeps its own fiscal year-end, its own figures and its own T183CORP signature.

There is no combined multi-year return and no reduced rate for filing several years at once. The work is the same per year whether one year or ten are outstanding.

The Order to File Them In

Oldest first. Each return carries balances forward into the next, so the earliest outstanding year is prepared and transmitted before the one that follows it.

Filing out of order produces returns whose opening balances do not match the closing balances of the year before, which is the kind of mismatch that draws a query rather than a clean assessment.

Finding Out Which Years Are Outstanding

The Canada Revenue Agency holds the record of which tax years it has received. Where the owner is unsure, the outstanding years are confirmed against the Agency record rather than reconstructed from memory.

A corporation that was registered and then forgotten often has more outstanding years than its owner expects, because the first tax year began on the incorporation date rather than the day trading started.

Confirming the Fiscal Year-End First

The year-end on the return must match the Canada Revenue Agency record or the transmission is rejected. The Agency already holds a year-end taken from the incorporation record.

A wrong year-end is the most common cause of a rejected transmission on a catch-up, because the owner remembers the date the business started rather than the date on the incorporation record.

A Demand to File

A demand to file is a formal written request from the Canada Revenue Agency for a return it has not received. It does not change the due date, which remains six months after the fiscal year-end, and it does not reduce what is owed on a true nil year, which is $0.00.

What it does signal is that the year has moved from unnoticed to actively pursued. See what happens if a CRA demand to file is ignored.

Arbitrary Assessments

An arbitrary assessment is an assessment the Canada Revenue Agency raises on its own figures when no return arrives, usually after a demand to file. It does not close the year the way a filed return does.

Filing the real return replaces the arbitrary figure with the corporation's own. An arbitrary assessment is not the end of the matter, and it is not a reason to leave the year unfiled.

What Unfiled Years Cost

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

The exposure on a true nil year is not a percentage charge. It is the open year itself: the demand, the arbitrary assessment, the blocked clearance certificate and the blocked dissolution. For a non-resident-owned corporation it is also a daily amount, which is why those years are the most urgent to close.

Catching Up Before Dissolution

Every earlier year must already be filed before a corporation can be wound up cleanly. An unfiled year is an open year, and a clearance certificate cannot be issued while one is open.

Owners who intend to close a corporation are the ones for whom the catch-up is urgent rather than optional. The sequence is set out in the guide to the final T2 and dissolution.

When the Back Years Are Not Nil

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Far Back Can I File Unfiled T2 Returns?

Every year since incorporation that has not been filed is still outstanding and can still be filed. An unfiled tax year does not expire.

Can Several Years Be Filed at the Same Time?

Yes. They are prepared and transmitted in one pass, oldest first, but each year is a separate return with its own fiscal year-end, its own figures and its own T183CORP signature.

Is There a Discount for Filing Several Years?

No. The fee is $300.00 plus HST per tax year, and the work is the same per year whether one year or ten are outstanding.

What Happens if the CRA Already Assessed the Year Arbitrarily?

Filing the real return replaces the arbitrary figure with the corporation’s own. An arbitrary assessment does not close the year the way a filed return does, and it is not a reason to leave the year unfiled.

Will Catching Up Trigger an Audit?

Filing an outstanding return is the ordinary way to close an open year, and a nil return reports nil amounts. What draws attention is the open year itself, along with the demand and the arbitrary assessment that follow it.

Which Year Is Filed First?

The earliest outstanding one. Each return carries balances forward into the next, so filing out of order produces opening balances that do not match the closing balances of the year before.
