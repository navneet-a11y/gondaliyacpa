Confirming the Fiscal Year-End

This is the first step and the one that most often goes wrong. The Canada Revenue Agency already holds a fiscal year-end for the corporation, taken from the incorporation record, and the return must carry that same date.

Owners usually remember the date the business started trading rather than the date on the incorporation record. A year-end that differs from the one the Agency holds rejects the transmission.

The Business Number and RC Account

The return is filed against the nine-digit business number and its RC corporation income tax account. Most corporations receive the number at incorporation.

A corporation that was never registered with the Canada Revenue Agency has to obtain one before anything can be transmitted. See whether a business number is needed to file a nil T2.

T2 Short or the Full T2

The T2 Short is a two-page version of the return, available to a Canadian-controlled private corporation that meets eight conditions. Most nil years qualify.

Either form is included in the fee. The choice is made from the facts of the year rather than by the corporation, and it changes nothing about the deadline.

The conditions are set out in the guide to T2 Short eligibility.

The GIFI Schedules

A corporation inactive all year with nothing at all to report may omit GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141. Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes them required.

This is where a return that looks nil is most often filed incorrectly. The detail is in the guide to GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141.

CPA Review Before Transmission

A CPA Ontario member reviews the completed return before it is transmitted: the year-end against the Agency record, the GIFI coding, the schedule selection and the nil status itself.

That review is what the fee buys. A corporation filing on its own with commercial tax software takes on the same checks. See whether a nil T2 can be filed without an accountant.

Signing Form T183CORP

An authorized signing officer signs Form T183CORP before the return is transmitted, never after. It carries a date and an HH:MM:SS timestamp that is never backdated, and may be e-signed through an access-controlled secure portal.

One form is signed per tax year, because each year is a separate return with its own transmission. The form is kept by the firm for six years and is not sent to the Agency unless requested.

The full detail is in the guide to Form T183CORP signing and transmission.

For tax years starting after 2023, e-filing is mandatory for virtually all corporations, and a paper T2 draws a $1,000.00 penalty plus a $100.00 per-return preparer penalty. Your CRA confirmation is emailed within 3 business days. One corrected transmission is included where the Agency rejects the first.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Long Does Filing a Nil T2 Take?

The return itself is short, and the usual wait is for the fiscal year-end to be confirmed and for the signing officer to sign Form T183CORP. The CRA confirmation is emailed within 3 business days of transmission.

What Do I Have to Send You?

The legal corporation name, the business number, the fiscal year-end, which years are outstanding, and any balances sitting on the books. For a true nil year there are no receipts and no statements to prepare.

Can I File Without Knowing My Fiscal Year-End?

No. The year-end must match the record the Canada Revenue Agency holds or the transmission is rejected, so it is confirmed before the return is prepared rather than guessed.

Do I Have to Sign Anything?

Yes. An authorized signing officer signs Form T183CORP before the return is transmitted, one for each tax year being filed. It can be e-signed through a secure portal with a single-use link.

What if the CRA Rejects the Return?

One corrected transmission is included in the fee. A rejection is usually a year-end that does not match the Agency record, and it is a filing that has not landed yet rather than a failed one.

Is a Paper T2 Still an Option?

Not in practice. For tax years starting after 2023, e-filing is mandatory for virtually all corporations, and a paper T2 draws a $1,000.00 penalty plus a $100.00 per-return preparer penalty.
