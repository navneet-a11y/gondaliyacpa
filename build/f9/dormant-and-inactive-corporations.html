What Dormant and Inactive Actually Mean

Dormant and inactive are descriptions of what a corporation did, not statuses the Canada Revenue Agency recognises. A corporation that holds no assets, earns nothing and trades not at all is described as dormant. Its filing obligation is identical to that of a corporation turning over millions.

The obligation comes from being an incorporated resident corporation. It does not depend on earning anything, holding a bank account, or having employees, and there is no minimum income below which the return can be skipped.

There Is No Dormancy Filing

There is no form that registers a corporation as dormant and no form that pauses the T2. Telling the Canada Revenue Agency that a corporation is inactive does not replace the return, and no letter or phone call changes the due date.

The only thing that ends the annual T2 obligation is formal dissolution. Until then the return is filed every year, with nil amounts where the corporation had no activity.

Share Capital, Shareholder Loans and GIFI

GIFI is the standard code set the Canada Revenue Agency uses for the balance sheet and income statement inside the T2. A corporation inactive all year with nothing at all to report may omit GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141.

Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes those schedules required, even though the corporation had no activity. This is the single most common reason a return that looks nil is not filed correctly.

GST/HST and Payroll Accounts Stay Open

An open GST/HST account or an open payroll account still needs nil filings of its own, or a closure using Form RC145. An inactive T2 does not close those accounts, and leaving them open while filing nothing against them creates a second trail of missed returns alongside the T2.

The Annual Return Is Separate

Filing the T2 does not satisfy a corporate annual return. The Ontario annual return has been filed through the Ontario Business Registry since October 2021, and a federal corporation files a $12.00 Corporations Canada annual return within 60 days of its anniversary date.

The full set is covered in the guide to provincial and federal annual returns.

Deadlines for a Dormant Corporation

A T2 is due within six months of the fiscal year-end, dormant or not. Where a due date falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday recognised by the Canada Revenue Agency, a return received the next business day is on time.

Every fiscal year-end and its due date is listed on T2 filing deadlines.

How Long a Corporation Can Stay Dormant

Indefinitely, so far as the Income Tax Act is concerned. There is no limit on how many consecutive years a corporation may report nil, and no point at which dormancy itself becomes a problem.

What creates the problem is a dormant year that was never filed. The corporation stays dormant and compliant for as long as the returns keep arriving.

Dormant Years That Were Never Filed

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Each year is a separate return with its own fiscal year-end and its own due date. There is no combined multi-year return and no reduced rate for filing several years together. The catch-up is covered in the guide to catching up on unfiled corporate tax returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Dormant Corporation Have to File a T2 Every Year?

Yes. The return is due within six months of each fiscal year-end, every year, until the corporation is formally dissolved. Dormancy changes nothing about the obligation or the due date.

Is There a Form That Tells the CRA My Corporation Is Dormant?

No. There is no dormancy form and no way to pause the T2. The annual return is the only thing that reports the year, and it reports nil amounts where there was no activity.

How Many Years Can a Corporation Stay Dormant?

There is no limit. A corporation may report nil for as many consecutive years as it exists. What causes trouble is a dormant year left unfiled, not the dormancy.

Is a Dormant Holding Company a Nil Filer?

Not where it earns investment income. A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil, and are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Does a Dormant Corporation Still Need GIFI Schedules?

Where it has nothing at all to report, GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 may be omitted. Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes them required, even with no activity in the year.

Should I Dissolve a Dormant Corporation Instead?

That depends on whether the name, the business number and the incorporation date are worth keeping. A dormant corporation costs the annual nil return and the annual return each year; a dissolved one costs nothing, and a later incorporation starts a new first tax year.
