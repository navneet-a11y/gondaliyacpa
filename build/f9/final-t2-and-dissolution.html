What the Final T2 Is

The final T2 is the last corporate return a corporation files. It is the same form as every other year, covering the period that ends on the dissolution date rather than on the usual fiscal year-end.

Filing it is what closes the corporate tax account. A corporation that is struck from a registry without one leaves an open year behind it.

The Period the Final Return Covers

From the start of the fiscal year in which dissolution happened, up to and including the dissolution date. Nothing after that date belongs on it, because the corporation no longer exists.

Where the corporation had no activity in that closing period, the final return is a nil return and is filed at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

The Closing Short Tax Year

The closing period is almost always shorter than twelve months, which makes it a short tax year. The fiscal year-end on the final return is the dissolution date, not the year-end the corporation used before.

Getting that date wrong is the usual cause of a rejected final transmission, because the Canada Revenue Agency matches it against the dissolution record.

Earlier Years Must Already Be Filed

A final return does not sweep up the years before it. Each earlier year is a separate return with its own fiscal year-end and its own due date, and each stays outstanding until it is filed.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Where several years are outstanding, the catch-up runs first, oldest to newest, and the final return is last. See catching up on unfiled corporate tax returns.

The Clearance Certificate

A clearance certificate is the confirmation that a corporation has no outstanding amounts before it is wound up. It cannot be issued while any tax year is open, because an unfiled year is by definition an unresolved one.

Directors who distribute the remaining property without one take on a personal exposure for whatever later turns out to be owed. That is the practical reason the returns come before the distribution.

The Registry and the Agency Are Separate

A corporate registry and the Canada Revenue Agency do not share a filing status. A registry may strike a corporation for a missed annual return while its tax years remain outstanding, and the Agency is not told that the corporation has gone.

Being struck from a registry is not dissolution for tax purposes and does not cancel a single outstanding return.

Filing After Dissolution

A dissolved corporation can still file, and still has to. The final return is due within six months of the dissolution date, and earlier unfiled years can be filed afterwards.

A dissolved corporation with an open year can also still be reassessed, so the returns are what finish the file rather than the dissolution date. See whether a T2 can still be filed after dissolution.

Closing the GST/HST and Payroll Accounts

An open GST/HST account or an open payroll account still needs nil filings of its own, or a closure using Form RC145. An inactive or final T2 does not close those accounts.

A corporation wound up with those accounts left open keeps generating obligations against a business number whose owner has stopped looking at the mail.

Frequently Asked Questions

When Is the Final T2 Due?

Within six months of the dissolution date. It covers the period from the start of that fiscal year up to and including the dissolution date.

Can I Dissolve With Unfiled Years Outstanding?

A registry may strike the corporation, but the unfiled years stay due and a clearance certificate cannot be issued until every return is filed. Dissolving first and filing later leaves a closed corporation with an open tax account.

Does Dissolution Cancel Earlier Unfiled Returns?

No. A final return does not sweep up the years before it, and a dissolved corporation with an open year can still be reassessed.

What Is a Clearance Certificate For?

It confirms the corporation has no outstanding amounts before it is wound up. Directors who distribute the remaining property without one take on a personal exposure for whatever later turns out to be owed.

Is Being Struck From the Registry the Same as Dissolution?

Not for tax purposes. A registry and the Canada Revenue Agency do not share a filing status, and a corporation struck for a missed annual return still owes every outstanding T2.

Is the Final Return Filed at the Same Price?

Where the closing period had no activity, the final return is a nil return and is filed at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.
