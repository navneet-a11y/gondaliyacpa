Nothing Assigns You a Year-End

A corporation picks its own fiscal year-end. There is no default, nothing is allocated at incorporation, and December 31 is a convention rather than a rule. The articles of incorporation do not set it either.

What sets it is the first T2 return filed. From then on the date is on the record, it recurs annually, and every other date in the corporation’s tax life is measured from it. That makes this the one decision that is free to make now and awkward to revisit.

The first T2 year-end options generator lists every date available from a given incorporation date, with the filing deadline each one produces.

The First Tax Year Cannot Exceed 53 Weeks

This is the one hard limit on the choice. A first tax year runs from the date of incorporation to the chosen year-end and may not exceed 53 weeks, which is 371 days counting the day of incorporation.

A year-end beyond that point is not available at all. Where someone wants one anyway, the consequence is not a longer first year: it is a first year ending earlier, then a full year, and two returns due within months of each other.

Counting it by eye from two dates in different years is where the mistakes come from, because 371 days does not line up with any month boundary. The 53-week first tax year checker tests one proposed date and the fiscal period length calculator measures a period you already have.

A Short First Year Is a Return for Almost Nothing

Choose the earliest month-end after incorporating and the first tax year might be three weeks long. It is still a tax year, it still gets a return, and that return is $300.00 plus HST per tax year like any other. There is no part-year rate.

So an early first year-end means paying for a return covering almost nothing, and then a second return for the first full year. A year-end closer to twelve months out produces one return instead of two over the same stretch of time.

Short years also arise for reasons nobody chooses: an approved change of year-end, an amalgamation, and a final period ending at dissolution. The short tax year deadline calculator covers the deadline on any of them, and the guide to first-year T2 filing covers the first return itself.

Changing It Needs Written Approval

A fiscal year-end is fixed by the first return filed, and changing it needs the written approval of the Canada Revenue Agency. The request is made before the change rather than demonstrated by filing.

That sequence is the part most often got wrong, and the next section is about what happens when it is.

An approved change closes a period on the new date. That period is a tax year in its own right with its own return and its own deadline, so the cost of a change is an extra return that would not otherwise exist. A Change Cannot Reach More Than 53 Weeks Out

The same 53-week limit that bounds a first tax year bounds the period a change creates. Where the gap between the current year-end and the proposed one is longer than 371 days, that gap is not a single tax year and cannot be reported as one.

Reaching a year-end that far out needs an intervening year-end first, which means two returns rather than one. It is the same arithmetic as the first-year limit, applied at a different moment.

Length of the period a change createsWhat it is
Under twelve monthsA short tax year, with its own return
Twelve monthsA full tax year
Up to 371 daysStill one tax year
More than 371 daysNot one tax year; an intervening year-end is needed

None of This Changes for a Nil Year

A corporation that has never traded still has a year-end, still has it fixed by its first return, and still needs written approval to change it. Being nil affects the figures on the return and nothing about the calendar.

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who decides my corporation’s fiscal year-end?

The corporation does. There is no default and nothing is assigned at incorporation. The first T2 return filed is what fixes the date on the record.

How long can my first tax year be?

Up to 53 weeks, which is 371 days counting the day of incorporation. A year-end beyond that is not available, so the first year has to end earlier.

Can I change my fiscal year-end later?

With the written approval of the Canada Revenue Agency. The request is made before the change, and filing a return carrying a different date does not change the date on record.

What happens if I file against the wrong year-end?

The transmission is rejected against the year-end on record. A refused transmission is not a filed return, so the year stays outstanding.

Does changing a year-end cost an extra return?

Usually. The change closes a period on the new date, that period is a tax year in its own right, and it gets its own return at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

Does a short tax year have an earlier deadline?

No. The six-month rule runs from the day the period ends, so a three-month period gets the same six months as a full year.
