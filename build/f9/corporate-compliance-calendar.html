What Belongs on a Corporate Compliance Calendar

A compliance calendar holds the dates a corporation owes something to somebody, whether or not it traded. Two kinds of date sit on it, and they behave differently.

Kind of dateDerived fromExample
Year-end drivenThe corporation's own fiscal year-endThe T2 deadline
Anniversary drivenThe date of incorporationThe annual return in some registries
Fixed calendarThe calendar yearThe T4 deadline at the end of February

No two corporations have the same calendar unless they share a year-end and an incorporation date, which is why a calendar has to be derived rather than copied.

The T2 Deadline

Six months after the fiscal year-end, every year, for every corporation. This is the one date on the calendar that never has an exception.

A balance of tax, where there is one, is due earlier: two months after the year-end, or three for a Canadian-controlled private corporation meeting the conditions. A corporation with no activity has no balance.

Every year-end and its due date is listed on the deadlines page, and the rules behind them are in the guide to T2 filing deadlines.

The Annual Return Deadline

The annual return is a registry filing. It confirms the directors, the registered office and that the corporation still exists, and it reports no income.

Its date is set by the registry rather than by the tax year, and it is commonly tied to the anniversary of incorporation. A federally incorporated corporation files one with its own fee.

This is the filing whose absence gets a corporation struck from a registry. A corporation can be perfectly current on tax and still be dissolved involuntarily for missing it.

See the guide to provincial and federal annual returns.

Provincial Returns and Their Own Dates

Two provinces collect corporate income tax on their own returns, filed separately from the T2 and on their own deadlines.

ProvinceReturnRelationship to the T2
AlbertaAT1A separate return with its own deadline
QuebecCO-17A separate return with its own deadline
ElsewhereNoneProvincial tax is calculated within the T2

GST/HST Filing Dates

A corporation with a GST/HST account files on the frequency assigned to it: monthly, quarterly or annually. The frequency determines the dates, and the account generates them whether or not there was any activity.

A nil GST/HST return is still a return. It does not disappear because there was nothing to report, and it is not covered by the fee for a nil T2.

The account is closed with Form RC145, which is the only thing that stops the dates recurring.

Payroll Remittance and the T4

A payroll account carries two kinds of date: the remittance dates through the year and the annual information return.

ObligationTimingWith no employees
Source deduction remittancesOn the assigned frequencyNil remittances expected
T4 information returnThe end of FebruaryStill expected while the account is open

Remittance amounts are the ones that can be assessed against directors personally, which makes an unused open payroll account worth closing rather than ignoring.

Changing a Fiscal Year-End

A year-end is fixed by the first return filed and changing it needs the written approval of the Canada Revenue Agency. The change is not made by filing a return with a different date on it.

An approved change produces a short tax year, and the whole calendar moves with it: the T2 date, the balance date and the instalment pattern all shift.

See fixing a return filed with the wrong year-end.

Registered Office and Director Changes

These are not annual dates. They are event-driven, and the clock starts when the change happens rather than at a year-end.

A registry has to be told within a set period of a change of registered office or of directors. A corporation whose registered address is stale is a corporation that does not receive the registry notice warning it is about to be struck.

A dormant corporation is the most likely to have a stale address, because nobody is checking the post at an office nobody uses.

The Minute Book and Annual Resolutions

The minute book holds the articles, the register of directors and shareholders, the share certificates and the resolutions. It is maintained whether or not the corporation traded.

Annual resolutions are passed each year even in a dormant corporation: the shareholders consider the year, appoint or reappoint directors, and record the decision. A nil year has a short minute, not no minute.

Shareholdings recorded there are what Schedule 50 reports. See the guide to Schedule 50 and shareholder reporting.

The Shortest Calendar a Dormant Corporation Can Keep

Once the unused accounts are closed, a dormant corporation's calendar reduces to two recurring items and one maintained record.

ItemFrequencyConsequence of missing it
The nil T2Annually, six months after year-endThe year stays open indefinitely
The annual returnAnnually, per the registryThe corporation can be struck
The minute bookMaintainedGaps in the corporate record

That is the floor. It cannot be reduced further while the corporation exists, and the only way to end it is to wind the corporation up.

Building Your Own Calendar From Your Year-End

Take the year-end on record, add six months for the T2, add the registry date for the annual return, and add the frequency dates for any account still open. Everything else is event-driven.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Dates Does a Dormant Corporation Actually Owe?

Once unused accounts are closed, two recurring filings: the nil T2 six months after each fiscal year-end, and the annual return on the registry's date. The minute book is maintained alongside them.

Is the Annual Return on the Same Date as the T2?

No. The T2 is six months after the fiscal year-end. The annual return is set by the registry and is commonly tied to the anniversary of incorporation.

Can I Change My Fiscal Year-End to Make the Calendar Simpler?

Only with the written approval of the Canada Revenue Agency. Filing a return with a different date on it does not change the year-end on record.

Do I Still Owe GST/HST Returns With No Activity?

Yes, while the account is open. A nil GST/HST return is still a return, and it is separate from the nil T2. The account is closed with Form RC145.

Do I Need Annual Resolutions in a Dormant Year?

Yes. The shareholders still consider the year and record the decision. A nil year has a short minute rather than no minute.

Why Does the Registered Office Address Matter?

Because that is where a registry sends the notice warning that a corporation is about to be struck for a missed annual return. A dormant corporation is the most likely to have a stale address on file.
