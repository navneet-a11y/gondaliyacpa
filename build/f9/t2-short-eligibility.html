What the T2 Short Is

The T2 Short is a two-page version of the corporate return. It is the same filing obligation discharged on a shorter form, available only to a corporation that meets every one of eight conditions.

It is not a different kind of return and not a lighter obligation. A T2 Short is a filed T2.

All eight must hold. Failing any one of them puts the corporation on the full T2 for that year.

The Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation Test

The corporation must be a Canadian-controlled private corporation throughout the tax year. A corporation controlled by non-residents is not one, which is why a non-resident-owned corporation files the full return.

Control is tested on the facts rather than on where the directors happen to live in a given month, and a change in control during the year affects the test.

Nil Net Income or a Loss

Net income for the year must be nil or negative. A corporation with no activity at all has nil net income and meets this condition.

A corporation with a loss also meets it, which is why the T2 Short is not limited to dormant corporations. A trading corporation that lost money in the year can qualify on this condition, though it will usually fail another one.

One Permanent Establishment

The corporation must have a single permanent establishment, in one province or territory. A corporation with establishments in two provinces allocates income between them, and that allocation does not fit on the short form.

A dormant corporation generally has one establishment, being its registered office. Where that office is the only Canadian address, the condition is met.

No Taxable Dividends Paid or Received

No taxable dividend may have been paid and none received during the year. A single dividend in either direction puts the corporation on the full T2.

It also takes the corporation outside a nil return altogether. A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Canadian Currency and No Refundable Credits

The return must be reported in Canadian dollars, and no refundable tax credit may be claimed for the year.

A corporation that functions in United States dollars files the full return. A corporation claiming a refundable credit does too, because the credit calculation has no place on the short form.

Ontario Transitional Debit and Section 34.2

Two residual conditions rule out particular histories. There must be no Ontario transitional tax debit, and no amount arising under section 34.2 of the Income Tax Act, which deals with a corporate partner and a partnership whose fiscal period differs from the corporation’s.

Neither applies to an ordinary dormant corporation. Both are worth checking on a corporation that once traded through a partnership.

Schedules Still Required

The short form shortens the return, not the schedules. GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 are required wherever there is anything to report, Schedule 24 is required on the first return after incorporation, and Schedule 50 is required for every shareholder holding 10% or more.

Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes the GIFI schedules required, even on a T2 Short and even with no activity in the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the T2 Short the Same as a Nil Return?

No. A nil return describes a year with no activity; the T2 Short describes a form. Most nil years qualify for the short form, but a corporation with a loss and real trading activity can also qualify, and a dormant corporation that fails one of the eight conditions cannot.

Who Decides Which Form Is Used?

The eight conditions decide it. Where the corporation meets all of them the T2 Short is filed, and where it fails any one the full T2 is filed. Both are included in the fee of $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

Can a Non-Resident-Owned Corporation File the T2 Short?

No. The first condition requires a Canadian-controlled private corporation, and a corporation controlled by non-residents is not one.

Does the T2 Short Skip the GIFI Schedules?

No. The short form shortens the return, not the schedules. GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 are required wherever there is anything at all to report.

What Is a Permanent Establishment for This Test?

A fixed place through which the corporation carries on business, which for a dormant corporation is generally its registered office. The condition requires exactly one, in one province or territory.

Does Filing the T2 Short Change the Deadline?

No. The return is due within six months of the fiscal year-end whichever form is used.
