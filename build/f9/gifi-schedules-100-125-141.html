What GIFI Is

GIFI stands for the General Index of Financial Information. It is the standard code set the Canada Revenue Agency uses for the balance sheet and the income statement inside the T2.

It exists so that every corporation reports the same item under the same code, whatever its own bookkeeping calls it. A corporation does not file its accountant’s statements; it files the figures mapped onto GIFI codes.

Schedules 100, 125 and 141

ScheduleWhat it reports
Schedule 100The balance sheet: assets, liabilities and equity
Schedule 125The income statement: revenue and expenses
Schedule 141A checklist about who prepared the figures and whether a review or audit was done

The three travel together. A return that files one generally files all three.

Schedule 100, the Balance Sheet

Schedule 100 reports what the corporation owns and owes at the fiscal year-end. On a dormant corporation this is often a single line of share capital and a matching line of cash or a shareholder loan.

A balance sheet does not become nil because the year was nil. Balances sit on the books until something removes them, and they are reported every year they exist.

Schedule 125, the Income Statement

Schedule 125 reports revenue and expenses for the year. On a true nil year this is where the nil actually appears: no revenue, no expenses, nil net income.

A single bank charge is an expense, and a corporation that paid one has something to report on Schedule 125 even though it traded not at all.

Schedule 141, the Notes Checklist

Schedule 141 is a short set of questions about the financial information itself: who prepared it, whether an accountant was involved, and whether the statements were audited, reviewed or neither.

For a nil return the answer is normally that no audit and no review was performed, which is accurate and expected. It is a disclosure rather than a judgement.

When a Nil Return May Omit Them

A corporation that was inactive all year and has nothing at all to report may omit GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141. Nothing at all means no assets, no liabilities, no equity balance, no revenue and no expenses.

That is a narrower case than most owners assume. A corporation with $100.00 of share capital does not meet it.

Share Capital and Shareholder Loans

These two are the usual reason a dormant corporation still files GIFI. Share capital was issued when the corporation was set up and has sat on the balance sheet ever since. A shareholder loan is whatever the owner put in or took out.

Neither is income and neither creates tax. They are balances, and a balance is reported because it exists, not because anything happened to it during the year.

The Most Common Nil-Return Error

Omitting the GIFI schedules on a corporation that has balances is the single most common reason a return that looks nil is not filed correctly.

The return goes in, the year looks closed, and the omission surfaces later as a query or a reassessment. Filing the schedules with nil amounts where they are required is what makes the year a filed nil year rather than an incomplete one.

No Audited Statements Are Needed

GIFI is not the same thing as financial statements. No audited or reviewed statements are needed for a nil T2, and none are prepared as part of this service.

What is filed is the GIFI schedules inside the return. See whether financial statements are needed for a nil T2 return.

How the Codes Work

Each line of the balance sheet and income statement carries a GIFI code: a four-digit number that tells the Canada Revenue Agency what the figure is. Cash has its own code, share capital has its own code, bank charges have their own.

Mapping a corporation’s own account names onto the right codes is the work in a nil GIFI filing, and it is what the CPA review checks before transmission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Nil Return Always Need GIFI Schedules?

No, but the exemption is narrow. A corporation inactive all year with nothing at all to report may omit Schedules 100, 125 and 141. Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes them required.

Is GIFI the Same as Financial Statements?

No. GIFI is a code set used inside the T2 for the balance sheet and income statement. No audited or reviewed financial statements are needed for a nil return.

My Corporation Only Has Share Capital. Do I File GIFI?

Yes. Share capital of any amount is a balance on the books, and a balance is reported in every year it exists. This is the case owners most often get wrong.

What Does Schedule 141 Actually Ask?

Who prepared the financial information, whether an accountant was involved, and whether the statements were audited, reviewed or neither. For a nil return the answer is normally neither, which is accurate and expected.

Does Filing GIFI With Zeros Create Tax?

No. GIFI reports figures; it does not calculate tax. A true nil year reports nil net income and the corporation owes $0.00.
