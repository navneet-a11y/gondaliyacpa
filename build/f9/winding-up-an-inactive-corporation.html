Winding Up Is Two Separate Processes

Closing a corporation involves two authorities that do not talk to each other on your behalf. The tax side is settled with the Canada Revenue Agency. The legal existence of the corporation is ended by the registry it was incorporated under.

SideWhoWhat it ends
TaxCanada Revenue AgencyThe filing and remittance obligations
LegalThe incorporating registryThe corporation's existence

Doing one and not the other is the usual failure. A corporation struck from a registry with open tax years still has those years open, and a corporation fully filed but never dissolved keeps acquiring new filing obligations every year.

The Order the Steps Have to Happen In

The sequence is not a preference. Each step depends on the one before it, and taking them out of order is what leaves a wind-up half-finished for years.

OrderStepDepends on
1File every outstanding tax yearNothing
2Distribute or dispose of the remaining assetsKnowing the tax position
3Request a clearance certificateStep 1 being complete
4File articles of dissolution with the registryStep 3
5File the final T2The date of dissolution existing
6Close the remaining accountsStep 5

Step 5 comes after step 4 because the final return runs to the date of dissolution, and that date does not exist until the registry sets it.

Every Return Due Before You Can Wind Up

A corporation cannot be wound up cleanly with an open year on its record. Every tax year from incorporation to the present has to be filed, including the years in which nothing happened.

Nine dormant years are nine returns. They are prepared oldest first and can be transmitted together, each at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

This is the step that stops most wind-ups. The directors discover the open years at the point of requesting a clearance certificate, which is the latest possible moment to find out.

The Clearance Certificate

A clearance certificate is the Agency's confirmation that the corporation has no outstanding amounts. It protects the directors personally: distributing the corporation's property without one can leave them liable for amounts the corporation turns out to owe.

It is requested in writing and it cannot be issued while a year is unfiled.

Clearance certificatePosition
All years filed, nothing owingCan be issued
A year unfiledCannot be issued
A balance outstandingNot until the balance is settled
Assets already distributed without oneDirectors exposed to the corporation's liabilities

A dormant corporation with all years filed at $0.00 is the simplest case there is for a clearance request.

The Final T2

The final return covers the period from the start of the last tax year to the date of dissolution. It is due six months after that date.

It is marked as the final return, which is what tells the Agency to expect no further years. Without that marking the account stays open and the corporation keeps appearing to owe returns.

The return itself is covered in full in the guide to the final T2 and dissolution, and the year of dissolution answers whether that year is still owed.

Closing the GST/HST and Payroll Accounts

A corporation often has program accounts beyond the corporate income tax one. Each is closed separately, and each keeps generating obligations until it is.

AccountClosed byUntil it is closed
GST/HSTForm RC145Nil returns remain due
PayrollForm RC145Nil remittances remain expected
Corporate income taxThe final T2, marked finalT2 returns remain due

An unclosed payroll or GST/HST account is the one that can reach the directors personally, because those are amounts the corporation was required to withhold or collect and remit.

Closing the accounts is not covered by the fee on this page. See closing a corporate tax account after dissolution.

Distributing Assets Before Dissolution

A corporation cannot be dissolved holding property. Whatever it still owns is distributed to the shareholders or disposed of first, and both have tax consequences the final return reports.

A disposition is activity. A corporation that sells or transfers property in its last period does not have a nil final year, and that return is handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

A genuinely empty corporation, holding nothing but perhaps a nominal share balance, has nothing to distribute and a nil final year.

Articles of Dissolution

The registry ends the corporation's existence on receiving articles of dissolution, and the date it records is the date the final tax period runs to.

The registry is the one the corporation was incorporated under: the federal registry for a federally incorporated corporation, and the provincial registry otherwise. Filing with the wrong one achieves nothing.

IncorporatedDissolved throughSeparate annual return
FederallyThe federal registryYes, with its own fee
In a provinceThat province's registryDepends on the province

The annual return is a registry filing and not a tax return. See the guide to provincial and federal annual returns.

What Can Still Arrive After Dissolution

Dissolution does not seal the record. An assessment, a reassessment or a demand relating to a period before dissolution can still be issued, and a return for a pre-dissolution year can still be filed.

Keeping the clearance certificate and the dissolution record is what makes those straightforward to deal with.

See filing a T2 after dissolution and returns left unfiled after dissolution.

Revival and Involuntary Dissolution

A corporation struck by a registry for failing to file its annual return has not been wound up. It has been dissolved involuntarily, with its tax years still open and nothing settled.

Registries provide for revival, which restores the corporation so the outstanding filings can be made and a proper wind-up completed. The open tax years survived the striking and are waiting.

A corporation found in this state is usually in it because the annual return lapsed, not because anyone decided to close it. The tax years are the part nobody looked at.

Keeping the Records Afterwards

Corporate records are kept after dissolution, not discarded with it. The books, the returns, the clearance certificate and the dissolution record all remain relevant for years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I Dissolve a Corporation With Unfiled T2 Returns?

Not cleanly. A clearance certificate cannot be issued while a year is unfiled, and distributing the corporation's property without one can leave the directors personally liable.

What Order Do the Steps Go In?

File every outstanding year, deal with the remaining assets, request a clearance certificate, file articles of dissolution, then file the final T2 and close the remaining accounts. The final return runs to the dissolution date, so it comes after the registry sets that date.

Is a Clearance Certificate Compulsory?

It is not a filing requirement, but distributing the corporation's property without one exposes the directors to amounts the corporation turns out to owe. For a dormant corporation with every year filed at $0.00 it is the simplest case there is.

Do I Have to Close the GST/HST and Payroll Accounts Separately?

Yes. Each is closed with Form RC145, and until it is closed nil returns and nil remittances remain due on it. Those closures are not covered by the fee for a nil T2.

What if the Registry Already Struck the Corporation?

It was dissolved involuntarily and its tax years are still open. Registries provide for revival, which restores the corporation so the outstanding filings can be made and a proper wind-up completed.

Does Dissolution Close the Tax Record?

No. An assessment, a reassessment or a demand relating to a pre-dissolution period can still be issued, and a return for such a year can still be filed.
