What Decides Which Schedules Are Needed

Not the amount of income, which is $0.00 on every nil return. What the corporation holds, who owns it, and whether this is its first return.

ScheduleDecided by
GIFI 100, 125 and 141What sits on the balance sheet
Schedule 50Who owns 10% or more
Schedule 24Whether this is the first return after incorporation
Schedule 8 and loss continuityWhether there is anything to carry forward

Three of the four have nothing to do with trading, which is why a corporation that did nothing all year still files schedules.

The GIFI Rule, Stated Exactly

A corporation inactive all year with nothing to report may omit GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141, but any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes them required.

Read the second half carefully. The exemption needs nothing at all to report. A corporation holding $100.00 of share capital issued at incorporation has something to report, and the schedules are required.

The full treatment is in the guide to GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141.

The Five Things That Make GIFI Required

TriggerWhy it counts
Share capitalIssued at incorporation and sits on the balance sheet
CashA balance in an account is an asset
A shareholder loanA balance either owed to or by the corporation
An incorporation costCapitalised, so it remains on the balance sheet
A bank chargeAn expense, so there is something on the income statement

Four of the five are present in most corporations from the day they are registered, which is why the exemption is narrower in practice than it reads.

Schedule 100, the Balance Sheet

The balance sheet in GIFI codes: what the corporation owns, what it owes, and the share capital and retained earnings that make up the difference.

On a nil return it is usually short. Share capital on one line, perhaps a bank balance, perhaps a shareholder loan. Short is not the same as omitted.

Schedule 125, the Income Statement

Revenue and expenses in GIFI codes. On a genuinely nil year both sides are $0.00, and the schedule says so explicitly rather than being left out.

A single bank charge is an expense, and it belongs on Schedule 125. It does not stop the year being nil for this service, but it does mean the schedule is filed.

Schedule 141, the Notes Checklist

The notes checklist records who prepared the financial information and what level of involvement they had. It accompanies Schedules 100 and 125 and is filed with them.

See whether financial statements are needed for a nil T2 return.

Schedule 50, Shareholders at 10% or More

Schedule 50 is needed for any shareholder at 10% or more. It lists each of them, their percentage of common and preferred shares, and their identification number.

The test is ownership of the shares, not involvement in the company. A shareholder who has never attended a meeting still appears where the holding reaches the threshold. The detail is on the Schedule 50 and the 10% shareholder rule post.

Schedule 24, First Return Only

Schedule 24 is needed on the first return after incorporation. It reports the corporation’s shareholders, its related corporations and how it came into existence, and it is filed once.

Where a first year was never filed, Schedule 24 is still filed, late, on whichever return turns out to be the first one transmitted. The detail is in the guide to first-year T2 filing.

Schedule 8 and Loss Continuity

Schedule 8 and loss continuity must be recorded. A nil year adds nothing to either, but the continuity has to be carried forward intact so a later year can use it.

This is the schedule most often skipped on a nil return, and the one that costs the most to skip. A break in loss continuity is discovered in the year the corporation finally has income to apply it against.

The full treatment is in the guide to Schedule 8 and loss continuity.

The T2 Short and Its Schedules

The T2 Short is available only to a CCPC with nil net income or a loss, one permanent establishment, no taxable dividends paid or received, Canadian currency, no refundable credits, no Ontario transitional debit and no section 34.2 amount.

Qualifying for the shorter form does not remove a schedule that is otherwise required. Eligibility is covered in the guide to T2 Short eligibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Schedules Does a Nil T2 Need?

It depends on what the corporation holds rather than what it earned. GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 where there is anything on the balance sheet, Schedule 50 for any shareholder at 10% or more, Schedule 24 on a first return, and Schedule 8 with loss continuity recorded.

Can a Nil Return Skip the GIFI Schedules?

Only where the corporation was inactive all year with nothing at all to report. Any share capital, cash, shareholder loan, incorporation cost or bank charge makes Schedules 100, 125 and 141 required.

Does Share Capital Alone Make GIFI Required?

Yes. Share capital issued at incorporation sits on the balance sheet, which is something to report, so the schedules are required.

Do I Need Schedule 24 Every Year?

No. Schedule 24 is filed once, on the first return after incorporation.

Why File Schedule 8 on a Year With Nothing in It?

To carry the continuity forward intact. A break is only discovered in the year the corporation finally has income to apply a loss against.

Is There an Extra Charge for Schedules?

No. The fee is $300.00 plus HST per tax year and covers whichever schedules the return requires.
