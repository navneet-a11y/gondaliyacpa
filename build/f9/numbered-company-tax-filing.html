What a Numbered Company Is

A corporation whose legal name is the number the registry assigned it, in the form 1234567 Ontario Inc. or 9876543 Canada Inc. Nothing was chosen; the registry issued a number and the corporation kept it.

Owners pick a numbered name to incorporate quickly, to avoid a name search, or because the corporation was never meant to trade under a name of its own.

The Obligation Is Identical

A numbered company is an ordinary corporation. It files a T2 within six months of each fiscal year-end, every year, until it is formally dissolved, on exactly the same terms as a corporation with a chosen name.

There is no lighter regime for a numbered company, no different deadline and no different form. The numbered name affects nothing about the filing.

The CRA Identifies It by Business Number

The Canada Revenue Agency works from the nine-digit business number and its RC corporation income tax account, not from the corporate name. For filing purposes the name is a label on a record the number already identifies.

That is why a numbered company is no harder to file for. See whether a business number is needed to file a nil T2.

Shelf Corporations

A shelf corporation is one registered and then left unused, held in case it is wanted later. Most are numbered, because no name was needed for a corporation that was not going to trade.

A shelf corporation is the textbook nil filer: no revenue, no expenses, no payroll, a return every year reporting nil amounts. What it is not is exempt.

Numbered Holding Corporations Are Often Not Nil

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Many numbered corporations are holding companies, and a holding company earning investment income has no operations, no staff and no customers while still falling outside a nil return. The portfolio earns income that belongs on the return.

A numbered holding corporation that genuinely holds nothing and earns nothing is nil. One holding investments is not.

The Registry Record and the Annual Return

A numbered company files an annual return with its registry in exactly the way a named one does, confirming directors and the registered office. Filing the T2 does not satisfy it.

The full set is in the guide to provincial and federal annual returns.

Numbered Companies Are the Ones Most Often Forgotten

A numbered corporation has no brand, no website and no letterhead to remind anyone it exists. It generates no mail the owner recognises and appears on no invoice.

That is why unfiled years cluster on numbered companies. The obligation did not stop; the reminder did.

See whether nil T2 returns can be filed for a forgotten corporation.

Unfiled Years on a Numbered Company

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Each year is a separate return with its own fiscal year-end and its own due date. Filing several at once is routine and closes the years in a single pass; the sequence is in the guide to catching up on unfiled corporate tax returns.

Adding a Business Name Later

A numbered company can register a business name and trade under it while keeping the number as its legal name. The corporation does not change and neither does its business number.

The T2 is filed under the legal numbered name whatever the corporation trades as. A registered business name is a separate registry filing, not a change to the corporation.

Dissolving a Numbered Company

Identical to dissolving a named one. Every earlier year must already be filed, the final T2 is due within six months of the dissolution date, and a clearance certificate cannot be issued while any tax year is open.

Because a numbered company usually has no name worth keeping, dissolution is more often the right answer than it is for a named corporation. The sequence is in the guide to the final T2 and dissolution.

Deadlines

Six months after the fiscal year-end, every year. Where a due date falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday recognised by the Canada Revenue Agency, a return received the next business day is on time.

Every fiscal year-end and its due date is listed on T2 filing deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Numbered Company File a Different Return?

No. It files the same T2, on the same deadline, with the same schedules. The numbered name affects nothing about the filing obligation.

How Does the CRA Know Which Corporation It Is?

By the nine-digit business number and its RC corporation income tax account. The corporate name is a label on a record the number already identifies.

Is a Shelf Corporation a Nil Filer?

Usually yes. A corporation registered and left unused has no revenue and no expenses, so it files a nil return every year until it is dissolved.

Is a Numbered Holding Company Nil?

Not where it earns investment income. A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil, and are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Can I Trade Under a Name Instead of the Number?

Yes, by registering a business name with the registry. The corporation and its business number do not change, and the T2 is still filed under the legal numbered name.

Why Do Numbered Companies End Up With Unfiled Years?

Because nothing reminds the owner they exist. No brand, no letterhead, no invoices. The filing obligation continues whether or not anything prompts it.
