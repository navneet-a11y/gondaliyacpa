What a Demand to File Is

A written requirement from the Canada Revenue Agency that a named return be filed by a named date. It is not a reminder and it is not an assessment. It is a legal obligation with its own deadline, separate from the original filing deadline the return already missed.

A demand is usually the first contact a dormant corporation receives. Nothing has gone to collections at this point, and the position is still straightforward to resolve.

Why It Arrived

Because the Canada Revenue Agency holds a record showing the corporation exists and a tax year for which no return was received. Incorporation created that record, and it persists whether or not the corporation ever traded.

TriggerWhat the Agency sees
Incorporated and never tradedAn open tax year with no return
Stopped trading, kept the corporationA gap after the last return filed
An open RC accountA filing obligation on the account
A year-end passing with no returnThe corporation moving into arrears

None of the four requires the corporation to have earned anything.

Reading the Letter

Three things on the letter decide what happens next: which tax year or years are named, the date the return is required by, and whether the letter names a specific officer of the corporation.

The named date is the one that matters. A demand answered by that date closes the matter. A demand answered late is treated as a repeated failure, which doubles the percentage penalty rate on any year where tax is owing.

A Nil Year Still Answers the Demand

Filing a nil return satisfies a demand in exactly the way a trading return does. The obligation is to file, not to owe.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

What Happens if It Is Ignored

The Canada Revenue Agency proceeds without the corporation. It may assess on its own estimate of income, and in a persistent case may pursue the failure through the courts.

StageWhat it means
Demand issuedA deadline to file is set
Demand unansweredThe repeated-failure penalty rate becomes available
Arbitrary assessmentA tax debt is raised on an estimate
CollectionThat debt is pursued like any other
ProsecutionAvailable where the failure persists

The corporation moves down that table by doing nothing.

How an Arbitrary Assessment Works

The Agency estimates income from whatever it holds, assesses tax on that estimate, and issues a notice. The figure is not a guess the corporation can correct by explaining; it is an assessment that stands until a filed return replaces it.

Interest runs on the assessed balance from the date it is raised. For a corporation that genuinely had no activity, this is how a year that owed $0.00 becomes a debt.

Displacing an Arbitrary Assessment

By filing the actual return for that year. The filed return is assessed on its own figures and supersedes the estimate.

A nil return displaces an arbitrary assessment to $0.00 where the corporation truly had no activity. The sooner it is filed, the less interest has accumulated on the estimate in the meantime.

Where the Demand Names Several Years

Each year named is a separate return on its own form for its own tax year. Five years named is five returns. They are prepared and transmitted together, oldest first.

Checking the Year Really Was Nil

Before filing, the year is tested rather than assumed. A corporation is nil for this service only where it had no activity at all in the tax year.

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Share capital, cash, a shareholder loan, an incorporation cost or a bank charge does not stop the year being nil, but it does make GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 required. The detail is in the guide to GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141.

The Accounts a T2 Does Not Close

Answering a demand for a T2 settles the corporate income tax obligation and nothing else. An open GST/HST account and an open payroll account each carry their own filing obligations and generate their own demands.

A GST/HST account is closed with Form RC145. Until it is closed or filed nil, it keeps producing letters of its own.

After the Return Is Transmitted

The confirmation of transmission is the record that the demand was answered. It is kept with the corporation’s minute book alongside the assessment that follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a CRA Demand to File?

A written requirement from the Canada Revenue Agency that a named return be filed by a named date. It sets its own deadline, separate from the filing deadline the return already missed.

Does Filing a Nil Return Answer a Demand?

Yes. The obligation is to file, not to owe. A nil return satisfies a demand in the same way a trading return does.

What if I Ignore It?

The Canada Revenue Agency may assess the corporation on its own estimate of income. That arbitrary assessment is a collectible debt with interest running on it, and it stands until a filed return displaces it.

Can an Arbitrary Assessment Be Reversed?

It is displaced rather than argued down. Filing the actual return for that year replaces the estimate, and a genuine nil year displaces it to $0.00.

The Demand Names Four Years. Is That Four Returns?

Yes. Each tax year is its own return on its own form. They are prepared and transmitted together, oldest first.

Does Answering the Demand Close My GST/HST Account?

No. A T2 settles the corporate income tax obligation only. An open GST/HST account carries its own filing obligation and is closed with Form RC145.
