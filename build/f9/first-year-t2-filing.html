When the First Tax Year Starts

On the incorporation date. Not on the day the first invoice goes out, not on the day the bank account opens, and not on the first of the following month.

This is the single most misunderstood point about a first return. A corporation registered in March and never traded still has a tax year that began in March, and a return due for it.

The 53-Week Rule

A corporation’s first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks. That is the only hard limit on where the first year-end falls.

Within that window the date is the corporation’s own choice, which is why two corporations incorporated on the same day can have different year-ends and different due dates.

Choosing the First Year-End

ConsiderationEffect
Any date within 53 weeks of incorporationPermitted
A month-end rather than a mid-month dateSimpler to administer, no tax effect
A short first yearBrings the first filing deadline forward
A full 53-week first yearPushes the first filing deadline out
Changing it laterNeeds the Agency’s permission

Once the first return is filed, the year-end is set and later years run twelve months from it.

The Year-End the CRA Already Holds

The Canada Revenue Agency takes a year-end from the incorporation record and holds it before the corporation has filed anything. The first return has to carry that same date.

Where the owner intended a different year-end than the one on record, that is settled with the Agency before the return is transmitted, not by filing a different date and hoping.

Schedule 24, Required on a First Return

Schedule 24 is the first-time filer schedule, required on the first return after incorporation. It reports the corporation’s shareholders, its related corporations and how it came into existence.

It is filed once and not again. It is included in the fee of $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

A First Year With No Trading

A corporation registered and then left unused has a nil first year, and a nil return is filed for it. Filing nothing is not an option the rules offer, and "we never started" is not a filing status.

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Why First Transmissions Get Rejected

Because the year-end on the return does not match the one the Agency holds. It is the most common cause of a rejected first transmission, ahead of every other error combined.

CauseFix
Year-end taken from when trading startedUse the date on the incorporation record
Year-end more than 53 weeks after incorporationBring it inside the 53-week limit
Business number or RC account not yet activeConfirm the account before transmitting
Corporation name not matching the registryUse the exact legal name

One corrected transmission is included in the fee where the Agency rejects the first.

The Business Number and RC Account

The return is filed against the nine-digit business number and its RC corporation income tax account. Most corporations receive the number at incorporation, but it is confirmed as active before the first return goes out.

A corporation that never received one obtains it from the Canada Revenue Agency first.

GIFI on a First Return

A first-year corporation almost always has something on its balance sheet: the share capital issued on incorporation, and often the incorporation costs themselves.

That makes GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 required on most first returns, even where the corporation never traded. The exemption for a corporation with nothing at all to report rarely applies in year one.

The detail is in the guide to GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141.

The First Filing Deadline

Six months after the first fiscal year-end, like every other year. A corporation incorporated in March with a December year-end has its first return due the following June.

Every fiscal year-end and its due date is listed on T2 filing deadlines.

A First Year That Was Never Filed

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

An unfiled first year is the one that compounds, because every later year sits on top of it and the Agency has no opening record for the corporation at all. Schedule 24 is still filed, late, on whichever return turns out to be the first one transmitted.

Frequently Asked Questions

When Does My First Tax Year Start?

On the incorporation date, not the day trading started. A corporation registered and never used still has a tax year running from the date on its incorporation record.

How Long Can My First Tax Year Be?

Up to 53 weeks. Within that window the year-end date is the corporation’s own choice, and it is fixed once the first return is filed.

Do I File a T2 if I Never Started the Business?

Yes. Incorporating creates the obligation. A corporation that never opened for business files a nil return rather than no return.

What Is Schedule 24?

The first-time filer schedule, required on the first return after incorporation. It reports shareholders, related corporations and how the corporation came into existence, and it is filed once.

Why Was My First Return Rejected?

Almost always a fiscal year-end that differs from the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds from the incorporation record. One corrected transmission is included in the fee.

Does a First-Year Nil Return Still Need GIFI?

Usually yes. Share capital issued on incorporation and capitalised incorporation costs both sit on the balance sheet, and either one makes Schedules 100, 125 and 141 required.
