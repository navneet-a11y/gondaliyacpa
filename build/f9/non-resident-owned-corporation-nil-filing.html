Which Corporations This Covers

This guide is about a corporation incorporated in Canada whose shares are held by people or entities outside Canada. It is a Canadian corporation with non-resident owners.

That is a different thing from a corporation incorporated abroad, and the distinction decides which rules apply.

CorporationWhat it is here
Incorporated in Canada, Canadian ownersAn ordinary resident corporation
Incorporated in Canada, owners abroadResident, and the subject of this guide
Incorporated abroad, managed from CanadaResidence is a question of fact and is not decided here
Incorporated abroad, no Canadian presenceOutside what this site files

Only the second row is straightforward, and it is the common case: a Canadian corporation set up by someone who has since left, or who never lived here.

Corporate Residence and Where It Is Decided

A corporation incorporated in Canada is a resident of Canada. Where a corporation was incorporated elsewhere, residence turns on where it is centrally managed and controlled, which is a question of fact.

Residence is the corporation's own, not its shareholders'. Owners living abroad do not make a Canadian corporation non-resident.

This site files for corporations incorporated in Canada. Where residence is genuinely in question, that is a determination for Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca rather than a form on this one.

The Filing Obligation

A Canadian corporation with non-resident owners files a T2 for every tax year, six months after each year-end, until it is dissolved. Having no activity does not change that and neither does having no Canadian resident involved in it.

The return is the same return. The owners' location changes who signs and how, not what is filed.

See whether a corporation with no activity files.

The Penalty Built on Days, Not Tax Owing

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

The last clause of that block is this guide's subject. A non-resident-owned corporation that fails to file faces a charge of $25.00 per day, to a maximum of $2,500.00, and it is built on days rather than on tax owing.

Days lateCharge at $25.00 a day
10 days$250.00
30 days$750.00
60 days$1,500.00
100 days$2,500.00, the maximum
A year or more$2,500.00, the maximum

The maximum is reached in a little over three months. After that the charge stops growing while everything else about the open year continues.

Why a Nil Year Carries the Full Exposure

A percentage of $0.00 is $0.00. Twenty-five dollars a day for a hundred days is $2,500.00 whether the corporation earned millions or nothing at all.

This is the one charge in this family of rules that reaches its maximum on a completely empty year. A dormant Canadian corporation owned from abroad is the single most exposed thing this site files for, and the exposure comes from the calendar rather than from anything it did.

Filing the year is what stops the days counting. See filing a nil T2 late.

Treaty-Based Returns

Where a tax treaty affects a corporation's Canadian tax position, the return may need to disclose the treaty relied on. That is a trading-return matter: a corporation with no income has no treaty position to take.

A nil year reports $0.00 of income from every source, so there is nothing for a treaty to exempt or reduce.

Where a treaty position is in play, the return is not nil and is not filed here.

Getting a Business Number Without a Canadian Address

A business number is needed before a return can be transmitted. A corporation obtains one whether or not its directors live in Canada.

The registered office address on the registry record is the corporation's own, and keeping it current matters more here than anywhere, because correspondence has further to travel and the directors are not nearby to collect it.

Catching Up Several Non-Resident Years

Each outstanding year is its own return for its own tax year, prepared oldest first. Six unfiled years are six returns, each at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

The daily charge is per return rather than per corporation, which is why several open years are worth dealing with together rather than one at a time.

See filing five years at once and the guide to catching up on unfiled corporate tax returns.

Dissolving a Non-Resident-Owned Corporation

The sequence is the same as for any other corporation: every outstanding year filed, a clearance certificate, articles of dissolution, then the final T2 running to the dissolution date.

Non-resident ownership gives it more urgency rather than a different shape. Each year the corporation is left standing is another year able to attract the daily charge.

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Canadian Corporation With Owners Abroad Still File a T2?

Yes, for every tax year, six months after each year-end, until it is formally dissolved. Owners living abroad do not change the obligation.

What Is the $25.00 a Day Charge?

A non-resident-owned corporation that fails to file faces $25.00 per day to a maximum of $2,500.00. It is built on days rather than on tax owing, so the maximum is reached in a little over three months.

Does It Apply to a Year With No Activity?

Yes, in full. It is calculated on days, so an entirely empty year reaches the same $2,500.00 maximum a trading year would.

Does Owning a Canadian Corporation From Abroad Make It Non-Resident?

No. A corporation incorporated in Canada is a resident of Canada. Residence is the corporation's own, not its shareholders'.

Can Form T183CORP Be Signed From Outside Canada?

Yes. An authorized signing officer signs it wherever they are, on a single-use access-controlled screen, with the date and time recorded on the server in Eastern Time.

Are the T106, T1134 and T1135 Covered by the Fee?

No. Those are separate information returns with their own deadlines, and a corporation that may owe one is handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.
