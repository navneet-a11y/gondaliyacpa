The Last Cohort That Could Have Filed Its First Return on Paper

For tax years starting after 2023, electronic filing is mandatory for virtually all corporations, and a paper T2 filed in breach of that draws a $1,000.00 penalty, with a separate $100.00 penalty on the preparer. A corporation incorporated in 2023 has a first tax year that started in 2023, so that first return sits on the near side of the line while every later year of the same corporation sits on the far side.

This produces a split that no other cohort has. The 2023 return could lawfully have gone in on paper. The 2024 and 2025 returns could not. A corporation catching up all three now is filing across a rule change, and the returns on the far side carry a penalty exposure that has nothing to do with how much tax is owed, because the penalty is for the method rather than the amount.

In practice the split changes nothing about how we file, because all three go electronically regardless. It matters for anyone who has been told that a dormant corporation can simply post its returns in. That was never good advice and for the later years it is now an expensive one.

There is a second reason the 2023 cohort is worth separating out. A corporation incorporated late in 2023 may have chosen a first year-end in 2024, which starts its first tax year in 2023 but ends it in 2024. The rule turns on when the tax year starts, not when it ends, so that corporation is still on the near side of the line for its first return. It is the one case where reading the year-end rather than the incorporation date gives the wrong answer.

Three returns, spanning a rule change that fell in the middle of the corporation’s life. 3 outstanding returns as at November 16, 2026, at $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year each, so $900.00 before tax for the series.

The three first-year rules. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks and the year-end must match CRA's record or the transmission is rejected. Schedule 24, the first-time filer schedule, is required on the first T2 a corporation ever files. The year-end you report is the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds on file, so a different date on the return is refused rather than corrected.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Confirm the incorporation date, which fixes when the first tax year started and therefore which side of the 2023 line it falls on.
  2. Confirm the year-end on file, reading the start of the first tax year rather than its end.
  3. Confirm all three years were nil.
  4. Complete Schedule 24 on the earliest return, which is the only one that carries it.
  5. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for each year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  6. Sign Form T183CORP for each of the three tax years.
  7. Transmit all three electronically, whatever the rule permitted for the earliest one.
