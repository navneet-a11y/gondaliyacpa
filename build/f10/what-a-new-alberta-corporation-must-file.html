No Sales Tax, and Still a Second Return

Alberta collects corporate income tax on the AT1, a separate return rather than a schedule of the federal T2. A new Alberta corporation therefore has two corporate returns for its first year even if nothing happened in it.

Having no provincial sales tax is the part people know. Having a second corporate return is the part that catches them out.

The AT1 Has Its Own Deadline

The AT1 is not filed with the T2 and does not inherit its dates. It is a separate return with its own filing requirement, and a year with no activity does not remove it.

That matters in the first year specifically, because a new director who files the federal return and stops will often not hear anything until the province asks.

The three first-year rules. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks and the year-end must match CRA's record or the transmission is rejected. Schedule 24, the first-time filer schedule, is required on the first T2 a corporation ever files. The year-end you report is the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds on file, so a different date on the return is refused rather than corrected.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Confirm whether Alberta wants a corporate return of its own, because that decides how many returns the first year needs.
  2. Confirm the year-end the Canada Revenue Agency holds, because the return is matched against it.
  3. Check the first tax year against the 53-week limit.
  4. Complete Schedule 24, which the first return always carries.
  5. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for the year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  6. Sign Form T183CORP for that tax year on a secure single-use screen.
  7. Transmit the return and keep the confirmation.
