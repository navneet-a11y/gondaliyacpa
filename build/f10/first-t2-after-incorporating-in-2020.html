A Year With Nothing in It Is Still a Tax Year

The first tax year of a 2020 corporation is usually the one people hesitate over, because the company was registered and then immediately did nothing at all. There is an instinct that a year with no activity whatsoever is not really a tax year and that the first return should start from whenever the company began operating. That is not how the return works.

The first tax year runs from the date of incorporation to the first year-end, and it is a tax year even if it is three weeks long and contains no transactions. It carries Schedule 24, which is the schedule a corporation files once in its life, on its first return. Skipping it because the year looks empty means the first return the Agency ever receives from the corporation is missing the schedule that identifies it as a first return.

Everything after that first year is an ordinary nil year. Six returns, each reporting no income and no expenses, each due six months after its own year-end, and each one still outstanding until it is filed.

The first tax year is also where the 53-week limit does real work. A corporation incorporated late in 2020 could not lawfully have run its first tax year to the end of 2021, because that would exceed 53 weeks. If somebody has told the Agency a year-end that implies a longer first year, the record needs correcting before any return is transmitted against it, because the return is matched to the date on file rather than the date on the return.

The three first-year rules. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks and the year-end must match CRA's record or the transmission is rejected. Schedule 24, the first-time filer schedule, is required on the first T2 a corporation ever files. The year-end you report is the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds on file, so a different date on the return is refused rather than corrected.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Confirm the year-end on file and test the first tax year against the 53-week limit, which a late-2020 incorporation can breach.
  2. Correct the year-end with the Agency first if the record and the intended date disagree.
  3. Confirm each of the six years had no activity.
  4. Complete Schedule 24 on the earliest return, which is the only one that carries it.
  5. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for each year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  6. Sign Form T183CORP for each of the six tax years.
  7. Transmit the six returns oldest year first.
