Eight Years on the Public Record

The Agency publishes a count of corporations by jurisdiction, and the earliest tax year in the current table is 2018. A corporation incorporated that year has therefore been counted in every year of the published series, from the first column to the last, whether or not it ever filed anything. The counts include inactive corporations, which is the point worth drawing out: being dormant does not take a corporation off the rolls, and it never has.

That is the whole logic of the nil return in one observation. The obligation attaches to the corporation existing, not to the corporation doing anything. A company registered in 2018 and left alone is in the same statistical population as one that traded every year, and it carries the same annual filing requirement.

What the published counts do not show is how many of those corporations are current on their returns, because the count is of corporations on the rolls rather than of returns received. A corporation can appear in all six years of the table and have filed in none of them. Being counted is not evidence of being compliant, and it is the reason a long-dormant corporation can feel invisible while remaining entirely visible.

The size of that population is worth stating, because it is where the intuition that somebody must have noticed comes from. The Agency assessed returns from 2,422,910 corporations across the thirteen jurisdictions for the 2018 tax year, and 2,698,230 for 2023, a rise of 275,320. A corporation that has filed nothing since 2018 is one dormant file among millions of active ones, which explains why the letters are slow and why they still arrive.

Worked for a corporation incorporated in 2018 that chose a December 31 year-end, which makes each T2 due June 30 of the following year. A different year-end moves every due date but not the number of returns. Position is stated as at November 16, 2026. The six-month rule comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province.

Eight returns, one for every year the corporation has been counted in the published figures. 8 outstanding returns as at November 16, 2026, at $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year each, so $2,400.00 before tax for the series.

The three first-year rules. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks and the year-end must match CRA's record or the transmission is rejected. Schedule 24, the first-time filer schedule, is required on the first T2 a corporation ever files. The year-end you report is the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds on file, so a different date on the return is refused rather than corrected.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Confirm the incorporation date, which puts the first tax year in 2018 and the first due date in 2019.
  2. Confirm the year-end the Agency holds, against which all eight returns are matched.
  3. Establish which of the eight years had no income and no expenses.
  4. Complete Schedule 24 on the earliest return, which is the only one that carries it.
  5. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for each year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  6. Sign Form T183CORP for each of the eight tax years.
  7. Transmit the series in order and keep the confirmations.
