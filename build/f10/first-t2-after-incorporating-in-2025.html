Your First Return Is Already Overdue

This is the cohort where the first return has just gone past its date, and it is worth being plain about what that means for a corporation that owes no tax. A percentage late-filing penalty is a percentage of tax owing, and a true nil return owes $0.00, so the percentage has nothing to apply to. What is left is everything else: the year stays open indefinitely, it invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, it blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and for a non-resident-owned corporation it runs at $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

The first return is also the one that has to be right about two things no later return raises. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks, which constrains the year-end a corporation incorporated part-way through 2025 could legitimately have chosen. And the year-end on the return has to match the one the Agency holds, or the transmission is rejected rather than corrected.

One return is the smallest version of this job and the one most worth doing immediately, because the series stops at one only if the next year-end is met on time.

The other thing worth settling now is whether the year was genuinely nil, because a first year often contains the one transaction nobody counts: incorporation costs paid by the corporation, a share subscription, or an opening bank deposit. None of those stops the year being nil. They do make the GIFI schedules required, and those are included in the fee rather than charged for separately.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Confirm the year-end on file and test the first tax year against the 53-week limit.
  2. Confirm the year had no income and no expenses, treating incorporation costs and a share subscription as what they are.
  3. Complete Schedule 24, which this return carries because it is the corporation’s first.
  4. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for the year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  5. Sign Form T183CORP for the tax year.
  6. Transmit the return electronically and keep the confirmation, which is the corporation’s first.
