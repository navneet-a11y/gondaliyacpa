Nothing Is Overdue Yet, and That Is the Useful Part

This is the only page in this series where the answer is not a catch-up. A corporation registered in 2026 has a first tax year that has not ended or has only just ended, and a first T2 that is not due until six months after whichever year-end it adopts. Everything about the rest of this series is avoidable from here.

The one decision with lasting consequences is the first year-end, because the first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks from the date of incorporation. That sets an outer limit on how far out the first year-end can be placed, and the date chosen then fixes every deadline the corporation will ever have. The Agency records the year-end it is given, and every later return is matched against that record.

The first return itself carries Schedule 24, which a corporation files once, and the nil GIFI schedules if the year had no activity. Filing it on time, even reporting nothing, is what keeps the corporation out of the position the 2015 page describes.

The common mistake at this stage is to assume the first return is due twelve months after incorporation. It is due six months after the first year-end, and the year-end is a date the corporation chooses rather than an anniversary. A corporation incorporated in March 2026 that picks a December 31 year-end has a first return due June 30, 2027; one that picks a March 31 year-end has it due September 30, 2027.

The three first-year rules. The first tax year cannot exceed 53 weeks and the year-end must match CRA's record or the transmission is rejected. Schedule 24, the first-time filer schedule, is required on the first T2 a corporation ever files. The year-end you report is the one the Canada Revenue Agency holds on file, so a different date on the return is refused rather than corrected.

The Order It Happens In

  1. Choose the first year-end, keeping the first tax year inside 53 weeks of the incorporation date.
  2. Tell the Canada Revenue Agency that year-end, because every later return is matched against the date it holds.
  3. Work out the first due date, which falls six months after the year-end chosen.
  4. Complete Schedule 24 when the first return is prepared.
  5. Prepare the nil GIFI schedules for the first year: Schedule 100, Schedule 125 and Schedule 141.
  6. Sign Form T183CORP for the first tax year.
  7. Transmit on or before the due date, so the corporation never starts a series at all.
