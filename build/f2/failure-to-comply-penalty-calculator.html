The Greater of $100.00 and $25.00 a Day, Counted to 100 Days

Subsection 162(7) is the residual penalty in the Income Tax Act. It reaches a failure to file an information return when no other provision covers it, and a failure to comply with any other duty or obligation the Act or the Regulations impose. The charge is the greater of $100.00 and $25.00 multiplied by the number of days the failure continues, with the day count stopping at 100.

Two things follow from that shape. The first four days all cost $100.00, because four days at $25.00 is exactly the floor and anything shorter is below it. And the charge stops growing at $2,500.00, which it reaches on the hundredth day, however many years the failure then continues.

Five Penalties, and the Base Each One Multiplies

Most of the late-filing provisions people have in mind are percentages of an amount of tax. This one is not, and that single difference is why it matters to a corporation with nothing to report.

Why This Is the Provision a Dormant Corporation Watches

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. That is a real and useful point, and it is also the point people stop at. The exposure on a quiet corporation is not the percentage provisions at all. It is the flat ones, the ones that do not ask what is owed.

The unfiled year also stays open indefinitely, invites a demand to file and then an arbitrary assessment, and blocks the clearance certificate a dissolution needs. The CRA demand to file deadline calculator and the arbitrary assessment response checker cover that sequence, and a non-resident-owned corporation has its own daily charge, which the non-resident T2 penalty calculator computes.

Nil T2 filing at $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year covers the federal corporate income tax return for one tax year, and each unfiled year is its own return at that fee. Where a charge has already been assessed, the taxpayer relief eligibility checker sets out the grounds for having it reconsidered, and a voluntary disclosure is the other route where the filings themselves are the problem.

A percentage of nothing is nothing, which is exactly why the flat and daily charges are the ones a dormant corporation has to watch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the penalty under subsection 162(7)?

The greater of $100.00 and $25.00 multiplied by the number of days the failure continues, with the day count stopping at 100. That puts it between $100.00 and $2,500.00.

Does it depend on how much tax is owing?

No. It is a flat and daily amount, which is what makes it the provision that still reaches a corporation owing $0.00.

What is the maximum?

$2,500.00. The day count stops at 100, so the figure does not grow after the hundredth day.

Is there a minimum?

Yes, $100.00. The first four days all come to $100.00, because four days at $25.00 is the floor exactly.

Does it apply to a late T2 return?

A return of income is charged under subsection 162(1) instead. This provision is the residual one, for information returns no other provision covers and for other duties under the Act.

Does it apply to late T4 slips?

No. An information return carrying slips is charged on the sliding scale in subsection 162(7.01), which is set by the slip count.

What kind of failure does it actually cover?

A failure to file an information return that no other provision reaches, and a failure to comply with another duty or obligation the Act or the Regulations impose.

Can it be charged on a nil year?

It can. Nothing in the provision asks whether any tax was payable, which is the difference between it and the percentage provisions.

Can it be cancelled?

Relief can be requested on the statutory grounds, and the request reaches back ten years. Cancelling a charge is discretionary rather than automatic, so the request has to be made on one of those grounds.

Does the nil T2 fee cover an assessed penalty?

No. The fee covers preparing and transmitting the corporate income tax return. An amount the Agency has already assessed sits outside it.
