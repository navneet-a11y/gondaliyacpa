Two Provinces Collect Their Own Corporate Tax

In most of Canada the province levies its corporate income tax through the federal return, so one T2 settles both. Quebec and Alberta do not. Each administers its own corporate tax and each has its own return.

Quebec’s is CO-17, filed with Revenu Quebec. Alberta’s is AT1. Both are separate returns in their own right, not schedules attached to the T2 and not something the T2 covers.

A Quiet Year Is Quiet on Both Returns

A corporation with no activity has nothing to report provincially either, so the provincial return for that year is nil in the same way the federal one is.

Being nil does not make it optional. It is a separate return, filed separately, on its own deadline, and the obligation follows from the permanent establishment rather than from there being anything to report.

What the Fee Covers, Said Plainly

The fee of $300.00 plus HST per tax year covers one nil federal T2 return for one corporation for one tax year. CO-17 is not covered by it, and neither is AT1.

That is worth being direct about rather than leaving a visitor to discover it. The nil T2 filing cost page sets out the whole boundary of what sits inside the fee and what does not.

The Registry Annual Return Is a Different Thing

Every corporation also owes an annual return to the registry it is incorporated with, federally or provincially. That is a registry filing confirming the directors and the registered office, and it reports no income.

It is easy to hear "provincial return" and think of that one. They are unrelated obligations with different consequences: missing a registry annual return gets a corporation struck, which the involuntary dissolution risk checker covers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Quebec have a separate corporate tax return?

Yes. CO-17 is a separate provincial return, filed with Revenu Quebec. It is not part of the federal T2.

Does Alberta have one?

Yes. AT1 is a separate provincial return in the same way.

Is CO-17 or AT1 covered by the fee?

No. The fee of $300.00 plus HST per tax year covers one nil federal T2 return for one corporation for one tax year.

What about the other provinces?

Elsewhere the province levies its corporate income tax through the federal return, so the T2 is the only corporate income tax return.

Is a nil provincial return still required?

The obligation follows the permanent establishment rather than there being something to report, so a nil year is still a return to file.
