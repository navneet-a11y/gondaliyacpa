Money Going Out Is Not Activity

A corporation that paid a bank charge, an annual registry fee or an accounting bill, and took nothing in, had no activity in the sense the nil test uses. The return reports expenses against no revenue, which is a loss for the year rather than income, and it is still a nil return at $300.00 plus HST per tax year.

The asymmetry is the part worth stating plainly: what ends a nil year is something coming in, not something going out.

The Loss Is Only Real Once the Year Is Filed

A year of costs with no revenue produces a loss, and that loss is only established by the filed return for that year. Leaving the year unfiled leaves the loss unestablished, which is the quiet cost of skipping a year that felt too small to bother with.

Schedule 8 and the loss continuity schedule travel with the return and are inside the fee. The loss continuity on a nil return checker covers what happens to a loss while its year sits unfiled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a year with expenses but no income still nil?

Yes, for this service. Costs going out do not make a year active. What ends a nil year is something coming in: revenue, interest credited on a balance, rent or a dividend either way.

Do the expenses make extra schedules necessary?

They make GIFI Schedules 100, 125 and 141 required, because something sat on the books during the year. Those schedules are inside $300.00 plus HST per tax year rather than charged separately.

What happens to the loss from a year like that?

It is established by the filed return for that year and carried on the loss continuity schedule. A year left unfiled leaves its loss unestablished, which is one reason to file a small year rather than skip it.
