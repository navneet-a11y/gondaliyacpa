Two Figures Decide It, and Nothing Else Does

A registrant does not choose a reporting period from a standing start. The period is assigned by annual taxable supplies, and there are only two numbers in the test. At $1,500,000 or less the assigned period is annual. Above that and up to $6,000,000 it is quarterly. Above $6,000,000 it is monthly.

From the assigned period a registrant may elect a shorter one, never a longer one. An annual filer can elect quarterly or monthly, a quarterly filer can elect monthly, and a monthly filer has nothing shorter to move to. The supplies figure is worldwide and includes the supplies of associated persons, which is the part that catches a group of small corporations under common control.

The Annual Filer Gets Three Months, the Others One

The deadline is not a fixed number of days from the period end. A monthly or quarterly filer has one month after the period closes to file and to pay. An annual filer has three months after the fiscal year end for both. That is a real difference and it is the reason the period matters to a quiet corporation at all.

There is one condition attached to annual filing. Where net tax for the year reaches $3,000.00, the annual filer also pays quarterly instalments through the year and the return reconciles them at the end. A registrant with nothing to remit never reaches that figure, so a dormant corporation on annual filing has one nil return a year and nothing in between.

The supplies set the frequency, an election can only make the periods shorter, and a dormant registrant still files on whichever one applies.

A Nil Return Is Still a Return, Once Per Period

Nothing about being dormant changes the assigned frequency. A monthly filer that stopped trading still has twelve returns a year to file, each of them at nil, until either the frequency is changed or the account is closed. That is the single strongest argument for electing or reverting to annual filing before a corporation goes quiet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the GST/HST reporting period decided?

By annual taxable supplies. At $1,500,000 or less the assigned period is annual, above that to $6,000,000 it is quarterly, and above $6,000,000 it is monthly.

Can a registrant choose a different period?

A shorter one, yes. An annual filer may elect quarterly or monthly and a quarterly filer may elect monthly. A longer period than the assigned one is not available.

When is a quarterly return due?

One month after the quarter ends, for both the return and the payment.

When is an annual return due?

Three months after the fiscal year end for a corporation, for both the return and the payment.

Do associated corporations count in the supplies figure?

Yes. The test is worldwide taxable supplies including those of associated persons, which is what catches a group of small corporations.

Does a dormant registrant still file?

Yes, once per period in the assigned frequency, at nil, until the frequency changes or the account is closed.

What are the instalments an annual filer pays?

Where net tax for the year reaches $3,000.00 the annual filer pays quarterly instalments and the return reconciles them. A registrant with nothing to remit does not reach that figure.

Is a monthly filer that stopped trading stuck with twelve returns?

Until the frequency is changed or the account is closed, yes. That is the usual reason to deal with the frequency before a corporation goes quiet.

Is the GST/HST return part of the nil T2 fee?

No. The fee covers the federal corporate income tax return. The sales tax account is a separate filing to a separate programme.

Does the fiscal year end matter?

For an annual filer it sets the deadline, because the three months run from it. For monthly and quarterly filers the period end does.
