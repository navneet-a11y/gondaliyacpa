A Short Year Does Not Get a Short Deadline

The assumption worth correcting first: a shorter tax year does not come with a proportionally shorter filing window. Six months runs from the year-end whatever the length of the period behind it.

A two-month stub period and a twelve-month year both get six months from their year-end. The stub period does not get one month because it was one-sixth the length, and it does not get longer either.

Where Short Years Come From

Four situations produce one. A first tax year running from incorporation to the first year-end chosen. A change of fiscal year-end leaving a stub period at the changeover. A dissolution ending the year on its own date. An amalgamation ending a predecessor’s year the day before.

In all four the short period is a full tax year for filing purposes, needing its own return at $300.00 plus HST per tax year for a nil one.

The 53-Week Limit Applies to a First Year

A first tax year may not exceed 53 weeks, which is 371 days counted from the date of incorporation to the year-end. A later tax year may run a full twelve months, so the limit only bites on a first year or on the first year of a corporation coming out of an amalgamation.

Where the period entered here is longer than 53 weeks and it is a first year, the year-end is probably not the one on record. The 53-week first tax year checker tests that question on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Short Year Filed Together With the Next Full Year?

No. It is a separate tax year and needs its own return. Two year-ends in one calendar year means two returns.

Does a Short Year Cost Less to File?

No. A nil return is $300.00 plus HST per tax year whatever the length of the period it covers.

Can a Tax Year Be Longer Than Twelve Months?

A first tax year may not exceed 53 weeks, which is 371 days. Later years run to the fiscal year-end on record.

What Makes a Period a Stub Period?

A change of fiscal year-end leaves a gap between the old year-end and the new one. That gap is a tax year of its own.

Does the Filing Date Still Move for a Weekend?

Yes. The shift applies to a short year exactly as it does to a full one, so six months landing on a Saturday moves to the Monday.
