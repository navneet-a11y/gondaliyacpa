Where the Line Sits

A nil return and a full return are the same form. What differs is whether anything happened in the tax year. One revenue entry, one expense, one dividend or one disposal and the year is a full return, however small the amount.

Existing is not activity. A corporation can hold a bank account, own assets and stay registered for years without carrying on business, and every one of those years is nil.

What Changes When It Is Not Nil

The schedules grow with the activity, the figures stop being zero, and the work is no longer a fixed-fee filing. That is why a full return is handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca rather than here.

A holding corporation with investment income, a rental corporation and a corporation that paid or received a dividend are not nil. Those are handled by Gondaliya CPA Professional Corporation at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

Fourteen Situations and Which Return Each One Needs

The test is not how small the amount was. It is whether anything happened at all. These are the situations that come up most often, and which side of the line each one falls on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Nil Return a Shorter Form?

No. It is a complete T2 with the figures at zero. The identification, the fiscal year dates and the GIFI schedules where they are required are all still part of it.

Does One Small Expense Really Make It a Full Return?

Yes. There is no threshold below which activity is ignored. A single bank charge paid from the corporate account is activity.

What If Different Years Are Different?

Each tax year is judged on its own. A corporation can file nil for three years, full for one, and nil again after it.

Is the T2 Short the Same as a Nil Return?

No. The T2 Short is a shorter version of the form that some corporations qualify to use. The T2 Short eligibility checker runs all eight conditions.
