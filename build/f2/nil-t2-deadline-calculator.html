The Rule This Applies

A T2 is due within six months of the corporation’s fiscal year-end, every year, until the corporation is formally dissolved. A final T2 is due within six months of dissolution.

Six months is counted two ways, and the difference matters:

Where the year-end fallsThe due dateExample
On the last day of a monthThe last day of the sixth month afterYear-end August 31 is due the last day of February
On any other dayThe same day of the sixth month afterYear-end August 15 is due February 15

The deadline comes from the Income Tax Act and does not vary by province.

Weekends and Ontario Statutory Holidays

Where the due date falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday, a return received on the next business day is on time. The calculator shows that next business day whenever it applies.

The business days remaining count excludes weekends and the nine Ontario public holidays under the Employment Standards Act: New Year’s Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day and Boxing Day. The August civic holiday is not among them and is not applied.

A Deadline Already Passed

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Unfiled years are filed oldest first, one return per tax year.
