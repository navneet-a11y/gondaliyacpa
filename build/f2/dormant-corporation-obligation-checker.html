Nothing Lapses Because Nobody Is Looking

A corporation must file a T2 within six months of its fiscal year-end, every year, until it is formally dissolved. Being dormant is not a status that suspends that, and no amount of time passing turns it into one.

The registry annual return runs alongside it on its own date. Two obligations, two bodies, two consequences for missing them, and neither of them stops because the corporation has gone quiet.

An Open Account Keeps Generating Returns

A payroll account expects a T4 information return even where nobody was paid, and a GST/HST account expects returns including nil ones. The obligation follows the open account rather than the activity.

Form RC145 closes either of them. An inactive T2 does not close those accounts, which is the mistake worth avoiding: filing the tax return does not tell the payroll account to stop expecting something.

One of These Is Inside the Fee

The nil T2 is $300.00 plus HST per tax year. The registry annual return, any T4 return and any GST/HST return sit outside it, as does closing an account.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a dormant corporation still have to file?

A T2 every year until it is formally dissolved, the annual return to its registry, and returns on any payroll or GST/HST account that is still open.

Does being dormant suspend the filing requirement?

No. A corporation must file a T2 within six months of its fiscal year-end, every year, until it is formally dissolved.

Does filing a nil T2 close my payroll account?

No. An inactive T2 does not close those accounts. Form RC145 closes a payroll or a GST/HST account.

Is the registry annual return part of the tax return?

No. It is a separate filing to a separate body, reporting the directors and the registered office rather than any income.

Which of these is covered by the fee?

The nil T2 return, at $300.00 plus HST per tax year. The registry annual return, T4 returns, GST/HST returns and account closures are outside it.
