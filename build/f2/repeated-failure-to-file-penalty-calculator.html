Both Conditions Have to Be Met

Subsection 162(2) is often described as the penalty for filing late again, which understates what it takes to trigger. Two things must both be true of the year in question: the Canada Revenue Agency served a demand to file for it, and a late-filing penalty under subsection 162(1) was assessed for any of the three tax years immediately before it.

Miss either one and the ordinary provision applies instead. A corporation with nine unfiled years and no demand ever served is on 162(1), not this one, however many years have gone by.

Both are percentages of the tax that was unpaid at the due date, which is the point most easily lost. Fifty per cent of nil is nil, so a corporation whose unfiled years were genuinely nil is not exposed to this figure at all, no matter how many demands arrive. Two conditions have to be met before this provision applies at all, and most corporations asking about it have met only one.

A Demand Is the Part Worth Acting On

Of the two conditions, only one is still in the corporation’s hands. A penalty assessed in an earlier year is history. A demand to file has a date on it, and filing by that date is what answers it. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the repeated failure to file penalty?

Subsection 162(2): ten per cent of the tax unpaid at the due date, plus two per cent of it for each complete month late, to twenty months.

What is the maximum?

Fifty per cent of the tax that was unpaid at the due date, reached at twenty complete months.

When does it apply instead of the ordinary penalty?

Only when a demand to file was served for the year and a late-filing penalty was assessed for one of the three preceding tax years.

I have filed late several years in a row. Does it apply?

Not on its own. Without a demand to file for the year in question, the ordinary five per cent provision applies however many years are late.

Does it apply to a nil return?

The percentage produces $0.00, because there is no unpaid tax. The unfiled year itself still has consequences.

How far back does the prior penalty have to be?

Within the three tax years immediately preceding the year in question.

Does a part month count?

No. Only complete months are counted, on the monthly anniversary of the due date.

What is a demand to file?

A written requirement under subsection 150(2) to file a return, with a date by which it must be filed.

Can both penalties apply to one year?

No. One provision or the other applies to a given tax year, not both.

Can it be cancelled?

It can be the subject of a taxpayer relief request on specified grounds, within the ten-year window that applies to those requests.
