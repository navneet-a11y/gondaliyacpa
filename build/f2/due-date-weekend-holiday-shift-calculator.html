A Due Date on a Closed Day Moves Forward

Where a filing date falls on a Saturday, a Sunday or a statutory holiday, it is met on the next business day. The calculator shows every day skipped and names what closed it, which matters most around Christmas and Boxing Day, where two holidays and a weekend can sit together.

A June 30, 2028 year-end is due on December 31, 2028, which is a Sunday, and the next day is New Year’s Day — so a year-end in one calendar year has a filing date in a different one. The Christmas row is the longest shift the engine produces: a Saturday followed by a Sunday, Christmas Day and Boxing Day, four closed days in a row.

The shift moves the filing date only. It does not add time to the six months, and it does not change the year being filed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Shift Ever Move a Date Backwards?

No. A date on a closed day is met on the next business day, never an earlier one.

What Happens When Christmas Falls on a Friday?

December 25 closes the Friday, December 26 and the weekend follow, and the date lands on the Monday. Put the date in and the tool lists each day it skipped.

Are These Federal or Provincial Holidays?

They are the Ontario statutory holidays, which is what this firm files against. A corporation elsewhere may see a different local list, and the date the Canada Revenue Agency accepts is the one that matters.

Does the Shift Change How Many Days Late a Return Is?

It changes the date the return was due, so it changes the count from it. For a non-resident-owned corporation that count carries a charge, which the non-resident T2 penalty calculator works out.

Does Filing Early Ever Hurt?

No. The shift only matters at the end of the window. A nil return can be transmitted as soon as the tax year has ended.
