What Schedule 1 Sits Between

The financial statements and the tax return disagree, on purpose. The statements are prepared to an accounting standard and the return is prepared to the Income Tax Act, and the two treat several ordinary items differently. Schedule 1 is where that disagreement is written down and resolved.

It starts at the net income or loss on line 9999 of Schedule 125, adds back what the statements deducted and the Act does not allow, deducts what the Act allows and the statements did not, and ends at net income for income tax purposes on line 300 of the T2. Everything the return then computes runs from line 300, not from the accounting figure.

The Two Items That Account for Most of It

Amortisation is the largest reconciling item on most returns and the easiest to describe. The statements write an asset down on whatever basis the accountant considers reasonable. The Act ignores that figure entirely and substitutes capital cost allowance, computed by class at a prescribed rate on Schedule 8. So the accounting figure comes out and the tax figure goes in, and the two are rarely the same.

Meals and entertainment is the one most often missed. The statements carry the whole cost as an expense; the Act allows half of it. The other half is added back on Schedule 1 every year, which on a modest expense account is a small number that nonetheless has to be there.

Where no asset is owned there is no amortisation and no capital cost allowance, and the Schedule 8 CCA requirement checker settles whether the schedule is needed at all. The Schedule 125 checker and the Schedule 141 checker cover the two statements this one reconciles, and the guide to the GIFI schedules covers the set.

Schedule 1 is a bridge rather than a second income statement: it starts at the accounting figure and ends at the one the return taxes.

A Nil Year Has Nothing to Reconcile

On a year with no revenue and no expenses the accounting figure is $0.00, there is no amortisation to add back and no capital cost allowance to deduct, and line 300 is $0.00 as well. The reconciliation has nothing to do, which is the honest description of the schedule on a dormant corporation rather than a reason to leave it out of the thinking.

A year with expenses and no revenue is different, and it is the one worth being careful about. The statements show a loss, the loss may or may not be deductible, and line 300 is then a negative figure that carries forward. The expenses only year checker covers whether such a year is nil at all, and the required schedules checker lists what the return then carries.

Nil T2 filing at $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year includes the GIFI schedules where they are required, Schedule 50 and Schedule 24 on a first return. The guide to T2 schedules on a nil return sets out the whole set, and the nil GIFI balance sheet checker covers the balance sheet side.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Schedule 1 for?

It reconciles the net income on the financial statements to net income for income tax purposes, which is line 300 of the T2.

Where does the starting figure come from?

Line 9999 of Schedule 125, the income statement information.

Why is amortisation added back?

Because the Act does not allow it. Capital cost allowance, computed by class on Schedule 8, is deducted in its place.

How much of meals and entertainment is added back?

Half. The statements carry the full cost and the Act allows fifty per cent of it.

Are club dues deductible?

No, so they are added back on Schedule 1 along with the other amounts the Act disallows.

What does Schedule 1 look like on a nil year?

It carries $0.00 through. There is no accounting income and nothing to reconcile, so line 300 is $0.00.

Can line 300 be negative?

Yes. A year with expenses and no revenue produces a loss for tax purposes, which carries forward rather than disappearing.

Is Schedule 1 the same as Schedule 125?

No. Schedule 125 is the income statement itself. Schedule 1 is the reconciliation that starts where Schedule 125 ends.

Does the nil T2 fee include it?

Yes, along with the GIFI schedules where they are required, Schedule 50 and Schedule 24 on a first return.

What if the accounting and tax figures are the same?

Then the schedule carries the figure through unchanged, which is the ordinary case on a corporation with nothing to reconcile.
