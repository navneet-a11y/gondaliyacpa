The Window Hangs Off the Year End, Which Is Why It Varies

Subsection 15(2) includes the amount of a loan from a corporation to a shareholder in income. Subsection 15(2.6) takes it back out where the loan is repaid within one year after the end of the corporation’s taxation year in which the loan was made. The deadline therefore hangs off the year end rather than off the date of the loan, which is what makes the available time vary so widely.

A loan taken the day after a December 31 year end has almost two years to run. A loan taken on December 30 has one year and a day. Same provision, same corporation, and the second one is the shape that goes wrong.

The Amount Lands on a Personal Return, in an Earlier Year

Two things about the inclusion are easy to get backwards, and getting either backwards changes the advice entirely.

The first is whose income it is. The amount is included in the shareholder’s income under subsection 15(2). It is not corporate income and it does not appear on the T2. A corporation that earned nothing, claimed nothing and lent its shareholder money that was never repaid still has a nil tax year, and the problem sits entirely on the personal side.

The second is which year. The inclusion is for the year the loan was received, not the year the deadline passed. That is very often a personal return filed and assessed long ago, which is why the practical consequence is a reassessment of an earlier year rather than a line on the next one. A later repayment does not undo it: paragraph 20(1)(j) gives a deduction in the year of repayment, which only helps if there is income in that year to apply it against.

The amount goes in the shareholder’s income for the year the loan was received, which is why the consequence usually lands on a personal return that was filed long ago.

The Exceptions, and the Benefit That Survives Them

Three routes keep an amount out of subsection 15(2). Repayment inside the 15(2.6) window is the common one, provided the repayment is not part of a series of loans and repayments, because paying a balance down at a year end and drawing it again afterwards does not satisfy the subsection. Subsection 15(2.3) covers a loan made in the ordinary course of a lending business. Subsection 15(2.4) covers certain loans to an employee, including one to acquire a dwelling or shares of the employer, where bona fide repayment arrangements were made when the loan was granted.

A separate charge survives all three. Where a loan carries no interest, or interest below the prescribed rate, subsection 80.4(2) brings an interest benefit into income for the period the loan is outstanding. The prescribed rate is set quarterly, so the amount is not computed on this page; the point is that repaying inside the deadline answers subsection 15(2) and does not answer subsection 80.4(2).

On the corporate side the balance is a reportable transaction with a shareholder, which the Schedule 11 shareholder transactions checker covers, and the shareholder may need naming on Schedule 50, which the Schedule 50 shareholder threshold checker settles. Frequently Asked Questions

How much of a shareholder loan is included in income?

The whole amount of the loan or indebtedness, where no exception applies and it is not repaid within the subsection 15(2.6) window.

Whose income is it included in?

The shareholder’s. It is not corporate income and it does not appear on the T2.

Which tax year does it land in?

The year the loan was received, not the year the repayment deadline passed. That often means an earlier personal return being reassessed.

What is the repayment deadline?

One year after the end of the corporation’s taxation year in which the loan was made.

Does repaying it later fix the inclusion?

It does not reverse the inclusion. Paragraph 20(1)(j) gives a deduction in the year of repayment instead.

Can the corporation still file a nil return?

Yes. A corporation that earned nothing and claimed nothing has a nil tax year whatever happened to the loan on the shareholder’s side.

What if the loan is repaid and drawn again?

A repayment that is part of a series of loans and repayments does not satisfy subsection 15(2.6), so the amount stays within subsection 15(2).

Are there exceptions to the inclusion?

Yes. Subsection 15(2.3) for a loan made in the ordinary course of a lending business, and subsection 15(2.4) for certain employee loans with bona fide repayment arrangements.

Is there interest on top of the inclusion?

A separate benefit can arise under subsection 80.4(2) where the loan carries no interest or interest below the prescribed rate. The rate is set quarterly and is not computed here.

Does the loan have to be reported on the T2?

The balance is a transaction with a shareholder and is reportable on the corporate return, which is a different thing from being corporate income.
