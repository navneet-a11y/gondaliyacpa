The Ten-Year Window Is the First Test

Subsection 220(3.1) of the Income Tax Act lets the Minister waive or cancel a penalty or interest. The discretion is bounded: a request may only reach a tax year that ended within the ten calendar years before the calendar year in which the request is made. A request made during 2026 therefore reaches tax years ending in 2016 or later.

The window moves every January, which cuts both ways: waiting a year to ask does not improve a request and can put the oldest year it covers permanently out of reach.

That last row is where most corporate enquiries about relief actually land. A true nil year carried no percentage penalty and no interest, so a request has nothing to cancel, and the thing that closes the year is the return. The Grounds, and the Order of Operations

Three groups of circumstances are recognised: extraordinary circumstances outside the taxpayer’s control, such as a natural disaster, a serious illness or a death; actions of the Canada Revenue Agency itself, such as processing delays or incorrect information; and an inability to pay or financial hardship. Form RC4288 is the request, and it is decided case by case.

Outstanding returns are filed before the request or with it. Asking for charges to be cancelled on a year that has not been filed leaves the year open, so nil T2 filing at $300.00 plus applicable sales tax per tax year or the catch-up filing schedule generator comes first. A correction made before the Agency acts is a different route again, and the voluntary disclosure eligibility checker covers it. Frequently Asked Questions

What is taxpayer relief?

A discretionary cancellation or waiver of penalties and interest by the Minister under subsection 220(3.1) of the Income Tax Act.

How far back can a request go?

To a tax year that ended within the ten calendar years before the calendar year the request is made in.

Can relief cancel the tax owing?

No. It reaches penalties and interest only. The tax itself stands.

What grounds are recognised?

Extraordinary circumstances outside your control, actions of the Canada Revenue Agency, and an inability to pay or financial hardship.

Which form is used?

Form RC4288, the request for taxpayer relief.

Is relief automatic once a ground applies?

No. It is discretionary and each request is decided on its own facts.

Does a nil year qualify for relief?

There is nothing to relieve. A true nil year carries no percentage penalty and no interest, so filing the return is what resolves it.

Do the returns have to be filed first?

They are normally filed before the request or with it. An unfiled year stays open regardless of the request.

Is this the same as a voluntary disclosure?

No. A disclosure corrects a position before the Agency acts; relief asks for charges already assessed to be cancelled.

Does the window move?

Yes, every calendar year, so the oldest year a request can reach moves forward each January.
