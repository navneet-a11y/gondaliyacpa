Two Authorities That Do Not Talk to Each Other

Closing a corporation involves two authorities that do not talk to each other on your behalf. The tax side is settled with the Canada Revenue Agency; the legal existence of the corporation is ended by the registry it was incorporated under.

Doing one and not the other is the usual failure. A corporation struck from a registry with open tax years still has those years open, and a corporation fully filed but never dissolved keeps acquiring new filing obligations.

So the question is not really which is cheaper this year. It is whether the corporation is being kept for a reason, because a return falls due every year until it is formally dissolved, and nothing about being empty changes that.

Only One Side Totals, and That Is Deliberate

The tool that prices both routes is direct about why, and the same applies here:

  • A corporation files a T2 within six months of its fiscal year-end, every year, until it is formally dissolved. Holding an empty corporation is a decision to pay for a return annually for as long as it exists.
  • The returns are priced here, so the cost of staying open totals exactly.
  • Winding up involves a clearance certificate request and the registry articles of dissolution, and neither is covered by this fee.
  • A page that invented a total for the dissolution side would be more satisfying to read and less use to rely on.
  • Where years are already outstanding the choice narrows, because both routes start in the same place. A clearance request rests on a complete filing record, and dissolving a corporation that still owes returns leaves those years open behind it.
  • Filing for another five years ends nothing. At the end of it the corporation is still on the register and the same question arrives again, five years of fees later.
  • A completed wind-up ends the obligation, and that is worth more weight than the arithmetic.

Keeping an empty corporation is often the right decision. There are good reasons to hold a name, a history or a registration, and this page is not an argument for either route. It is worth seeing as a subscription rather than as a formality, and then deciding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to dissolve or to keep filing?

Only one side of that totals. Keeping it is $300.00 plus HST per tax year for as long as it exists, plus the registry annual return. Winding up is one final return plus a clearance request and the registry dissolution, and those two are not covered by this fee.

Why will you not give me a total for dissolving?

Because a clearance certificate request and the registry articles of dissolution are not priced here. A page that invented a total for them would be more satisfying to read and less use to rely on.

How long do I have to keep filing if I do nothing?

Every year, within six months of the fiscal year-end, until the corporation is formally dissolved. Being empty changes nothing.

Does filing for a few more years settle anything?

No. At the end of it the corporation is still on the register and the same question arrives again, with the fees for those years spent.

Can I dissolve without filing the outstanding years?

Not cleanly. A clearance certificate cannot be issued while a year is unfiled, and dissolving anyway leaves those tax years open behind the corporation.

So both routes start the same way?

Yes. Every outstanding year is filed first, oldest first. The decision comes after that rather than instead of it.

Is a clearance certificate compulsory?

It is not a filing requirement. Distributing the corporation’s property without one can leave the directors personally liable for amounts the corporation turns out to owe.

What is the final T2?

The return covering the period from the start of the last tax year to the date of dissolution, due six months after that date and marked as the final return.

Why does the final return come after the registry filing?

Because it runs to the date of dissolution, and that date does not exist until the registry sets it.

Can the corporation still own things when it is dissolved?

No. A corporation cannot be dissolved holding property, and a disposition is activity, so that final year is not nil. It is handled at gondaliyacpa.ca at $350.00 plus HST per year.

What happens to the GST/HST and payroll accounts?

Each is closed with Form RC145 after the final T2. Until then nil returns and nil remittances remain due on them.

Is keeping an empty corporation ever the right answer?

Often. There are good reasons to hold a name, a history or a registration. The point is to see the annual return as a subscription rather than a formality.

Does dissolution close the file for good?

No. An assessment, a reassessment or a demand relating to a pre-dissolution period can still be issued, and a return for such a year can still be filed.

My corporation was struck off by the registry. Is that a wind-up?

No. It was dissolved involuntarily and its tax years are still open. Registries provide for revival so the outstanding filings can be made and a proper wind-up completed.
