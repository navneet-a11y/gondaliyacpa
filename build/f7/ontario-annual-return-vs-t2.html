Two Filings, Both Called a Return

Two different things are called a return, and confusing them is the most common compliance gap a dormant corporation has. One is a tax return and one is a registry filing, and they go to different bodies on different dates for different reasons.

Until 2021 the Ontario annual return was filed alongside the T2, so one act covered both and nobody had to know the difference. Since October 2021 it is filed through the Ontario Business Registry instead, and a corporation that has not looked at the rules since then is probably not filing it.

That is the whole problem in one sentence. Because the filing moved out of the T2, nothing in the corporate tax return now prompts an owner to do it. The prompt has to come from the owner or the adviser.

The Two Trails Are Independent

Being current on one says nothing at all about the other:

  • Filing the T2 does not satisfy an annual return, and filing an annual return does not satisfy the T2. A corporation needs both, every year, for as long as it exists.
  • A corporation can be fully current on its T2 filings and still be struck from its registry.
  • It can be in good standing at the registry while owing ten years of T2 returns.
  • An annual return is not a tax filing, so having no income does not excuse it. A dormant corporation confirms its directors and its registered office every year exactly as an active one does.
  • A missed annual return does not create tax. It creates a registry record that is out of date, and after enough missed years a corporation the registry treats as abandoned.
  • Where annual returns go unfiled for long enough, the registry may dissolve or strike the corporation. That is not dissolution for tax purposes, and it cancels no outstanding T2 return.

A corporation struck from a registry with unfiled tax years is in the worst of both positions: it cannot trade and it still owes returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ontario annual return the same as the T2?

No. The T2 is a tax return filed with the Canada Revenue Agency. The annual return is a registry filing that confirms the registered office address, the directors and the officers.

Does filing my T2 cover the annual return?

No, and the reverse is also true. Filing an annual return does not satisfy the T2. A corporation needs both, every year, for as long as it exists.

Do you file the Ontario annual return for me?

No. We file nil T2 returns only. The annual return is filed through the Ontario Business Registry.

Is it included in the $300.00 plus HST per tax year fee?

No. Registry annual returns are separate filings. The fee covers the federal nil T2 corporate tax return.

What does the Ontario annual return cost?

It carries no tax and no payment. What it costs is the filing itself, through the Ontario Business Registry.

Did it used to be filed with the T2?

Yes. Until 2021 it was filed alongside the T2. Since October 2021 it goes through the Ontario Business Registry instead, which is why many owners believe it still travels with the tax return.

Why did nobody tell me it changed?

Because the filing moved out of the T2, nothing in the corporate tax return now prompts an owner to do it. The prompt has to come from the owner or the adviser.

Does a dormant corporation have to file an annual return?

Yes. An annual return is not a tax filing, so having no income does not excuse it. A dormant corporation confirms its directors and registered office exactly as an active one does.

What happens if I miss an annual return?

It does not create tax. It creates a registry record that is out of date, and after enough missed years a corporation the registry treats as abandoned.

Can I be struck from the registry while my taxes are current?

Yes. The two trails are independent. A corporation can be fully current on its T2 filings and still be struck from its registry.

Can it work the other way round?

Yes. A corporation can be in good standing at the registry while owing ten years of T2 returns.

If the registry strikes my corporation, do the T2 returns go away?

No. Being struck is not dissolution for tax purposes and it cancels no outstanding T2 return.

When is each one due?

The T2 is due six months after the fiscal year-end. The Ontario annual return goes at the tax year-end, through the Ontario Business Registry.

What about Alberta and Quebec?

Those are a different thing again. Alberta administers its own corporate income tax through the AT1 and Quebec through the CO-17, each a separate provincial return rather than an annual return.
