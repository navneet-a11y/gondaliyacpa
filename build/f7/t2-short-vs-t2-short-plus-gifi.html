The Schedules Follow the Books, Not the Form

Qualifying for the T2 Short does not remove a schedule that is otherwise required. The eight conditions decide which form the return goes on; what the corporation holds decides which schedules that form carries. They are two separate questions and a yes to one says nothing about the other.

That is why a return can be on the short form and still carry all three GIFI schedules, which is the ordinary case rather than the exception. Omitting them is possible, but only for a corporation with nothing at all to report, and that is a narrower case than it sounds.

The Five Triggers

Any one of these answered yes makes all three GIFI schedules required for the year:

  • Share capital issued at incorporation or since.
  • Any cash or bank balance held during the year.
  • A shareholder loan outstanding, owed either way.
  • An incorporation cost carried on the books.
  • Any bank charge or other expense in the year.

A corporation that was inactive all year and has nothing at all to report may omit the three schedules. Nothing at all means no assets, no liabilities, no equity balance, no revenue and no expenses. Share capital of any amount does not meet it, and neither does a bank account that merely sat there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does omitting the GIFI schedules make the return cheaper?

No. The fee is $300.00 plus HST per tax year whether the three schedules attach or not. They are inside the fee, not an extra on top of it.

Can I choose to leave the schedules off?

It is not a choice. Any one of the five triggers answered yes makes all three required, and we check them before the return is prepared.

Does qualifying for the T2 Short remove the schedules?

No. The eight conditions decide which form the return goes on. What the corporation holds decides which schedules that form carries. The two questions are separate.

My corporation has share capital and nothing else. Does that count?

Yes. Share capital of any amount is the first of the five triggers, so all three schedules are required.

The bank account never moved. Does it still count?

Yes. A cash or bank balance held during the year is a trigger whether or not anything went through it. A balance sheet does not become nil because the year was nil.

What does Schedule 100 report?

The balance sheet: what the corporation owns and owes at the fiscal year-end. On a dormant corporation that is often a single line of share capital and a matching line of cash or a shareholder loan.

What do Schedules 125 and 141 report?

Schedule 125 is the income statement and Schedule 141 is the notes checklist. They are filed alongside Schedule 100 where any trigger applies.

Is it really all three, or can one be left off?

All three. Any one trigger answered yes makes the set required, and they are filed together.

How common is it to omit them?

Uncommon. It needs a corporation with no assets, no liabilities, no equity balance, no revenue and no expenses, which is narrower than most owners expect.

Does a shareholder loan make them required?

Yes. A shareholder loan outstanding, owed in either direction, is one of the five triggers.

Do bank charges count as expenses?

Yes. Any bank charge or other expense in the year is a trigger on its own, even where nothing else moved.

Does this change from year to year?

It can. The triggers are tested against each tax year on its own facts, so one year may need the schedules and another may not.

Does the deadline change if the schedules attach?

No. The return is due six months after the fiscal year-end either way.

Is Form T183CORP different?

No. An authorized signing officer signs Form T183CORP before the return is transmitted, with or without the three schedules.
