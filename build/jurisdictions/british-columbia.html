Nil T2 filing in British Columbia is the annual T2 return a corporation with no activity in the tax year still files with the Canada Revenue Agency. Every British Columbia corporation must file it within six months of its fiscal year-end, every year until it is dissolved. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil.

British Columbia Filing Note

British Columbia corporate income tax is reported on the federal T2 return, so there is no separate British Columbia corporate tax return. Alberta and Quebec are the two provinces that do administer their own, through the AT1 and the CO-17.

The British Columbia annual report is a different filing. It is a registry filing that confirms the company’s directors and registered office, it carries no tax, and it is not covered by the $300.00 fee. It is due within two months after each anniversary of the date of incorporation.

Nil T2 Filing in British Columbia: Frequently Asked Questions

Does a British Columbia corporation file a separate provincial corporate tax return?

No. British Columbia corporate income tax is reported on the federal T2 return filed with the Canada Revenue Agency, so a nil T2 covers both.

Is the British Columbia annual report covered by the nil T2 fee?

No. It is filed with BC Registries and Online Services, within two months after each anniversary of the date of incorporation. It is not covered by the $300.00 fee, and we tell you if you still owe it.

What happens if the British Columbia annual report is not filed?

The company stops being in good standing, so the registry will not issue a certificate of good standing for it. After two consecutive years of non-filing the registrar may dissolve the company. None of that cancels an outstanding T2: the corporate tax account and the registry record are separate trails.

What if my British Columbia corporation is not nil?

This service is for a corporation with no activity in the tax year. A corporation with investment income, rental income, or dividends paid or received is not nil. Other cases that are not nil include a corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation, an amalgamation, or a corporation already dissolved or struck.
