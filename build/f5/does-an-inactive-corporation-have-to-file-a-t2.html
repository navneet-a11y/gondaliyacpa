Nil T2 filing in Canada › Does an Inactive Corporation Have to File a T2?

Yes. Every resident corporation, including an inactive one, must file a T2 return within six months of its fiscal year-end, every year, until it is formally dissolved. A final T2 is due within six months of dissolution. Having no revenue, no bank activity and no employees does not remove the obligation.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Inactive is a description of what the corporation did, not a status the Canada Revenue Agency recognises. There is no form that pauses the T2 while a corporation sits idle, and no income level below which the year can be skipped. The duty comes from being an incorporated resident corporation, and it runs for every year the corporation is on the register.

The Exceptions Are Narrow

Three kinds of corporation are outside the rule: a tax-exempt Crown corporation, a Hutterite colony, and a registered charity. Everything else files, including a non-profit organisation and a corporation that has never traded.

An ordinary numbered company that stopped trading is not in any of those three categories, so it keeps filing. So does a shelf corporation that has never opened a bank account, and a holding corporation whose only asset is a single share it has held for years.

Inactive, Dormant and Nil Are Not the Same Word

Inactive and dormant describe the corporation. Nil describes one tax year of it, and it is decided year by year: a corporation can be nil for three years, earn something in the fourth, and be nil again in the fifth.

The distinction matters because a year that looks quiet is not always nil. Investment income, rental income, and a dividend paid or received each break it, even where nothing else moved and the bank statement is empty.

Which Form the Return Goes On

A corporation that meets all eight of the Canada Revenue Agency’s conditions for the short return may file the two-page T2 Short instead of the full T2. Most quiet years meet them, but the test is run against each year on its own.

That is a separate question from whether the year is nil at all, which is set out in nil T2 filing vs T2 Short filing. The T2 Short eligibility checker runs all eight conditions in one screen.

How the Obligation Ends

Only formal dissolution stops it. Letting a corporation lapse, closing its bank account or simply not filing does none of that: the corporation stays on the register and each year it does not file stays open.

Dissolution needs the earlier years filed first, because an unfiled year blocks the clearance certificate, and the clearance certificate is what confirms nothing is owed before the corporation closes. The last tax year ends on the dissolution date and its final T2 is due six months after it.
