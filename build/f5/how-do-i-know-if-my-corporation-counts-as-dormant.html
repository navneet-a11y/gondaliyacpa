Nil T2 filing in Canada › How Do I Know if My Corporation Counts as Dormant?

A corporation is treated as dormant for a tax year when it carried on no business, earned nothing and paid nothing out during that year. Investment income, rental income, and dividends paid or received all break it. A dormant corporation still files a T2 for every year until it is formally dissolved.

A true nil return carries no percentage late-filing penalty, because that penalty is a percentage of tax owing and the corporation owes $0.00. The unfiled year still stays open forever, invites a demand to file and an arbitrary assessment, blocks a clearance certificate and dissolution, and costs a non-resident-owned corporation $25.00 per day up to $2,500.00.

Dormant is decided year by year, not once for the corporation's life. A corporation can be nil for several years, operate for one, and be nil again, and each year is judged on what happened inside it.
