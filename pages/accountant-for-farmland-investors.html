Accounting That Understands Owning Land You Do Not Farm

Four things decide a farmland investor's tax position, and a preparer who treats you as a farmer will get at least two of them wrong. At Gondaliya CPA we set them out plainly and say which questions belong to a lawyer rather than to us.

💵

Rent Is Usually Property Income

Cash rent generally goes on Form T776 as property income, not Form T2042 as farming income. That changes your deductions and your eventual exemption position.

🌍

Land Is Not Depreciable

No capital cost allowance on the land itself. Tile drainage, fencing and buildings are depreciable, so the purchase price split matters from the day you close.

⚠

Restricted Farm Losses

Where farming is not your chief source of income, ITA section 31 restricts the loss rather than letting it offset your salary. The excess carries forward.

🔑

The Exit Is the Whole Game

Qualified farm or fishing property, the ITA section 110.6 exemption, and the rollover to a child under subsections 73(3) and 70(9). Planned years ahead, not at closing.

Stay Compliant and Keep the Exemption Available

On a farmland file the risk is rarely this year's return. It is a characterisation or a records gap that sits quietly for fifteen years and then costs real money at the one moment you cannot fix it — the sale.

Three Lines We Draw, and Why

First, we do not tell you whether any parcel qualifies as farm property on a web page. Eligibility under ITA section 110.6 turns on ownership period and a "used principally in the business of farming" test applied to that specific parcel and that specific history. We reach it on your facts. Second, we give no legal advice on severance, zoning, the Planning Act or a developer's option agreement — those belong to a real estate lawyer, and the tax consequence follows the legal structure rather than the reverse. Third, we do not advise on whether to buy or sell farmland. We can tell you the tax cost of a decision with precision; whether the investment is wise is not an accountant's call.

✅

Your CRA Obligations as a Landowner

We manage your T1 or T2 reporting rental income from the land, the Form T776 statement with the correct expense set for property income, capital cost allowance on the depreciable improvements only, instalments where nothing is withheld at source, the GST/HST position on rent and on an eventual sale, partnership reporting where the land is co-owned, Form T1135 where you hold foreign farmland and total cost of specified foreign property exceeds $100,000, and the six years of records ITA section 230 requires. Where you are a non-resident, rent attracts 25% Part XIII withholding under ITA section 212 remitted with an NR4, unless a section 216 return is filed to be taxed on the net instead.

📈

The Two Records We Ask You to Keep

Only two, and both are nearly impossible to reconstruct later. The first is the purchase price allocation between bare land and depreciable improvements, settled at acquisition with whatever appraisal, assessment or agreement evidence exists. Nobody can rebuild it credibly a decade on, and it drives both your annual deduction and your gain on sale. The second is a running file of what you spent on the land and why — the tile you installed, the fence you replaced, the drainage you paid for — split between capital improvements that add to your cost base and repairs you deducted. That file is the difference between a defended cost base and an estimate CRA is free to reject.

Personal and Corporate Tax Filing

  • We keep the six years of records ITA section 230 requires, and on a land file we keep the acquisition documents indefinitely because the cost base question outlives any retention rule.
2

Cash Rent and Crop Share Reporting

  • We read the actual lease before deciding anything, because the characterisation of your income turns on what the agreement makes you responsible for rather than on what the arrangement is called.
  • Straight cash rent, where the tenant takes the crop risk and pays you a fixed amount, is generally property income reported on Form T776 and is not farming income no matter how agricultural it feels.
  • A crop share can land on either side. Where you genuinely bear production risk and share in inputs, it can be farming income on Form T2042; where you simply take a slice of proceeds, usually not.
  • The distinction is not academic. It reaches which expenses you may deduct, whether anything is active business income, and whether the land is being used in a farming business for exemption purposes.
  • We document the conclusion and the reasoning at the time, so a CRA review years later is answered with a contemporaneous analysis rather than a reconstruction built after the question arrives.
3

Purchase Price Allocation

  • We split the purchase price between bare land and depreciable improvements at acquisition, using the appraisal, the municipal assessment breakdown and the agreement of purchase and sale as evidence.
  • This is the single most valuable hour on a new farmland file, because land attracts no capital cost allowance at all while tile drainage, fencing and buildings do, each in its own class.
  • Nobody can rebuild the allocation credibly ten years later. An investor who never did it either claims nothing on real depreciable assets or claims something they cannot support on review.
  • We separate capital improvements that add to your cost base from repairs that are deducted in the year, and we keep that split as a running file rather than a year-end scramble.
  • On inherited land we establish the cost base from the deceased's final return and the valuation used there, which is frequently the only figure that stands between you and an arbitrary assessment.
4

Restricted Farm Loss Review

  • Where you farm part of the land yourself alongside a job or a business, ITA section 31 can restrict the farm loss instead of letting it reduce your other income, and the surprise is always unwelcome.
  • The test is whether farming is your chief source of income, judged on the whole picture rather than on a single year's numbers, and an investor with a salary rarely satisfies it.
  • The restricted portion is not lost. It carries forward to be used against farming income in a later year, which matters if the operation is genuinely building toward something.
  • We test the position before the return is filed rather than after a reassessment, because the planning options are real while the year is open and almost nil once it has closed.
  • Where the activity is genuinely commercial we document the evidence for that, since the difference between a restricted loss and a denied loss is the quality of the file behind it.
5

Farm Property and Exemption Planning

  • We review whether the parcel is on track to be qualified farm or fishing property for the lifetime capital gains exemption in ITA section 110.6, which is the biggest single number on most of these files.
  • Eligibility turns on how long the property has been owned and on a "used principally in the business of farming" test, and both are applied to that specific parcel with its specific history.
  • We will not tell you on a web page that your land qualifies. We will tell you what the test looks at, what in your history helps or hurts, and what could still be changed while there is time.
  • Cash renting to a third party is exactly where this gets delicate, because the question becomes whose farming business the land is being used in and the answer depends on the period and the facts.
  • We state no dollar amount for the exemption anywhere on this page, deliberately, and the reason is set out openly in the FAQs below rather than buried or quietly left out.
6

Transfer to a Child and Succession

  • Farm property can transfer to a child at an elected amount under ITA subsection 73(3) during your lifetime, deferring the gain rather than triggering it, where the statutory conditions are met.
  • The same deferral is available on death under ITA subsection 70(9), which is why a will drafted without reference to the farm parcels can cost a family far more than the drafting ever saved.
  • The elected amount is a decision, not a default. Choosing it deliberately can use up an exemption now, shelter a gain later, or step the cost base up for the child, depending on the family's position.
  • We model the outcomes across the family rather than for one taxpayer, because a rollover that is optimal for you and punitive for your child is not a plan, it is a deferral of an argument.
  • We work alongside the lawyer drafting the will or the transfer, since the tax result follows the legal structure and an accountant designing that structure alone gets both halves wrong.
7

Corporate and Trust Ownership

  • We model whether a parcel belongs in a corporation, a family trust or your own name on your actual numbers, because the right answer differs sharply between a rented parcel and a development hold.
  • Rental income in a corporation is often not active business income, so the small business deduction under ITA section 125 and its $500,000 limit may not apply the way an owner expects.
  • We flag the serious cost of getting this wrong in the other direction: land already held personally cannot be moved into a company without triggering tax unless a rollover genuinely applies.
  • Where the land is co-owned we settle whether it is a partnership or a co-ownership, which determines whether a partnership information return is required and how each owner reports.
  • We keep the associated corporation picture current across the family, since families holding several parcels in several companies routinely share a single limit none of them realised they were sharing.
8

GST/HST on Rent and Sale

  • The sale of farmland is generally a taxable supply for GST/HST purposes, with specific exceptions, and that single sentence has derailed more farmland closings than any other tax point.
  • We check the registration and self-assessment position before a sale rather than after it, because the obligation lands at closing and the agreement rarely allocates it the way either party assumed.
  • Cash rent of farmland is generally taxable where the landlord is registered, so we settle whether you should be registered at all and what that means for the rent you charge your tenant.
  • Where you are registered we claim the input tax credits available on land improvements and professional fees, which on a drainage or fencing project is a meaningful recovery most owners miss.
  • We coordinate with the lawyer on the closing documents, because the certificate and the allocation in the agreement are legal instruments and the tax outcome depends on getting them right.
9

Non-Resident Landowner Filings

  • Rent paid to a non-resident owner attracts Part XIII withholding at 25% under ITA section 212, which the payer is required to withhold and remit, reported on an NR4 slip.
  • A section 216 return can be filed instead to be taxed on net rental income after expenses, which for a parcel with real costs frequently produces a materially better result than the flat withholding.
  • We prepare the undertaking and the return on the right timetable, because the election is time-sensitive and missing the window leaves the gross withholding as the final answer for that year.
  • Ontario farmland is taxable Canadian property, so a disposition by a non-resident brings a clearance certificate process that must be started before closing rather than after the money moves.
  • We coordinate with the purchaser's lawyer on the holdback, since a closing that ignores the certificate requirement leaves funds trapped and both sides exposed for longer than anyone wants.

Catch-Up Filing for Landowners

  • We reconstruct unfiled years from deposit records, lease agreements, tenant statements and municipal tax bills, rebuilding the six years of records ITA section 230 requires from source documents.
  • Late filing costs 5% of the balance owing plus 1% per month up to twelve months, so we file the oldest unfiled year first to stop the penalty compounding and limit the arrears interest CRA charges.
  • Unreported cash rent is the single most common finding on these files, usually because the owner treated an annual cheque from a neighbour as something other than income from property.

  • We bring the position forward on Form RC199 before CRA contacts you, confirming the disclosure is genuinely voluntary, which is the single condition that makes the application valid at all.

Guelph Investor — Ten Years of CCA on Land

The problem: A Guelph professional had bought a hundred-acre parcel and rented it to a neighbouring farmer for cash rent. Her previous preparer had treated the whole purchase price as a depreciable asset and claimed capital cost allowance on it for ten years, which is simply not available on land. There was no allocation between land and the tile drainage and fencing that genuinely were depreciable, and no record of the drainage project she had paid for in year three.

What we did: We rebuilt the allocation from the original appraisal and the municipal assessment breakdown, established the correct undepreciated balances for the improvements that actually qualified, and corrected the open years through T1 adjustment requests. We brought the years beyond that forward voluntarily and documented the drainage project as a capital improvement adding to her cost base.

The result:

  • Incorrect CCA on land corrected before CRA found it
  • Real depreciable improvements identified and claimed properly
  • Cost base documented, including the drainage project

Windsor Family — Inherited Land, No Cost Base

The problem: Three siblings inherited farmland near Windsor and rented it out. None of them farmed. What we did: We established the cost base from the valuation used on the deceased's final return, settled whether the three held a co-ownership or a partnership and filed accordingly, reported the rental income for the open years and disclosed the earlier ones on Form RC199. The result:

  • Cost base established from the estate's own filed valuation
  • Unreported rent disclosed voluntarily with penalties cancelled
  • Farm property position reviewed before any sale was negotiated

Ottawa Owner — A Side Operation and a Restricted Loss

The problem: An Ottawa software manager had bought acreage, rented most of it out and farmed a small section himself, running a loss on the farming portion every year. Farmland Investor Accounting & Tax FAQs

Is my farmland rent business income or property income?
Usually property income, and the distinction matters more than almost anything else on your return. Straight cash rent, where your tenant takes the crop risk and pays you a fixed amount per acre, is generally income from property reported on Form T776. It is not farming income on Form T2042, however agricultural the setting feels. A crop share is the genuinely arguable case: where you bear real production risk and share in the inputs, it can be farming income; where you simply take a percentage of the proceeds without exposure, usually not. Why it matters: the characterisation reaches which expenses you may deduct, whether any of it is active business income for a corporation, and — the big one — whether the land is being used in a farming business for the purposes of the farm property exemption. We read the actual lease before deciding, and we document the reasoning at the time rather than reconstructing it when CRA asks.
Can I claim capital cost allowance on farmland?
Not on the land. Land is not depreciable property, full stop, and claiming capital cost allowance on it is one of the most common errors we find on farmland files that arrive from a general preparer. What is depreciable is the improvements: tile drainage, fencing, farm buildings, and other structures, each in its own class with its own rate. This is exactly why the split of your purchase price between bare land and those improvements matters from the day you close. An investor who never did the allocation ends up in one of two bad places — claiming nothing on genuinely depreciable assets they paid real money for, or claiming something they cannot support when asked. The allocation should be built at acquisition from the appraisal, the municipal assessment breakdown and the agreement of purchase and sale. Nobody rebuilds it credibly ten years later, which is why we treat it as the first hour of work on a new file.
What is a restricted farm loss and will it hit me?
ITA section 31 restricts the deductibility of farm losses where farming is not your chief source of income. If you have a salary or a business and you farm a corner of your acreage at a loss, the rule is very likely in play, and the loss you expected to offset your employment income may be restricted instead. The restricted portion is not destroyed — it carries forward to be applied against farming income in a later year — but it is not available this year, which is usually the opposite of what the owner was counting on. The test looks at your whole picture rather than a single year's figures: where your time goes, where your capital is, where your income comes from. We test the position honestly before the return is filed, because once a year closes the planning options are close to nil, and we document the commercial evidence where the activity genuinely is building toward something.
Does my land qualify for the farm capital gains exemption?
We cannot answer that from a web page, and you should be wary of anyone who does. The lifetime capital gains exemption for qualified farm or fishing property under ITA section 110.6 is real and it is often the largest number on these files, but eligibility turns on how long the property has been owned and on a "used principally in the business of farming" test applied to that specific parcel and its specific history. Cash renting to a third party is precisely where it gets delicate, because the question becomes whose farming business the land is being used in during which periods. What we do is concrete: we tell you what the test examines, what in your ownership history helps or hurts, and what could still be changed while there is time to change it. The worst outcome on these files is discovering at closing that a position was lost years earlier through an arrangement nobody reviewed.

Because we would rather give you nothing than give you a number we are not certain is current. In reviewing this site we found conflicting figures for the lifetime capital gains exemption across published pages — one page carries one amount, roughly twenty others carry a different one. That conflict is unresolved and has been flagged internally. When you speak to us we will give you the current figure and the computation on your facts. Can I transfer farmland to my child without triggering tax?
Often yes, through a rollover, and the mechanism is worth understanding before a lawyer drafts anything. Farm property can transfer to a child at an elected amount under ITA subsection 73(3) during your lifetime and under subsection 70(9) on death, deferring the gain rather than realising it, where the statutory conditions are satisfied. Two things people miss. The first is that the elected amount is a decision rather than a default: choosing it deliberately can use an exemption now, shelter a gain later, or step up the cost base for your child, and the right choice depends on the whole family's position rather than yours alone. The second is that a will drafted without reference to the farm parcels can cost a family far more than the drafting ever saved. We model the outcomes across the family and work alongside the lawyer, because the tax result follows the legal structure and an accountant designing that structure alone gets both halves wrong.
Do I pay GST/HST when I sell farmland?
Generally yes — the sale of farmland is normally a taxable supply for GST/HST purposes, with specific exceptions — and this single point has derailed more farmland closings than any other tax issue. The obligation lands at closing, and the agreement of purchase and sale very often does not allocate it the way either party assumed when they shook hands. We check the registration and self-assessment position before a sale is negotiated rather than after the agreement is signed, and we coordinate with the lawyer on the closing documents, because the certificate and the allocation in the agreement are legal instruments with real tax consequences. On the rent side, cash rent of farmland is generally taxable where you are registered, so we also settle whether you should be registered at all and what that means for what you charge your tenant. Where you are registered, there are input tax credits available on drainage, fencing and professional fees that most owners never claim.
I bought land hoping to sell it to a developer. Is that capital or income?
It depends on your intention at acquisition and on what you can prove about it, which is why this is a question to settle when you buy rather than when you sell. Land bought to hold and rent, generating income from property over years, is normally on capital account, so only part of the gain is taxed. Land bought with a view to severing, rezoning and reselling can be an adventure in the nature of trade, taxed as business income at full rates. The factors CRA weighs include your stated and actual intention, how long you held it, what you did to the land, how the purchase was financed and whether you have done similar transactions before. Nothing about this is decided by what you call it. We document the intention and the supporting evidence at the time of purchase, because an owner who produces a contemporaneous file is in a vastly stronger position than one reconstructing a story after a reassessment lands.
How is inherited farmland taxed when I eventually sell?
Your cost base generally comes from the deceased's final return, and establishing it is the first thing to do rather than the last. Where the land passed through an estate, the valuation used on that final return — or the elected amount if a rollover to a child applied — is normally your starting point, and that figure is frequently the only thing standing between you and an arbitrary assessment decades later. We have seen families sell inherited land with no idea what the cost base was, and the practical consequence is tax on a gain that was partly someone else's. Three things to find now, not later: the deceased's final return and the valuation behind it, any rollover election that was filed, and whether the parcel was qualified farm property in the deceased's hands. While you are collecting that, settle whether co-owning siblings hold a partnership or a co-ownership, because that determines whether a partnership information return is required and how each of you reports the rent.
Should I hold farmland in a corporation?
Sometimes, and the honest answer is that it depends on numbers we have not seen yet — but there are two traps worth knowing before anyone sells you a structure. The first is that rental income earned in a corporation is often not active business income, so the small business deduction under ITA section 125 and its $500,000 limit may not apply the way an owner assumes, and the corporate tax outcome on passive rent can be worse rather than better. The second is harder: land already held personally generally cannot be moved into a company without triggering tax unless a rollover genuinely applies, so this is a decision that is far cheaper before you buy than after. Where families hold several parcels in several companies, we also keep the associated corporation picture current, because they routinely share a limit none of them realised they were sharing. We model it on your actual figures and tell you plainly when the answer is to leave things as they are.
What if I rent out Canadian farmland as a non-resident?
Two routes, and the better one is usually not the default. Rent paid to a non-resident attracts Part XIII withholding at 25% under ITA section 212, which the payer is required to withhold and remit, reported on an NR4 slip. That is a flat charge on the gross rent with no credit for your expenses. The alternative is to file a section 216 return and be taxed on net rental income after expenses instead, which for a parcel with real property tax, drainage and professional costs frequently produces a materially better result. The election is time-sensitive, so the window matters: miss it and the gross withholding stands as the final answer for that year. Separately, Ontario farmland is taxable Canadian property, so a disposition by a non-resident brings a clearance certificate process that has to start before closing. We prepare the undertaking and the return on the right timetable and coordinate with the purchaser's lawyer on the holdback.
I have never reported the cash rent. What happens now?
You come forward, and it is usually far less painful than the worry suggests. Unreported cash rent is the single most common finding on these files, almost always because an owner treated an annual cheque from a neighbouring farmer as something other than income from property. Late filing costs 5% of the balance owing plus 1% per month to twelve months, so we file the oldest unfiled year first to stop the penalty compounding. For years beyond the normal reach we bring the position forward on Form RC199 before CRA contacts you — confirming the disclosure is genuinely voluntary is the single condition that makes the application valid — which cancels penalties in full and gives roughly 50% interest relief on the older years. One thing that surprises people: the corrected years often produce less tax than feared, because the property tax, the professional fees and the depreciable improvements were deductible throughout and had never been claimed against the rent.
