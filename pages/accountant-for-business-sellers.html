Corporate and Personal Tax Filing

  • We claim the small business deduction under ITA section 125 against the $500,000 limit where it remains available, which on an asset sale depends on how much of the year's income is still active business income.
  • We set instalments for the year of the gain, because a capital gain on a sale is not withheld at source and a seller who has never paid instalments now has the largest tax bill of their life.
  • We file on time so the late filing penalty never starts: 5% of the balance owing plus 1% for each complete month to twelve months, charged in the one year you can least afford it.
  • We keep the six years of records ITA section 230 requires, including the asset-use schedule behind the exemption claim, because the year of sale is the year most likely to be looked at.
2

Share Sale or Asset Sale, Quantified

  • This is the first conversation and the most valuable one. The vendor generally wants a share sale: a capital gain in your hands, one level of tax, and the exemption if the shares qualify.
  • The buyer generally wants an asset sale: a cost base stepped up to what they paid, the liabilities they choose rather than the ones you have, and none of the corporation's history.
  • On an asset sale the gain, the recapture and the income arise inside the corporation, so getting the money to you is a second step with its own tax. That second step is what sellers forget to price.
  • So we model both on your own figures and give you the after-tax number in each case, which is the only number that matters when you are comparing two offers.
  • And we do it before the letter of intent, because a structure agreed in principle is very hard to reopen later without giving something else away to get it.
3

Qualifying the Shares Under Section 110.6

  • The lifetime capital gains exemption applies to qualified small business corporation shares, and the conditions are specific. We test them rather than assume them, because most owners assume.
  • At the time of sale the corporation must be a small business corporation: substantially all of the fair market value of its assets used principally in an active business carried on primarily in Canada.
  • Throughout the 24 months before the sale, more than half of that asset value must have been so used, and the shares must not have been owned by anyone other than you or a related person.
  • That backward-looking test is why this work cannot wait. A corporation that passed two years ago and has been accumulating cash since may not pass today, and nothing at closing changes the history.
  • We will not tell you on a web page whether your shares qualify. We look at the balance sheets, we tell you plainly, and where they do not qualify we say what would have to change and over what period.
4

Purification and the Two-Year Runway

  • Most owner-managed corporations that fail the test fail it for the same reason: they are good businesses that kept their money. Cash, a portfolio, a rental unit, a second property.
  • So we identify what is not being used in the active business and what proportion of value it represents, which is the arithmetic the test actually runs on rather than a general impression.
  • Then we map a route: what can be moved, what it costs to move it, what has to be paid out and in which year, and how long the whole sequence takes before the shares would qualify.
  • The honest part of this conversation is the timing. A seller who wants to close in four months and needs two years of history cannot have both, and we say that rather than implying otherwise.
  • Where purification is not realistic we price the alternative properly, including the asset sale and the cost of the second step out of the corporation, so the decision is made on comparable after-tax numbers instead of on hope.
5

Section 84.1 and the Non-Arm's-Length Sale

  • If the purchaser is a corporation you or your family are connected to, ITA section 84.1 is in play, and it is the provision that most often surprises a seller who thought the hard part was agreeing a price.
  • It applies where an individual resident in Canada disposes of shares to another corporation with which they do not deal at arm's length, where the two corporations are connected afterwards.
  • Where it applies it can grind paid-up capital or deem a dividend to you instead of a capital gain, which removes both the capital treatment and the exemption in a single step.
  • This is the normal shape of a sale to a child, to a key employee who has incorporated, or to a partner buying you out through their own holding company, so it comes up far more often than people expect.
  • We review the structure against the provision before the papers are drawn, and where it reaches the transaction we say so plainly rather than leaving it for the buyer's accountant to find afterwards.
6

Restrictive Covenants and Purchase Price Allocation

  • What the agreement calls the money changes what the money is, and the clearest example is the non-compete. ITA section 56.4 governs amounts received for a restrictive covenant.
  • Subject to its exceptions and elections, such an amount is included in income rather than treated as proceeds of disposition, so a figure moved into that clause can be taxed very differently.
  • The same is true of a consulting or employment agreement signed at closing: amounts under it are ordinary income to you, and a buyer who wants deductions has a reason to prefer them.
  • On an asset sale the allocation across inventory, depreciable property, goodwill in Class 14.1 and receivables decides how much is recapture taxed in full and how much is a capital gain.
  • We read the allocation schedule before it is signed and tell you what each line costs you, which is a different question from whether the total is the number you agreed.
7

Earnouts, Holdbacks and the Capital Gains Reserve

  • Very few sales pay everything on closing. Part is held back, part depends on next year's results, and the tax treatment of each is not the same as the other.
  • Where part of the proceeds is not due until a later year, ITA subparagraph 40(1)(a)(iii) allows a capital gains reserve, so the gain is brought into income over a maximum of five tax years including the year of sale.
  • A longer reserve period applies to certain transfers of farm or fishing property and of small business corporation shares to a child, which matters on an intergenerational sale.
  • An earnout that depends on future performance and a holdback against a warranty claim are different arrangements, and we look at the actual terms rather than the label used in the draft.
  • Then we plan the cash: the tax on a gain can be due long before the final instalment of the price arrives, and that mismatch is the one that catches sellers out after closing.
8

GST/HST on the Sale and the Section 167 Election

  • A sale of shares is a supply of a financial instrument and is exempt, so no GST/HST arises on the price. That alone is a reason buyers and sellers reach different conclusions about structure.
  • An asset sale is generally a taxable supply, and on a business worth several million dollars the tax on it is not a rounding error even where the buyer would recover it.
  • ETA section 167 permits a joint election where a supplier sells a business or part of a business and the recipient acquires all or substantially all of the property reasonably necessary to carry it on.
  • The election is made on Form GST44 and filed with the recipient's return, and it has conditions: it does not cover a supply of real property to a recipient who is not a registrant, among others.
  • We check whether the election is available on your facts, make sure it is filed by the party who has to file it, and deal with closing the HST account afterwards rather than leaving it open and filing nil returns forever.
9

Statements, Schedules and the Data Room

  • A buyer's advisers will ask for years you had stopped thinking about, and the quality of what comes back sets the tone for everything that follows in the negotiation.
  • We prepare compilation engagement financial statements and the supporting schedules, and we reconcile them to the corporate returns already filed so that the statements and the returns tell the same story without needing a covering explanation.
  • Where the books have been kept for tax rather than for a reader, we rebuild them to a standard a third party can follow, which is a different job from keeping them compliant.
  • We also flag what diligence will find before it finds it: the long-term contractor who looks like an employee, the unremitted payroll balance, the shareholder loan nobody wrote down, the related-party rent nobody documented.
  • Each of those becomes a price adjustment, an indemnity or a holdback if the buyer raises it first, and usually costs a fraction of that to deal with beforehand.
10

Catch-Up Filing and Voluntary Disclosure

  • Overdue returns are the most common reason a deal slows down, and they are entirely fixable. We bring the corporate years, the HST returns and the missing slips current.
  • Where there is an exposure rather than simply a delay, the Voluntary Disclosures Program on Form RC199 is available before the CRA contacts you, and the timing of that is the whole point.
  • Once diligence has raised the issue the voluntary route is harder to argue, so the sequence matters: disclose, then open the data room, rather than the other way around.
  • We request relief from penalties and interest on Form RC4288 where the facts support it, and we deal with the balance so the buyer is not holding back against an unknown number.
  • And we say plainly what the exposure is rather than minimising it, because an indemnity you signed without knowing the number is worse than a holdback you negotiated knowing it.
11

Payroll, Slips and Employees Through Closing

  • A closing date rarely falls on a pay period boundary, so the final remittance, the accrued vacation and the bonus paid before the deal all need to land in the right year.
  • On an asset sale the employees are generally terminated by the vendor and hired by the purchaser, which means final pay, accrued entitlements and T4 slips for a year that ends mid-stream.
  • On a share sale the employer does not change, so payroll continues, and the question becomes which accruals sit in the price and which are settled before it.
  • Directors remain personally liable for unremitted source deductions under ITA section 227.1, so an arrears balance left behind on closing does not quietly stay behind with the corporation once you have handed over the shares.
  • We reconcile the payroll and source deduction accounts before diligence rather than after, because an unremitted balance is the single easiest thing for a buyer's adviser to find and the hardest to explain away.
12

CRA Audit Resolution After the Sale

  • Selling the business does not close the years you ran it. The normal reassessment periods keep running, and on a share sale the corporation carries its own history to its new owner.
  • That is exactly why the agreement has an indemnity, so a reassessment of an operating year usually comes back to you whether or not you still own anything.
  • When the exemption claim is reviewed, we answer with the asset-use schedule and the balance sheets kept at the time, which is worth considerably more than a reconstruction.
  • When the purchase price allocation or a restrictive covenant amount is questioned, we produce the allocation schedule and the correspondence behind the figures the parties actually agreed at the time they agreed them.
  • We file the Notice of Objection within 90 days of a reassessment, and we handle the correspondence so the buyer's advisers are not answering questions about your years for you.

Business Seller Accounting & Tax FAQs

Should I sell shares or assets?
It is the first question and it is answered with arithmetic, not a preference. A share sale puts a capital gain in your hands, one level of tax, and the lifetime capital gains exemption under ITA section 110.6 if the shares qualify. An asset sale is made by the corporation, so the gain, the recapture and the income arise inside it, and getting the money out to you is a second step with its own tax cost. Buyers prefer assets for the cost step-up and because they take the liabilities they choose rather than the ones you have. That is why the two offers are rarely the same number, and why we model both on your own figures before you agree a structure. The honest answer is that the right route depends on whether your shares qualify, what the corporation holds, and how much the buyer will pay to get the assets instead.
How much tax will I pay on selling my business?
We will not put a number or a rate on a web page, because the figure depends on four things we need to see: whether the sale is of shares or assets, whether the shares are qualified small business corporation shares, what the corporation holds that is not used in the business, and how the price is allocated in the agreement. What we can say is which of those you still have the ability to change. The structure is negotiable until the letter of intent. The allocation is negotiable until the agreement is signed. Qualification depends on the 24 months behind you and on what you do over the next two years. By closing week, almost all of it is fixed, which is why this conversation is worth having early rather than late.
Do my shares qualify for the lifetime capital gains exemption?
That depends on your own balance sheet, and we test it rather than assume it. The shares must be qualified small business corporation shares: at the time of sale the corporation must be a small business corporation, meaning substantially all of the fair market value of its assets is used principally in an active business carried on primarily in Canada; throughout the 24 months before the sale more than half of that asset value must have been so used; and in that period the shares must not have been owned by anyone other than you or a person or partnership related to you. Most owner-managed corporations that fail do so for an unsurprising reason: they were profitable and kept the money. Cash, a portfolio, a rental unit held in the company. We measure it properly and tell you where you stand.
What is the 24-month test, and why does it matter so much?
It is the part of the qualification conditions that looks backwards, and it is the reason a sale cannot be tax-planned in the final month. Through the whole of the 24 months before the disposition, more than 50% of the fair market value of the corporation's assets must have been used principally in an active business carried on primarily in Canada, and the shares must not have been held by an unrelated party in that period. Nothing you do at closing can change what the balance sheet looked like eighteen months ago. So if a sale is a possibility in the next few years, the useful time to look at this is now, while the history is still being written rather than after it is finished.
How do I purify the balance sheet, and how long does it take?
Purification means moving value that is not used in the active business out of the corporation, or into a form that does not break the test, so that the share conditions can be met. The route depends on what is there: surplus cash, a portfolio account, real property, a loan to a shareholder, an investment in an unrelated company. Each has a different cost and a different mechanism, and some of them trigger tax on the way out. The timing is the part sellers do not want to hear. Because the test looks back 24 months, a purification completed today does not immediately produce qualifying shares. We map the sequence, state the cost of each step and the period before the shares would qualify, and where the timetable does not work we price the asset sale honestly instead.
Can I sell to my own holding company, or to my child's corporation?
You can, and that is exactly where ITA section 84.1 applies. It operates where an individual resident in Canada disposes of shares of a Canadian corporation to another corporation with which the individual does not deal at arm's length, where the two corporations are connected after the sale. Where it applies it can grind paid-up capital or deem a dividend to you instead of a capital gain, which takes away both the capital treatment and the exemption at once. This is the normal shape of a sale to a child, to a key employee who has incorporated, or to a partner buying you out through their own company, so it arises far more often than people expect. We review the structure before the papers are drawn, because afterwards the options are narrower.
Is a non-compete payment taxable as income?
It can be, and that is the point to settle before signing. ITA section 56.4 governs amounts received for a restrictive covenant, and subject to its exceptions and elections such an amount is included in income rather than treated as proceeds of disposition of capital property. So consideration moved out of the share price and into a non-compete clause is not the same money arriving by another route: it can be taxed quite differently, and the exemption does not apply to it. The same logic reaches a consulting or employment agreement signed at closing, where amounts are ordinary income to you. A buyer may have perfectly good reasons to prefer that allocation. We quantify what it costs you so it is negotiated as a price point rather than accepted as drafting.
How is an earnout taxed, and can I spread the gain over several years?
Where part of the proceeds is not due until a later year, ITA subparagraph 40(1)(a)(iii) allows a capital gains reserve, so the gain can be brought into income over a maximum of five tax years including the year of sale. A longer period applies to certain transfers of farm or fishing property and of small business corporation shares to a child, which matters on an intergenerational sale. An earnout that depends on future performance and a holdback against a possible warranty claim are different arrangements with different treatment, so we read the actual terms rather than the label in the draft. The practical point is cash flow: tax on a gain can fall due well before the last instalment of the price arrives, and that mismatch is what catches sellers out after closing.
Do I charge HST on selling my business?
On a share sale, no: shares are a financial instrument and the supply is exempt. On an asset sale, generally yes, and on a business worth several million dollars that is not a small number even where the buyer would recover it. ETA section 167 permits a joint election where a supplier sells a business or part of a business and the recipient acquires ownership, possession or use of all or substantially all of the property reasonably necessary to carry it on. The election is made on Form GST44 and filed with the recipient's return. It has conditions and exclusions, including that it does not apply to a supply of real property to a recipient who is not a registrant. We check whether it is available on your facts and make sure the party who has to file it does.
What happens to the corporation after an asset sale?
It still exists, and it is holding the proceeds. That is the step sellers most often leave out of their arithmetic: the corporation has paid tax on the gain, the recapture and the income, and getting what remains into your hands is a separate transaction with its own tax consequences. Sometimes the answer is to pay it out, sometimes to keep it invested in the company, and sometimes to wind the corporation up once the final returns are filed. A dormant shell left behind still has filing obligations of its own. We price that second step as part of comparing the two routes, and if the plan is to close the company afterwards, our businesses winding down page covers that work.
What will the buyer's accountant ask for?
More than you expect, and about years you had stopped thinking about. Typically three to five years of financial statements and corporate returns, the HST and payroll account positions, the general ledger, major contracts, the share register and minute book, details of related-party transactions, and an explanation of anything in the statements that moves. What they are really looking for is a liability that should be priced: an unremitted payroll balance, a long-term contractor who looks like an employee, an undocumented shareholder loan, an unfiled return. Anything they find first becomes a price adjustment, an indemnity or a holdback. Nearly all of it costs a fraction as much to deal with before the data room opens, which is why we prepare the statements and clear the filings first.
What if I have unfiled corporate returns?
It is common and it is fixable, but the order matters. We bring the overdue corporate years, the HST returns and the missing slips current first. Where there is a real exposure rather than simply a delay, the Voluntary Disclosures Program on Form RC199 is available before the CRA contacts you, and relief from penalties and interest can be requested on Form RC4288 where the facts support it. The sequence is the useful part: disclose and file, then open the data room. Once the buyer's diligence has surfaced the issue, the voluntary route is harder to argue and the problem is now being negotiated against you. We also tell you what the exposure actually is, because an indemnity signed without knowing the number is worse than a holdback negotiated knowing it.
