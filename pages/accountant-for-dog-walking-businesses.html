A dog walking business keeps the simplest-looking books in the pet care block and, for exactly that reason, the ones most likely to be wrong. There is no premises, almost nothing on the asset schedule and no inventory to value, so the ledger collapses into one revenue line and a fuel card — and then two separate problems arrive at once. The first is the money that comes in before the walking is done. A block of ten or twenty walks sold up front is included in income under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a) and reserved out under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m) to the extent the visits have not yet been performed, so an owner who treats a sold package as earned revenue is paying tax this year on walks still owed next year. Cancellations and no-shows pull in the opposite direction, and whether a package is a gift certificate under ETA section 181.2 or a cancellation charge a forfeited deposit under ETA section 182 is reviewed on your own agreements rather than assumed. Bylaws, Boundaries and Real Authorities

Dog walking is not a licensed or regulated profession in Ontario — there is no provincial college and no protected title, and we will not suggest otherwise. What does apply is real. Municipal bylaws reach business licensing, how many animals one person may walk or have under control, leash and park rules and home-based operation, and they differ from one municipality to the next, so each is confirmed with the municipality rather than assumed here. The Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019, enforced by Animal Welfare Services, applies to anyone with custody of an animal. Practising veterinary medicine is restricted under the Veterinarians Act, regulated by the College of Veterinarians of Ontario: where that line falls for anything you are asked to do is a question for your own counsel and your veterinarian, never for us. Insurance and bonding are commercially decisive and the premiums are deductible; the terms are your broker's.

The problem: A Toronto operator ran four routes a day with three walkers and read one revenue line and one fuel line at year-end. The result:

  • $47,200 of annual travel cost made visible for the first time
  • 2 unprofitable routes consolidated, 1 discontinued
  • Route margin now reported monthly instead of guessed at

Oakville Multi-Walker Business — A Subcontract Line With Nothing Behind It

The problem: An Oakville business paid seven walkers as subcontractors for three years. No T4A slips had been filed, no written agreements existed, and nobody had ever worked through the Form RC4110 factors to document why the arrangement was characterized the way it was. The subcontract line was the largest expense on the income statement, and the owner could not answer a single question about who set the schedules, who held the client relationships or who supplied the leads and the vehicles.

What we did: We documented the Form RC4110 factors in writing for each engagement, filed the missing T4A slips on the walkers the analysis supported as contractors, quantified the back source deduction exposure so the owner could size it, and referred the characterization decision itself to the owner's employment counsel.

The result:

  • 7 engagements documented against the RC4110 factors
  • 3 years of missing T4A slips filed, penalties cancelled on disclosure
  • Exposure quantified before CRA raised it, not after

Ottawa Solo Walker — Twenty-Walk Blocks Taxed Before They Were Walked

The problem: An Ottawa walker sold twenty-walk blocks every autumn for the winter, took the whole amount into income on the day it was paid, and had never been registered for HST. The result:

  • $18,400 of package revenue reserved into the year it was walked

  • Penalties cancelled in full, roughly 50% interest relief obtained

Dog Walking Accounting & Tax FAQs

Should I incorporate my dog walking business?
Incorporating gives you limited liability, which matters in a trade where an injury to an animal, a person or a client's property is a live exposure, plus roughly a 12.2% Ontario combined rate on the first $500,000 of active income under ITA section 125 against a personal rate up to 53.53% when unincorporated. The decision turns on whether you earn more than you withdraw, because that surplus is what a corporation lets you defer. There is a second reason specific to this business: a book of recurring weekly visits is an asset a corporation can hold, finance and eventually sell in a way a sole proprietor cannot. When it makes sense, we handle the section 85 rollover on Form T2057.
Are the walkers I use employees or independent contractors?
That is a question of fact and we will not answer it for you from a web page. It is decided on the factors in Form RC4110 rather than on the invoice or on what the arrangement was called: who controls when and how the walking happens, who holds the client relationship, who supplies the leads, the vehicle and the phone, whether the walker can subcontract or refuse work, and whether the walker can genuinely profit or lose. Paying walkers as subcontractors is the norm in this trade, which is exactly why it is the largest exposure on the file. What we do is document the factors in writing for each engagement so you and your employment counsel can make the call with the facts in front of you, instead of discovering the question for the first time in a CRA payroll review.
What happens if CRA decides my contractor walkers were employees?
The consequence lands on your largest cost line, which is what makes this different from most classification arguments. A reassessment on that basis means the employer and employee portions of CPP and EI, income tax that should have been withheld, arrears interest and penalties, assessed across the years CRA can reopen. On a business where walker payments are most of the expense base, that is not a rounding error. CRA's graduated late-remittance penalty on source deductions reaches 10%, and a business that has never registered with WSIB or opened a payroll account has further exposure on top. We quantify the number so you can size it, document the factors properly going forward, and where past years need correcting we bring them forward through a Voluntary Disclosures Program application on Form RC199 rather than waiting for the letter.
Do I charge HST on dog walking, and when do I have to register?
Yes. Dog walking is an ordinary taxable supply at 13% in Ontario, and there is no animal health care exemption that reaches it, so the question is only when registration becomes mandatory. It becomes mandatory once your taxable supplies pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. That is the line most walkers cross without noticing, because revenue here arrives as a very large number of small visits and $30,000 does not feel like a milestone when it accumulates thirty dollars at a time. Place of supply follows the recipient's address. We monitor the rolling four-quarter total for you, register at the right date, and where a registration should have started earlier we correct the unregistered period voluntarily rather than leaving the interest to compound.
When do I recognize revenue on a prepaid block of walks?
As the walks are performed, not on the day the block is paid for. A ten-walk or twenty-walk package is included in income under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a) when you receive it, and then reserved out under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m) to the extent the visits have not yet been performed at your year-end. An owner who skips the reserve is paying tax this year on walks they still owe next year, which is the most expensive timing error we see on these files because packages tend to sell in the autumn for the winter. Whether a particular block also engages ETA section 181.2 on gift certificates depends on your own terms and documentation, and we review that rather than assuming it either way. What it is not is profit you can plan around while the visits are still outstanding.
How should I handle late cancellations and no-show charges?
As their own revenue account, and with the tax treatment reviewed against what your service agreement actually says. Cancellations are endemic in a business built on short scheduled visits, and a retained charge is not automatically the same thing as consideration for a supply you made. ETA section 182 deals with an amount retained on the breach or termination of an agreement, and whether it applies to a given charge turns on the terms you wrote and what the client agreed to, so we read the agreement rather than characterizing the charge by reflex. Keeping cancellation charges separate from visit revenue also matters commercially: it tells you your real completion rate per route, which is one of the few numbers that explains why a full schedule did not turn into the revenue you expected.
How do I tell whether a route is actually profitable?
By costing the route rather than the month, which almost no set of books does by default. Revenue in this business is a very large number of small visits, and the only thing that makes a walk profitable is how close the next client is. Two businesses with identical revenue and identical hourly pay can finish a year with completely different margins because one spent its day walking dogs and the other spent it driving between them. Unpaid travel time between paid visits is a real cost that appears nowhere on an income statement unless somebody puts it there. We load walker hours and drive legs onto each route, report visits, labour and travel cost per route every month, and show you which routes earn and which ones only look busy. That reporting is usually the first time an owner sees that the fullest route is the weakest one.
How do group walks change the economics?
They are where the margin in this business actually comes from. A walker taking several dogs on one outing earns several fees for one hour of labour, so the revenue per walker hour on a group walk is a different number entirely from the revenue per walker hour on back-to-back solo visits. That is the ceiling worth understanding, and it is constrained in two ways: by what one walker can realistically handle, which is a capacity question we can model against your routes, and by municipal animal bylaws governing how many animals one person may walk or have under control, which is a real and variable requirement we do not state for any municipality. We report revenue and labour cost per walker hour for solo and group work separately, so the pricing and the hiring decision are both made on numbers rather than on impressions.
How many dogs is one walker allowed to walk at once, and do I need a licence?
Both of those are municipal questions and we will not answer either one here. Dog walking is not a licensed or regulated profession in Ontario: there is no provincial college, no protected title and no registration to obtain, and anyone telling you otherwise is wrong. What is real is municipal regulation. Municipal bylaws reach business licensing, how many animals one person may walk or have under control, leash and off-leash and park rules, and whether a home-based operation is permitted at your address, and they differ from one municipality to the next, so the answer for Toronto is not the answer for Ottawa. Confirm each with the municipality directly. Separately, the Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019, enforced by Animal Welfare Services, applies to anyone with custody of an animal. We will cost the compliance once you know what applies; we will not tell you what applies.
What CCA class is my car, and what records does CRA want?
A vehicle used in the business generally sits in Class 10. Where it meets the definition of an automobile in ITA subsection 248(1) and cost more than the prescribed limit it falls into Class 10.1 instead, where each such vehicle sits alone in its own class and neither recapture nor terminal loss arises when you dispose of it. We will not tell you from here whether your particular vehicle is an automobile for that purpose or state the cost limit, because both are reviewed on the facts and the figures change. What matters just as much is the evidence. A mileage logbook is the single most valuable record a dog walking business keeps, because driving is most of the working day and the vehicle claim is proportionally the largest deduction on the return. An unsupported business-use percentage on a business that drives all day is the first thing a reviewer removes. ITA section 67.1 limits the food and beverage portion of expenses to 50%.
Can I deduct a workspace in my home?
That is reviewed on your facts and we do not assert a conclusion for anyone. A dog walking business typically has no premises at all, so the scheduling, invoicing, client administration and record keeping happen somewhere in the house, which makes the question come up on almost every file in this trade. Whether a workspace in the home is deductible for a particular claimant depends on how the space is used and on the specific rules that apply to that claimant's circumstances, and it is one of the areas CRA reviews most consistently, so the answer needs to be supported rather than assumed. Separately, whether a home-based business is permitted at your address at all is a municipal bylaw and zoning question to confirm with your municipality. We will set the claim up properly where it is supportable and tell you plainly where it is not.
What can a dog walking business write off?
Vehicle operating costs including fuel, maintenance, insurance and licensing on the supported business-use portion, leads, harnesses, collars, crates, carriers and transport equipment, scheduling and routing software subscriptions, phone and data, general liability and care, custody and control insurance premiums, bonding where it is required of you, walker subcontract payments and staff wages, professional fees, advertising, and bank and payment processing charges. On capital, the vehicle goes to Class 10 or Class 10.1, leads, crates and equipment to Class 8 at 20%, phones and computers to Class 50 at 55%, scheduling software to Class 12 at 100%, and goodwill or an acquired client list to Class 14.1 at 5%, all reported on Schedule 8. A bad debt on an unpaid client invoice is deductible under ITA paragraph 20(1)(p). Records are kept six years under ITA section 230.
How do I value my dog walking business if I sell it?
A buyer prices the client list and the routes, in that order, because what they are acquiring is a book of recurring weekly visits and a map tight enough to service it profitably. A list spread thinly across a wide area is discounted hard, for the same reason route density drives your own margin. A business that depends entirely on the founder walking the dogs personally is discounted too, because the buyer cannot continue it without you. Unwalked prepaid packages are deducted rather than added, since they are an obligation the buyer inherits. The structure decides what you keep: a share sale can access the $1.25M Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under ITA 110.6 where the shares qualify, with purification and a two-year runway. An asset sale triggers recapture where proceeds beat undepreciated capital cost, and goodwill and the client list land in Class 14.1 at 5%.
