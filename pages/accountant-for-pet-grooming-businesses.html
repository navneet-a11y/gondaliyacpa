Toronto Grooming Salon — One Products Account for Six Years

The problem: A Toronto salon with four tables and a retail counter ran every bottle, blade and towel through a single account called products, whether it was used in a groom or sold to the client on the way out. There had never been an inventory count, so there was no closing stock figure, no cost of sales a reviewer could follow, and no way to say whether the counter made money. The owner believed the retail shelf was profitable and could not prove it.

What we did: We separated consumable supplies from product held for resale, counted the retail stock, established a consistent valuation basis under ITA section 10, rebuilt three open years on that split, and set a monthly count into the bookkeeping routine.

The result:

  • $41,300 of closing inventory recognised for the first time
  • Cost of sales and cost per groom now separately reportable
  • Three open years restated on a defensible section 10 basis

Mississauga Salon Build-Out — A Fit-Out Expensed in One Year

The problem: A Mississauga grooming business took a bare industrial unit and built it out: wash-bay plumbing, floor drains, sealed flooring, ventilation, a hydraulic table, two tubs and a bank of dryers. The prior accountant had put the entire spend through repairs and maintenance in the year it was paid. The corporation reported a loss it did not really have, the balance sheet showed almost no assets, and the equipment lender it approached for a second tub declined the file on the statements as presented.

What we did: We rebuilt the spend from the contractor and supplier invoices, moved the plumbing, drainage, flooring and ventilation into Class 13 over the lease term, placed the tubs, table and dryers in Class 8, the point-of-sale hardware in Class 50, and compiled two years of CSRS 4200 statements on the corrected basis.

The result:

  • $96,500 of fit-out and equipment capitalised correctly
  • Class 13 and Class 8 pools established and carried forward
  • Equipment financing approved on the restated statements

Hamilton Shop and Van — Nobody Knew Which One Earned

The problem: A Hamilton grooming business ran a two-table shop and a fitted-out van through one set of books. Fuel, the van's generator and water system, the shop's rent, both groomers' pay and all the product sat in the same accounts. The owner was about to buy a second van on the strength of a busy schedule. Nothing in the ledger could say whether the first van covered its own costs, and the van's fit-out had been put through vehicle expense rather than capitalised.

What we did: We built two segments in the bookkeeping and allocated wages, product, fuel, insurance and depreciation to each, capitalised the generator and water system, confirmed the vehicle class on the purchase invoice, and put a mileage logbook in place before the next fiscal year opened.

The result:

  • Shop and van reported as two segments every month
  • $23,800 of van fit-out moved from expense into capital
  • Second van decision deferred on the segment numbers

Pet Grooming Accounting & Tax FAQs

Should I incorporate my pet grooming business?
Incorporating gives you limited liability plus roughly a 12.2% Ontario combined rate on the first $500,000 of active income under ITA section 125, against a personal rate reaching 53.53% when unincorporated. The decision turns on whether you earn more than you withdraw, because that surplus is what a corporation lets you defer. There are two reasons specific to grooming. A fitted-out salon and a fitted-out van are substantial assets that a corporation can hold, finance and eventually sell in a way a sole proprietor cannot. And a client list built over years is a real asset in its own right. When incorporating makes sense, we handle the section 85 rollover on Form T2057 so the equipment, the van, the retail stock and the list move in at elected amounts.
Do I charge HST on grooming, and when do I have to register?
Yes. Pet grooming is an ordinary taxable supply, charged at 13% in Ontario. There is no health care exemption for animals, so none of the exemptions that apply to human health services reach a groom, a bath, a nail trim or a de-shed. Registration becomes mandatory once your taxable supplies pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters, measured on a rolling basis rather than on your fiscal year. That is the line most grooming businesses cross without noticing, usually in the season they add a second groomer, open on Saturdays or put a van on the road. Retail product sales count toward the same threshold as the grooming itself. Once registered, you also start recovering the tax on your supplies, your equipment, your fit-out and your rent.
What is the difference between grooming supplies and retail inventory?
It is the most important distinction in grooming bookkeeping and the one most often missed. Shampoo, conditioner, blades, cologne, towels and cleaning product consumed in performing a groom are supplies, expensed as they are used. The same or a similar product sold to the client at the counter is inventory under ITA section 10, held on the balance sheet until it sells and valued on a consistent basis year to year. A business that runs both through one products account can do neither job: it cannot state what its stock is worth, and it cannot work out what a single groom costs in product. Two accounts, never one. Where a bottle is partly used in the salon and partly sold, we set a method and apply it consistently rather than guessing a split each year.
How do I account for prepaid groom packages and memberships?
The cash is income when you receive it under ITA paragraph 12(1)(a), and then a reserve is claimed under ITA paragraph 20(1)(m) for the part of the service you have not yet performed. In practice that means a six-groom card sold in November with two grooms used by your December year-end leaves four grooms of deferred revenue carried as a liability into the next year. A monthly membership works the same way for any period billed ahead. This matters most at year-end, because a salon that sells packages hard in December and performs them in February otherwise pays tax a full year early. On the sales tax side, ETA section 181.2 on gift certificates is the provision to review, and we reach a documented position on your actual package terms rather than assuming one.
Are cancellation and no-show fees taxable?
They need a position rather than a reflex, and in grooming they are frequent enough to be worth getting right once. A charge for a missed or late-cancelled appointment is not automatically the same thing as a payment for a service you performed, and the GST/HST treatment turns on what the charge actually is under your booking terms. ETA section 182 deals with an amount paid as a consequence of a breach, modification or termination of an agreement, and that is the provision we review against your terms. We do not characterise your cancellation charge as a forfeited deposit, and we do not simply drop it into sales either. We read the terms you actually publish, reach a documented conclusion, and apply it consistently so the position holds up if a reviewer asks.
What CCA class is my grooming equipment?
Grooming tubs, hydraulic tables, dryers, clippers and blade sets, cages, runs, fencing, reception furniture and retail shelving generally go to Class 8 at 20%, claimed on Schedule 8. Point-of-sale terminals, tablets, laptops and computers belong in Class 50 at 55%, and booking and application software in Class 12 at 100%. Goodwill and an acquired client list land in Class 14.1 at 5%. The error we inherit most often is everything pooled into Class 8, which understates the deduction on precisely the hardware that becomes obsolete first, and buries software that should have been written off immediately. Correcting it recovers real money, and the correction has to be made while the years are still open to adjustment, which is why we look at it in the first month.
Can I expense my salon renovation?
Generally not, and this is the single most expensive mistake on grooming files. Wash-bay plumbing, floor drains, sealed or non-slip flooring, ventilation and extraction, electrical upgrades for dryers and partition work are improvements, not repairs. Where you are a tenant, they are a leasehold improvement in Class 13, amortised over the term of the lease plus permitted renewal periods. Where you own the building, the work generally goes to Class 1. A shop that expenses its whole fit-out in the year it paid reports a loss it does not really have, shows a balance sheet with almost no assets, and then cannot borrow against the very build-out it funded. We rebuild the spend from the contractor and supplier invoices and allocate it across Class 13, Class 8 and Class 50 on evidence rather than on memory.
What class is my mobile grooming van?
A grooming van generally sits in Class 10. Where a vehicle meets the definition of an automobile in ITA subsection 248(1) and cost more than the prescribed limit, it falls into Class 10.1 instead, where each vehicle sits alone in its own class and neither recapture nor terminal loss arises when you dispose of it. Whether your particular van is an automobile for that purpose depends on how it is built and used, and we review the purchase documents rather than assuming. Separately, the grooming equipment, generator, water tank and heating system fitted into the van are capital additions in their own right, not vehicle repairs. Business use is proved by a mileage logbook, which is also the first thing CRA asks for, so it goes in place before the year starts rather than being reconstructed afterwards.
Are my groomers employees or independent contractors?
That is a question of fact and we will not answer it from the label on the arrangement. Commission splits, subcontract groomers and station rentals are all normal in this trade, and CRA tests the relationship on the factors set out in Form RC4110: control over how and when the work is done, who provides the tools, clippers and product, whether the worker can subcontract or hire help, the chance of profit and the risk of loss, and how integrated the worker is into your business. A groomer who works your hours, on your table, with your product and takes no financial risk looks different on those factors from one who brings her own kit, sets her own book and grooms elsewhere too. We run the analysis, document it, file T4A slips for the genuine contractors, and tell you where the exposure sits before a payroll auditor does.
What if a groomer rents a station from me?
Then your revenue from that groomer is rent, not a service fee, and that is a different supply with different bookkeeping. The groomer bills the client directly and is responsible for their own registration and reporting; you are supplying space and facilities. The practical consequences are real: your revenue line changes shape, your HST analysis is on a rental rather than a groom, and the retail product the groomer uses may be theirs rather than yours. Whether any particular arrangement genuinely is a station rental rather than employment or a contractor engagement is again a question of fact that turns on the Form RC4110 factors and on what actually happens day to day, not on the words in the agreement. We look at the substance, set the ledger up to match, and keep the two revenue streams apart.
Do I need a licence to run a pet grooming business in Ontario?
Grooming is not a provincially licensed or regulated profession in Ontario. There is no college, no protected title and no provincial certification requirement for a groomer, and anyone who tells you otherwise is mistaken. What is real sits elsewhere. Municipal business licensing and animal-related bylaws apply to the activity and differ from one municipality to the next, so what your city requires is confirmed with your city rather than assumed from a neighbouring one. The Provincial Animal Welfare Services Act, 2019, enforced by Animal Welfare Services, applies to anyone with custody of an animal. And practising veterinary medicine is restricted under the Veterinarians Act, with the College of Veterinarians of Ontario as the regulator, so where grooming ends and treatment begins is a boundary for you to settle with your own counsel and veterinarian. We name all of these and interpret none of them.
What can a pet grooming business write off?
Consumable supplies used in grooming, retail product cost as it sells, rent and utilities, laundry, waste and sharps disposal, equipment repairs and blade sharpening, booking and payment software subscriptions, card processing and platform fees, groomer and bather wages or subcontract fees, WSIB premiums, insurance, professional fees, advertising, and vehicle costs supported by a logbook. On capital, tubs, tables, dryers and cages go to Class 8 at 20%, the leasehold fit-out to Class 13 over the lease, an owned building to Class 1, hardware to Class 50 at 55%, software to Class 12 at 100%, a van to Class 10 or Class 10.1, and goodwill or an acquired client list to Class 14.1 at 5%, all on Schedule 8. A bad debt on an unpaid client invoice is deductible under ITA paragraph 20(1)(p).
How do I value my grooming business if I sell it?
A buyer prices three things: the client list and its rebooking rate, the lease and the fit-out that comes with it, and the equipment. A shop whose clients book one named groomer rather than the business is discounted hard, because the buyer cannot continue it without that person. Unredeemed prepaid packages are deducted, not added, since the buyer inherits the obligation to perform those grooms. Structure decides what you keep: a share sale can access the $1.25M Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption under ITA 110.6 where the shares qualify, which needs purification of retained cash and roughly a two-year runway. An asset sale triggers recapture where proceeds exceed undepreciated capital cost on the Class 8 and Class 13 pools, and goodwill and the client list land in Class 14.1. We model both well before you list.
