Differences in name protection and operational jurisdictions

Federal incorporation protects your company name all over Canada. Provincial protection applies only within that province’s borders:

  • Choose federal if you want nationwide brand protection.
  • Choose provincial if you want simpler local coverage.

Both types require yearly returns filed with their registries after incorporating.[4][5]

Director residency and filing obligations

Residency rules differ between federal and provincial rules:

  • Ontario has required no resident Canadian director since July 5, 2021.
  • Federal corporations need at least 25% resident Canadian directors and have filed individuals with significant control information with Corporations Canada since January 22, 2024.

These rules affect who can be a director depending on where founders live or work.

Suitability scenarios for federal or provincial Incorporation

CPA firms say consider these points when picking jurisdiction:

  • Businesses selling across provinces often prefer federal status, even with extra-provincial registrations to pay, because it offers wider protections.
  • Local sole owners might choose provincial setup to keep filings simple even if they accept limits on geography.

Federal vs. Provincial Incorporation: Choosing the Best Option

Federal or Provincial

Jurisdiction

Picking between federal and provincial incorporation depends on your business plans and where you want to operate. The $35 incorporation fee covers our service only; government fees differ depending on the province or territory.

Federal incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) protects your business name across all provinces and territories. However, if you work in multiple provinces, you may need extra-provincial registration in each one. Provincial incorporation limits your name protection to that province but is simpler if your business stays local.

The business incorporation fee Canada-wide includes government filing charges, which are separate from our $35 service fee, which includes HST. Affordable incorporation Canada options try to balance these costs with what’s required for compliance.

Think about whether your business will grow beyond one province or needs national brand recognition. Federal corporations pay a lower government fee at first ($200 online versus $300 in Ontario) but get wider legal protection. Provincial incorporations cost more upfront in Ontario but can be simpler, though they lead to extra expenses if you register in other provinces later.

Our CPA-backed online service explains these differences clearly and avoids hidden fees. We help founders make smart choices that fit their growth goals.

Incorporation Costs Across Canadian Provinces
