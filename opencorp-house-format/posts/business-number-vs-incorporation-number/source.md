Business Registration and Identification Numbers in Canada
Understanding the CRA Business Number (BN) and Its Purpose
In Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) gives each business a Business Number (BN). This is a unique 9-digit code that helps track tax stuff and other government dealings.

The BN makes things easier by linking all tax accounts under one number. So, if you have to pay corporate taxes, GST/HST, or payroll taxes, they all connect back to your BN.

Here’s what you need to know about the CRA Business Number:

It’s made up of nine digits plus two letters that show the type of tax account.
You get your BN when you incorporate your company or register as a sole proprietor.
Use it anytime you file taxes or apply for government permits.
If you incorporate federally or in a province like Ontario, you’ll usually get this number automatically when you register your business.

Distinguishing Between Business Number and Incorporation Number
These two numbers sound similar but mean different things. Here’s how they differ:

Business Number (BN):
Issued by CRA.
Used mainly for taxes and related programs like GST/HST.
Incorporation Number:
Given when you officially form your corporation.
Comes from Corporations Canada if federal, or a provincial registry like Ontario's if local.
Identifies your company legally but isn’t for taxes directly.
Mixing these up can cause problems later, so it pays to know which is which!

HST Number vs. Business Number
Lots of new business owners confuse their HST number with the business number, but they’re not the same thing:

The HST number comes from your BN but has an extra suffix showing it relates to GST/HST taxes (like 123456789 RT0001).
You need this number on invoices when charging customers sales tax. This helps follow Canadian sales tax rules properly.
Knowing the difference keeps your invoicing clear and legal when collecting sales tax from clients.

Getting these basics right—what a BN is versus an incorporation number—gives you a solid start in handling your business ID numbers correctly in Canada.

What Is an Incorporation Number and How Is It Assigned?
An incorporation number is a special ID your company gets when it officially becomes a legal entity. It shows your business exists under Canadian law.

Federal vs Provincial Incorporation Numbers
Federal incorporation numbers come from Corporations Canada under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA). They apply if you register at the federal level.
Provincial incorporation numbers, like those from the Ontario Business Registry under the Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA), are for companies registered within that province only.
Each place has its own system with different numbering styles. For example, Ontario uses its own numeric codes.

When You Receive an Incorporation Number
You get your incorporation number right after your incorporation papers are approved:

If federally incorporated, Corporations Canada issues your federal incorporation number.
If provincially incorporated, like in Ontario, you get it from that province’s registry after approval.
This number proves your company exists but doesn’t replace other registrations like tax accounts with the CRA.

What Is a GST/HST Number and When Is It Required?
A GST/HST number is an ID the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) uses to track businesses that collect Goods and Services Tax (GST) or Harmonized Sales Tax (HST). This number lets businesses report, collect, and pay these taxes. The GST/HST number isn’t separate from the business number; it’s actually a program account linked to it.

You have to register for a GST/HST number if your taxable sales go over $30,000 in four straight calendar quarters. Some businesses can register even if they don’t reach that amount. This applies to sole proprietors, corporations, partnerships, and even some non-residents who do business in Canada.

Here’s a key thing: the "GST number" or "HST number" means your business number plus an RT program account suffix. It doesn’t replace your main CRA business number Canada.

How the GST/HST Number Relates to the Business Number
The CRA gives each legal business one unique nine-digit business number (BN) in Canada. This BN acts as the base ID for many tax programs. When you register for GST/HST, CRA adds an RT program account suffix with four digits starting at 0001. For example: 123456789 RT0001 (just an example).

So, your GST/HST “number” always has your main BN, followed by “RT” and some digits. That means:

Your business number identifies your whole legal entity.
Your GST/HST program account shows you’re registered to collect sales tax under that BN.
Other program accounts use different two-letter codes like RC for corporate income tax or RP for payroll deductions. But they all share that same nine-digit BN.

This setup means you don’t get separate BNs for each tax type—just program accounts tied to one central BN issued when you register with CRA.

Common CRA Program Account Identifiers Linked to the Business Number
The CRA uses specific two-letter codes attached to your nine-digit BN when registering various tax accounts:

Identifier	Program Account	Typical Use Case
RC	Corporate Income Tax	Corporations filing T2 returns

RP	Payroll Deductions	Employers remitting employee source deductions

RZ	Information Returns	Filing info slips like T4/T5
RR	Charities	Registered charities reporting donations
Each looks like this: 123456789 XX0001 where XX is the identifier and 0001 shows primary location or division.

Keep in mind: incorporation numbers come from provincial or federal registries (like Ontario’s OBCA or Corporations Canada). They are not the same as BNs or CRA program accounts.

Which accounts you need depends on your situation. A CPA can help set things up right so you follow rules properly.

Automatic Business Number Issuance Upon Incorporation
If you incorporate federally with Corporations Canada or provincially through Ontario’s Business Registry, the CRA gives you a business number automatically. They connect the dots between incorporation records and tax registration. No need to apply twice unless you want extra program accounts.

Adding or Closing CRA Program Accounts Linked to the Business Number
Once you have a main BN, you can open or close specific program accounts depending on what your business needs:

Open an RT account if you charge GST/HST.
Add an RP account if you hire employees and must handle payroll deductions.

To close accounts, use the My Business Account portal or contact CRA directly. Be sure before opening accounts—you don’t want extra registrations making things messy.[3]

Updated Information on Business Number vs Incorporation Number and HST Number
Getting the differences between a business number, incorporation number, and HST number can be confusing at first. But if you’re starting a business or working for yourself in Canada, it helps to know what each one means. They each have a different role with the government.

What Is the Difference Between a Business Number and an Incorporation Number?
A business number (BN) comes from the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). It’s used mainly for tax stuff. If your business signs up for programs like GST/HST, payroll, or income tax accounts, you’ll get this number.

An incorporation number is something else. You get it when you legally form your corporation through Corporations Canada or the Ontario Business Registry. This number shows your company is registered but isn’t used for taxes.

So here’s the main difference:

The BN is for taxes and CRA accounts.
The incorporation number is for legal registration with the government.
Both numbers are needed if you have an incorporated business. But they work in different ways.[1][2]

How Is a CRA Business Number Structured?
The CRA gives one 9-digit business number to each legal business in Canada. For example:

Part	Digits	Example	What It Means
Base BN	9 digits	123456789	Identifies your business
This base number is the main ID for all other program numbers connected to your business.[3]

What Are Program Account Numbers Under Your Business Number?
Your base BN can have extra program account numbers. They look like this:

123456789 XX0001
Here:

XX is two letters showing the program (like RC, RT, RP).
0001 tells which branch or division.
Some common codes:

Code	Program
RC	Corporate Income Tax
RT	GST/HST
RP	Payroll
RM	Import/Export
Z	Information Returns

Each program account lets you handle tax stuff separately but under one BN.[4]

In short: Your CRA business registration has different linked program accounts based on what your company does. This doesn’t change your corporation number or incorporation status.

Is My HST Number the Same as My Business Number?
Nope! Your HST/GST number includes your 9-digit BN plus an RT code and reference like 0001. For example:

123456789 RT0001
This shows you’ve signed up to collect GST/HST for that part of your business.[5]

People often say “HST number,” but it’s just one type of CRA program account tied to your main BN — not a separate ID.

What Does an Incorporation Number Look Like in Ontario and Federally?
Incorporation numbers look different depending on where you register:

Ontario Corporation Numbers: Usually start with “0” and have six digits after it (like 0123456). You get this from the Ontario Business Registry under their rules.
Federal Corporation Numbers: Seven-digit numbers given by Corporations Canada when you incorporate federally.
You’ll see these numbers on your certificate of incorporation. They don’t relate to taxes or GST/HST registrations.[6][7]
