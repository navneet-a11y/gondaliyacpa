Advantages of Medical Practice Incorporation: Tax Deferral, Income Splitting, and Liability Protection
Incorporating a medical practice in Canada gives doctors some useful perks. A physician professional corporation lets you keep money inside the company. This means you pay lower corporate tax rates now and delay personal taxes until you take the money out. It’s not a permanent saving but a way to control when you pay tax.

You can also share income with family members who own non-voting shares. This may lower your family’s total tax bill. But watch out: each province’s medical college has its own rules, and TOSI (Tax on Split Income) laws apply too. So, it’s smart to ask an accountant before setting this up.

The other big plus is liability protection. Incorporation won’t stop malpractice claims — those still come at you personally. But it does protect your business from debts or contracts that aren’t related to patient care.

Potential Disadvantages: Administrative Burden, Costs, and Restrictions on Funds Retention
Setting up a medical corporation costs money—fees vary by province. You pay once to set it up, then again every year for renewals and filings. These extra steps make running your practice more complex than being unincorporated.

You also have to manage bookkeeping, payroll slips (T4/T5), file corporate taxes (T2), and keep proper minutes. Missing deadlines can lead to penalties or loss of good standing with regulators.

Money kept inside the corporation can’t be used freely without causing personal tax events. That limits how easily you can access your cash compared to getting paid directly as salary or dividends.

If your corporation makes over $50,000 in passive investment income each year, extra taxes kick in (“passive income grind”). That reduces some benefits of holding onto earnings inside the company.

Costs and rules differ across provinces because medical colleges and government offices set their own fees. Doctors should think about these challenges when deciding if incorporating makes sense.

Main downsides include:
Setup and annual fees that vary by location
Extra paperwork and reporting duties
Limits on withdrawing retained funds without tax hits
Higher taxes on passive income inside the corporation

Step-by-Step Guide to Setting Up a Physician Professional Corporation
Setting up a physician professional corporation (PC) in Canada takes a few key steps. The cost to incorporate medical practice varies by province. Knowing these steps helps doctors handle the process and follow the rules.

Choosing a Name and Business Structure
Picking a good name is the first step in doctor incorporation Canada. The name must be unique and meet your province's medical college rules. You’ll need to do a NUANS Name Search. This search checks if the name is already taken.

The NUANS Report comes from this search and shows if your name is free to use. It also holds the name while you file papers. The cost for this report can range from about $13 to $75, depending on where you are [1](https://www.nuans.com/).

You also need to choose the right business structure before filing your articles of incorporation.

Filing Articles of Incorporation and Obtaining Provincial Approval
To officially create your PC, you file articles of incorporation with your provincial registry:

Ontario: Ontario Business Registry charges $360 for this [2](https://www.onbiz.ca/articles-incorporation-fees).
British Columbia: BC Registries fee is $350 [3](https://www.bcregistryservices.gov.bc.ca/incorporate-fees).
Alberta: Alberta Corporate Registry charges $275 per application [4](https://www.alberta.ca/corporate-registry-fees.aspx).
These fees cover government processing only. Extra fees like legal help or accounting aren’t included.

After filing, your provincial medical college must approve before you start working under the PC.

Frequently Asked Questions Addressing Incorporation Eligibility, Naming, Shareholders, and Renewal
Physicians who want to incorporate often wonder about eligibility, naming rules, shareholders, and renewals. Each province’s medical college has its own set of rules.

Eligibility: Only licensed doctors in good standing can incorporate. If you're an International Medical Graduate (IMG), you must be licensed first. Incorporation doesn’t replace or grant your license — that’s up to the college.

Naming: Your corporation’s name must follow the college’s rules. Avoid using protected words like “doctor” or “medical” without approval. For example, the CPSO checks the name during their Certificate of Authorization process.

Shareholders: In many provinces, family members can hold non-voting shares if conditions are met (see Ontario Business Corporations Act s. 3.2). But share setups need a legal review to avoid tax problems like TOSI (Tax on Split Income).

Renewal: You have to renew your permits yearly with your regulatory body and file annual returns with corporate registries. Missing renewal deadlines can cause permit suspensions and stop you from billing through your corporation.

Knowing these points helps when budgeting for the cost to incorporate a medical practice across Canada.
