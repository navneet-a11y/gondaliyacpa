Understanding Business Ownership Requirements for Newcomers

Who Can Be a Shareholder or Director of a Canadian Corporation?

Eligibility

Anyone over 18 years old can own shares or be a director in a Canadian corporation—even if they don’t live in Canada. But some rules do apply:

  • You need a certain number of directors who live in Canada, depending on where you incorporate—federal or provincial laws apply differently.
  • Federal incorporations still require at least 25% of directors to be resident Canadians, or at least one director if there are four or fewer.
  • Ontario removed its resident director requirement on July 5, 2021 [source: OBCA].

This means newcomers—whether permanent residents or foreigners—can own and manage companies while figuring out life here.

Worth Knowing

A resident Canadian generally means a Canadian citizen or permanent resident who lives in Canada. A newcomer on a work permit who is not yet a permanent resident does not count toward the federal 25%.

Entrepreneur Work Permit (C11) and Eligibility Criteria

The Entrepreneur Work Permit (C11) lets immigrant entrepreneurs run or buy businesses in Canada temporarily. It also helps with applying for permanent residence through certain programs. To be eligible, you must show a solid business plan, have enough investment money, and actively manage the business.

Here are the key points:

  • Own at least 50% of the Canadian company.
  • Take part in daily operations.
  • Present a detailed business plan that benefits Canada’s economy.

This permit helps with immigrant entrepreneur incorporation Canada but doesn’t guarantee permanent residency or work permission beyond its duration. It’s smart to talk to licensed immigration experts for advice.

Intra-Company Transfer (ICT) and Treaty Work Permits (CUSMA, CETA)

If you want to incorporate a company in Canada as a foreigner, intra-company transfer permits might help. These allow executives or managers from related companies abroad to work temporarily at Canadian branches without needing Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA).

Also, treaty permits under deals like CUSMA and CETA offer easier entry for certain professionals linked to multinational firms.

These paths let foreigners manage their Canadian businesses legally but require an existing connection with an overseas company. They don’t replace normal incorporation rules or give ownership rights.

Provincial Nominee Programs for Entrepreneurs

Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) support newcomers wanting to start businesses that fit local economic goals. Newcomer business incorporation Canada gets a boost from PNPs through nomination certificates. These speed up permanent residence after meeting investment amounts, job creation needs, and milestones set by provinces like Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, and Nova Scotia.

Each province sets different rules on net worth and industries allowed. So, check the specific program details before applying.

Start-Up Visa Program and Current Status

The Start-Up Visa Program focuses on innovative immigrant entrepreneurs who can launch high-growth startups. These startups need backing from designated groups like venture capital funds or angel investors approved by Innovation Science Economic Development Canada.

For immigrant entrepreneur incorporation Canada through this program, you must get commitment letters from these endorsers showing your startup’s potential value before applying for permanent residence. The corporation should be set up federally or provincially.

Federal Incorporation vs. Provincial Incorporation: Pros and Cons

Choosing federal incorporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) or provincial incorporation like Ontario’s Business Corporations Act (OBCA) depends on where you want to do business.

Federal Incorporation Pros:

  • Protects your company name across all of Canada.
  • Lets you use the same name in every province without extra steps at first.
  • Uses consistent federal rules under CBCA.3

Provincial Incorporation Pros:

  • Sometimes cheaper fees if working only in one province.
  • Simpler forms if you stay local (like Ontario).
  • Local rules about directors might differ.4

For newcomers planning to work in many provinces or build a national brand, federal is better. If you only plan local operations, provincial works fine.

Directors and Tax Filings

Directors and Tax

Starting a business as a newcomer to Canada means you need to know who can be a director. Directors must be at least 18 years old. There is no maximum age limit. They also have to be legally capable of making decisions. People who are undischarged bankrupts can't be directors.

Federal rules (CBCA) still require at least 25% of directors to be resident Canadians, while Ontario dropped its requirement in 2021. Some other provinces still ask for a resident director. Directors are responsible for running the company properly. They must follow laws like Ontario’s Business Corporations Act (OBCA). Their job is to protect shareholders and make sure the company follows the rules.

Immigrant entrepreneur incorporation Canada means picking directors who fit these rules. Remember, some provinces have their own special rules.

Tax Filing Obligations: Corporate Income Tax, GST/HST, Payroll Slips, and T4/T5 Reporting

When you incorporate a newcomer business in Canada, you must file several tax forms on time:

  • T2 Corporate Income Tax Return: File this within six months after your fiscal year ends.
  • GST/HST Returns: File if your annual sales go over $30,000.
  • Payroll Slips & T4/T5 Reports: Employers file T4 slips for employees and T5 slips for dividends paid.
  • Part XIII Withholding Tax: This tax is usually 25% on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders unless reduced by treaties.
  • NR4 Filing Deadline: You must file NR4 returns by March 31 after the calendar year ends.

Filing these on time keeps you in good standing with the CRA and avoids penalties.

CCPC Rules, Small Business Limits, and Part XIII Withholding Tax

Your company’s status as a Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation (CCPC) depends on who owns or controls it.

  • If non-residents hold or control over 50% of voting shares directly or indirectly, your company loses CCPC status.
  • The Small Business Limit (SBD) is fixed at $500,000 federally in 2026.
  • CCPCs get lower tax rates on active business income up to that limit.
  • The default Part XIII withholding tax rate is 25% on some payments like dividends to non-residents unless treaty relief applies.

It’s smart for immigrant entrepreneurs to get professional advice when sorting out ownership and tax benefits.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: The corporation, not the shareholder, is liable when Part XIII tax is not withheld. Please do not pay a dividend to a non-resident without withholding or a valid NR301 on file.

Reporting Foreign Affiliates and Investments: T1134 and T1135 Forms

If your company owns foreign affiliates or foreign property as a newcomer:

  • You might need to file the T1134 Form annually if you control foreign affiliates, even if cost is very low.
  • The T1135 Form reports foreign property over CAD $100,000 during the year. This includes real estate outside Canada not used in an active business.

You need accurate records about ownership percentages and asset values each year. Missing these reports can cause fines.

Worth Knowing

An individual does not file Form T1135 for the year they first become resident in Canada. The requirement starts the following year.

Managing Non-Resident Corporation Incorporation and Treaty Relief Processes

This change makes immigrant entrepreneur incorporation Canada easier but doesn’t remove all other requirements like having a registered office address.

Treaty relief can lower Part XIII withholding taxes when your country has an agreement with Canada. To claim it, the shareholder gives the corporation forms like NR301 before dividends are paid. The corporation keeps them on file, and CRA can review them.

Frequently Asked Questions on Incorporating as a Newcomer

7

Newcomer Incorporation FAQ

FAQ
What is the typical online incorporation fee with Open Corporation for $35?+

Open Corporation charges $35 CAD, HST included, plus government fees. This covers preparing and filing your articles the same day; the NUANS report and the registered office address service are separate.

How long is a NUANS report valid for when incorporating?+

A NUANS report is valid for 90 days from the issue date. You must file incorporation within this period.

What is the minimum age requirement to be a director in Canada?+

Directors must be at least 18 years old to serve under both CBCA and OBCA rules.

When is the T2 corporate tax return deadline after incorporation?+

The T2 return is due six months after your fiscal year-end date. File on time to avoid penalties.

What is the default Part XIII withholding tax rate on dividends to non-residents?+

The default rate is 25%, but it may be reduced by applicable tax treaty benefits.

What does removal of resident director requirement mean for newcomers?+

Ontario removed its resident director requirement on July 5, 2021, so a newcomer or non-resident can be the sole director of an Ontario corporation; federal corporations still need 25% resident Canadian directors.

What is extra-provincial registration and when is it required?+

Extra-provincial registration applies if you operate in provinces other than your incorporation jurisdiction. It ensures legal compliance across provinces.

Why is having a registered office address important in Canadian incorporation?+

A registered office address is where official communications are sent. It must be within your jurisdiction.

How do tax treaty benefits affect Part XIII withholding tax?+

Tax treaties can lower withholding tax rates on dividends or interest paid to foreign shareholders when the shareholder gives the corporation a completed NR301; no advance CRA approval is needed.

What are foreign reporting requirements like T1134 and T1135 forms?+

T1134 reports foreign affiliates; T1135 reports foreign property over CAD 100,000. Both must be filed annually if applicable.

How does business number registration work after incorporation?+

The CRA assigns a business number linking GST/HST, payroll, and import/export accounts after you register online or automatically during federal incorporation.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: A NUANS report more than 90 days old will be refused with your articles. Please do not file with an expired report.
