Quick Answers: Key Numbers at a Glance

At a Glance
  • Ontario incorporation: $300 online government fee
  • Our fee: $35 including HST, plus government fees
  • Small business rate: 12.2% on the first $500,000 of active business income
  • General corporate rate: 26.5%
  • Personal services business rate: 44.5%
  • Top personal rate: 53.53% over $258,482
  • CPP 2026: $74,600 maximum pensionable earnings, $4,230.45 maximum employee contribution
  • GST/HST registration: Once taxable sales pass $30,000 in four calendar quarters
  • Ontario annual return: No fee, within six months after year-end

Who This Is For / Not For

Fit Check
  • For: Ontario employees with a profitable side business, several clients, and money they can leave in a corporation.
  • Not For: Side businesses still losing money, and contractors who work mainly for one client who controls their hours, where personal services business rules apply.
Verdict

Stay a sole proprietor while your side business is small or losing money: the losses reduce the tax on your salary and CPP stops once your job maxes it. Incorporate once profit is steady, comes from more than one client, and you can leave money in the company, so the 12.2% rate works for you. Check your employment contract before either step.

2026 Update

2026 Update — what is current: This article reflects rules current to November 2026. Ontario's combined small business rate is 12.2% on the first $500,000 and the general rate is 26.5%. The top personal rate of 53.53% starts at $258,482. CPP maximum pensionable earnings are $74,600, with a maximum employee contribution of $4,230.45 plus up to $416 of CPP2 on earnings up to $85,000. Ontario charges $300 to incorporate online and no fee for the annual return. The GST/HST small supplier threshold remains $30,000.
