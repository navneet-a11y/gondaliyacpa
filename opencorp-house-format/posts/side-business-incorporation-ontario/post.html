How Incorporating a Side Business Works Under Ontario Law

2

How It Works in Ontario

The Law

If you want to incorporate a side business while employed in Ontario, you create a separate legal entity. The Ontario Business Corporations Act (OBCA) governs this process. Starting a corporation while employed means you file articles of incorporation with the Ontario Business Registry. You’ll also need a NUANS name search and to appoint directors.

Side business incorporation Ontario requires meeting some rules. For example, your corporation must have a registered office address in Ontario. You must also submit annual returns on time. If your revenue passes CRA thresholds, you have to register for GST/HST accounts.

Incorporation changes how you report income. Corporate profits get taxed at corporate rates, not personal tax rates like sole proprietorships do. But incorporating does not change your employment status or duties unless your contract or employer says so.

Key Differences Between Sole Proprietorship and Incorporation for Side Businesses

Choosing between sole proprietorship vs corporation for a side business affects many things: liability, taxes, costs, and reporting.

Here’s a quick comparison:

Legal Status

Sole proprietorship isn’t separate from you personally. A corporation is its own legal entity. Incorporation separates your personal assets from the business.

Tax Treatment

Sole proprietorship income is taxed at your personal rate. Corporations pay corporate tax rates and may defer some taxes on retained earnings.

Liability

Sole proprietors face unlimited personal liability. Corporations offer limited liability protection, which can protect your personal assets.

Compliance

Sole proprietors file very little paperwork. Corporations must submit annual returns and keep detailed records.

Losses

Losses from sole proprietorships can offset other income on your tax return. Corporate losses stay inside the company and can’t reduce your salary income.

For many employees with small side projects, sole proprietorship might be simpler at first. Incorporation makes sense if you want liability protection or plan to keep profits in the company—but it comes with more work and cost.

Liability Protection: Unlimited Personal Liability vs. Limited Corporate Liability

When you choose side business incorporation Ontario, your personal assets stay separate from business debts because of limited liability protection.

If you run a sole proprietorship, all your assets — like your house or savings — are at risk if something goes wrong.

Limited liability only works if you follow rules properly:

  • Keep personal and company money apart.
  • Don’t mix up transactions.
  • Keep records as OBCA requires.

If you don’t do these things, courts might “pierce the veil” and make you personally liable anyway.

Employees thinking about incorporating should decide if shielding themselves from lawsuits is worth the extra steps involved in starting a corporation while employed.

Tax Implications of Operating a Side Business as Sole Proprietor or Corporation in Ontario

3

Tax Implications

Tax

If you run a side business while working in Ontario, taxes can get tricky depending on how you set it up. You can either operate as a sole proprietor or create a corporation. Each choice affects your taxes differently.

As a sole proprietor, you report your side business income along with your regular job salary on your personal tax return (Form T2125). That means the profit is taxed at your personal tax rate. If your side business loses money, those losses can reduce the tax on your other income.

Starting a corporation separates your side business income from your personal earnings. The corporation files its own tax return (T2) and pays corporate taxes on profits. When you take money out as salary or dividends, you pay personal tax on that amount separately. Any losses stay with the company and can’t reduce your employment income.

Incorporation comes with extra paperwork like annual filings and bookkeeping rules. You may also need payroll records if you pay yourself wages. One benefit is you might defer some taxes by keeping earnings inside the corporation, but that doesn’t mean you save money forever.

Personal Tax Rates Compared to Corporate Small Business Tax Rates

Knowing how personal and corporate tax rates compare helps decide if incorporating makes sense for your side hustle in Ontario.

Ontario’s top combined federal and provincial personal tax rate hits about 53.53%. This kicks in when income goes over $258,482 per year in 2026.

On the other hand, Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) get a small business deduction (SBD). This lowers tax on active business income up to $500,000 each year.

Here’s how rates stack up for 2026:

  • Combined federal & Ontario SBD rate: 12.2% on first $500K active CCPC business income
  • General corporate rate above $500K: about 26.5%
  • Top marginal personal income rate: 53.53% over high-income levels

Because corporate rates are lower, you might hold profits in the company to pay less tax now. But this depends on things like when and how you take money out.

Our Actual Experience

The question we hear most from employees is whether their corporation can skip CPP because their job already maxed it. It cannot: salary from your own corporation attracts CPP again, so for employees who already max out CPP at work we usually look at dividends first.

Benefits and Limitations of the Small Business Deduction for Incorporated Side Businesses

The small business deduction cuts federal and provincial taxes on active business income up to $500,000 for CCPCs in Ontario.

Benefits:

  • Cuts combined corporate tax from around 26–27% down to about 12%
  • Lets you keep more after-tax profits inside the corporation
  • Helps reinvest or delay taking money personally

Limitations:

  • Only applies if your business is “active” – no passive or investment income
  • Associated corporations share one $500K limit together
  • If labeled a Personal Services Business (PSB), you lose this deduction

About GST/HST:

If your revenue passes CAD$30,000 over four straight quarters (including related parties), you must register for GST/HST even if incorporated. Below that, registration is optional but brings filing rules.

Understanding Personal Services Business Rules and Their Effect on Taxation

Personal Services Businesses (PSBs) happen when an incorporated company works like an employee under contract with one client — often controlled by that client like a boss would be.

If you start a corporation but basically do employee-like work for one client:

  • Your company might be called a PSB by tax authorities
  • PSBs don’t get the small business deduction; they pay a higher corporate rate (44.5% combined in Ontario)
  • They also face limits on what expenses they can deduct

What raises PSB risk? Think:

  • Working for just one client who controls how/when/where you work
  • Client provides tools or equipment instead of you having them yourself
  • Your work looks like traditional employment

If your side client seems close to an employer, check with a CPA before incorporating. Wrong classification means higher taxes later.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: If your side corporation works mainly for one client who controls your hours and tools, the CRA can treat it as a personal services business and tax its income at 44.5% in Ontario. Please have the client relationship reviewed before you incorporate.

Tax Planning Strategies: Salary, Dividends, and CPP Contributions from Your Corporation

When running a corporation while employed:

Salary:

Paying yourself wages boosts RRSP contribution room because it counts as earned income. But watch CPP contributions—they apply even if you already maxed out through your job paycheques, and only the extra employee share comes back when you file. In 2026, max pensionable earnings hit $74,600 with max employee CPP contribution of $4,230.45, plus up to $416 of CPP2 on earnings up to $85,000.

Dividends:

These don’t create RRSP room nor require CPP payments. They get taxed differently based on whether they are eligible or non‑eligible dividends reflecting underlying corporate taxes paid.

Choosing between salary and dividends depends on things like:

  • How much CPP you've contributed via regular job paycheques
  • Cash flow needs from your corporation

Mixing salary and dividends right takes planning since wrong moves can cost more taxes or lost benefits federally and provincially.

Costs

Picking between a sole proprietorship and incorporating your side business in Ontario means looking at costs, rules, and taxes. Incorporating usually costs more up front and each year. But for some employees, those costs might pay off.

A sole proprietorship costs little to start. Usually, you just pay for business licenses. You don’t pay incorporation fees or file corporate returns. You report income on personal tax forms like the T2125.

Incorporation means paying Ontario’s government filing fee plus service fees from providers like Open Corporation, which charges about $35. After that, you must file a corporate tax return (T2), keep detailed minutes, and send annual returns to the Ontario Business Registry.

You can do incorporation yourself to save money but risk mistakes that cost more later. Financial Considerations When Transitioning from Sole Proprietorship to Corporation

Losses affect taxes differently in each setup — this matters when switching structures:

As a sole proprietor using Form T2125 on your personal return (T1), losses directly reduce other income like salaries that year [ITA].

This means early losses won’t lower your personal taxes once incorporated. That might mean paying more taxes until the corporation makes money.

It helps to plan carefully if waiting until steady profits show before incorporating works better financially than switching too soon with unused losses.

Registering for GST/HST and Other Required Business Accounts

You need to think about GST/HST registration for your side business. The CRA says you must register once your taxable revenue goes over $30,000 in four straight calendar quarters. That’s called the small supplier threshold.

If your side business makes less than $30K during its first year or as you switch from sole proprietor to corporation, you don’t have to register yet. But some people choose to register early to get input tax credits or better cash flow.

Besides GST/HST, other accounts might be necessary:

Comparing Reporting Side Business Income Personally Versus Through a Corporation

6

Reporting, Losses and Risks

Reporting

When you have a side business while employed in Ontario, you can report income personally or through a corporation. Both ways have different tax and compliance effects.

If you run your side business as a sole proprietor, you report income on Form T2125 with your personal T1 tax return. Your business profit adds to your salary and is taxed at your regular personal rate. So, the money flows straight to you without any corporate layer.

If you start a corporation while employed, the company files its own T2 tax return. The corporation pays taxes on profits at corporate rates. You might keep some earnings inside the company to delay taxes. But any salary or dividends paid to you are taxed personally.

Here are key points about tax implications when incorporating a side business:

  • Corporate earnings can be kept inside to delay personal taxes.
  • Corporations file separate returns from your personal taxes.
  • Incorporation brings more paperwork like bookkeeping and payroll.
  • Your salary plus dividends increase your personal taxable income.

Choosing how to report depends on profits, cash needs, risk, and how much admin work you want. Both ways have pros and cons around tax timing and complexity.

Handling Start-Up Losses, Home Office, and Vehicle Expenses for Tax Purposes

Start-up losses happen often with new businesses. How you handle them depends on whether you’re incorporated or not.

As a sole proprietor using Form T2125 on your T1 return, losses can reduce other income like your job wages right away.[1] This lowers the taxes due in that year.

Corporations keep losses inside the company. These can only offset future profits within the corporation.[2] You can’t use these losses against your personal employment income outside the company.

For home office expenses when incorporating a side business in Ontario:

  • Sole proprietors claim parts of utilities, rent (or mortgage interest), and property taxes based on workspace size.
  • Corporations can reimburse you for home office costs under a written arrangement; otherwise claiming those expenses needs good records.[3]

Vehicle costs need careful logs showing how much is for business versus personal use.[4] Deductible amounts depend on kilometres driven strictly for work related to either sole proprietorship or incorporated side hustle.

Do you need a GST/HST number for your side business? If combined revenues go over $30,000 in four straight calendar quarters across all businesses—even incorporated ones—you must register.[5] This affects collecting GST/HST and input credit claims.

FAQ
What is the Ontario Small Business Deduction and how does it help incorporated side businesses?+

The Ontario Small Business Deduction reduces corporate tax rates on active business income up to $500,000. This lowers taxes and allows corporations to retain more profits.

How does the Ontario Combined Small Business Rate compare to general corporate tax rates?+

The combined small business rate in Ontario is about 12.2% on the first $500,000 of active business income. The general corporate rate above that is approximately 26.5%.

What is the Top Combined Marginal Personal Tax Rate in Ontario?+

The top personal tax rate in Ontario reaches about 53.53%, applying to income over $258,482 annually in 2026.

What are the CPP Maximum Pensionable Earnings and Employee Contribution limits?+

For 2026, CPP maximum pensionable earnings are $74,600, with a max employee contribution of $4,230.45, plus up to $416 of CPP2 on earnings up to $85,000.

What is the RRSP Dollar Limit and how does paying yourself salary impact it?+

RRSP contribution room depends on earned income. Salary paid from your corporation increases your RRSP limit, unlike dividends.

When do you need to register for GST/HST as a side business?+

You must register if your total taxable revenues exceed $30,000 in four consecutive calendar quarters.

What is the Ontario Business Registry Filing Fee for incorporation?+

The fee for filing articles of incorporation in Ontario is $300 when filed online.

Worth Knowing

The $30,000 GST/HST threshold counts the taxable sales of associated businesses together. Please add up every business you and your corporation run before deciding you are under it.

Summary

Sole Proprietorship vs Corporation for a Side Business: Which fits?

Sole proprietorship suits small side gigs with low risk; incorporation benefits those needing liability protection or tax deferral.

Does the Small Business Deduction create a deferral or a saving?

It creates tax deferral by lowering corporate taxes but personal taxes apply when withdrawing funds.

What is the Personal Services Business risk for a side client?

If your corporation acts like an employee of one client, PSB rules apply, raising taxes and limiting deductions.

How should you pay yourself when you already earn a salary?

A mix of salary and dividends often works; salary creates RRSP room but attracts CPP even if your job already maxed it, while dividends avoid payroll costs.

What happens to start-up losses in each structure?

Sole proprietors can deduct losses against other income immediately; corporations carry losses forward but can’t offset personal wages.

Do you need a GST/HST number for a side business?

Yes, if combined revenues cross $30K over four quarters; otherwise registration is optional but beneficial.

What do you file with the CRA: T1, T2 or both?

You file T1 for personal income including sole proprietorship earnings; corporations file T2 for business profits separately.

What should you check with your employer first?

Review contracts for conflicts of interest, moonlighting rules, non-compete clauses and IP ownership before starting a side corporation.
