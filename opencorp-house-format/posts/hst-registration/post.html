Understanding HST and GST/HST Registration in Canada

1

Understanding GST/HST Registration

The Basics

In Canada, businesses deal with two main taxes: the Goods and Services Tax (GST) and the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST). These taxes follow federal rules. Getting your HST registration right matters a lot. It tells you when to start charging tax on what you sell.

Overview of GST, PST, and HST Taxes Across Canadian Provinces

Canada has three main sales taxes: GST, PST, and HST.

  • GST is a federal tax at 5% everywhere.
  • PST is different in each province; some places don’t have it.
  • HST combines GST and some provincial taxes. For example, Ontario’s HST rate is 13%.

Knowing these helps your business understand what taxes you must charge.

Key Definitions: Small Supplier, Taxable Supplies, and Exempt Supplies

Here are some terms that matter for HST registration threshold Canada:

  • A Small Supplier makes $30,000 or less in taxable sales over four quarters. Pass this limit? You must register for HST.
  • Taxable Supplies are most goods or services where you add tax. Like things sold in stores or services you offer.
  • Exempt Supplies don’t get taxed. Think of some health care or education services.

These words help you see if your business needs to sign up for HST.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: The $30,000 test counts the taxable sales of associated businesses as well as your own. Please do not test your own sales alone.

Importance of Compliance with HST Registration Requirements

Following GST/HST registration requirements keeps your business legal in Canada. If you skip registration, CRA might fine you. Also:

  • Registered businesses can get back money spent on taxes through input tax credits.
  • Customers trust invoices from registered companies more.
  • Being on time avoids costly fees from late registration or audits.

Staying on top of this keeps your money safe.

When to Register for HST in Canada

2

When to Register

Timing

Knowing when to register for HST depends on your sales hitting the $30,000 mark soon. If you think sales will go over that during one quarter or four quarters combined, don’t wait—sign up quickly.

The $30,000 HST Registration Threshold Explained

The $30,000 threshold means if your taxable supplies go over this number in a quarter—or in total over four quarters—you have to register with CRA, and in the single-quarter case you charge tax starting with the sale that takes you over.

This table shows when your business must act once sales cross certain points.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: If one quarter's sales go over $30,000, tax applies to the very sale that takes you over. Please do not wait for the 29-day registration window to start charging.

Worth Knowing

If your last four quarters together go over $30,000, you stop being a small supplier at the end of the month after that quarter, and you charge tax from the first day of the following month.

Mandatory vs Voluntary HST Registration Criteria

There are two kinds of GST/HST registration requirements: mandatory and voluntary.

  • Mandatory: You must register when taxable sales go over $30K in those periods above.
  • Voluntary: Even below $30K, some businesses register early to claim input tax credits faster.

You decide based on your situation.

Special Considerations for Taxi Operators and Commercial Ride-Sharing Drivers

Taxi drivers have special rules for GST/HST because they take many fares daily compared to others.

No matter how much they earn yearly—they must register from their first fare, because a special rule in the Excise Tax Act removes the small supplier exemption for taxi and commercial ride-sharing services.

How New Businesses Should Determine Their Registration Timing

New businesses should watch their sales closely from the start. Check quarterly numbers often! This helps spot if you’re nearing that $30K mark early on so you can register before it becomes a problem later on after business grows big enough!

Our Actual Experience

The late registration we see most often is a consultant who crosses $30,000 over four quarters without noticing, because no single quarter looked large. We suggest adding up the last four quarters at the end of every quarter, not just checking the latest one.

How to Register for a GST/HST Account

4

How to Register

Registration

It’s simple to register your GST/HST account once you have all info ready that meets HST registration Canada rules.

Using Business Registration Online (BRO) for GST/HST Registration

Business Registration Online (BRO) is CRA’s web portal where you can quickly open a new Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax account linked to your Business Number (BN).

Here’s how:

  1. Go to BRO at Canada.ca.
  2. Fill in your legal name, contact info, and fiscal year end.
  3. Pick “GST/HST” as program type.
  4. Send it online — usually get confirmation right away.

Using BRO follows all current GST/HST online registration rules so no paper delays happen.

Alternative Registration Methods Beyond BRO

If BRO doesn’t work for you or you don’t want to use it:

  • Call CRA's Business Enquiries line to register by phone
  • Mail in Form RC1 - Request for a Business Number

Both ways follow the same sales threshold rules but take longer than online (CRA Form RC1 and Guide RC4022).

You can also choose voluntary HST registration options if your sales are below $30,000 but want input tax credits earlier. Just remember once registered, full filing rules apply.

Requirements for Non-Resident GST/HST Registrations

Non-resident businesses doing commercial activities where HST applies in Canada must follow the GST/HST rules for doing business in Canada:

  • Get a BN with an RT account before selling taxable goods here
  • Might need to post security with CRA if they have no permanent establishment in Canada
  • Digital sellers into consumers can use simpler registrations

HST non-resident registrant rules make sure tax gets collected even without physical offices (CRA Memorandum 19‑6).

Worth Knowing

Non-resident sellers of digital products and services to Canadian consumers can register under a simplified system once those sales pass $30,000 over 12 months. Simplified registrants charge GST/HST but cannot claim input tax credits.

Process for Opening Separate GST/HST Accounts for Branches or Divisions

If your business runs many places across provinces, each branch or division can apply for its own GST/HST account. Each can have its own RT program account if it keeps separate records and qualifies under section 239.

Multiple GST/HST program accounts help track taxes right per place especially where supply places vary between provinces like Ontario vs British Columbia (Excise Tax Act s239).

Responsibilities After GST/HST Registration

5

After You Register

Obligations

After you register for HST in Canada, you have some new responsibilities. You need to know when to charge GST/HST, how to file your returns, how to claim input tax credits, and how to stay compliant. The CRA sets rules for GST/HST registration requirements Canada. Following these helps your business run smooth.

When to Start Charging GST/HST to Customers

You start charging GST or HST from your registration date. This date depends on when you pass the $30,000 HST registration threshold Canada or if you sign up earlier by choice. Your start date could be:

  • The day of the sale that takes you over $30,000 in taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter.
  • The first day of the second month after the calendar quarter in which your last 4 straight calendar quarters total more than $30,000.
  • A voluntary start date if you register before hitting the limit.

Don’t charge tax before this date—that might cause problems. But if you don’t charge tax after this date, you might owe money and get penalties.1

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Tax you should have charged but did not is still owed by your business. Please do not assume you can bill customers for it later.

Filing Obligations and Tax Remittance Requirements

Once you register under GST/HST registration requirements Canada, you must file tax returns regularly. These returns show the taxes you collected and what you owe.

Here’s what matters:

  • How often you file depends on your sales: once a year (up to $1.5 million), every quarter ($1.5 million–$6 million), or monthly (above $6 million).
  • Returns show sales with GST/HST and input tax credits you can claim.
  • You pay taxes one month after each monthly or quarterly reporting period ends, or three months after the year-end for most annual filers.

Missing deadlines leads to interest and penalties.2 Keep track of your filing dates so you don’t miss them.

Worth Knowing

An individual annual filer with business income and a December 31 year-end files by June 15 but must still pay by April 30.

Claiming Input Tax Credits and Managing Cash Flow

If registered, businesses can get back some GST/HST they paid through input tax credits (ITCs). ITCs lower the tax amount you owe by covering eligible business expenses.

Key points:

  • Save all receipts with supplier numbers.
  • Claim ITCs only for things used in your business; exempt items don’t count.
  • Claiming ITCs on time helps cash flow but needs careful record keeping.3

Some people register early just to claim ITCs even if sales are low. Talking with a CPA helps decide what’s right for you.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: An ITC claim on a purchase of $100 or more can be denied if the invoice does not show the supplier's GST/HST registration number. Please do not claim from receipts that lack it.

Maintaining Compliance and Avoiding Late Registration Penalties

You must register within 29 days after crossing the threshold or starting certain services like taxis or digital sales.

If you delay:

  • You may owe taxes for past sales without charging customers.
  • Interest and penalties can apply on tax you should have collected; Excise Tax Act section 240 sets the duty to register.4

Good records help spot when thresholds are passed. If unsure about timing or types of sales, ask an expert early.

HST Rates and Provincial Variations

7

Rates and Provinces

Rates

Explanation of GST Federal Rate and Participating Provinces’ HST Rates

The federal GST rate in Canada is 5%. Some provinces mix their provincial sales tax with the GST to make the HST. In 2026, here are the HST rates: Ontario has 13%, Nova Scotia has 14%, and New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island all have 15%. Businesses need to know these rates for HST registration Canada. You must register if your taxable supplies go over $30,000 in four straight quarters or even one quarter.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Nova Scotia's HST fell from 15% to 14% on April 1, 2025. Please do not use the old rate on Nova Scotia invoices.

Sales Tax Collection Based on Customer Location (Place-of-Supply Rules)

Businesses collect GST or HST depending on where their customers are. This is called the place-of-supply rule. If you sell to a customer in an HST province, use that province’s HST rate. If your customer lives outside those provinces, only charge federal GST or local provincial sales tax if it applies. This rule helps you decide when to register for HST because you might owe different rates for different places.

Impact of the 2026 GST/HST Rates Update on Businesses

In 2026, the small supplier threshold stayed at $30,000. Filing rules also stay the same: file annually if you make less than $1.5 million; quarterly if you make between $1.5 million and $6 million; monthly if more than $6 million. The Quick Method remittance rates follow CRA rules too. These rules matter for GST/HST registration requirements Canada, especially if your business is growing or changing how often it files.

Key Stat

Key Stat: The thresholds to know are $30,000 for small suppliers, $50,000 for public service bodies, $400,000 for the Quick Method, $1,500,000 for annual filing and $6,000,000 for monthly filing.

GST/HST Application on Imports, Exports, and Zero-Rated Supplies

When goods come into Canada, usually GST/HST applies based on where they go unless a rule says otherwise. Exports are taxed at zero percent — that means no tax charged but these sales count when checking your $30,000 limit. Zero-rated supplies let you claim input tax credits but you don’t charge tax to buyers. This info helps figure your HST registration threshold Canada.

Worth Knowing

GST, or the federal part of HST, on goods imported into Canada is collected by the Canada Border Services Agency at the border, not by the seller.

Special Rules and Considerations

8

Special Rules

Special Cases

Taxi and Ride-Sharing Driver Registration Rules

Taxi drivers and ride-share folks must register for GST/HST no matter how much money they make. A special law says they can’t use the small supplier exemption even if they earn less than $30,000. So they have to follow rules on when to register for HST as soon as they start working in Ontario or other provinces.

Filing-Frequency Thresholds and Quick Method Remittance Rates

How often you file depends on how much taxable supplies you have yearly:

  • Under $1,500,000: file once a year
  • Between $1,500,000 and $6 million: file every quarter
  • Over $6 million: file monthly

Some registrants with annual taxable supplies of $400,000 or less can use the Quick Method with preset remittance rates that differ by province—like Ontario’s Quick Method rate (check current numbers). This method makes filing easier but be sure you qualify under the $400,000 limit before using it.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Under the Quick Method you cannot claim input tax credits on most operating expenses. Please do not elect it before comparing it with the regular method.

Voluntary Registration Backdating Limits and Conditions

If you register voluntarily, CRA may let you backdate your start date up to 30 days before applying. This can help get input tax credits from early expenses. But:

  • You can’t backdate before your first sale.
  • Once backdated, you must charge tax from that earlier date.
  • A CPA should check this carefully because wrong timing causes trouble tied to GST/HST registration requirements Canada.
Risk Warning

Risk Warning: A backdated registration means tax is owed on sales from the earlier date. Please do not backdate unless you can charge or absorb that tax.

Associated Persons and Their Effect on Registration Thresholds

When figuring out if you passed the $30,000 small supplier limit, include taxable supplies from associated persons too—businesses connected by ownership or control.[9] Forgetting these could mean missing mandatory registration deadlines under Canadian law.[10] Talk with a CPA about how this affects your HST registration threshold duties.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on HST Registration Canada

11

HST Registration FAQ

FAQ
What is the public service body threshold for HST registration?+

The public service body threshold is $50,000 in taxable supplies over four consecutive quarters. Public bodies must register when they exceed this limit.

What is the registration deadline after crossing the HST threshold?+

You must register within 29 days after exceeding the $30,000 taxable supply threshold or starting a registrable activity.

How does Excise Tax Act s. 148 affect registration?+

Section 148 defines the small supplier threshold and when businesses must register for GST/HST based on taxable revenues.

What penalties exist under Excise Tax Act s. 240 for late registration?+

Section 240 sets the duty to register; it does not set the penalty. Tax you should have charged is still owed, with interest, and penalties under other sections such as 280.1 and 285 can apply.

What are filing frequency thresholds for GST/HST returns?+

Filing frequency depends on annual taxable supplies: annually up to $1.5M; quarterly between $1.5M and $6M; monthly over $6M.

Who qualifies for Quick Method remittance?+

Businesses with taxable supplies under $400,000 generally qualify to use the Quick Method, which simplifies tax remittance calculations.

Can I backdate voluntary registration for GST/HST?+

Yes, CRA generally allows backdating up to 30 days before applying if you were eligible and want to claim input tax credits earlier.

What are CRA Guide RC4022 and RC4027?+

RC4022 explains registration, filing frequencies, input tax credits and payment methods; RC4027 covers GST/HST for non-residents doing business in Canada.

How do digital economy suppliers handle HST registration?+

Digital suppliers selling to Canadian customers must register if their sales exceed thresholds, following place-of-supply rules for proper taxation.

What is a Business Number and RT0001 account?+

The Business Number (BN) is your unique federal ID. RT0001 is the GST/HST program account attached to your BN.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Taxi and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register before their first fare. Please do not rely on the $30,000 threshold if you drive commercially.

Quick Answers: Key Numbers at a Glance

At a Glance
  • Small supplier threshold: $30,000 in one calendar quarter or four straight quarters
  • Public service bodies: $50,000
  • Registration deadline: 29 days after crossing the threshold
  • Taxi and ride-sharing: Register from the first fare
  • Voluntary backdating: Generally up to 30 days
  • GST rate: 5%
  • HST rates: 13% Ontario, 14% Nova Scotia, 15% New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and Prince Edward Island
  • Quick Method limit: $400,000 in annual taxable supplies
  • Filing frequency: Annual up to $1,500,000, quarterly up to $6,000,000, monthly above
  • ITC invoice detail: Supplier's GST/HST number needed on purchases of $100 or more

Who This Is For / Not For

Fit Check
  • For: New businesses tracking their sales, owners close to $30,000, anyone whose customers are mostly GST/HST-registered businesses, and taxi or ride-sharing drivers.
  • Not For: Businesses that only make exempt supplies, such as most residential rent or certain health services, which do not register for those sales.
Verdict

Register when your taxable sales, with any associated businesses, pass $30,000 in a quarter or over four quarters, and work out your effective date first, because tax can start before you register. If most of your customers are registered businesses, registering early usually costs them nothing and lets you recover tax on your start-up costs.

2026 Update

2026 Update — what is current: This article reflects rules current to 2026. The $30,000 small supplier threshold, the $50,000 threshold for public service bodies, the 29-day registration window, the $1,500,000 and $6,000,000 filing-frequency limits, the $400,000 Quick Method limit and the 5% GST rate are unchanged. HST is 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia since April 1, 2025, and 15% in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and Prince Edward Island. Please note that taxi and commercial ride-sharing drivers must register from their first fare whatever their sales; that the $30,000 test counts worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports and those of associated persons, and not exempt supplies; that a voluntary registration can generally be backdated by no more than 30 days; and that an ITC claim on a purchase of $100 or more needs the supplier's GST/HST registration number on the invoice.
