Business Account Checklist for Canadian Corporations

The Document Checklist

Checklist

If you want to open a corporate bank account in Canada, you’ll need to gather some key documents first. This business account checklist helps new business owners figure out what to prepare. Different banks might ask for different things, but this list covers the basics.

Essential Documents Needed to Open a Corporate Bank Account

To get started, you usually need these important papers:

  • Certificate of Incorporation: This shows your corporation is legally registered, either federally or provincially.
  • Articles of Incorporation: This document lays out how your company is set up and what it does.
  • CRA Business Number (BN): The Canada Revenue Agency gives this number to identify your business for taxes.
  • Government-Issued Identification: You’ll need valid photo ID like a driver’s license or passport for all owners and anyone who can sign on the account.

Keep in mind, each bank may have its own specific list because of rules from PCMLTFA and FINTRAC. Always check with your bank before applying.

Key Stat

25%: the ownership level at which banks must identify a person as a beneficial owner under FINTRAC rules, and the same level that puts a person on your ISC register under the CBCA and OBCA.

Corporate Banking Checklist: What You Should Prepare Before Applying

Besides incorporation papers, banks want extra info about who runs the company and where it operates. Here’s what you should have ready:

  • Directors’ Register: A current list of people who manage the corporation.
  • Shareholders’ Register: Records showing who owns shares and how much.
  • Individual with Significant Control (ISC) or Beneficial Ownership Information: Info about anyone owning 25% or more, as per FINTRAC rules.
  • Proof of Registered Office Address: Paperwork that confirms your official business address filed with Corporations Canada or provincial registry.

Having these ready helps avoid delays when banks check identities for anti-money laundering laws.

CRA Business Number and Its Role in Business Banking

Your CRA business number isn’t just for taxes. It also shows banks that the government officially recognizes your business. Here’s why it matters:

  • It lets you register for program accounts like GST/HST, payroll deductions, or import/export accounts banks might ask about.
  • Banks see having an active BN as a sign you're set up for tax reporting.

Some banks may open accounts without a BN, but having one speeds things up when dealing with government programs.1

Articles of Incorporation and Shareholder Information Requirements

Banks want clear articles of incorporation because they show who has legal power in your company. Along with shareholder info, this helps them understand control and ownership.

Articles explain share classes if there are different types.

They outline director powers which affect who can sign banking documents.

Keeping shareholder registers updated ensures clear ownership details. This supports proper disclosure under CBCA/OBCA rules about individuals with significant control.2

Step-by-Step Guide to Opening a Corporate Bank Account in Canada

3

Step-by-Step Opening

Steps

Opening a corporate bank account in Canada means you have to prepare well. You need to know the business bank account requirements Canada before you start. This guide shows you each step and what documents you’ll need. It’s like your own business account checklist.

Eligibility Criteria for Opening a Corporate Bank Account

To open a corporate bank account Canada banks want to see some proof first. Your company needs to meet certain rules, which follow Canadian law and FINTRAC regulations. Here’s what most banks ask for:

  • Your business must be officially incorporated either federally or provincially.
  • You must have valid Articles of Incorporation as proof.
  • The company needs a real registered office address in Canada.
  • Banks want ID details for the directors or signing officers who will use the account.
  • They ask for info about people who own 25% or more of shares, because of PCMLTFA/FINTRAC rules.

Every bank has its own slight differences but they all follow these legal standards. So, check with your chosen bank about their specific requirements.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Please do not deposit business income into your personal account while the corporate account is being opened. Mixed funds make the corporation's records harder to prove and can be treated as a shareholder loan or benefit.

Procedure for Opening a Business Banking Account After Incorporation

Once your company is incorporated, here’s what you do next:

Collect these key documents:

  • Certificate of Incorporation
  • Articles of Incorporation
  • Corporate Profile Report from Corporations Canada or your province
  • By-laws or banking resolution that names authorized signers
  • Lists of directors and shareholders showing who owns what
  • Register of Individuals with Significant Control (ISC) if needed
  • CRA Business Number confirmation
  • Verify identity: All signing officers must give government photo ID following FINTRAC rules.
  • Prepare ownership records: Show owners with at least 25% shares per regulations.
  • Provide proof of office address: Bills or lease papers usually work.

This list matches common business bank account requirements Canada but may change depending on the bank.

Worth Knowing

A CRA business number is not legally required to open a corporate account, but Ontario and federal incorporations receive one automatically, so please bring the business number confirmation to the bank.

How to Apply for a Business Bank Account Online

Applying online makes things faster but you still need good documents:

  • Go to the bank’s website for corporate accounts.
  • Fill out the forms carefully with info that matches your incorporation papers.
  • Upload clear scans of all required documents.
  • Authorized signers should give digital signatures if asked.

Banks check all this under PCMLTFA rules. They verify owners and IDs remotely when allowed. Keep in mind, each bank decides whether to approve based on their own policies.

What to Expect During the Account Opening Process

Here’s what usually happens after you send in your application:

  • The bank checks your papers against their compliance list based on Canadian laws like PCMLTFA/FINTRAC.
  • Sometimes, they ask for an interview by video call or in person with directors or signers to confirm identity.
  • When approved, they send you confirmation and instructions on how to use your new account online.

The time it takes can vary a lot since each bank works differently. No one can promise how fast it will go.

Delays happen mostly because names don’t match across documents, ownership info is missing, IDs are expired, ISC registers are absent, or public business names aren’t registered.

FAQs on Corporate Bank Account Setup and Compliance

7

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ
What is a corporate profile report and why do banks require it?+

A corporate profile report shows your company’s official details. Banks use it to verify incorporation status and director information.

What are by-laws or banking resolutions in relation to bank accounts?+

By-laws or banking resolutions authorize who can open and operate the bank account. Banks require these documents to confirm signers’ authority.

How important are shareholder registers for corporate banking?+

Shareholder registers list owners and share distribution. Banks review them to identify beneficial owners and comply with FINTRAC rules.

What is an ISC register and who must be included?+

An ISC (Individuals with Significant Control) register tracks people owning 25% or more shares or voting rights. It is required under corporate law, the CBCA federally and the OBCA in Ontario, and banks often ask to see it.

Can non-resident directors open a corporate bank account in Canada?+

Yes, but banks may ask for notarized ID or apostilled foreign documents to verify identity under FINTRAC guidelines.

What role does a share subscription agreement play in banking?+

This agreement confirms how shares were issued and paid for. Banks may request it to verify ownership legitimacy.

Why do banks ask for a shareholder loan agreement?+

It clarifies loans from shareholders to the corporation. Banks use this to assess financial obligations and compliance with tax rules.

What is a minute book, and do banks require it?+

A minute book stores all corporate records, including resolutions. Some banks request it as proof of proper governance.

How does banking resolution drafting help in account setup?+

Properly drafted resolutions ensure authorized persons can transact. It avoids delays during bank approval processes.

What is post-incorporation setup CPA support?+

CPA support helps organize documents, register accounts, and advise on compliance after incorporation for smooth banking relations.

How does legal drafting of resolutions affect banking approvals?+

Well-drafted resolutions meet bank policies for authorization. Poorly prepared documents cause rejections or delays.

What are business account funding entries?+

These are initial deposits made when opening the account. Banks record them to confirm active business operations.

Worth Knowing

Canada joined the Apostille Convention on January 11, 2024, so many foreign documents for non-resident directors can now be apostilled instead of going through consular legalization. Please ask your bank which format it accepts.
