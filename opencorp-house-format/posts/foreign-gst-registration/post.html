GST/HST for Foreign Businesses

The Basics

If you run a foreign business and want to sell stuff in Canada, you need to understand GST and HST registration. The rules for foreign business GST registration in Canada can be tricky but knowing them helps you avoid problems.

Understanding GST/HST and Its Application to Non-Resident Businesses

GST is a federal tax. It adds 5% to most goods and services sold in Canada. In Ontario, GST joins with provincial tax to make HST, which totals 13%. Quebec has its own tax called QST, set at 9.975%, which makes 14.975% together with the 5% GST. These taxes work differently depending on who you sell to — customers or other registered businesses.

Who Must Register: Criteria for Foreign Businesses and Non-Residents

Not every foreign business must register. You should consider these points:

  • Small Supplier Threshold: If your taxable sales go over $30,000 in four straight quarters, registration is required.
  • Carrying On Business in Canada: Doing active business here, like holding contracts or stock, might mean you must register.
  • Non-Resident Security Deposit: Some non-residents need to provide a security deposit when they register.

These rules tell you if you must sign up under Canadian laws.

The Small Supplier Threshold and Its Impact on Registration Requirements

The small supplier threshold is key. If your taxable revenue goes above $30,000 in any 12-month span, you can’t stay exempt. You have to register either under the regular system or use simplified options made for digital sellers.

Key Stat

$30,000: the same threshold triggers both regimes. Under the regular regime it counts all your taxable supplies, including zero-rated ones; under the simplified regime it counts sales to Canadian consumers over 12 months.

Simplified GST/HST Registration Options for Foreign Companies

For foreign companies selling online mostly to Canadians, there’s a simplified GST/HST registration. It applies once their sales to Canadian consumers pass $30,000 in 12 months. You file quarterly returns but don’t claim input tax credits. This process cuts down on paperwork compared to the regular registration.

Differences Between GST, HST, PST, and QST for Foreign Sellers

Every province handles these taxes differently. Knowing where your customers live helps figure out which rate applies.

If foreign businesses get these basics right about non-resident GST/HST registration, they’ll be ready before entering Canada’s market and won’t break local tax rules.

Determining If You Are Carrying On Business in Canada

If you're a foreign business, you need to figure out if you're carrying on business in Canada. This helps decide if you must register for non-resident GST/HST registration. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) looks at many things like having contracts with Canadian customers, owning inventory or staff in Canada, or using agents inside the country. No single thing proves you're carrying on business; it’s about a mix of activities.

Here’s what to check:

  • Do you have contracts with customers in Canada?
  • Do you keep stock or have workers here?
  • Do you use local agents or representatives?

If your sales pass the small supplier threshold while doing business in Canada, registration becomes necessary[1]. Open Corporation for $35 is part of Gondaliya CPA and can help check your situation.

Defining Permanent Establishment and Its Relevance to GST/HST

A permanent establishment means a fixed place where a foreign company does business in Canada. This mainly affects income tax but can matter for GST/HST too.

Having a permanent establishment might mean your company has to register regularly for GST/HST rather than using simpler rules. Still, getting a Canadian GST number depends more on actual taxable sales made in Canada than just on permanent establishment status[2].

Taxable Supplies, Zero-Rated Supplies, and Exempt Supplies

Taxable supplies are most goods and services foreign businesses sell into Canada that attract GST or HST at regular rates. Zero-rated supplies are taxable but charged at 0%, like basic groceries or exports outside Canada. Exempt supplies don’t have any tax, such as some health care services.

Why does this matter? Only taxable supplies count toward the small supplier threshold that forces registration[3]. So you must track sales carefully by these types.

Calculating Taxable Revenue and Thresholds for Registration

The small supplier threshold sets the limit when non-residents must register based on taxable revenues from worldwide sales linked to Canadian buyers over four quarters. In 2026, that amount is $30,000 CAD exactly[4].

There’s also a simplified regime for some digital sellers without physical presence here. Crossing $30,000 CAD over 12 months means quarterly filings but no input tax credits allowed[5].

You calculate by adding all taxable supply revenue, including zero-rated sales, and skipping exempt sales.

Voluntary Registration for Foreign Businesses Below the Threshold

If your foreign company earns less than the small supplier threshold, you can still register voluntarily. This helps if you want to claim input tax credits or start compliance early.

Voluntary registrants must file fully even with lower revenue[6]. This option is good if you expect growth beyond thresholds or want formal recognition by CRA now.

Voluntary non-resident GST/HST registration allows claiming input tax credits not available under simplified rules but requires more complex filings and security deposits unless exempt.

Security Deposit Requirements and Exceptions for Non-Residents

Non-residents who register under regular rules usually need to post security deposits to cover possible unpaid taxes plus interest under section 240(6) of the Excise Tax Act[7]. The deposit is generally 50% of the estimated net tax for the first year, with a minimum of $5,000 and a maximum of $1,000,000.

There are exceptions:

  • Simplified regime registrants generally don’t post deposits.
  • Non-residents whose annual taxable supplies are $1,500,000 or less and whose net tax is $3,000 or less do not post security.
  • Low-risk applicants might qualify too[8].

These deposits protect the government since it’s harder to collect from overseas businesses that don’t comply.

Most foreign businesses registering normally will face deposit rules unless they qualify for exceptions—check with experts first.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: The CRA will not finish a regular non-resident registration until the security is posted. Please budget for the deposit, generally at least $5,000, before you apply, so your registration and first invoices are not held up.

Business Number (BN) and Its Role in GST/HST Registration

A Business Number (BN) from CRA identifies your company’s federal accounts like GST/HST across provinces[9]. When registering for GST/HST as a foreign business using Form RC1, you get an RT account suffix (“RT0001”) linked to your BN specifically for Canadian GST/HST[10].

With this BN, you file returns and pay taxes properly based on each province’s rate—for example, Ontario’s HST is 13%[11].

Registration Methods for Non-Resident GST/HST Accounts

Non-resident businesses have two main ways to register: the regular GST/HST registration or the simplified GST/HST registration Canada program.

  • The regular way applies if you do business in Canada and your sales pass $30,000 CAD over 12 months.
  • This method lets you claim input tax credits but needs detailed filings.
  • The simplified method works well for digital sellers who don’t have a physical place or permanent setup in Canada.
  • It’s easier to manage.
  • You file quarterly.
  • No input tax credits are allowed.
  • Registration is required once sales to consumers pass $30,000 in 12 months.

Both options require applying through CRA channels but differ in who qualifies and what you must do after registering.

Step-by-Step Guide to Registering for a GST/HST Account

  1. Check if you meet the rules for doing business and sales amounts.
  2. Pick either the regular or simplified registration based on what you sell and who buys it.
  3. Collect all info and papers needed (see next section).
  4. Fill out Form RC1 and send it to the CRA by mail or fax, since non-residents cannot use Business Registration Online.
  5. Get your Business Number (BN) after approval (like BN123456789RT0001).
  6. Start filing your returns as required—monthly, quarterly, or yearly depending on your choice.

This process helps foreign firms follow Canadian tax rules without fuss.

  • Contact info like phone number and email
  • Info about any agents or reps you have inside Canada
  • Banking info if you want refunds sent by direct deposit

CRA may ask for extra proof of identity or business activities before they finish your registration.

Effective Date of GST/HST Registration and Backdating Rules

Your GST/HST account starts either on the day CRA gets your application or an earlier date if you ask for it within limits tied to when you began doing business in Canada.

Backdating a voluntary registration can go back up to 30 days if you have good reasons, like not knowing about the need sooner. You must include proof when applying for this.

Registering on time keeps you safe from penalties for taxes owed since your first taxable sale that went over thresholds.

Post-Registration Responsibilities for Foreign Businesses

After You Register

Compliance

When foreign businesses complete their GST registration in Canada, they face several ongoing duties. Non-resident GST/HST registration means you must follow Canadian tax rules closely. You’ll have to file returns, make payments, keep records, and report accurately. Open Corporation for $35, part of Gondaliya CPA, helps non-residents handle these tasks.

Filing GST/HST Returns: Reporting Periods and Due Dates

Non-residents file GST/HST returns based on their reporting period set by CRA. Most foreign businesses registered regularly file monthly or quarterly returns depending on annual sales in Canada. The CRA wants your return by the last day of the month after your reporting period ends.

If you registered under the simplified GST/HST registration Canada system — often digital sellers with sales over $30,000 over 12 months — you file quarterly. These forms are simpler and don’t let you claim input tax credits.

Late filings can trigger penalties and interest. Always check your filing frequency when you register, either via Form RC1 or CRA letters.

Remitting GST/HST Payments and Accepted Payment Methods

After collecting GST/HST, foreign businesses must send payments on time. The CRA may ask non-residents to post a security deposit under Excise Tax Act section 240(6) if they think there's a risk of unpaid tax.

You can pay by online banking, wire transfer, the CRA's My Payment service, or cheque payable to the Receiver General for Canada. Using these approved methods helps avoid delays or fines.

Security deposits vary based on factors like sales size and past compliance. Some businesses might get exemptions, but this needs expert review.

Input Tax Credits (ITCs): Eligibility and Claim Procedures for Non-Residents

Input tax credits help recover GST/HST paid on business costs tied to taxable sales in Canada.

  • If you’re under the simplified regime, you cannot claim ITCs.
  • Non-residents without a permanent establishment usually can claim only if they register regularly.

To claim ITCs:

  • Keep detailed invoices showing taxes paid.
  • Ensure expenses relate directly to taxable business activities in Canada.
  • Report claims correctly on your filed returns following CRA’s RC4027 guide.

Claiming ITCs wrongly can cause reassessments with added interest and penalties from audits.

Record Keeping and Books Maintenance Requirements

All foreign businesses with GST/HST registration must keep accurate records of all Canadian taxable transactions. This includes:

  • Sales invoices
  • Purchase receipts
  • Import documents when needed
  • Accounting journals
  • Bank statements linked to Canadian activities

You must keep these records for at least six years after the fiscal year ends according to Excise Tax Act rules.[1] Good record keeping helps during audits or reviews by CRA or CBSA and supports compliance across provinces like Ontario with its 13% HST rate example.

Bad bookkeeping can cause mistakes that lead to fines or delayed refunds. Even simplified registrants must prove sales volumes properly.[2]

Using Streamlined Accounting Methods and the Quick Method

Businesses registered regularly can use easier accounting methods approved by CRA like:

  • The Quick Method: calculates net tax owed by using fixed rates instead of tracking every input credit.

This method cuts admin work but limits deductions compared to full accrual accounting.[3]

Simplified registrations can’t use these methods since they don’t claim ITCs; their quarterly returns show gross collections only.[4]

Your registration type decides if you qualify for streamlined methods—get advice first because it affects cash flow management big time.

Verdict: Your choice of accounting method depends on your registration type.

Handling Amendments, Cancellations, and Changes to Registration

If your non-resident GST/HST registration details change — like your address or ownership — tell CRA fast using Form RC1 amendments online or by mail.[5]

If you stop doing business in Canada above thresholds needing registration, request cancellation similarly.[6] Don’t delay; otherwise, CRA might still expect filings and payments for inactive periods.

Reporting changes on time avoids extra assessments beyond your actual business activity.

Worth Knowing

A Canadian GST/HST number is a tax account only. It does not create a Canadian company, and it gives no one the right to work in Canada.

GST/HST Obligations for Digital Services and Marketplace Sales

Non-resident suppliers offering digital services to Canadians might have to register under simplified GST/HST registration Canada if their sales go over $30,000 CAD in 12 months[1]. Here’s what happens then:

  • Registration becomes mandatory.
  • You file returns every quarter with no input tax credits.
  • You charge GST/HST based on where your customer lives.

Marketplace platform operators also have duties. They must collect and send GST/HST for non-resident sellers using their platform[2]. This applies even if the vendor didn’t register.

Digital sellers should check if they fit simplified or regular non-resident GST/HST registration. Simplified suits small digital sales with no physical presence in Canada but doesn’t allow input tax credits.

Rules for Fulfillment Warehouses and Low-Value Imported Goods

When goods sit in Canadian fulfilment warehouses, different rules apply than with direct exports[3]. Using a warehouse in Ontario means:

  • Usually needing regular non-resident GST/HST registration.
  • Charging 13% HST (Ontario rate) when selling to Canadian buyers.

The CBSA’s CARM Client Portal has managed imports since October 21, 2024[4]. Courier shipments from the United States or Mexico are duty-free up to $150 CAD and tax-free up to $40 CAD; from other countries the limit is $20 CAD[5].

Foreign businesses should track where their inventory is. Stock in fulfillment centres can mean doing more than just simplified compliance.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: Moving stock into a Canadian fulfilment warehouse usually takes a seller out of the simplified regime and into regular registration. Please check your registration before your first shipment arrives, not after the first sale.

Treatment of Imports, Exports, and Drop-Shipments under GST/HST

Imports pay 5% federal GST plus provincial rates when they enter Canada[6]. The importer of record—often the foreign seller or their agent—must declare goods via CBSA tools like CARM.

Exports sent out of Canada get zero-rated tax status. But proof of export is needed. Drop-shipment deals where goods pass through third parties need careful tax location checks.

CARM helps with import reporting but doesn’t replace non-resident GST/HST registration when you sell imported goods inside Canada.

Platform Operators and Accommodation Providers’ GST/HST Responsibilities

Since July 1, 2021, platform operators helping non-residents sell must collect and pay taxes[7]:

  • Platforms registering under simplified rules if sales to Canadians pass $30,000 yearly.
  • Charging tax based on where buyers live.

Booking platforms for short-term rentals also follow these rules. They act as registrants making sure taxes are collected even if hosts aren’t registered themselves.

This setup makes it easier for smaller foreign sellers and puts tax duties on platforms recognized by CRA.

Non-Taxable Imports and Customs Duties Related to GST/HST

Some imports don’t pay customs duty but may face other charges:

  • Courier shipments from the United States or Mexico skip duty up to $150 CAD and taxes up to $40 CAD; from other countries both stop at $20 CAD, according to CBSA rules[8].

Customs clearance fees are separate from taxes. Importers should know these fees when planning supply chains since exemption limits don’t remove all paperwork or registrations needed.

Knowing this prevents surprises on costs tied to low-value parcels common among online stores selling to Canadians from abroad.

Foreign Conventions and Installation Services GST/HST Rebates

Some rebates exist for special cases that let you recover or avoid paid taxes:

  • Rebates may apply when installation services come with goods brought temporarily into Canada by foreign businesses[9].

This often happens at conventions or exhibitions where equipment setup counts as zero-rated supply if certain conditions are met. These include how long the equipment stays in Canada, ownership staying outside, or contracts that define responsibilities (CRA Guide RC4027).

Businesses should get advice before claiming these rebates because CRA closely checks that reliefs aren’t misused during temporary international events here.

Frequently Asked Questions on Foreign Business GST Registration Canada

Non-Resident GST/HST FAQ

FAQ
What is the Security Deposit Minimum for non-resident GST/HST registration?+

The CRA sets a security deposit minimum based on estimated net annual taxes. The deposit is generally 50% of estimated net tax for the first year, with a minimum of $5,000; small non-residents and simplified regime registrants do not post one.

How do platform and fulfilment warehouse rules affect GST/HST registration?+

Holding inventory in Canadian warehouses usually triggers regular GST/HST registration. Marketplace platforms have collected GST/HST for non-registered sellers since July 1, 2021.

What are the key 2026 updates impacting foreign business GST registration?+

The main recent changes are the platform operator and fulfilment warehouse rules in force since July 1, 2021, and the CBSA's CARM import system since October 21, 2024.

What is the Courier Low-Value Threshold for GST/HST on imports?+

Courier shipments from the United States or Mexico are duty-free up to $150 CAD and free of GST/HST up to $40 CAD; from other countries the limit is $20 CAD, easing compliance for small parcel imports.

How does the CRA simplified GST/HST registration Canada process work?+

It requires digital sellers whose sales to Canadian consumers pass $30,000 CAD to register quickly online, file quarterly returns, but not claim input tax credits.

What filing frequency options exist under regular and simplified GST/HST registration?+

Regular registrants may file monthly or quarterly. Simplified regime registrants file quarterly only with simpler forms.

How do I apply for a Canadian GST Number through Form RC1?+

Complete Form RC1 with company details and submit it by mail or fax. CRA processes it to assign a Business Number with RT account suffix.

When should a foreign business consider extra-provincial registration besides GST/HST?+

If physically operating or having significant presence in provinces like Quebec, extra-provincial business registrations may be required alongside federal GST/HST accounts.

Can a foreign business use tax software or DIY methods for GST/HST compliance?+

Yes, but complex cases often need CPA help. Open Corporation for $35 offers expert CPA guidance to avoid costly mistakes and penalties.

Risk Warning

Risk Warning: A business that should have registered but did not still owes the GST/HST it should have charged, from the day it crossed the threshold. Please register as soon as your Canadian taxable supplies pass $30,000, rather than waiting for the CRA to contact you.

Verdict

Work out first whether you carry on business in Canada. If you do, or you keep stock here, register under the regular regime once your taxable supplies pass $30,000 and expect to post security.
