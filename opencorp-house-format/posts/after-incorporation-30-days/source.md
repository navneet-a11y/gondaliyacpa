What Incorporation Means for Your Business in Canada
Incorporating a business in Canada means your company becomes its own legal entity. This change gives you benefits like limited liability and some tax breaks. After you incorporate, you’ll want to act fast during the first 30 days. Taking the right steps early helps your corporation follow rules and run smoothly.

Benefits and Legal Implications of Incorporation
Incorporation comes with some important perks:

Limited Liability Protection: You usually don’t owe money personally if the business owes debts.
Corporate Compliance: Following provincial or federal rules keeps your company legit.
Tax Advantages: Corporations often pay less tax on earnings they keep than individuals do.

How Incorporation Separates Personal and Business Liabilities
A corporation stands apart as a separate legal entity. This means debts belong to the company, not to its owners. Your personal stuff stays safe if the business runs into money trouble.
